<section id="s2001"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Background</h3>
<p>Metabolic syndrome (MS) has grown in recognition to contribute to the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis (OA), which is the most prevalent arthritis characterized by joint dysfunction. However, the specific mechanism between OA and MS remains unclear.</p></section><section id="s2002"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<p>The gene expression profiles and clinical information data of OA and MS were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The genes in the key module of MS were identified by weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), which intersected with the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between control and MS samples to obtain hub genes for MS. The potential functions and pathways of hub genes were detected through the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Gene and Genome (KEGG) analyses. The genes involved in the different KEGG pathways between the control and OA samples overlapped with the DEGs between the two groups via the Venn analysis to gain the hub genes for OA affected by MS (MOHGs). Additionally, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) was performed on the MOHGs to establish a diagnostic model for each disease.</p></section><section id="s2003"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p>A total of 61 hub genes for MS were identified that significantly enriched in platelet activation, complement and coagulation cascades, and hematopoietic cell lineage. Besides, 4 candidate genes (ELOVL7, F2RL3, GP9, and ITGA2B) were screened among the 6 MOHGs to construct a diagnostic model, showing good performance for distinguishing controls from patients with MS and OA. GSEA suggested that these diagnostic genes were closely associated with immune response, adipocytokine signaling, fatty acid metabolism, cell cycle, and platelet activation.</p></section><section id="s2004"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h3>
<p>Taken together, we identified 4 potential gene biomarkers for diagnosing MS and OA patients, providing a theoretical basis and reference for the diagnostics and treatment targets of MS and OA.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> osteoarthritis, OA, metabolic syndrome, MS, diagnostic biomarkers, pathogenesis, bioinformatics</p></section></section><section id="s0001"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p>OA is the most prevalent arthritic joint disease and a leading cause of disability and joint pain worldwide, affecting almost 37% of the US population over 60 years of age.<a href="#cit0001" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0001"><sup>1</sup></a> OA is a multifactorial disease, affected by aging, joint trauma, bio-tribology of joint contact surface, genetic susceptibility, cardiovascular disorder, obesity and metabolic dysregulation.<a href="#cit0001" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0001"><sup>1–3</sup></a> It has been long regarded as a degenerative disease of the cartilage that results from simple wear and tear. In recent decades, however, it is more appreciated that OA is a disorder of the joint as a whole, with an increasing amount of research has dug deeper into molecular mechanisms underlying OA pathogenesis.<a href="#cit0004" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0004"><sup>4</sup></a> According to the structure and elements that constitute a synovial joint, the pathology of an OA joint can be divided into the breakdown of cartilage, remodeling of the underlying bone, ectopic bone formation, joint capsule hypertrophy and inflammation of the synovial lining or synovitis, among which the low-grade chronic inflammation plays a central role.<a href="#cit0001" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0001"><sup>1</sup></a> The interplay between the innate and adaptive immune system and inflammatory mediators could lie in the center of a vicious cycle of local tissue damage and failed tissue repair in joints.<a href="#cit0005" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0005"><sup>5</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0006" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0006"><sup>6</sup></a> Chondrocytes and/or their progenitors start secreting matrix-degrading enzymes, cytokines and chemokines when the attempt to repair the damaged cartilage matrix fails.<a href="#cit0002" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0002"><sup>2</sup></a> In turn, inflammation of the synovium ensues, contributing to cartilage degradation. Previous research has demonstrated that inflammatory mediators from activated B lymphocytes and primary T cells and macrophages from OA patients could induce synovial stromal cells and chondrocytes to produce matrix metalloproteases 3 (MMP-3), a tissue-destructive enzyme, and a disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs (ADAMTS).<a href="#cit0007" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0007"><sup>7</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0008" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0008"><sup>8</sup></a> Immune cells, thereby, acquired potential cartilage invasion properties that contribute to the progression of arthritis. Obesity and the associated increase in adipokine levels also add to the inflammatory state in OA.<a href="#cit0002" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0002"><sup>2</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0009" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0009"><sup>9</sup></a></p>
<p>MS, mainly characterized by central obesity, diabetes and insulin resistance, has grown in recognition to contribute to the pathogenesis of OA.<a href="#cit0010" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0010"><sup>10</sup></a> Evidence from epidemiological studies has shown a strong correlation between MS and OA of the knee.<a href="#cit0011" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0011"><sup>11</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0012" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0012"><sup>12</sup></a> This association could be partly explained by a greater body mass in MS that is consequently with an increase in the forces acting on the load-bearing joints. On the other hand, obesity has been linked to the development of OA in non-weight-bearing joints of the hand in a population-based cohort study.<a href="#cit0011" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0011"><sup>11</sup></a> Besides, it is reported in a large Netherlands Epidemiology of Obesity cohort study that fat mass and waist-to-hip ratio were closely related to hand OA, with an association with visceral fat in men.<a href="#cit0013" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0013"><sup>13</sup></a> Leptin, a biomarker of MS, could be involved in OA pathogenesis at a local and systemic level.<a href="#cit0014" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0014"><sup>14</sup></a> Leptin levels in serum/plasma and synovial fluids were associated with joint pain, radiographic progression and knee OA incidence.<a href="#cit0015" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0015"><sup>15</sup></a> Hui et al suggested that leptin from joint white adipose tissues acted as a pro-inflammatory adipokine in cartilage degradation via the increased induction of proteolytic enzymes; it also synergized with IL-1, with a concomitant activation of STAT1, STAT3, STAT5, MAPK (JNK, Erk, p38), Akt and NF-κB signaling pathways, linking obesity and cartilage catabolism in OA.<a href="#cit0016" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0016"><sup>16</sup></a> Additionally, hyperlipidemia and hypertension have been reported to increase the risk of OA progression, as components of MS that are independent of the BMI.<a href="#cit0017" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0017"><sup>17</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0018" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0018"><sup>18</sup></a></p>
<p>So far, the metabolic OA phenotype has been well identified. However, the underlying mechanisms between OA and MS remain to be elucidated. In this study, we explored shared biomarkers between OA and MS and the mechanisms through which MS could influence the development of OA. Subsequently, we aimed to uncover the novel insight of the comorbidity between MS and OA, and identify candidate genes for the diagnosis and therapeutic targets.</p></section><section id="s0002"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials and Methods</h2>

<p>WGCNA can be utilized to identify global gene expression profiles as well as co-expressed genes. The relationship between the genes and the phenotype of MS was investigated by the WGCNA algorithm. In our study, the WGCNA analysis was performed on the data in the <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE145412" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSE145412</a> dataset for MS using the WGCNA package in R.<a href="#cit0019" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0019"><sup>19</sup></a> Firstly, the clustering analysis for all samples was operated to remove the outlier samples, which can ensure the accuracy of the subsequent study. All gene pairs were then analyzed by the Pearson algorithm and a weighted adjacency matrix was created to calculate the topological overlap measurement (TOM) representing the overlap in the shared neighbors. Secondly, based on the distribution of the criteria scale-free, an optimal soft-thresholding power was selected where the co-expression similarity was raised to generate an approximate scale-free network. Then, the dynamic tree cutting method was used to overlap the similar modules identified by the TOM with the minimum number of 30 genes in each module. Finally, the module-trait relationships were detected by Pearson correlation analysis and further screened a key model highly related to the trait of MS. The <em>P</em>-value less than 0.05 was regarded as a significant correlation.</p></section><section id="s0002-s2003"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Differentially Expressed Analysis</h3>
<p>Between the control samples and MS patient samples, the limma package in R was applied to conduct the differentially expressed analysis of the data in the <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE145412" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSE145412</a> dataset. The genes that met the threshold of <em>P</em>-value &lt; 0.01 were considered as DEGs (<span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supplementary Table 1</a></span>). The Venn analysis (<span class="text-underline"><a href="http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/</a></span>) was utilized to identify hub genes for MS by integrating DEGs and genes in the selected key module identified by the WGCNA analysis.</p></section><section id="s0002-s2004"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Functional Enrichment Analysis</h3>
<p>GO annotation (<span class="text-underline"><a href="http://www.geneontology.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.geneontology.org/</a></span>) and KEGG pathway (<span class="text-underline"><a href="http://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html</a></span>) analyses were performed on the hub genes for MS to obtain the possible biological processes of these genes using the clusterProfiler package in R. GO analysis can be divided into three categories: molecular function (MF), biological process (BP), and cellular components (CC).<a href="#cit0020" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0020"><sup>20</sup></a>
<em>P</em>-value &lt; 0.05 indicates a statistically significant result. The detailed information of GO and KEGG results was illustrated in <span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supplementary Tables 2</a></span> and <span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">3</a></span>.</p></section><section id="s0002-s2005"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Screening of MOHGs</h3>
<p>According to the enriched pathways of hub genes of MS selected from the KEGG analysis, single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was adopted using the gene set variation analysis (GSVA) package in R to calculate the ssGSEA score of the control and OA patient samples in the <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE51588" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSE51588</a> dataset, and further screened the significantly different KEGG pathways between the control and OA patient samples<a href="#cit0021" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0021"><sup>21</sup></a> based on the ssGSEA score.Genes involved in the identified different KEGG pathways between the two groups were further identified and then intersected with OA-related DEGs (<span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supplementary Table 4</a></span>) to acquire the MOHGs for subsequent study. |log<sub>2</sub>FC| &gt; 1 and <em>P</em>-value &lt; 0.05 were defined as screening standard to obtain DEGs for OA.</p></section><section id="s0002-s2006"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">LASSO Analysis for MS and OA</h3>
<p>Based on the above results, the MOHGs were considered as genes both related to OA and MS. To explore the diagnostic value of these genes, LASSO regression was then operated to select the candidate genes significantly associated with the diagnosis of MS and OA among the harvested MOHGs using the R package named Glmnet. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was conducted and the area under the curve (AUC) of the ROC curve was calculated using the pROC package in R to detect the diagnostic ability of the constructed model built on all candidate genes and each candidate gene for MS and OA, respectively.</p></section><section id="s0002-s2007"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">GSEA for OA</h3>
<p>In the <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE51588" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSE51588</a> dataset, samples of OA were classified into high- and low-expression groups according to the expression level of each diagnostic gene. The potential functions and pathways of each diagnostic gene were analyzed by GSEA (<span class="text-underline"><a href="https://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/index.jsp" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/index.jsp</a></span>) using the clusterProfiler package of the R based on GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. The enrichment result was regarded significant difference if the value of <em>P</em> &lt; 0.05.</p></section><section id="s0002-s2008"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Statistical Analysis</h3>
<p>R software was performed to analyze the statistical analyses in our study. Wilcoxon test was utilized for the significance of differences between two groups. The ggplot2 package in R was applied to draw the box plot and heatmap plot. Data were considered to be significant differences when <em>P</em> &lt; 0.05.</p></section></section><section id="s0003"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h2>
<section id="s0003-s2001"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Identification of Hub Genes for MS</h3>
<p>WGCNA was performed to construct a co-expression network for MS based on the <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE145412" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSE145412</a> dataset. To begin this process, sample 18, an obvious outlier after clustering analysis for all MS samples, was removed with the cut-off value = 20,000 (<span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supplementary Figure 1A</a></span> and <span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">B</a></span>). The optimal soft threshold power was necessary for building the scale-free network for MS, and the soft threshold power was chosen = 8 (scale-free R<sup>2</sup> = 0.9), ensuring the network was scale-free (<a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">Figure 2A</a>). The dynamic tree cutting method identified 51 modules labeled with different colors with the minimum number of genes in each module to 30 (<a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">Figure 2B</a>). The relationship between the identified modules and the trait (MS phenotype) was illustrated in <a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">Figure 2C</a>, <span class="text-underline"><a href="https://www.dovepress.com/get_supplementary_file.php?f=325561.pdf" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supplementary Figure 1C</a></span>. The sky-blue module (R = 0.48, <em>P</em> = 0.007) and indianred4 module (R = 0.4, <em>P</em> = 0.03) were positively correlated with clinical trait of MS. The heatmap displayed the TOM of 400 randomly selected genes, indicating the independence of each module (<a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">Figure 2D</a>). Besides, 372 DEGs were screened between the control and MS patient samples with 195 upregulated and 177 downregulated genes (<a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">Figure 2E</a> and <a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">F</a>). The result of Venn analysis suggested that 61 hub genes related to MS were identified for further study by overlapping the 372 DEGs and 212 genes in the key module (<a href="#f0002" class="usa-link">Figure 2G</a>).</p>

<p>4 genes were identified, namely ELOVL7, F2RL3, GP9, and ITGA2B, as candidate genes by LASSO regression analysis among these MOHGs to develop a diagnostic model for OA (<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Figure 5A</a>). The AUC of the constructed diagnostic model was 1, indicating a better diagnostic power and accuracy for diagnosing OA (<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Figure 5B</a>). Interestingly, we discovered the differentiated expression levels of 4 candidate genes in OA and MS samples. All of the 4 candidate genes were decreased in patients with OA, while increased in MS patients compared with their corresponding control samples (<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Figure 5C</a> and <a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">D</a>). For OA, the AUC value of ELOVL7, F2RL3, GP9, and ITGA2B reached 0.785, 0.74, 0.758, and 0.785, respectively, which showed an excellent diagnostic ability (<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Figure 5E</a>). In addition, ELOVL7 (AUC = 0.82), F2RL3 (AUC = 0.816), GP9 (AUC = 0.824) and ITGA2B (AUC = 0.82) had promising reliability for diagnosing MS patients (<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Figure 5F</a>). These findings suggested that 4 candidate genes all possessed a discriminatory power to stratify patients from controls for both diseases.</p>

<p>In order to investigate the possible biological processes associated with these candidate genes, the GSEA method was conducted. The enrichment analysis demonstrated that ELOVL7 was centralized on the olfactory transduction, fatty acid metabolism, and immune-related signaling pathway, such as adaptive immune response and antigen receptor signaling pathway (<a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">Figure 6A</a> and <a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">B</a>). F2RL3 was mainly implicated in cytoplasmic translation, cytosolic ribosome, and some metabolism pathways, such as insulin signaling pathway, adipocytokine signaling pathway, and glycolysis gluconeogenesis (<a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">Figure 6C</a> and <a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">D</a>). For GP9, cell cycle-related pathways, DNA replication, oxidative phosphorylation, and primary immunodeficiency were mostly enriched pathways (<a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">Figure 6E</a> and <a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">F</a>). GSEA results suggested that ITGA2B was mostly involved in the cell cycle, DNA replication, and some immune-related response (<a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">Figure 6G</a> and <a href="#f0006" class="usa-link">H</a>).</p>

<p>Both OA and MS are major public health problems with increasing prevalence and disability.<a href="#cit0006" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0006"><sup>6</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0025" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0025"><sup>25</sup></a> MS is not well defined as one disorder, but it can be extracted into four central features: insulin resistance, visceral obesity, atherogenic dyslipidemia and hypertension.<a href="#cit0026" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0026"><sup>26</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0027" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0027"><sup>27</sup></a> Metabolic OA, a newly characterized subtype of OA, links individual MS components or MS as a whole with OA phenotype. Increasing evidence has suggested that OA is a whole-joint disease that is influenced by systemic mediators, inflammaging, innate immunity and the low-grade inflammation induced by MS.<a href="#cit0028" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0028"><sup>28</sup></a> Adipokines, hyperglycaemia and endocrine imbalance are the most common pathways that could be responsible for the involvement of MS in OA pathogenesis.<a href="#cit0029" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0029"><sup>29</sup></a> However, the specific mechanism between OA and MS has not been fully elucidated. Our results revealed that 4 candidate genes (ELOVL7, F2RL3, GP9, and ITGA2B) were involved in the mechanism through which MS could influence the development of OA, and these genes could be potential biomarkers for the diagnosis of OA and MS or metabolic OA.</p>
<p>Our results suggested that ELOVL7, F2RL3, GP9 and ITGA2B gene have potential diagnostic value for both OA and MS. Very-long-chain elongases are required for the synthesis of essential fatty acids in non-ruminants.<a href="#cit0030" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0030"><sup>30</sup></a> Fatty acid elongase 7 (ELOVL7) has been reported to be preferentially involved in elongation of saturated very-long-chain fatty acids and lipid accumulation that could increase MS activity.<a href="#cit0031" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0031"><sup>31</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0032" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0032"><sup>32</sup></a> In addition, OA is associated with increased angiogenesis and fatty acid oxidation (FAO).<a href="#cit0033" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0033"><sup>33</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0034" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0034"><sup>34</sup></a> Recent study has demonstrated that low lipid levels are the main nutritional determinant for the chondrogenic commitment of skeletal progenitor cells.<a href="#cit0035" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0035"><sup>35</sup></a> High rate of FAO in chondrocytes could impair their function and thus contribute to OA phenotype. All these phenomena suggest that manipulation of ELOVL7 and its related lipid metabolism could be a therapeutic target for OA and MS.</p>
<p>F2RL3 (F2R Like Thrombin or Trypsin Receptor 3) is a gene that encodes the protein named protease-activated receptor 4 (PAR-4) or coagulation factor II (thrombin) receptor-like 3. Enrichment analyses in our study suggested the potential role of F2RL3 in platelet activation that could contribute to MS. Previous study has reported that soluble tissue factors contribute to inflammatory arthritis through a thrombin-dependent pathway, in which PAR-4-induced platelet activation is an essential step.<a href="#cit0036" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0036"><sup>36</sup></a> Besides, increasing evidence has linked MS and OA with platelet dysfunction. A strong positive correlation between blood platelet count and the number of present MS components was documented upon adjustment for a variety of biosocial factors (age, smoking, alcohol use, and physical activity).<a href="#cit0037" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0037"><sup>37</sup></a> Kwon et al revealed that high platelet counts within the normal range are significantly associated with knee and hip OA in Korean women over 50 years.<a href="#cit0038" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0038"><sup>38</sup></a> A possible explanation lies in that platelets are the major source of the cytokines such as IL-1β, and increased activation of them is believed to be involved in the development and progression of inflammation.<a href="#cit0039" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0039"><sup>39</sup></a></p>
<p>In addition to F2RL3, GP9 and ITGA2B genes were found to be involved in platelet activation. ITGA2B encodes integrin alpha-IIb/beta-3, which is a receptor for fibronectin, fibrinogen, plasminogen, prothrombin, thrombospondin.<a href="#cit0040" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0040"><sup>40</sup></a> Following activation, integrin alpha-IIb/beta-3 brings about platelet-platelet interaction through binding of soluble fibrinogen, leading to rapid platelet aggregation. GP9 (glycoprotein IX platelet) encodes platelet glycoprotein IX. Defects in GP9 could lead to Bernard-Soulier syndrome and thrombocytopenia; among its related pathways are GP1b-IX-V activation signaling and hematopoietic stem cell differentiation.<a href="#cit0041" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0041"><sup>41</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#cit0042" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0042"><sup>42</sup></a> Although so far the expression levels of them are not reported in MS studies, our results indicated higher expression of them in MS samples compared with normal controls potentially contributes to platelet-related inflammation that could stabilize the MS phenotype. Interestingly, we found adverse expression of GP9 and ITGA2B in MS and OA, suggesting their different role in OA pathogenesis.</p>
<p>GSEA analysis of GP9 and ITGA2B gene suggested that cell cycle-related pathways and DNA replication were involved in OA progression. The lower expression of GP9 and ITGA2B in OA samples compared with normal groups in the OA dataset suggested a decline in OA cell proliferation. In consistence with our results, Wang et al reported that human OA cartilage and synovium expressed very little αIIb mRNA (ITGA2B).<a href="#cit0043" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0043"><sup>43</sup></a> Increasing evidence has emerged focusing on the harmful biological mechanisms of inflammatory factors in OA joints. However, the understanding of how inflammation impacts cartilage regeneration remains unclear.<a href="#cit0044" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0044"><sup>44</sup></a> Recent study demonstrated that impaired chondrocyte function was associated with G0/G1 cell-cycle arrest and concomitant inhibition of DNA replication and cell proliferation.<a href="#cit0045" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="cit0045"><sup>45</sup></a> According to these clues, GP9 and ITGA2B could be involved in a novel mechanism through which MS could contribute to OA progression, potentially connecting OA cartilage regeneration with impaired cell proliferation and concomitant cell-cycle arrest.</p>
<p>However, our study has several limitations. First, the number of samples in the raw dataset was relatively small; further investigations based on a larger sample should be performed. Second, hub genes were identified using bioinformatics analysis; thus, experimental studies are needed to validate our results. Thirdly, since subjects that have both OA and MS were not explored, the validity of our results needs further experimental verification. Despite these limitations, this study provided some new insights into the biomarkers and pathogenesis of comorbid MS and OA. Further studies are needed to corroborate these findings and investigate the potential mechanisms highlighting comorbidity between OA and MS.</p></section><section id="s0005"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h2>
<p>In summary, the present study identified 4 potential diagnostic biomarkers, including ELOVL7, F2RL3, GP9, and ITGA2B for OA and MS based on comprehensive bioinformatics methods, which may provide new insights into the diagnosis and treatment of comorbid OA and MS. However, further studies are necessary to confirm these findings in our research on MS and OA pathogenesis due to some limitations mentioned above.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgments</h2>
