<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Background</h3>
<p id="Par1">Acne vulgaris (AV) is a chronic inflammatory dermatological condition, and its underlying pathogenesis may be associated with dyslipidemia. Omega-3 fatty acids regulates lipid metabolism and reduce inflammation. However, their effects on blood lipids in AV have not yet been reported. This study’s objective is to investigate the serum lipid metabolomic differences between patients and healthy individuals and explore the effects of isotretinoin combined with omega-3 fatty acids on serum lipid metabolism in AV.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<p id="Par2">Between July 2020 and June 2021, 46 patients with acne vulgaris and 20 healthy controls were recruited from the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University. Untreated patients were randomly allocated to receive oral isotretinoin combined with or without omega-3 fatty acids for 6 months. Serum lipid metabolomics was performed using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS). Statistical analysis of the lipid metabolites was conducted by partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA). Correlations between differential lipid metabolites and disease severity indicators were tested by Spearman’s correlation analysis. Metabolic pathways in the isotretinoin and omega-3 combination treatment group were analyzed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database.</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p id="Par3">PLS-DA showed a clear separation of serum lipid metabolites between healthy controls and AV patients, with the combination treatment group showing a trend towards the healthy controls. The lipid metabolite composition of the combination treatment group showed significant changes, with 245 differential metabolites identified before and after treatment (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05). The Spearman’s coefficient showed a strong correlation between the serum level of StE (20:5) (6.44 ± 0.33 vs.7.18 ± 0.22, <em>P</em> &lt; 0.001) and the Global Acne Grading System score (<em>R</em> = 0.73, <em>P</em> &lt; 0.001). The KEGG analysis identified 18 different pathways before and after treatment in the combination treatment group.</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusions</h3>
<p id="Par4">We found that omega-3 fatty acid supplementation improved the clinical responses in AV patients, correlating with improved lipid metabolism, thereby confirming its potential as a valuable adjunct therapeutic strategy.</p></section><section id="sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Trial registration</h3>
<p id="Par5">The comprehensive clinical trial protocol has been retrospectively registered on the ISRCTN Registry of Clinical Trials website (NO. ISRCTN31758284) on 2022/07/09.</p></section><section id="sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Supplementary Information</h3>
<p>The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-026-02902-9.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Acne vulgaris, Lipid metabolism, Isotretinoin, Omega-3 fatty acids, Combined Modality Therapy</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Background</h2>
<p id="Par6">Acne vulgaris (AV) is a chronic inflammatory condition that primarily affects the sebaceous gland units ranks the eighth most frequent cutaneous disorder with a global morbidity of up to 9.38% [<a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>]. Traditionally, the pathogenesis of AV is thought to involve multiple factors, including androgen-mediated increased sebum secretion, altered keratinization, inflammation, and <em>Cutibacterium acnes</em> colonization [<a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>]. Several investigations have suggested that dysregulation of serum lipid metabolism represents a contributing factor in the onset and advancement of AV condition, including dyslipidemia, such as elevated levels of serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglyceride (TG), cholesterol (TC) and free fatty acids (FFA) [<a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a>]. Despite it being a major global health issue, existing treatment options for AV are limited by adverse reactions, making drug safety and efficacy the primary considerations in clinical decisions. The existing traditional treatments, such as using isotretinoin, have been confirmed to cause side effects, including cheilitis, dryness, photosensitivity, teratogenicity, and depressive mood [<a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a>]. Additionally, research on the utility of monitored isotretinoin intervention for AV has revealed that patients may experience dyslipidemia, such as hypertriglyceridemia, even during treatment [<a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a>]. Therefore, safer therapies and adjuvant medications are urgently required for patients with AV.</p>
<p id="Par7">Omega-3 (ω-3) fatty acids belongs to the polyunsaturated category often obtained from marine fish and certain plant oils [<a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">6</a>]. Compelling evidence suggests that ω-3 fatty acids, owing to it’s anti-inflammatory capability, offers a promising new therapeutic approach for inflammatory skin conditions, including psoriasis and atopic dermatitis [<a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a>, <a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a>]. Moreover, its well-recognized efficacy against AV has been reinforced by positive outcomes in clinical trials [<a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">9</a>]. Concurrently, the capacity of ω-3 fatty acids to reduce lipid levels has attracted widespread attention in populations with overweight individuals or those with hyperlipidemia [<a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a>, <a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">11</a>]. Despite recognizing both anti-acne and lipid-modulating capability of ω-3 fatty acids, whether its lipid-regulating capacity contributes to its therapeutic efficacy in AV remains unexplored. Previous studies had not explored the potential of ω-3 fatty acids on the serum lipid profile thoroughly, specifically in AV.</p>
<p id="Par8">Therefore, the current randomized controlled trial (RCT) aimed to assess the benifit and potential mechanisms of the combination of isotretinoin and ω-3 fatty acids intervention in clinical efficacy and serum lipid metabolites in AV patients. The findings provided a mechanistic rationale to support ω-3 fatty acid supplementation acts a novel and safe adjunctive strategy for AV management.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h2>
<section id="Sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Population</h3>
<p id="Par9">Prior to the study, all participants allowed the use of data and samples for scientific purposes and signed written informed consent form. From July 2020 to June 2021, 46 patients diagnosed with moderate-to-severe AV, matched along with 20 healthy controls (the HC group) were enrolled in the study from the dermatology department of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University. Disease severity was determined by the Global Acne Grading System (GAGS) score, with GAGS ≥ 19 defined as moderate to severe AV [<a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a>]. Beyond adhering to the criteria established in prior study [<a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a>], the participants who had already received intervention of isotretinoin, antibiotics, glucocorticoids, ω-3 fatty acids, immunosuppressive drugs, or herbal treatments within the past 6 months, or were unwilling to sign the informed consent form, or were known to be allergic to the intervention were all excluded. Moreover, daily lifestyle changes were recommended during the treatment, including less sweet intake, a high-fat diet, and dairy products. The Ethics Committee had given approval for the use of human subjects in this study (No. KY2020115).</p></section><section id="Sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Clinical trial design</h3>
<p id="Par10">The patients were randomized to receive orally taken isotretinoin treatment (Huabang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. Chongqing, China), either combined with (the AVW group) 2400 mg/day ω-3 fatty acids (Nature Made, LA, USA) or monotonously (the AVP group) for 6 months. When the medicines were administered to patients, identifiable packaging was removed so that the patients could not know the name of the medicine they were taking. The isotretinoin dosage was adjusted based on drug tolerance and treatment efficacy. Therapeutic effects were evaluated using the GAGS score. When GAGS score was between 19 and 30, the isotretinoin dose was set at 0.5 mg/ kg/day; when the score exceeded 30, the dose was applied to 1.0 mg/ kg/day. Demographic data of the participants, including sex, age, body mass index (BMI), and disease duration were recorded at enrollment. GAGS score evaluation, skin analysis by vital imaging system for aesthetic analysis (VISIA), and blood serum sample were collectted at baseline and at the end of treatment. The study used the CONSORT reporting guidelines to draft this manuscript and the CONSORT reporting checklist for editing [<a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a>].</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Efficacy evaluation criteria and VISIA</h3>
<p id="Par11">GAGS divides acne-prone areas into the nose and chin area (1 point), forehead (2 points), left cheek (2 points), right cheek (2 points), and chest and upper back (3 points) regions, based on the approximate area and density of the pilosebaceous units. Different zones correspond to different weight scores, 1–18 points for mild, 19–30 points for moderate, 31–38 points for severe, and ≥ 39 points for extremely severe. Only moderate to severe cases were enrolled in this study.</p>
<p id="Par12">A VISIA-CR™ imaging system (Canfield Scientific, Inc., USA) was used to evaluate the average percentage of facial red and purple areas of the participants before and after treatment. The red area index reflects the condition of capillaries in patients with AV, whose blood vessels and hemoglobin in the dermal papilla layer of the skin appear reddish. On the other hand, the purple area index measures porphyrins, which are metabolites of <em>Propionibacterium acnes</em> at the hair follicle opening and appear as brick-red highlights. A higher percentage of VISIA indices indicates milder disease severity.</p></section><section id="Sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Sample collection and detection</h3>
<p id="Par13">Serum were collected from 2 mL of venous blood at fasting in non-anticoagulant tubes at the beginning of enrollment and the end of their 6-month treatment; samples were centrifuged after collection at 3,000 rpm for 15 min after 30 min of resting at 20–25 ℃, and subsequently stored in a -80 ℃ refrigerator. An automated biochemical detector (Synchron LX-20; Beckman Coulter, USA) was used to determine the blood glucose (Glu), TC, TG, LDL-C, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels. Insulin resistance was assessed by homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR). Fasting insulin (INS) levels were measured by the ELISA kit (Promega, USA).</p></section><section id="Sec7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Serum sample processing for lipidomics analysis</h3>
<p id="Par14">For lipid extraction, 200 µl of serum samples, 80 µl of methanol (Cat: M742213, Macklin, Shanghai, China) and 400 µl of methyl-tert-butyl ether (MTBE, Cat: B802533, Macklin, Shanghai, China) were mixed; the mixtures were vortexed for 30 s and then ultrasonically extracted for 30 min on 40 kHz power at 5 ℃. The samples were settled at -20 ℃ for 30 min then centrifuged (13000 rpm, 4 ℃) for 15 min. Next, supernatant was drawn out and dried in a vacuum concentrator (Cat: Y-TN-<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/C19819" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">C19819</a>, Yuyan Machinery Co., LTD, Shanghai, China), and 100 µl of isopropanol (Cat: I766839, Macklin, Shanghai, China): acetonitrile (1:1, v/v, Cat: A801556, Macklin, Shanghai, China) solution was added for re-dissolution. After 30 s of vortexing, the mixtures were ultrasonically extracted in ice water bath and centrifuged (13000 rpm, 4 ℃) for 5 min then transfer 80 µl of supernatant to UPLC-Q Exactive System (Thermo, CA).</p></section><section id="Sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Acquisition of LC-MS/MS data</h3>
<p id="Par15">The study obtained metabolites were processed by an Ultra High Performance Liquid Chromatography Tandem Fourier Transform Mass Spectrometry UPLC-Q Exactive system (Thermo Scientific™ Vanquish™ UHPLC, CA). Two microliters of the sample were separated by a BEH C18 column (0.1 cm × 0.0021 cm i.d., 1.7 μm) and then operated by mass spectrometry. Solvent A, which contained 0.1% formic acid (Catalog: F809715, Macklin, Shanghai, China) dissolved in 50% acetonitrile, 10 mmol/L ammonium acetate solution; solvent B, which consisted of 2 mmol/L ammonium and 0.02% formic acid in acetate solution mixed with isopropanol, acetonitrile and water in a volumetric ratio of 10:88:2, these two distinct solvent systems in which the mobile phase was composed. The solvent gradient elution protocol was established as follows: an initial composition of 65% solvent A and 35% solvent B was maintained from 0 to 4 min, transitioning to 40% A and 60% B. Subsequently, from 4 to 12 min, the gradient shifted from 40% A:60% B to 15% A:85% B. Between 12 and 21 min, the composition was further adjusted from 15% A:85% B to 0% A:100% B. A rapid re-equilibration step occurred from 24.0 to 24.1 min, returning the mobile phase to 65% A and 35% B. Finally, from 24.1 to 28 min, the system was held isocratically at 65% solvent A and 35% solvent B to ensure column reconditioning. Chromatographic separation was performed with a constant flow rate of 0.40 mL/min and a column temperature maintained at 40 °C. For mass spectrometric detection, signals were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes simultaneously. The operational parameters were configured under mass scan range (m/z), 200–2000, spray voltage, 3.00 kV or both modes; ion source temperature, 370 °C, collision energy, 20–60 V.</p></section><section id="Sec10"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Data process and statistical analysis</h3>
<p id="Par16">The raw LC-MS data were imported into LipidSearch (Thermo Fisher Scientific) software to obtain a data matrix for subsequent analysis. Multivariate statistical analyses were applied from the Majorbio Cloud website (https://cloud.majorbio.com) utilizing the R programming environment (version 4.1.1). Partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) or orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was employed to provide a comprehensive view of the metabolic dataset, facilitating the assessment of overall clustering patterns, discernible trends, and identification of potential outliers. Volcano plot analysis of differential expression provided dual validation (P-value+ FC), emphasizing statistical significance, magnitude of biological change, and the direction of changes before and after treatment. The metabolites of significantly difference were selected out on the basis of the variable weight value (VIP) ≥ 1, fold change (FC) &gt; 1/ FC &lt; 1 and P value &lt; 0.05. The biochemical pathways and associated metabolic processes were identified through metabolic enrichment analysis and pathway mapping by the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (kegg_v2021. 09. 18) and Scipy. stats (Python package) (https://docs.scipy. org/doc/scipy/) to identify those significantly enriched pathways.</p></section><section id="Sec11"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Statistical analysis用means ± standard deviation</h3>
<p id="Par17">All statistical analyses were conducted by the SPSS (version 25.0). Quantitative data are presented as means ± standard deviation. This study employed the Mann-Whitney U test to assess the differences in the data both between and within the two groups, the Wilcoxon test to appraise the intervention before and after within the same group, the Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient to estimate the monotonic correlation between variables. Statistical significance standard was set at <em>P</em> &lt; 0.05. GraphPad Prism (version 8.0) was employed for data visualization.</p></section></section><section id="Sec12"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h2>
<section id="Sec14"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Background and efficacy of combination therapy in AV</h3>
<p id="Par18">Three patients withdrew from the AVP group due to nosebleeds, generalized eczema, and severely chapped lips, and three were lost to follow-up in the AVW group. Twenty patients were eventually included in each group for the clinical analysis (Supplement. 1). The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are exhibited in Table <a href="#Tab1" class="usa-link">1</a>. The differences in baseline data among the three groups in terms of gender, BMI, HOMA-IR, INS, HDL-C, LDL-C, TG, and TC were not statistically significant (<em>P</em><sub><em>1</em></sub> &gt; 0.05). While there was an age difference existed in the HC group and the patient group (<em>P</em><sub><em>1</em></sub> &lt; 0.001), all individuals enrolled in the study were within the 18–30 age range specified by the inclusion criteria. Regardless of the application of ω-3 fatty acids, the intervention group showed varying degrees of improvement in facial GAGS scores (<em>P</em><sub><em>2</em></sub> &lt; 0.001, <em>P</em><sub><em>3</em></sub> &lt; 0.001), sclererythrin levels (<em>P</em><sub><em>2</em></sub> &lt; 0.001, <em>P</em><sub><em>3</em></sub> &lt; 0.001) and percentage of red areas (<em>P</em><sub><em>2</em></sub> &lt; 0.001, <em>P</em><sub><em>3</em></sub> = 0.006) after treatment. More importantly, the AVW group achieved a significantly greater improvement in GAGS scores (<em>P</em><sub><em>4</em></sub> = 0.023 for AVP1 vs. AVW1) and TG levels (<em>P</em><sub><em>4</em></sub> = 0.01 for AVP1 vs. AVW1) than the AVP group.</p>

<p id="Par20">Lipid metabolism was investigated to explore the effect of ω-3 fatty acids on serum lipid metabolomics composition, a total of 1021 quantifiable peaks were detected among the 60 serum samples. Due to lipid extraction failure, a total of five samples were excluded, two from the HC group, two from the AVP group, and one from the AVW group, and 258 lipid metabolites were finally measured after removing all false positives and noise data. A PLS-DA model was applied on the complete dataset to estimat the clustering tendency from the patients before and after treatment and the HC group. As shown in Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>A, the separation trend of metabolite compositions across the HC group and the patients in both AVP0 and AVW0 groups was clearly observed at baseline, especially in the cation model (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>A), suggesting that the lipid metabolite structures in AV patients differ from those in HC. Following intervention, the lipid metabolism profiles of both AVW1 and AVP1 groups drifted closer to that of the HC group (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>C, D). However, the drift was more pronounced in AVW1 group, rather than AVP1 group, a disparity more evident in the cation model (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>C). Further permutation tests for the anion and cation models established by PLS-DA showed that the models had no overfitting problem and had good reliability and validity (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>E-H).</p>

<p id="Par27">The heatmap shows significantly different lipid metabolites and their main biological functions before and after the combined treatment with isotretinoin and ω-3 fatty acids (Fig. <a href="#Fig3" class="usa-link">3</a>). Correlation analysis indicated that metabolites located in the upper region exhibited a negative association with the GAGS score, that downregulation of their expression could have potential positive therapeutic effects on patients with AV and vice versa; metabolites in the lower part were positively correlated with the GAGS score, that upregulation of their expression could have potential negative therapeutic effects on patients with AV. Next, Spearman’s analysis was conducted between the metabolites listed in Table <a href="#Tab2" class="usa-link">2</a> and the GAGS score, which denotes the severity of acne. Among the metabolites, strong co-expression was observed between the serum level of StE (20:5) and the GAGS score (<em>R</em> = 0.73, <em>P</em> &lt; 0.001) while the remaining metabolites were moderately correlated (Fig. <a href="#Fig3" class="usa-link">3</a>). Meanwhile, the serum level of StE (20:5) in AV patients decreased compared to that in HC (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.001), suggesting that it could be a key substance for the intervention of ω-3 fatty acids in lipid metabolites (Table <a href="#Tab2" class="usa-link">2</a>).</p>

<p id="Par31">Previous studies had confirmed the presence of lipid metabolism disorders in patients with AV. Comparative analysis and in-vitro epidermal models have shown that patients with AV have higher skin fatty acid levels and elevated serum TC, TG, and LDL-C levels than healthy individuals [<a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a>]. However, the lipid metabolism disorder may exacerbate inflammation [<a href="#CR16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR16">16</a>]. Specifically, excessive unsaturated fatty acids (UFAs) in sebum are decomposed by bacteria to produce pro-inflammatory lipids such as leukotriene B4 (LTB4), which might lead to skin barrier damage and subsequent thickening of the hair follicle wall and stratum corneum, resulting in inflammation [<a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a>].</p>
<p id="Par32">Oral isotretinoin, a classic traditional therapy for treating moderate to severe AV, has been reported to cause dose-related adverse reactions, such as dyslipidemia, manifesting with an increase in serum TG, stimulates the production and release of very low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (VLDL-C), leading to elevated levels of LDL-C and apolipoprotein B (apoB), alongside a reduction in HDL-C [<a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a>]. Consistent with these findings, although isotretinoin treatment alleviates disease severity, differences still exist in serum lipidomics between healthy controls and patients with AV after treatment. This suggests that the therapy may not fully recover the systemic metabolic disturbances. Even after the skin lesions are alleviated, patients might retain an altered lipid metabolic state induced by the drug itself, a phenomenon that occurs during drug-induced persistent metabolic states and tolerance [<a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">19</a>]. This persistent dysregulation could partly explain disease recurrence in some patients after treatment cessation as well as the observed metabolic side effects associated with long-term use, such as dyslipidemia and insulin resistance [<a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">20</a>]. However, persistent abnormalities in the lipidomic profile following systemic treatment might provide a molecular basis for understanding the potential metabolic risks associated with isotretinoin use.</p>
<p id="Par33">Notably, the effect of ω-3 fatty acids has received widespread attention, being capable of promoting the clearance of VLDL-C by reducing its synthesis and secretion in the liver, thereby lowering serum TG levels and increasing HDL-C content, which is believed to alleviate the lipid metabolism disorders [<a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a>]. In rat models, the levels of blood TG, TC, and LDL-C in the ω-3 fatty acid supplementation group were significantly decreased [<a href="#CR22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR22">22</a>]. The absence of dramatic fluctuations in serum lipid levels post intervention could be attributed to a potential linear relationship between the ω-3 intervention duration and its metabolic effects [<a href="#CR23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR23">23</a>]. An obvious regulatory effect was observed in the subsequent lipid metabolomic analysis. Stronger evidence suggested that ω-3 fatty acids have a lipid-decreasing effect in other metabolic diseases. The ω-3 fatty acids supplementation in nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) has confirmed an association with reduced gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) activity, decreased liver fat in weight loss individuals, and amelioration in plasma lipid profiles; in a meta-analysis investigating the potential of ω-3 fatty acids in gestational diabetes treatment, ω-3 fatty acid supplements were validated to reduce TG and VLDL-C and increased HDL-C while lowering fasting plasma glucose, levels of inflammatory factors, and reducing insulin resistance [<a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a>, <a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">25</a>]. Both published literature and the current study supported that ω-3 fatty acid supplementation is beneficial for correcting lipid metabolism disorders in AV.</p>
<p id="Par34">The current study revealed that the metabolic profiles of both intervention groups were similar to those of healthy controls in the PLS-DA score plot, indicating that the interventions effectively modulated the lipid metabolism in AV patients. Notably, the metabolic profiles of the two interventions exhibited modest separation, particularly in the anion model. Lipid metabolism is a conserved biological pathway, and therapeutic interventions typically induce targeted modulation of certain lipid substances rather than a drastic metabolic reprogramming [<a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a>]. Since isotretinoin was applied in both interventions, its metabolic effects may have partially overshadowed the modifications conferred by ω-3 fatty acids, making the PLS-DA model exhibit limited distinction between the two groups. Despite the modest separation, a permutation test effectively confirmed the validity of the PLS-DA model, excluding the possibility of random separation. Subsequent OPLS-DA filtered out intragroup unrelated variations, clearly demonstrating a significant separation between the AVP and AVW groups. This confirmed that the isotretinoin and ω-3 fatty acid combination therapy indeed introduced beneficial metabolic moderation.</p>
<p id="Par35">Following ω-3 fatty acid supplementation, DG(18:1/22:6) and DG(20:5/18:2) exhibited pronounced differences and displayed a clear trend towards normalization. Both the DG species are long-chain molecules, with the former containing a docosahexaenoic acid chain (DHA, 22:6) and the latter containing an eicosapentaenoic acid chain (EPA, 20:5). Through which ω-3 fatty acids exert therapeutic benefits are the main components DHA and EPA; previous study had indicated that they could significantly ameliorate lipid metabolism not only by reducing serum and hepatic TG and TC levels and inhibiting lipid accumulation via suppression of fatty acid synthase activity, but also by promoting lipid homeostasis and anti-inflammatory processes through modulation of the PPAR pathway and expression of related genes such as FADS2 [<a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a>, <a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">28</a>]. Inflammation is well known to serve as a significant trigger for hyperkeratinization of the pilosebaceous duct [<a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a>]. Although EPA lacks direct antimicrobial activity, its anti-inflammatory character and ability to modulate the sebaceous microenvironment could indirectly influence the proliferation of <em>Cutibacterium acne</em> and its capacity to induce inflammatory responses, thereby reducing inflammation triggered by bacterial metabolites and correcting keratinization and the microbial environment [<a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a>]. Furthermore, as a precursor for the synthesis of specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs), EPA is a potent signaling molecule in the natural resolution of inflammation, capable of suppressing the migration and activation of pro-inflammatory cell types, including neutrophils, while promoting macrophage phagocytosis and apoptotic cells and debris’ clearance [<a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a>]. EPA acts as a competitor, vying with pro-inflammatory substrates like arachidonic acid, aω-6 fatty acids, for accessing the enzymes cyclooxygenase (COX) and lipoxygenase (LOX) [<a href="#CR32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR32">32</a>]. This type of competition ultimately reduces pro-inflammatory product, such as prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), LTB4, and thromboxanes, and increases that of other lipid mediators, such as PGE3 and LTB5, which exhibit significantly lower pro-inflammatory effects than PGE2 and LTB4 [<a href="#CR33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR33">33</a>].</p>
<p id="Par36">Notably, StE (20:5) was the only product that demonstrated a strong correlation with the GAGS score. This compound refers to eicosapentaenoic acid ethyl ester (EPA ethyl ester), an anti-inflammatory polyunsaturated fatty acid conferred by its EPA structure that can directly address critical therapeutic targets in acne management. Large-scale clinical trials specifically investigating pharmaceutical-grade EPA ethyl ester, an oral formulation found in prescription medications like Lovaza and Vascepa, were previously conducted for TG reduction in AV treatment [<a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a>]. In the current study on moderate-to-severe AV, patients receiving combined ω-3 fatty acid therapy exhibited significantly improved trends in lipid metabolomics and demonstrated greater improvement in severity indices compared to the isotretinoin monotherapy group. This aligned with the findings from other studies that reported reductions in both inflammatory and non-inflammatory diseases through fish oil supplementation or high-fish diets [<a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>].</p>
<p id="Par37">The superior clinical efficacy of the combination treatment invited a deeper interpretation of the relationship between systemic lipid metabolism and acne resolution. While the OPLS-DA model confirmed a distinct lipidomic signature for the combination therapy, and metabolites such as StE (20:5) had strong correlation with clinical severity (<em>R</em> =- 0.73), the serum lipid profile as a whole may not serve as a precise, linear biomarker for an individual acne lesion. The therapeutic effect of systemic isotretinoin intervention is rooted in the targeting of the pilosebaceous unit, leading to a distinct reduction in sebum production and modulation of follicular keratinization [<a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a>]. Therefore, a significant decrease in GAGS scores is a direct reflection of the improvement in local microenvironment [<a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a>]. Shifts in the serum lipidome represent systemic metabolic adjustments, which offer a valuable window into the overall metabolic state and treatment-related biochemical shifts. While being informative, they have limitations as a standalone biomarker for tracking acne evolution during therapy. Future investigations aimed at identifying more robust biomarkers should consider including the analysis of pilosebaceous unit lipidomics to better capture the direct therapeutic effects on the target tissue while exploring the metabolic pathways and mechanisms involved.</p>
<p id="Par38">In addition, KEGG enrichment pathway analysis indicated that ω-3 fatty acid is involved in lipid metabolism-related pathways. Glycerophospholipid metabolism, a classic lipid metabolism pathway, is related to the properties of sebaceous gland cell membranes and sebum components [<a href="#CR37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR37">37</a>]. The stability of this metabolic pathway contributes to the preservation of skin barrier and the normal differentiation of keratinocytes at the hair follicle orifice; the latter is a key step in hair follicle blockage at the onset of AV [<a href="#CR38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR38">38</a>]. Moreover, glycerophospholipid metabolism may affect the generation of substances such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes, pro-inflammatory mediators, thereby indirectly exerting anti-inflammatory effects that are closely related to the pathological mechanism of AV [<a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a>]. Therefore, the intervention of ω-3 fatty acids on the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway may become a potential research direction for regulating sebaceous gland function and improving hair follicle hyperkeratosis in patients with AV.</p>
<p id="Par39">Substantial research has revealed that combining isotretinoin with other therapies yields synergistic effects, enhances efficacy, and significantly improves safety [<a href="#CR40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR40">40</a>]. An RCT employing a combination of desloratadine and isotretinoin demonstrated superior clinical outcomes, lower recurrence rates, and more tolerable adverse events compared to monotherapy with isotretinoin alone [<a href="#CR41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR41">41</a>]. Same combination in another comparative study showed that the benefits of a lower dose of isotretinoin (0.25 mg/kg) were equivalent to those of the standard dosage (0.25 mg/kg) [<a href="#CR42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR42">42</a>], and co-therapy with cyproterone could even effectively mitigate isotretinoin-induced dyslipidemia [<a href="#CR43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR43">43</a>]. Studies are not only limited to the optimization of drugs; adjunctive physical treatments such as 675-nm laser therapy, when combined with isotretinoin, can lead to faster and pronounced clinical improvement compared to that in the laser-only group [<a href="#CR44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR44">44</a>].</p>
<section id="Sec25"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Strengths and limitationslimitations</h3>
<p id="Par40">The study presented the first RCT that evaluated the efficacy of ω-3 fatty acid and isotretinoin combination therapy on the serum lipid metabolome in AV patients, providing novel insights that contribute to the understanding of this combination regimen’s potential mechanisms of action. However, the generalizability of these results and the evaluation of long-term effects may be constrained by the study’s modest sample size. Although the observed alterations in the serum lipid metabolome reflect systemic metabolic adjustments, the changes may not precisely correspond to the therapeutic effects within the pilosebaceous unit, which is the primary site of acne pathology. Future investigations incorporating direct lipidomic profiling of target tissues are warranted.</p></section></section><section id="Sec26"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusions</h2>
<p id="Par41">In conclusion, ω-3 fatty acid supplementation can positively modulate serum lipid metabolites in patients with AV, resulting in improved therapeutic responses. Consequently, the nutritional intervention should be integrated into management as a promising adjunctive strategy to optimize therapeutic outcomes in patients.</p></section><section id="Sec27"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Supplementary Information</h2>
