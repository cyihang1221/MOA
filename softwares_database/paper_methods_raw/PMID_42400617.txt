
Methods
The study was observational and conducted among 106 healthy
individuals and 10 patients with systemic lupus erythematosus,
aged 18 to 64. Adult participants of both sexes, aged 18–64 years,
were eligible for inclusion in the study. All participants were
required to be neurologically and mentally healthy, able to
cooperate during the examination, and able to follow the study
instructions. In addition, participants were required to have a
clinically healthy tongue, with normal colouration and without
visible pathological lesions. The systemic lupus erythematosus
group consisted of patients with a documented diagnosis of
systemic lupus erythematosus confirmed in their medical records.
Healthy individuals without a documented systemic disease
relevant to the study protocol served as the control group.
The exclusion criteria were pregnancy, elevated body temperature,
active bacterial or viral inflammatory disease, active inflammatory
lesions within the oral cavity, and current or previous neoplastic
disease in the head and neck region. Participants were also
excluded if they were unable to cooperate during the examination,
unable to comply with the study protocol, or did not provide
written informed consent.
The measurements were performed in a draft-free room with the air
conditioning and heating systems turned off (01.05.2025–
30.09.2025). Ambient temperature was maintained within the
range of 20–24 °C. Before data acquisition, environmental
conditions, including temperature and humidity, were monitored to
ensure measurement repeatability. The environmental conditions
and the method of recording and analysing thermal images were
consistent with the recommendations of the international protocol
for standardising thermographic examinations [7, 12].
Before inclusion in the study, each participant underwent an oral
health assessment performed by a dentist. Active inflammation
within the oral cavity constituted an exclusion criterion. Prior to the
experiment, all participants were informed about the study’s
purpose and procedures and provided written informed consent.
Eligible participants were required to be neurologically and
mentally healthy, cooperative, and to have a clinically healthy
tongue, free from pathological lesions and with normal colouration.
The local bioethics committee approved the project (Bioethics
Committee of the Pomeranian Medical University in Szczecin,
KB-006/98/2024).
Participants were instructed to refrain from eating or drinking for at
least 60 min before the examination. Upon arrival, each participant
underwent a 15-minute acclimatisation period under the conditions
of the examination room. Before the measurements began,
participants received detailed information about the study, signed
the required consent forms, and completed the relevant documents
and questionnaires. The researcher explained the procedure before
each examination and answered any questions the participant
raised.
Before stimulus application, preliminary thermal images were
acquired. A Fluke Ti400 thermal imaging camera (Fluke
Corporation, Everett, WA, USA) was positioned as perpendicular as
possible to the tongue surface. The camera is equipped with an
uncooled microbolometer detector with a resolution of 320 × 240
pixels and enables temperature measurements from − 20 °C to + 
1200 °C, with a thermal sensitivity of ≤ 0.05 °C and an accuracy of
± 2 °C or ± 2%.
Although the camera’s absolute accuracy is ± 2 °C or ± 2%, the
present analysis focused primarily on within-subject relative
temperature changes and recovery indices, thereby limiting the
impact of potential systematic measurement uncertainty. The device
also features LaserSharp automatic focusing and emissivity
compensation. The distance between the camera and the participant
was standardised using the participant’s nose as an anatomical
reference point; the upper edge of the thermal image was aligned
with the nasal tip, corresponding to an approximate camera-to-
subject distance of 35 cm.
The emissivity was set to 0.98, consistent with commonly reported
emissivity values for biological tissues. As the camera system did
not allow manual adjustment of the reflected apparent temperature,
all measurements were performed under controlled, stable ambient
conditions to minimise potential environmental influences. During
image acquisition, the tongue remained protruded from the oral
cavity.
Before the thermographic examination, participants were instructed
to remain still, avoid speaking, and minimise tongue movement
during image acquisition. Between consecutive measurements,
participants were asked to keep their mouths closed and to refrain
from unnecessary tongue movements. The participant then took 20
mL of water at 8 °C into the mouth and held it there for 60 s. The
water temperature was selected to provide a sufficiently strong yet
safe cold stimulus that would elicit a measurable thermovascular
response without inducing discomfort or protective reflexes. This
temperature corresponds to typical refrigeration conditions,
supporting both reproducibility and ecological validity of the
experimental protocol.
After 60 s, the participant swallowed the entire volume of water,
and thermal images were acquired immediately after stimulus
completion. Subsequent tongue temperature measurements were
performed at 2, 5, and 10 min after completion of the thermal test.
The duration of image acquisition was not included in the
predefined time intervals; timing was initiated only after each
measurement had been completed. During and between
measurements, participants kept their mouths closed and were
instructed not to speak or move their tongues.
The mean temperature was analysed within three regions of interest
(ROIs): the dorsum, central region, and apex of the tongue. This
approach enabled assessment of both global temperature changes
and local thermal responses. Quantitative analysis was performed
using Fluke Connect software (Fluke Corporation, version 2.0.5.0,
2021).
Thermal image acquisition was performed by a dentist, whereas
quantitative image analysis, including manual ROI delineation, was
conducted by the same medical physicist. This approach ensured
clinical and metrological consistency of the research procedure.
Manual ROI delineation was selected because of the tongue’s
complex and variable anatomy, which may limit the reliability of
automated segmentation methods.
To reduce potential observer bias, ROIs were defined using
standardised anatomical landmarks and delineated by the same
trained examiner, following a consistent protocol across all
participants and time points. To assess the repeatability of ROI
placement, repeated measurements were performed in a subset of
participants for selected representative regions of interest. The
variability of repeated measurements was quantified using the
coefficient of variation (CV). Low CV values indicated good intra-
rater consistency of ROI delineation and temperature measurement.

The following parameters were recorded:
T - baseline tongue temperature, before the thermal stimulus [°C].
Tn - tongue temperature measured at time point n after completion
of the thermal stimulus, where n = 0, 2, 5, or 10 min. T₀ denotes
the temperature measured immediately after the stimulus, whereas
T₂, T₅, and T₁₀ denote the temperatures measured 2, 5, and 10 min
after stimulus completion, respectively [°C].
Descriptive statistics were performed for all analysed variables. Due
to the small sample size in the individual groups, differences in
median values were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test.
Statistical analyses were performed using Statistica version 13
(TIBCO Software Inc., Palo Alto, CA, USA). Because multiple
comparisons were performed across thermal parameters within each
tongue region, p-values were additionally adjusted using the
Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate procedure. Both
unadjusted and FDR-adjusted p-values are reported. Given the pilot
and exploratory nature of the study, unadjusted findings were
interpreted cautiously, and statistical significance was primarily
assessed using FDR-adjusted p-values.
Results
All participants who were assessed for eligibility and met the
inclusion criteria agreed to participate, completed the full
thermographic protocol, and were included in the final analysis. No
participants were excluded after enrolment, and no non-
participation occurred at subsequent stages of the study. Baseline
demographic and physiological characteristics of the study groups
are presented in Table 4S. Given the small size and clinical
heterogeneity of the systemic lupus erythematosus group, these
variables were reported descriptively and were not used for
subgroup or adjusted analyses (see Table 1).
