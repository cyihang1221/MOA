>Methods   Study design and participants   This study is a cross-sectional study, in which a total of 210 participants were enrolled, including 24 cases of heart failure at risk stage (Stage A), 84 cases of pre-heart failure stage (Stage B), and 102 cases of symptomatic heart failure stage (Stage C). Using the stratified sampling method, 60 participants were selected from the 210 patients, with 15 cases of heart failure at risk stage (Stage A), 15 cases of pre-heart failure stage (Stage B), and 30 cases of symptomatic heart failure stage (Stage C); samples were selected from each stratum using the random number table method (see Fig.  1 ). All participants were patients who visited Xiyuan Hospital of China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences from October 2023 to October 2024. The diagnostic criteria for heart failure staging were developed with reference to the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure [ 6 ]: STAGE A: At-Risk for Heart Failure, patients at risk for HF but without current or previous symptoms/signs of HF and without structural/functional heart disease or abnormal biomarkers; STAGE B: Pre-Heart Failure, patients without current or previous symptoms/signs of HF but evidence of 1 of the following: Structural heart disease; Evidence of increased filling pressures; Risk factors and increased natriuretic peptide levels or persistently elevated cardiac troponin in the absence of competing diagnoses; STAGE C: Symptomatic Heart Failure, patients with current or previous symptoms/signs of HF. Inclusion criteria for cases: Meeting the Western medicine diagnostic criteria for heart failure staging; Aged 50–90 years, regardless of gender; Patients or their family members providing consent and signing the informed consent form. Exclusion criteria for cases: Patients meeting any of the following criteria will be excluded: Heart failure caused by congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease, myocarditis, autoimmune diseases, or other conditions; Suffering from malignant tumors; Suffering from mental illnesses; Having participated in other studies within the past 3 months. Open in a new tab   Research flow chart (Created in  https://BioRender.com ) Sample preparation for metabolomics   Instruments and Reagents: Mass spectrometer (Manufacturer: Thermo Scientific, Model: Q Exactive Plus), mass spectrometer (Manufacturer: AB SCIEX, Model: QTRAP6500+), ultra-high performance liquid chromatograph (UPLC) (Manufacturer: Shimadzu, Model: Nexera X2 LC-30AD), chromatographic column (Manufacturer: Waters, Model: ACQUITY UPLCB@HSS T3 (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm)), ultrasonic system (Manufacturer: Diagenode, Model: Bioruptor), vacuum centrifugal concentrator (Manufacturer: Eppendorf, Model: Concentrator plus), centrifuge (Manufacturer: Eppendorf, Model: Centrifuge 5424R), visible-ultraviolet spectrophotometer (Manufacturer: Thermo, Model: MultiSkan FC), acetonitrile (Manufacturer: Millipore, Model: 1.00030.4008), methanol (Manufacturer: Millipore, Model: 1.06007.4008), ammonia water (Manufacturer: Merck, Model: 105426), formic acid (Manufacturer: Millipore, Model: 111670), and ammonium acetate (Manufacturer: Sigma, Model: 73594).   Untargeted LC-MS: For each participant, 2 mL of venous blood was collected in the early morning while fasting, and the blood was anticoagulated. The sample was centrifuged at 2000×g for 10 min to separate the serum, which was then numbered. After vortexing the serum to mix it thoroughly, 100 µL of each serum sample was taken, and 300 µL of pre-cooled 100% methanol solution was added. The mixture was incubated at -20 °C for 30 min, then centrifuged at 16,000×g and 4 °C for 20 min. The supernatant was collected and stored for subsequent mass spectrometry injection and analysis.   Targeted LC-MS: 100 µL of the sample was taken, and 400 mL of pre-cooled 100% methanol-water solution was added to it respectively. The mixture was vortexed to mix thoroughly, then sonicated in an ice bath for 20 min, and incubated at -20 °C for 1 h. After that, it was centrifuged at 16,000×g and 4 °C for 20 min. The supernatant was evaporated to dryness using a high-speed vacuum centrifugal concentrator, and the sample was reconstituted in 100 µL of pre-cooled 50% methanol-water solution. Finally, it was centrifuged at 20,000×g and 4 °C for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected for subsequent mass spectrometry injection and analysis. Untargeted LC-MS analyses   Chromatographic Separation: The chromatographic column used was ACQUITY UPLC ®  HSS T3 (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm) (Waters, Milford, MA, USA). The chromatographic mobile phases were as follows: Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution, and Mobile phase B was acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The injection volume was 10 µL, the column temperature was 40 °C, and the flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. The gradient elution conditions were: 0–2 min, 0% mobile phase B; 2–6 min, mobile phase B linearly increased from 0% to 48%; 6–10 min, mobile phase B linearly increased from 48% to 100%; 10–12 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%; 12–12.1 min, mobile phase B linearly decreased from 100% to 0%; 12.1–15 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%.   Mass Spectrometry Data Acquisition: Each sample was detected in positive (+) and negative (−) ion modes using electrospray ionization (ESI). After separation by UPLC, the samples were analyzed by a QE Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) with ionization performed using a heated electrospray ionization (HESI) source. The ionization conditions were as follows: Spray Voltage: 3.8 kV (+) and 3.2 kV (−); Capillary Temperature: 320 °C (±); Sheath Gas: 30 (±); Aux Gas: 5 (±); Probe Heater Temp: 350 °C (±); S-Lens RF Level: 50. The mass spectrometry acquisition settings were as follows: Total acquisition time: 15 min. Parent ion scan range: 75–1050 m/z. Full scan resolution (MS1): 70,000 @ m/z 200. AGC target value (MS1): 3e6. Maximum ion injection time (MS1): 100 ms. For MS2 analysis, data were acquired using the following method: After each full scan, MS2 spectra of the 10 parent ions with the highest intensity were triggered and collected. MS2 resolution: 17,500 @ m/z 200. AGC target value (MS2): 1e5. Maximum ion injection time (MS2): 50 ms. MS2 Activation Type: higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD). Isolation window: 2 m/z. Normalized collision energy (stepped): 20, 30, 40. Targeted LC-MS analyses   Chromatographic Separation: The chromatographic column used was Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (1.7 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm column). The chromatographic mobile phases were configured as follows: Mobile phase A was a 5% acetonitrile aqueous solution containing 10 mM ammonium acetate (pH = 9); Mobile phase B was a 95% acetonitrile aqueous solution containing 10 mM ammonium acetate (pH = 9). The injection volume was 5 µL, the column temperature was 40 °C, and the flow rate was 300 µL/min. The gradient elution conditions were: 0–2 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 95%; 2–9 min, mobile phase B linearly changed from 95% to 70%; 9–10 min, mobile phase B linearly changed from 70% to 30%; 10–11 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 30%; 11–11.5 min, mobile phase B linearly changed from 30% to 95%; 11.5–15 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 95%.   Mass Spectrometry Data Acquisition: Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a QTRAP6500 + mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX) in positive/negative ion modes. The parameters of the ESI source were as follows: For positive ion mode: Source Temperature: 550 °C; Ion Source Gas1 (GAS1): 40 psi; Ion Source Gas2 (GAS2): 50 psi; Curtain Gas (CUR): 35 psi; Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF): 5500 V. For negative ion mode: Source Temperature: 550 °C; Ion Source Gas1 (GAS1): 40 psi; Ion Source Gas2 (GAS2): 50 psi; Curtain Gas (CUR): 35 psi; Ion Spray Voltage Floating (ISVF): -4500 V. MRM (multiple reaction monitoring) mode was used to detect the target ion pairs. Metabolomics data processing   Raw data were subjected to peak alignment, retention time correction, and peak area extraction using MSDIAL software. For metabolite structure identification, accurate mass matching (mass tolerance < 10 ppm) and MS/MS spectrum matching (mass tolerance < 0.01 Da) were employed, with searches conducted in public databases including HMDB, MassBank, and GNPS, as well as the self-built Baipu Metabolite Database (BP-DB). For the extracted data, ion peaks with missing values > 50% within the group were removed and excluded from subsequent statistical analysis. The positive and negative ion data were separately subjected to total peak area normalization; positive and negative ion peaks were then integrated, and Python software was used for pattern recognition. After the data were preprocessed via Unit Variance Scaling (UV), subsequent data analysis was performed. Statistical analyses   Data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0. For missing values, they were imputed using the mean (for normally distributed variables) or median (for skewed distributed variables) of the respective variables (High-sensitivity C-reactive protein, Glycated Hemoglobin, High-density Lipoprotein Cholesterol, low-density Lipoprotein Cholesterol and E/A). Categorical data were expressed as frequency/percentage [n/(%)]. For continuous data, normality test was first performed: those conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), while those not conforming to normal distribution were expressed as median and interquartile range. For continuous variables between two groups: if they showed normal distribution and homogeneity of variance, t-test was used; if they showed normal distribution but heterogeneous variance, corrected t-test was selected; if they did not show normal distribution, Mann-Whitney test was applied. For continuous variables among multiple groups: if they conformed to normal distribution, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used; if they did not conform to normal distribution, Kruskal-Wallis test was used to examine the difference distribution between groups. For data with  P  < 0.05 in ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis test, Bonferroni correction was used for pairwise comparisons. Categorical data were compared using Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The screening threshold for differential metabolites was PLS-DA VIP > 1 and  P adjust (FDR)  < 0.05. The normalized peak values of differential metabolites screened by non-targeted metabolomics were divided into 4 groups according to quartiles. Taking the Q1 group as the reference group, an ordered multinomial logistic regression analysis was performed on the metabolites with inter-group differences. If the P value of the parallel line test for the metabolites was < 0.05, an unordered multinomial logistic regression analysis was used. A multivariate binary logistic regression analysis was used to examine the associations between different concentration groups of metabolites and heart failure (stages A + B vs. stage C). The goodness - of - fit of the model was evaluated by the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and the dose-response trend was verified by the chi - square test for trend. A  P  < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For the differential metabolites screened by targeted metabolomics, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to establish different models. Ethics   This trial was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients in this study signed the informed consent form. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Xiyuan Hospital of China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences (No. 2023XLA112-2) and registered with the International Clinical Trial Registry Platform for Traditional Medicine (No. ITMCTR2024000120). Results   Characteristics of study participants   Untargeted metabolomics included a total of 210 heart failure patients, consisting of 24 cases in Stage A, 84 cases in Stage B, and 102 cases in Stage C. Targeted metabolomics included 60 heart failure patients, with 15 cases in Stage A, 15 cases in Stage B, and 30 cases in Stage C (Table  1 ). There were no significant statistical differences in gender, Body Mass Index (BMI), Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP), or Heart Rate (HR) among the three groups ( P  > 0.05). Regarding past medical history, there were no significant differences in the prevalence of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, or hyperlipidemia among the groups ( P  > 0.05). Additionally, there were no significant differences in the use of medications—including Angiotensin Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEI), Angiotensin Receptor Blocker (ARB), Angiotensin-Receptor Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI), Calcium Channel Blocker (CCB), statins, antiplatelet drugs, oral hypoglycemic agents, and subcutaneous insulin injection—across the three groups ( P  > 0.05). Levels of N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) and Cardiac Troponin T (cTnT) in patients with Stage C heart failure were higher than those in patients with Stage A or B. Compared with patients in Stage A or B, those in Stage C had a lower Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF).   Table 1.
