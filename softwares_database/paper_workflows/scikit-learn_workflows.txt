complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data in .mzXML (MS1) and .mzML (MS2) formats. Preprocessing is performed using XCMS for peak detection, MzMatch for peak alignment and filtering, and RMassBank for matching MS1 features to their corresponding MS2 spectra (i.e., forming MS1–MS2 pairs). This step generates a collection of MS2 spectra per sample, with fragment and neutral loss peaks annotated and intensity-weighted. Crucially, no retention time alignment across samples is performed — instead, MS2 spectra are aligned directly based on m/z and intensity of fragment/loss features. These preprocessed MS2 spectra are then input into MS2LDA+, a custom Python-based implementation extending Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) to jointly model multiple sample groups using a shared vocabulary of Mass2Motifs (co-occurring fragments and losses). MS2LDA+ decomposes each spectrum into a mixture of Mass2Motifs, estimating motif prevalence per sample. Downstream analyses include: (1) differential prevalence testing of Mass2Motifs between sample groups (e.g., beer vs. urine); (2) variance-based ranking of Mass2Motifs within a homogeneous group (e.g., urine cohort) to prioritize xenobiotic-derived motifs; (3) structural interpretation of Mass2Motifs via manual or database-assisted annotation (e.g., matching to known substructures like paracetamol mercapturate or dimethylpyrogallol); and (4) integration with MS1 feature intensity matrices to link Mass2Motif prevalence to abundance patterns of core vs. rare metabolites. The workflow culminates in biologically interpretable structural families rather than individual feature-level statistics.
