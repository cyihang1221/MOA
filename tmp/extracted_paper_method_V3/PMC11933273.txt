</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="Abs1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p id="Par1">A dereplication strategy was developed for the screening of secondary metabolites from <em>Sophora flavescens</em>. The strategy consisted of 4 procedures. First, the extract of the <em>Sophora flavescens</em> root was subjected to LC–MS/MS analysis with both data-independent acquisition (DIA) mode and data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Then the DIA results were used to construct a molecular networking (MN) according to the GNPS workflow and consequently obtain annotations. In parallel, the DDA results were projected to both MN analysis and direct databases matching to obtain annotations. Finally, the isomers were discriminated and annotated by their extracted ion chromatogram. Through the combination of these approaches, a total of 51 compounds were annotated and dereplicated in the <em>Sophora flavescens</em> samples. The annotation results showed DIA and DDA approach are complementary to each other. MN on GNPS can overcome the challenges of trace compound identification compared to direct DB matching. This strategy provides a powerful tool for the dereplication study in plant chemistry.</p>
<section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> <em>Sophora flavescens</em>, Metabolites, Dereplication, Molecular networking, Alkaloids, Flavonoids</p></section><section id="kwd-group2" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Subject terms:</strong> Liquid chromatography, High-throughput screening, Mass spectrometry</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p id="Par2"><em>Sophora flavescens</em> is a perennial subshrub in the genus <em>Sophora</em>, family <em>Fabaceae</em>. It is distributed throughout China and also in India, Japan, Korea, and some European countries. The secondary metabolites of <em>Sophora flavescens</em> are mainly alkaloids, flavonoids, triterpenoids and other active constituents. Typical alkaloids such as matrine, oxymatrine, sophoridine<sup><a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a></sup>, etc., have significant antioxidation, anti-tumor<sup><a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a></sup>, anti-inflammatory<sup><a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a></sup> and other biological activities<sup><a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a></sup>. Flavonoids such as kurarinone, xanthohumol, etc., have antioxidant, anti-inflammatory<sup><a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a></sup> and anti-allergic<sup><a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">6</a></sup> effects.</p>
<p id="Par3">The secondary metabolites of <em>Sophora flavescens</em> are the basis of evidence-based pharmacy. It is important to characterize the component. Although <em>Sophora flavescens</em> has been extensively studied, new compounds are still being discovered<sup><a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a>–<a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">9</a></sup>. In order to find new compounds in a well-studied plant, dereplication is necessary<sup><a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a></sup>. Dereplication means to rapid identify those previously characterized molecules. To date, there is a lack of a systematic strategy to achieve dereplication together with characterization and relative quantification of the constitution of <em>Sophora flavescens</em>.</p>
<p id="Par4">Liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) is a powerful tool for compound analysis and characterization<sup><a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">11</a></sup>. Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled to high-resolution mass spectrometry (UPLC-HRMS) provides better separation and more accurate mass-to-charge (<em>m/z</em>) ratio of the analyte ion<sup><a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a></sup>. In mass spectrometry, multiple-reaction monitoring (MRM) acquisition were developed for targeted metabolomics, while either data-independent acquisition (DIA) or data-dependent acquisition (DDA) were used for untargeted metabolomics<sup><a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a></sup>. Nevertheless, the annotation of metabolites in untargeted datasets represents a significant challenge for researchers<sup><a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a></sup>.</p>
<p id="Par5">A straightforward dereplication approach is to compare the mass spectrum of the candidate compound with those of known natural products. However, as the amount of library data increases<sup><a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a></sup> and each LC–MS run generates a significant number of spectra<sup><a href="#CR16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR16">16</a></sup>, it becomes impossible to match spectra manually. The simple library search often resulted in mismatch because of the data variation from the experimental conditions<sup><a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a>,<a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a></sup>. The development of algorithms to systematically screen and dereplicate these structurally diverse compounds is necessary<sup><a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">19</a></sup>.</p>
<p id="Par6">Molecular networking (MN) is a method for organizing, visualizing and annotating untargeted MS/MS data<sup><a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">20</a>,<a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a></sup>. In MN, the mass spectra of molecules are connected based on the similarity of fragmentation patterns<sup><a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a>,<a href="#CR22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR22">22</a></sup>. Molecular networking within the Global Natural Products Social (GNPS) platform<sup><a href="#CR23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR23">23</a></sup> has been successfully applied to discovering and identifying natural products<sup><a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a></sup>. Recently, feature-based molecular networking (FBMN) in the GNPS community has also been used as an analysis tool for chemical annotation<sup><a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">25</a>,<a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a></sup>. Other modified MN approaches have also annotated global metabolites from known to unknowns in untargeted metabolomics<sup><a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a></sup>.</p>
<p id="Par7">For the purpose of dereplication, we tend to provide more spectral information to MN in order to obtain more component identification. Although all MS/MS fragmentation information is included in the DIA data, the information is complex. Even after the deconvolution, the data from DIA are still complicated and difficult to interpret. On the other hand, the MS/MS spectra in DDA data are simpler and can be used directly for DB matching.</p>
<p id="Par8">Here, we proposed a dereplication strategy for the metabolomic study of <em>Sophora flavescens</em>. The pipeline of strategy was illustrated in Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>.</p>
<figure class="fig xbox font-sm" id="Fig1"><h3 class="obj_head">Fig. 1.</h3>
<p class="img-box line-height-none margin-x-neg-2 tablet:margin-x-0 text-center"><a class="tileshop" target="_blank" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/core/lw/2.0/html/tileshop_pmc/tileshop_pmc_inline.html?title=Click%20on%20image%20to%20zoom&amp;p=PMC3&amp;id=11933273_41598_2025_94958_Fig1_HTML.jpg"><img class="graphic zoom-in" src="https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/7adb/11933273/7893e35035f1/41598_2025_94958_Fig1_HTML.jpg" loading="lazy" id="MO1" height="307" width="669" alt="Fig. 1"></a></p>
<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="figure/Fig1/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div>
<figcaption><p>Schematic workflow of the proposed strategy for dereplication of secondary metabolites in <em>Sophora flavescens</em> root.</p></figcaption></figure><p id="Par9">The analytical pipeline consisted of 4 procedures. First, the extract of the <em>Sophora flavescens</em> sample was subjected to LC–MS/MS analysis with both DIA and DDA modes. Then, the raw DIA data were processed and aligned into compatible version to construct the MN. Next, the raw DDA data were searched directly in public databases for the complementary annotation to the MN approach. Finally, the putative annotations were combined and the isomers were annotated by their extracted ion chromatogram (EIC). This dereplication strategy could be a useful tool for the discovery of new metabolites in the plant metabolomics studies.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Experimental section</h2>
<section id="Sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials and reagents</h3>
<p id="Par10">Standards of matrine, sophoridine, kurarinone, anagyrine, sophoramine, neosophoramine, and calycosin-7-O-beta-D-glucoside were purchased from Chengdu Zhibiao Biotech Corporation Limited (Chengdu, China). Standards of trifolirhizin, oxysophocarpine, xanthohumol and isoxanthohumol were purchased from Wuhan Tianzhi Biotech Corporation Limited (Shanghai, China).</p>
<p id="Par11">The sample of the <em>Sophora flavescens</em> root was purchased from a grower in Wenshan County, Yunnan Province, China. The grower grew and sold the sample with the Wenshan Government license (851749 K). The sample was identified by the Dr. Mei Deng of China Pharmaceutical University. The voucher specimen (No. YCTU-IAC-2024-Mar-26-01) is deposited in the Instrumental Analysis Center of Yancheng Teachers University.</p>
<p id="Par12">Chromatographic grade methanol and acetonitrile (ACN) were both purchased from Tedia Company, Inc. (Fairfield, USA). Formic acid (purity &gt; 98.0%) was purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan). Ammonium acetate (purity &gt; 98.0%) was purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Water was purified using a Milli-Q system (Molsheim, France).</p></section><section id="Sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Sample preparation</h3>
<p id="Par13">The <em>Sophora flavescens</em> root was dried and ground to pass through a 0.1 mm sieve. 50 mg powder was extracted with 10 mL solvent mixture of methanol/water/formic acid (49:49:2; v/v/v) by sonication for 60 min. An aliquot of extractant was served as blank sample and subjected with the same treatments. After centrifugation, the supernatant was reserved. This extraction was carried out in triplicate. The supernatants were combined and then dried by nitrogen blowing. The dried extract was dissolved in a H<sub>2</sub>O/ACN (95:5; v/v) solution. The concentration of <em>Sophora flavescens</em> powder in the reconstituted sample solution is 10 mg/mL. The sample solution was filtered through a 0.22 μm polytetrafluoroethylene membrane before testing. The solutions of the standard sample were all prepared at a concentration of 100 ng/mL.</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Instrumental experiment procedures</h3>
<p id="Par14">All acquisitions were conducted on an UPLC-Q-TOF system. The system consisted of an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC set (Santa Clara, CA, USA) and an ABSciex triple TOF 5600<sup>+</sup> mass spectrometer. A 2.1 × 150 mm, 1.8 μm ECLIPSE PLUS-C<sub>18</sub> column (Agilent) was used.</p>
<p id="Par15">The mobile phase <strong>A</strong> was an ammonium acetate/water solution (8.0 mmol/L). Mobile phase <strong>B</strong> was acetonitrile. The total flow rate was 0.300 mL/min. The column temperature was 40 °C. The LC gradient program was as follows: 3 − 5% B for 0–3 min, 5 − 5% for 3–5 min, 5 − 15% for 5–8 min, 15 − 60% for 8–12 min, 60–98% for 12–20 min, 98–98% for 20–21 min. The injection volume was 2.0 μL. All samples were analyzed in triplicate.</p>
<p id="Par16">The mass spectrometer worked in positive mode. The ionization voltage was set at + 5.5 kV with 55 psi for the nebulizing gas, 55 psi for the auxiliary gas, and 35 psi for the curtain gas. The source temperature was set at 550 °C. For MS scanning, the TOF scan range covered <em>m/z</em> 100–2000.</p>
<p id="Par17">For DDA analysis, the mass spectrometer was operated in Information Dependent Acquisition (IDA) mode<sup><a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">28</a></sup>. The mass range of survey scans was set to 100–2000 Da. The top 4 ions were selected for collision induced dissociation. The collision energy was set to 50 eV, the collision energy spread to 10 eV, the ion release delay to 67 ms, and the ion release width to 25 ms.</p>
<p id="Par18">In order to conduct a DIA analysis, the sequential window acquisition of all theoretical fragment-ion spectra (SWATH)<sup><a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a></sup> was employed. The precursor ions, spanning the range of 100–1000 Da, were sequentially and cyclically isolated using a 50 Da mass window, and then subjected to fragmentation with a 50 eV collision energy.</p></section><section id="Sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Raw data conversion</h3>
<p id="Par19">Two MNs were built for DIA and DDA data separately. The DIA and DDA raw spectra (MassIVE: MSV000096717) were both converted to mzML format data with the software MSConvert (ProteoWizard 3.02).</p>
<p id="Par20">The MS<sup>2</sup> features in DIA data were then extracted with MS-DIAL (v5.3) to construct the pseudo-MS/MS spectra for MN. The parameter of acquisition type was set to SWATH for imported DIA data. MS<sup>1</sup> tolerance was set at 0.01 Da while MS<sup>2</sup> tolerance at 0.025 Da. For peak detection, the minimum peak height was 50 amplitude and mass slice width was 0.1 Da. The alignment parameters of the triplicate results were set with a 0.1 min RT tolerance and a 0.015 Da MS<sup>1</sup> tolerance. The aligned results were exported to a peak table file and a MS/MS spectral file. The two files were then uploaded to the GNPS platform.</p>
<p id="Par21">The converted DDA data were processed with MZmine (v4.3.0)<sup><a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a>,<a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a></sup> to perform MS detection, chromatogram building<sup><a href="#CR32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR32">32</a></sup>, chromatogram resolving, isotope grouping, blank subtraction, and feature alignment. The parameters for isotope grouping were 0.01 m<em>/z</em> tolerance and 0.1 min RT tolerance. The alignment of the triplicate results was performed using the parameters of 0.015 m<em>/z</em> tolerance, 0.1 min RT tolerance and same charge state. The aligned data were exported to a feature quantification table file and a MS/MS spectral file. The table and spectral file were also uploaded to the GNPS platform.</p></section><section id="Sec7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Molecular networking construction</h3>
<p id="Par22">Both DIA and DDA data based MNs were created using the METABOLOMICS-SNETS-V2 GNPS workflow on GNPS<sup><a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a></sup>. The data were filtered by removing all MS/MS fragment ions within + /− 17 Da of the precursor <em>m/z</em>. MS/MS spectra were window filtered by choosing only the top 6 fragment ions in the + /− 50 Da window throughout the spectrum. The precursor ion mass tolerance was set to 0.5 Da and a MS/MS fragment ion tolerance of 0.5 Da. A network was then created. The cosine score of edges was set to 0.6 or more. The number of matched peaks was set to 4 or more. Further, edges between two nodes were kept in the network if and only if each of the nodes appeared in each other’s respective top 10 most similar nodes. Finally, the maximum size of a molecular family was set to 100, and the lowest scoring edges were removed from molecular families until the molecular family size was below this threshold. The spectra in the network were then searched against GNPS’ spectral libraries. The library spectra were filtered in the same manner as the input data. All matches kept between network spectra and library spectra were required to have a score above 0.6 and at least 4 matched peaks.</p>
<p id="Par23">The molecular networking was further visualized and analyzed using Cytoscape (Ver. 3.10)<sup><a href="#CR33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR33">33</a></sup>.</p></section><section id="Sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Direct spectral matching for DDA data</h3>
<p id="Par24">The converted DDA data were subjected to MS-DIAL (ver. 5.3) to perform direct spectral matching. The MS-DIAL parameters were set as follows: MS<sup>1</sup> accurate mass tolerance at 0.01 Da; MS/MS accurate mass tolerance at 0.1 Da; minimum peak height at 50 amplitude; mass slice width at 0.1 Da. The alignment parameters of the triplicate results were set with a 0.1 min RT tolerance and a 0.015 Da MS<sup>1</sup> tolerance. All identification databases were in MSP format including MSMS-Public_experimentspectra-pos-VS19, MSMS-Pos-GNPS, MoNA-export-Vaniya-Fiehn_Natural_Products_Library, MoNA-export-MassBank, MoNA-export-LC–MS–MS_Positive_Mode, and MoNA-export-GNPS.</p></section></section><section id="Sec9"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Results and discussion</h2>
<section id="Sec10"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Annotation through DIA data-based MN</h3>
<p id="Par25">Metabolites were assigned with three levels of confidence. Level 1 is the identification. The identification is based on RT and MS/MS spectra that are identical to those of the standard chemical. Level 2 is the annotation. The annotation corresponds to the metabolites with confident MS/MS spectra. Level 3 is the tentative annotation corresponding to the metabolites with an accurate parent ion mass (&lt; 5 ppm). These three levels comply with classification proposed by Schrimpe-Rutledge and colleagues<sup><a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a>,<a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a></sup>. The compounds name adopted for annotation and tentative annotation was recommended by library hits or literature reports.</p>
<p id="Par26">The MN (Fig. S1) for DIA data of <em>Sophora flavescens</em> samples was built in GNPS (<a href="https://gnps.ucsd.edu/ProteoSAFe/status.jsp?task=eab88b9bca7048cb880c3dc647780ad6" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://gnps.ucsd.edu/ProteoSAFe/status.jsp?task=eab88b9bca7048cb880c3dc647780ad6</a>).</p>
<p id="Par27">In cluster 1 (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>) of the built MN, 7 components (red nodes) were tentatively annotated based on the GNPS library hits. Among them, 6 annotations were achieved at different evidence levels. They are all flavonoids. The information of them is listed in Table <a href="#Tab1" class="usa-link">1</a>.</p>

<div class="tw-foot p"><div class="fn" id="_fn_p37"><p>*USI (Universal Spectrum Identifier): mzspec:MassIVE:TASK-eab88b9bca7048cb880c3dc647780ad6-spectra/specs_ms.mgf:scan:#</p></div></div></section><p id="Par32">In cluster 2 (Fig. S3), the alkaloids of sophocarpine (<strong>104</strong>), sophoridine (<strong>110</strong>), and oxymatrine (<strong>197)</strong> were annotated based on the GNPS library hits. <p id="Par39">The primary method for identifying natural compounds is to directly match their MS/MS spectra with library spectra. However, DIA spectra are inherently complex, containing uncertain precursor ions. As a result, they cannot be used to match libraries directly. DDA data are well suited for direct spectral matching because each spectrum in a DDA result is an MS/MS spectrum of a single precursor ion.</p>
<p id="Par40">Complementary to the MN approach, the DDA data of <em>Sophora flavescens</em> were subjected to matching the offline database. When the match score was set to 1.4 or more, 18 metabolites were annotated (Table <a href="#Tab6" class="usa-link">6</a>) that did not get annotated in both MN approaches. These annotations showed that DDA is the necessary technological approach to achieve dereplication.</p>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="table/Tab6/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div></section></section><section id="Sec13"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Isomer discrimination (LC/MS)</h3>
<p id="Par41">There are lots of isomers in the constituents of <em>Sophora flavescens</em>. In order to distinguish the isomers, the chromatographic method in this study was optimized elaborately based on previous work. The LC–MS was then performed in total scanning acquisition mode. The total ion chromatogram (TIC) was shown in Fig. S5. The zoomed TIC between 0 and 7 min was shown in Fig. S6. The EIC of a particular isomer was extracted from the TIC. The Peaks in EIC were analyzed by the feature of RT and accurate <em>m/z</em> value. The MS/MS spectrum of the peak was extracted from the DIA data at the corresponding RT. The MS/MS spectrum was then checked with fragmentation patterns, the molecular polarity, and literature reference.</p>
<p id="Par42">Six matrine isomers (<em>m/z</em> 249.1961) were observed in the EIC (Fig. <a href="#Fig3" class="usa-link">3</a>). By referencing the RT of the standard chemicals, the peaks at 8.053 min and 10.413 min were identified as matrine and sophoridine, respectively. The remaining isomers were putatively annotated according to their MS/MS patterns in reference databases and in the reported literature<sup><a href="#CR37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR37">37</a></sup>. The peaks at 6.643 min, 8.559 min, 9.304 min, and 9.669 min were annotated as (+)-lupanine, α-isolupanine<sup><a href="#CR37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR37">37</a></sup>, isomatrine<sup><a href="#CR38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR38">38</a></sup>, and allomatrine<sup><a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a></sup> respectively. Details are listed in Table S2.</p>

<p id="Par46">Although the focus of this study is the dereplication strategy, the quantitation of the identified compounds is always the concern. A simple external standard method was applied to quantify the contents of matrine and oxysophocarpine. The peak area in the EIC was calculated. The calibration curves of them are shown in Fig. S7. As a primary evaluation measure, the quantification method was not rigorously validated for specificity, limit of detection, linear range, and precision. According to the external standard results, the content of matrine in the dried root of <em>Sophora flavescens</em> was about 6.05 mg/g. The content of oxysophocarpine was about 5.81 mg/g.</p>
<p id="Par47">Semi-quantification for all the identified compounds was estimated based on the hypothesis that the peak intensity is proportional to the number of molecules. The peak heights of the annotated compounds in their EIC were recorded in Table S4 for the <em>Sophora flavescens</em> sample at a concentration of 10 mg/mL. It shows that the most abundant compound of the tested sample was oxymatrine. Other abundant alkaloids are sophoridine and matrine. The most abundant flavonoids are kurarinone and xanthohumol. It was noteworthy that for the 1.0 mg/g sample, compounds such as calycosin, lycorine, and nomilin were not annotated in both the MN and direct DB matching approaches.</p>
<p id="Par48">It was found that the MN approach annotated some trace compounds that could not be annotated by direct DB matching. Typical trace compounds such as glycitin, sophoricoside, and lycorine were all annotated through the DIA data-based MN. It proves that MN on GNPS can overcome the challenges of trace compound identification compared to direct DB matching.</p></section></section><section id="Sec15"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h2>
<p id="Par49">The present study proposed a dereplication strategy for the screening of secondary metabolites in <em>Sophora flavescens</em>. The UPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS analysis in both DDA and DIA mode was performed firstly. DIA and DDA data were then used to build a MN for the propagation annotation. The DDA data were directly matched to the offline DB to obtain complementary annotations. Isomers were annotated and distinguished by their retention time difference. Through a combination of GNPS MN annotation, direct DB matching and isomer discrimination, 51 compounds were annotated and dereplicated at 3 evidence levels. The results provided a closer and more comprehensive understanding of the chemical constitution of <em>Sophora flavescens</em>. This dereplication strategy can be used as a general approach for the plant metabolomics research.</p></section><section id="Sec16"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Supplementary Information</h2>
