>MATERIALS AND METHODS   Fungal and plant materials.   The fungal species used in this study were:  Metarhizium acridum  (ARSEF 7486 and ARSEF 324),  M. brunneum  (ARSEF 2974),  M. flavoviride  (ARSEF 2133), and  M. robertsii  (ARSEF 2575). All cultures were maintained on potato dextrose agar (PDA; Bioshop, Canada). Plant seeds of corn ( Zea mays  “Sunnyvee”) and haricot bean ( Phaseolus vulgaris  “Soldier”) were purchased from OSC Seeds (ON, Canada). Before use, all plant seeds were surface sterilized by submersion in 2% sodium hypochlorite for 5 min and rinsed with sterile, distilled water (3×), followed by submersion in 15% hydrogen peroxide for 20 min. Seeds were rinsed and stored at 4°C overnight for stratification. Seeds were germinated on water agar plates incubated at 25°C for 5 to 7 days. A subsample of the seeds was checked for sterility by incubating on PDA at 25°C, and the final water rinse (100 μL) was inoculated onto PDA and incubated at 25°C. Sample preparation.   Germinated seedlings of corn and bean were placed in pairs onto 20 mL M-100 agar plates. The radicle of the seedling was dipped into sterile 0.01% Triton-X before placement for better dispersion of the conidial suspension. Eight 10 μL droplets of a 1.5 × 10 6  conidia/mL suspension were dispersed over the radicles and the lower portion of the plate where the roots would develop. M-100 plates inoculated with an equivalent amount of conidial suspension or seedlings treated only with Triton-X were controls. Samples were run in duplicate, and plates were sealed with parafilm and incubated at 25°C in a 16/8-h light/dark cycle for 4 and 7 days. Metabolite extraction.   For coculture experiments and uninoculated plant controls, the plant material was cut 1 cm above the hypocotyl and discarded. The remaining contents of the agar plate (including agar with embedded plant roots and fungal growth) were transferred to a mortar and ground under liquid nitrogen. The entire contents of the fungal pure culture plates and the uninoculated agar plate controls were ground under liquid nitrogen. The contents were transferred to a 150 mL flask to which 50 mL of high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)-grade methanol was added, and the flask was placed into a sonicating water bath set to 40 kHz for 15 min. The solution was then filtered twice (Whatman filter followed by a 0.2 μm filter) and reduced to a volume of 5 mL by rotary evaporation. A 1 mL aliquot was transferred to a 2 mL LC-MS/MS vial (Thermo Scientific) for analysis. LC-MS/MS analysis.   High-performance liquid chromatography (LC) coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) was performed on the Q Exactive Quadrupole-Orbitrap (Thermo Fisher). The ionization source was electrospray (ESI) and the samples were analyzed in positive ion mode. The LC column was SB-C18 (2.1 × 50 mm × 1.8 μm, Thermo Fisher) at a column temperature of 35°C. The mobile phase consisted of phases A (water with 0.1% formic acid) and B (acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid). The gradient program was as follows: 0 to 2 min, 10% B; 2 to 17 min, 10 to 100% B; 17 to 18 min, 100 to 10% B. The flow rate was set at 0.4 mL/min. A 10 μL aliquot of the supernatant was injected for each analysis. The MS analysis conditions were as follows: mass range:  m/z  150 to 2000; ion spray voltage: 4500 V; electrospray ionization probe temperature: 425°C; heated capillary temperature: 300°C; sheath gas: 50 arb (arbitrary units); auxiliary gas: 15 arb; collision energy: 30 V; resolution: 70,000; MS/MS resolution: 17,500; isolation window: 4  m/z ; and (S)-lens RF level: 55. To control for potential cross-contamination from the LC column, LC-MS/MS samples were randomized before analysis, and arranged as follows: 3 MeOH > 7 samples > MeOH control > 7 samples > MeOH > 7 samples > 3 MeOH. Processing of LC/MS data and statistical analysis.   The raw data files were preprocessed using the program MS-DIAL ( 18 ) for baseline correction, peak picking, alignment, and adduct identification. Agar plate extractions and methanol controls were used as the analysis baseline to further control for the metabolite background. The publicly available spectral file (29,269 spectra, as of June 2019;  http://prime.psc.riken.jp/Metabolomics_Software/MS-DIAL/ ) was combined with the in-house database curations of known DTX analogs. A table with peak height information for each sample was normalized using the mTIC method within MS-DIAL and then exported for transformation, data scaling and analysis using the web-based server, MetaboAnalyst 4.0 ( 43 ). MetaboAnalyst 4.0 was used to generate the principal-component analyses (PCAs), and heatmaps and to determine significant differences in DTX production between groups using an analysis of variance (ANOVA; Tukey honestly significantly different [HSD]  post hoc ). Feature-based molecular networking (FBMN) was performed using the Global Natural Product Social Molecular Networking (GNPS) site. The data were deposited to the Mass Spectrometry Interactive Virtual Environment ( http://massive.ucsd.edu ) with MassIVE accession numbers:  MSV000085394  (bean cocultures) and  MSV000085395  (corn cocultures). Confirming the presence of the destruxin synthetase ( dtxS1 ) gene.   Genomic DNA of the fungi  Metarhizium   robertsii  (ARSEF 2575),  M. acridum  (ARSEF 7486),  M. acridum  (ARSEF 324),  M. brunneum  (ARSEF 2974), and  M. flavoviride  (ARSEF 2133), was extracted (DNeasy Plant Minikit, Qiagen) and amplified using the previously described primer pair, dtxF ( AGATTTGCCGCAGCTACCTA ) and dtxR ( CACCAGATGCGAGTTCTCAA ) for the  dtxS1  gene ( 3 ).  M. acridum  ARSEF 324 was included as a negative-control because, in a previous study, the  dtxS1  gene could not be amplified ( 3 ). The primers, itsF ( TTACGTCCCTGCCCTTTGTA ) and itsR ( ACCTCACCAAAAGCATCCTC ) were used to amplify the internal transcribed spacer (ITS) regions to confirm successful DNA extraction ( 3 ). The 25 μL reactions consisted of 2.5 μL 10× ThermoPol Buffer (New England Biolabs), 1 μL DNA, 0.5 μL 10 mM dNTPs, and 0.2 μM each primer pair ( 3 ). The PCR protocol was: 30 s initial denaturation at 95°C, followed by 40 cycles of 20 s at 95°C, 1 min at 65°C, and 45 s at 68°C for extension, then held at 68°C for 5 min. Gel electrophoresis was performed with 5 μL of each PCR product at 90 V for 40 min on a 1.2% agarose gel. The resulting band(s) for  dtxS1  were cut from the gel and purified by MinElute Gel Extraction kit (Qiagen) and sent for Sanger sequencing (The Centre for Applied Genomics, Toronto). Once the presence of the  dtxS1  gene in all fungal strains used in this study was confirmed (Fig. S1), the assembled sequences were submitted to GenBank (Accession numbers:  ON359876  to  ON359881 ). Data availability.   The MS/MS data were deposited to the Mass Spectrometry Interactive Virtual Environment ( http://massive.ucsd.edu ) with MassIVE accession numbers:  MSV000085394  (bean cocultures) and  MSV000085395  (corn cocultures). The assembled sequences for  dtxS1  amplicons were submitted to GenBank ( https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/ ) with accession numbers  ON359876  to  ON359881 .
