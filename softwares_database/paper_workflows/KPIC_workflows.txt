========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: KPIC
sample_description: The KPIC2 paper validated the framework on three LC-MS datasets: (1) the MM48 dataset, a mixture of 48 chemical standards used to benchmark pure-ion detection performance against existing tools; (2) a quantitative dataset for assessing quantification accuracy; and (3) a soybean seed dataset for classification performance. All are untargeted high-resolution LC-MS metabolomics data suitable for pure ion chromatogram extraction. Specific biological sample preparation details (extraction solvents, chromatography columns) are not reported in the abstract.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end KPIC2 workflow: (1) raw untargeted LC-MS data is read into a three-column (retention time, m/z, intensity) matrix via LoadData/readfiles (mzR-backed; supports mzXML, CDF, and mzML); (2) pure ion chromatograms (PICs) are extracted by iterative landmark selection — the highest-intensity ion seeds a region of interest (ROI) delimited by a flexible ppm m/z tolerance, and optimal one-dimensional k-means clustering (Ckmeans.1d.dp) on the squared m/z differences isolates the same ion from noise; PIC intensities are refined by exponential smoothing (getPIC / PICset; key parameters level, mztol, gap, width, min_snr); (3) multiscale peak detection (MSPD) runs in continuous-wavelet space with the Mexican hat wavelet, locating peak positions from ridges, valleys, and zero-crossings of the wavelet coefficients and filtering peaks by signal-to-noise ratio and intensity (PICset.getPeaks); (4) multi-peak PICs may be split (PICset.split) and resolved; (5) PICs are aligned across samples (PICset.align), grouped into isotope/adduct clusters (PICset.group), and missing peaks are filled (fillPeaks.EIBPC); (6) a peak table (m/z, retention time, intensity, area, SNR) is produced for downstream statistics. In benchmark comparisons, KPIC2 extracted more true ions with fewer detected features than MZmine2 and XCMS.
source_paper: Ji et al. (2017), Analytical chemistry, DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b01547, PMID: 28621925

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: KPIC
sample_description: Two distinct biological datasets were analyzed: (1) a liquor dataset, comprising samples of different types of Chinese liquor (specific types and sample sizes not detailed in the abstract or provided methods section), and (2) a left-sided colon cancer (LCC) dataset, consisting of human clinical samples — likely including both LCC patient samples and healthy controls, though exact numbers, tissue sources (e.g., serum, plasma, or tissue), collection protocols, and preprocessing steps (e.g., extraction method, LC-MS platform parameters) are not specified in the provided text. Both datasets were acquired via liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS) in untargeted metabolomics mode.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with raw LC-MS data from two untargeted metabolomics datasets (liquor and left-sided colon cancer). First, pure ion chromatograms are extracted using KPIC2 — a method that applies K-means clustering to deconvolve co-eluting features and isolate chemically pure signals. Next, the resulting feature matrix is subjected to uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) for nonlinear dimensionality reduction and visualization of group separation. Finally, extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) is applied to build discriminant classification models using the KPIC2-extracted features. Model performance is evaluated on test sets, achieving 100% classification accuracy for both datasets — outperforming XCMS-based feature extraction followed by XGBoost (92% and 96%, respectively). The integration of UMAP and XGBoost into the KPIC2 package constitutes a unified, automated pipeline extending beyond feature extraction to include visualization and supervised modeling.
source_paper: Tian M et al. (2021), Molecules, DOI: 10.3390/molecules26092715, PMID: 34063107
