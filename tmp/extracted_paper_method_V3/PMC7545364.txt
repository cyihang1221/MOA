</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="ABS1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p id="P1">This protocol provides a step-by-step guide to shotgun sphingolipid analysis of aqueous humor. We describe the Bligh and Dyer crude lipid extraction method and electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (ESI-MS/MS) coupled with MZmine 2.21 data processing for identification and ratiometric quantitation of sphingosine, sphingosine 1-phosphate, sphingomyelin and ceramide.</p>
<section id="kwd-group1" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Aqueous humor, Sphingolipids, Sphingosine, Sphingosine 1-phosphate, Sphingomyelin, Ceramide, Shotgun lipidomics, Electrospray ionization-tandem mass spectrometry (ESI-MS/MS), MZmine 2.21</p></section></section><section id="S1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">1. Introduction</h2>
<p id="P2">Being once attributed to structural roles only, it is now known that many sphingolipid species are biologically active and play a role in development and progression of neurodegenerative diseases (<a href="#R1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R1">1</a>–<a href="#R5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R5">5</a>). Due to many similarities with the central nervous system, the eye has become an object of sphingolipid research as well. However, until recently, the functions of sphingolipids in the eye (<a href="#R6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R6">6</a>–<a href="#R10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R10">10</a>) including the aqueous humor (<a href="#R11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R11">11</a>,<a href="#R12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R12">12</a>), have been explored rarely.</p>
<p id="P3">Here, we describe techniques for shotgun sphingolipid analyses to be used for human aqueous humor, and with modifications for other selected classes of lipids and/or other eye tissues. The procedure is simpler, easier and faster to perform in comparison to many others reported for sphingolipids. Nevertheless, the readers should be aware of its limitations <em>i.e.</em> Bligh and Dyer extraction method is not specific to sphingolipids, resulting in the complexity of the sample and potential matrix effect. This method also does not ensure total sphingolipid recovery, especially of more polar species <em>e.g.</em> sphingosine 1-phosphate. We recommend the use of both internal and external standards to improve the precision and accuracy of the method.</p></section><section id="S2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. Materials</h2>
<section id="S3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1. Lipid Extraction (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN1" class="usa-link">Note 1</a>)</h3>
<ol id="L1" class="list" style="list-style-type:decimal">
<li><p id="P4">Methanol-Chloroform: LC-MS grade methanol and LC-MS grade chloroform in 2:1 ratio (freshly prepared in an amber glass bottle at room temperature).</p></li>
<li><p id="P5">Chloroform: LC-MS grade (stored in an amber glass bottle at room temperature).</p></li>
<li><p id="P6">Internal lipid standards (Avanti Polar Lipids Inc., Alabaster, AL, USA) (<em>see</em>
<a href="#T1" class="usa-link">Tab. 1</a>).</p></li>
<li><p id="P7">Glassware: Teflon capped tubes, homogenizers and pipette tips (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN2" class="usa-link">Note 2</a>).</p></li>
<li><p id="P8">Argon gas: compressed, ultra-high purity grade.</p></li>
<li><p id="P9">Protein quantitation assay kit of choice.</p></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P10">Other:
<ol id="L2" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P11">Freezer: −80<sup>o</sup>C</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P12">Ice bucket or styrofoam container with ice</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P13">Vortexer</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P14">Centrifuge</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P15">Centrifugal vacuum concentrator.</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P16">Optional:
<ol id="L3" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P17">Antioxidant: butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT)</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P18">Water: LC-MS grade.</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
</ol>

<div class="caption p"><p id="P144">Parameters for analyses of different sphingolipid classes. Internal standards are indicated with *. They are also available as parts of Sphingolipid Mix I/II (LM-6002/6005).</p></div>

<section id="S7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.1. Lipid Extraction (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN1" class="usa-link">Notes 1</a>–<a href="#FN5" class="usa-link">5</a>)</h3>
<p id="P33">Before starting lipid extraction, transfer an aliquot of each sample to a new tube for determination of total protein content using bicinchoninic acid (BCA (<a href="#R13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R13">13</a>) or Bradford method (<a href="#R14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R14">14</a>). For lower protein concentrations not measurable using BCA or Bradford method, we recommend the use of the PhastGel system and densitometry using a bovine serum albumin (BSA) standard for protein quantitation (<a href="#R15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R15">15</a>–<a href="#R17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R17">17</a>).</p>
<ol id="L8" class="list" style="list-style-type:decimal">
<li><p id="P34">Add the same amount of each internal standard (<em>e.g.</em> 250 pmols) to each of your sample.</p></li>
<li>
<p id="P35">Add Methanol-Chloroform in a solvent/sample ratio of 3:1 depending on the volume of the aqueous humor specimen (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN6" class="usa-link">Note 6</a>):</p>
<p id="P36"><em>e.g</em>. add 300 μL of Methanol-Chloroform to 100 μL of aqueous humor sample.</p>
</li>
<li><p id="P37">Homogenize for 2 minutes using a glass homogenizer (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN2" class="usa-link">Note 2</a>).</p></li>
<li>
<p id="P38">Add Chloroform in a Chloroform/sample ratio 2–1:1 depending on the volume of aqueous humor specimen in <strong>Step 3.1(2)</strong>:</p>
<p id="P39"><em>e.g.</em> add 100–200 μL of Chloroform if the volume of aqueous humor sample in <strong>Step 3.1(2)</strong> was 100 μL.</p>
</li>
<li><p id="P40">Homogenize as in <strong>Step 3.1(3).</strong></p></li>
<li><p id="P41">Vortex for 30 seconds.</p></li>
<li><p id="P42">Centrifuge samples at 20,000 x g for 15 minutes at 4<sup>o</sup>C.</p></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P43">You should have obtained phase separation into (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN7" class="usa-link">Notes 7</a>–<a href="#FN8" class="usa-link">8</a>):
<ol id="L9" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P44">Superior aqueous layer (aqueous-soluble compounds in methanol-water)</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P45">Inferior organic layer (lipids in chloroform).</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
<li><p id="P46">Place the lipid fractions into new tubes. To prevent contamination by proteins due to repeated pipetting through the aqueous phase, use a new tip each time</p></li>
<li><p id="P47">Aliquot each lipid fraction into 4 parts.</p></li>
<li><p id="P48">To remove chloroform dry lipid extracts in a centrifugal vacuum concentrator for 90 minutes or longer if needed (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN9" class="usa-link">Note 9</a>).</p></li>
<li><p id="P49">Dried lipids can be stored at −80<sup>o</sup>C until mass spectrometric analysis (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN10" class="usa-link">Note 10</a>).</p></li>
</ol></section><section id="S8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.2. Mass Spectrometric Analysis</h3>
<ol id="L10" class="list" style="list-style-type:decimal">
<li><div class="p" id="P50">To prepare lipid samples and external standard calibrators (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN10" class="usa-link">Notes 10</a>–<a href="#FN11" class="usa-link">11</a>):
<ol id="L11" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P51">Reconstitute dried lipid samples obtained in <strong>Step 3.1(12)</strong> with Acetonitrile:Isopropanol, except for samples dedicated to analysis of sphingomyelin that should be reconstituted in Lithiated Chloroform:Methanol. Vortex for 1 minute.</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P52">Create each external standard calibration curve (<em>e.g.</em> 6-point from 0 to 300 pmol) in Acetonitrile:Isopropanol except for sphingomyelin standard that should be diluted in Lithiated Chloroform:Methanol. To each calibrator add the same amount of matching internal standard as added to samples in <strong>Step 3.1(1).</strong>
</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
<li><p id="P53">For different sphingolipids set the following parameters: <em>see</em>
<a href="#T1" class="usa-link">Tab. 1</a>, in Thermo Scientific Xcalibur and Tunes Software.</p></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P54">Set the following parameters for ESI:
<ol id="L12" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P55">Flow rate infusion: 10 μL/min</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P56">Spectrum recording: 2 min</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P57">Scan: 0.5 sec</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P58">Ionization voltage: 1.55 kV for Positive ion mode or 1.3 kV for Negative ion node</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P59">Gas pressure: 0.45 psi for Positive ion mode or 0.6 psi for Negative ion mode</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
<li><p id="P60">For quality control: run lipid standards separately before and after running the samples. Moreover, a blank (Acetonitrile:Isopropanol) can be run before and after each sample to exclude carryover.</p></li>

</ol></section><section id="S11"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.2. Mass Detection</h4>
<p id="P63">The purpose of this step is to create a list of masses for each raw file. The settings depend on how the data were acquired and the scan quality. For centroided data, the Centroid Mass Detector is applied and assumes that every signal above the specified noise level is a peak.</p>
<ol id="L14" class="list" style="list-style-type:decimal">
<li><p id="P64">Raw Data Methods &gt; Peak Detection &gt; Mass Detection</p></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P65">Adjust the parameters according to data resolution and precision:
<ol id="L15" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P66">MS level: depends on type of scan. 2 for NLS and 1 for PIS or full MS1 scans</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P67">Polarity: depends on type of ionization</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P68">Mass Detector: Centroid</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P69">Noise level = 0</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P70">Mass list name: masses</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
<li><p id="P71">Other mass detectors may be used as the exact mass detector: local maxima, recursive threshold or wavelength transform mass detectors.</p></li>
</ol></section><section id="S12"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.3. Chromatogram Builder (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN13" class="usa-link">Note 13</a>)</h4>
<p id="P72">After running the mass detection, the chromatogram builder makes a constant chromatogram across the scans for each mass. While ideal setting for each chromatogram may change depending on the data set, common baseline settings for several classes are given.</p>
<p id="P73">The min time span parameter specifies the minimum time during which a peak must be detected to be included in the chromatogram. Min height is determined by the least possible intensity of the highest data point. Any chromatogram found below the set level is rejected as noise. The m/z tolerance is the greatest allowed m/z difference between data points to be grouped into the same chromatogram.</p>

<li>

</ol></section><section id="S19"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.8. Post-MZmine Analysis</h4>
<p id="P117">For experimental purposes it is often useful to organize and analyze the data as presented in the MZmine-exported file. Further manipulation is often required to analyze data from large experiments or for selecting species for detailed study.</p>
<ol id="L60" class="list" style="list-style-type:decimal">
<li><div class="p" id="P118">Ratiometric Quantification: The quantitation of different sphingolipid species is obtained by the use of constant amount of non-naturally occurring internal standard together with calibration curves obtained with different amounts of naturally-occurring species. To compensate for some limitations of the method <em>e.g.</em> matrix effect or machine instabilities, instead of analyte or external standard calibrator areas, their ratios to respective internal standard areas are used.
<ol id="L62" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P119">Construct external standard calibration plots of concentration of external (C<sub>ES</sub>) standard (<em>x</em>-axis values) <em>versus</em> area ratio of external (A<sub>ES</sub>) and internal (A<sub>IS</sub>) standard (A<sub>ES</sub>/A<sub>IS</sub>) (<em>y</em>-axis values).</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P120">For each external standard calculate linear regression curves.</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P121">To determine analyte concentration from obtained linear regression equation (<span xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><math id="M1" display="inline"><mi>y</mi><mo>=</mo><mi>a</mi><mi>x</mi><mo>+</mo><mi>b</mi></math></span>), substitute <em>y</em> with area ratio of analyte (A<sub>A</sub>) and internal standard (A<sub>A</sub>/A<sub>IS</sub>) in the sample of interest (<em>see</em>
<a href="#FN16" class="usa-link">Note 16</a>).</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P122">The amount of lipid species obtained from the ratiometric quantification analyses above is then divided by the total amount of protein in the sample for normalization. The final amount of lipid is expressed in pmol of lipid/μg protein.</div></li>
</ol>
</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P123">Multi-sample analysis: The nature of experimentation often requires manipulations of parameters across different sheets to obtain a single data point. For lipidomics this may include selecting the lowest m/z and manually averaging the associated peak areas for a single species in many data sheets in tandem, quickly becoming a very time-consuming task. Third party software exists but may be difficult to locate and secure. As such, it can be necessary to design simple programs (<em>e.g.</em> in Excel’s VBA language). Some useful program schematics follow:
<ol id="L64" class="list" style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
<li><div class="p" id="P124">Programs which will average the m/z and peak area values for each occurrence of a certain named species, assigning results to a new sheet.</div></li>
<li><div class="p" id="P125">Programs which can indicate the frequency of occurrence of identified peaks across multiple sheets from different replicates.</div></li>
