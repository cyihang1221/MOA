</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="Abs1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p id="Par1">Diabetic osteoporosis (DOP) is a long-term musculoskeletal complication of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) in the population. One well-known traditional Chinese medicine(TCM) medication that works effectively for DOP is Jingui Shenqi Pill (JGSQP). However, more research is needed to understand the underlying mechanisms. In this study, both the potential active targets and therapeutic mechanisms of JGSQP on DOP are explored by integrated analysis of metabolomics and network pharmacology. The metabolic pathways closely linked to the function of JGSQP, and differential metabolites were validated using plasma metabolomics. Subsequently, network pharmacology was utilized to reveal additional essential targets against DOP caused by JGSQP. JGSQP can significantly reduce bone loss, injury to trabecular bone tissue, and aberrant glucose metabolism in DOP mice. Furthermore, JGSQP also successfully restored the MC3T3-E1 cells’ impoverished proliferation brought on by excessive hyperglycemia. Using network pharmacology analysis in conjunction with liquid chromatography coupled with liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (LC-Q-TOF/MS), 143 JGSQP components—or 277 targets—were found. 3168 targets were connected to DOP. Among them, 152 targets overlapped with known DOP treatment-related targets as potential targets for JGSQP treatment of DOP. TP53, TNF, IL-1β, and NFKBIA2 were identified by protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis as possible targets. Moreover, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis revealed that the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway and NF-κB signaling pathway in diabetic complications were the key pathways through which JGSQP exerts its therapeutic effects on DOP. The compound reaction-enzyme-gene network diagram was created by enriching the major metabolic pathways of tryptophan metabolism and amino sugar metabolism. Finally, further experimental data verified that JGSQP treatment could significantly reduce the expression levels of inflammatory factors IL-1β and IL-6, and AGE, NFKB in MC3T3-E1 cells. JGSQP can reduce DOP by regulating the pathway to reduce inflammation and improve metabolism. The study offers a theoretical foundation for the investigation of TCM in the treatment of DOP from various perspectives and uncovers the intricate mechanism of JGSQP in the treatment of DOP.</p>
<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Supplementary Information</h3>
<p>The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-35129-w.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Jingui shenqi pill, Diabetic osteoporosis, Metabolomics, Network pharmacology, Inflammation</p></section><section id="kwd-group2" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Subject terms:</strong> Inflammation, Medical research</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p id="Par2">Diabetic osteoporosis (DOP) represents a significant complication affecting bone health in individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Globally, over 424 million individuals aged 20–79 are affected by T2DM<sup><a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a></sup>. The rising prevalence of T2DM among adults is associated with an increased risk of deterioration in trabecular microstructure and a higher incidence of fragility fractures annually. DOP has emerged as a global health problem affecting the quality of human life, and a major threat to the social economy<sup><a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>,<a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a></sup>. Unfortunately, osteoporosis has received less attention than other T2DM complications such as cardiovascular disease and kidney disease<sup><a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a></sup>. At present, there is no established method for effectively preventing, delaying, or treating DOP. Physicians primarily use glucose-lowering medications in conjunction with calcium supplements and bisphosphonates to manage DOP. While these treatments can reduce blood glucose levels and alleviate some symptoms, they are not consistently effective and may have adverse side effects. As such, patients remain at an elevated risk of fractures due to inadequate treatment and prevention strategies<sup><a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a></sup>. Therefore, it is essential to explore therapeutic options that effectively manage hyperglycemia while preserving bone health for the treatment of DOP.</p>
<p id="Par3">Over the past decade, while the pathological features of DOP have been well-documented, the underlying mechanisms leading to DOP development and progression remain to be deciphered. Currently, accumulated evidence indicates that systemic impairment of bone metabolism is a key characteristic of DOP<sup><a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">6</a></sup>. Notably, hyperglycemia is a critical factor contributing to the development of DOP<sup><a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a></sup>. Additionally, this condition is associated with abnormally elevated levels of extracellular metabolites in bone cells and muscles. These elevated metabolite levels affect the physiological function of the bone matrix, disrupt the microenvironment of glucose and lipid metabolism, and ultimately contribute to an increased risk of fractures<sup><a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a>,<a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">9</a></sup>. Chinese herbal medicine has garnered significant attention recently due to its bone-protective effects in treating osteoporosis. It has been found effective in reducing pain associated with DOP, improving the hyperglycemic microenvironment, and regulating bone metabolism, all while demonstrating satisfactory outcomes and safety<sup><a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a></sup>. Jingui Shenqi Pill (JGSQP) is a famous traditional Chinese herbal compound prescription composed of eight different botanical drugs. It was first documented in the <em>Synopsis of the Golden Chamber</em> by Zhang Zhongjing during the Han Dynasty. Numerous studies have confirmed that JGSQP possesses hypoglycemic effects, enhances insulin sensitivity in diabetic patients, and exhibits antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties<sup><a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">11</a></sup>. In recent years, multiple studies have confirmed the significant efficacy of JGSQP in the treatment of DOP, primarily associated with its role in maintaining bone metabolic homeostasis. For instance, a randomized controlled trial demonstrated that the combination of JGSQP with conventional treatment significantly increased patients’ bone mineral density (BMD)<sup><a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a></sup>. Meta-analyses and experimental studies conducted by researchers such as Zhipeng Hu further indicated that JGSQP can effectively regulate serum bone turnover markers, including downregulating the expression of the receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL) and its receptor RANK, while upregulating osteoprotegerin (OPG), thereby reducing fracture risk<sup><a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a></sup>. However, the impact and pathophysiological mechanisms of JGSQP on DOP remain to be explored.</p>
<p id="Par4">Traditional Chinese herbal compounds are grounded in the holistic principles of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), typically consisting of a blend of herbs that work synergistically to enhance therapeutic efficacy. Yet, this complexity also defines the intricate nature of their composition<sup><a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a></sup>. The advancement of information science has led to the development of ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-Q-TOF-MS), which offers high sensitivity, resolution, and mass accuracy, rendering it an invaluable tool for the separation and identification of the chemical components in Chinese herbal medicines. Network pharmacology, which examines drug pharmacological mechanisms from a macro or holistic regulatory perspective, is widely used to elucidate the potential effects of drugs, targets, and diseases<sup><a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a></sup>. Emerging evidence suggests that the development of DOP is often linked with elevated levels of plasma glycolipid metabolites. Metabolomics, a high-throughput quantitative and qualitative analysis technique, is employed for disease diagnosis and to clarify the pathogenesis, pathways, and potential effects of drugs<sup><a href="#CR16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR16">16</a>,<a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a></sup>. Combining metabolomics with network pharmacology has emerged as an effective approach for elucidating disease mechanisms and understanding the pharmacodynamic effects of Chinese herbal treatments. This approach holds significant value in enhancing the current understanding of the potential benefits and underlying mechanisms of JGSQP in the treatment of DOP<sup><a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a></sup>.</p>
<p id="Par5">In the present study, mouse and cell models of DOP were successfully established, and the pharmacodynamics of JGSQP on DOP were further investigated. In order to accurately obtain the bioactive compounds of JGSQP, UPLC-Q-TOF-MS technology was utilized to analyze its active components. The analysis results were subsequently integrated with network pharmacology to predict potential targets of JGSQP. Finally, the effects of JGSQP on DOP were assessed, and the essential plasma metabolites were analyzed using untargeted metabolomics. The integrated results reveal a more comprehensive underlying mechanism of DOP, and identify potential therapeutic targets for JGSQP in the treatment of DOP, paving the way for broader application.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials and methods</h2>

<p id="Par8">Eight-week-old FVB mice (weighing 23–25 g) were purchased from Beijing Spelford Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) (Certificate No. SCXK-Jing 2019-0010). After 1 week of adaptive feeding, FVB mice were used as a normal control group for further analysis. MKR mice were kindly provided by the laboratory of Dr. D. LeRoith at the Diabetes Research Center, National Institutes of Health (MD, USA). The MKR mice are generated by overexpressing the insulin-resistant diabetic muscle IGF-I receptor (IGF-IR)-lysine-arginine (MKR) in skeletal muscle, and a useful model for exploring the glucose-induced disease. In this study, MKR mice (8 weeks old) were bred by natural mating for experimental research and housed at the specific-pathogen-free Laboratory Animal Center of Hunan University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, License No. SYXK (Xiang) 2013-0005. All mice were maintained under standard laboratory conditions, with a temperature of 25 °C ± 2 °C, relative humidity of 50% ± 15%, and a 12-h dark–light cycle with free access to food and water. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the guidelines set by the same committee.</p>
<p id="Par9">To investigate whether JGSQP rescues the progression of DOP in mice, fifty MKR mice were fed a special high-fat diet (HFD) for 8 weeks to induce diabetic osteoporosis<sup><a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a></sup>. HFD containing 10% lard, 20% sucrose, 5% cholesterol, and 65% standard feed was customized by XieTong biological company (Wuhan, Jiangsu Province, China). After 8 weeks of HFD feeding, DOP mice were randomly divided into five groups (<em>n</em> = 10 per group): the DOP model group (model); the high-dose JGSQP (2.6 g/kg)-treatment group (JGSQP-H); the middle-dose JGSQP (1.3 g/kg)-treatment group (JGSQP-M); the low-dose JGSQP (0.65 g/kg)-treatment group (JGSQP-L); and the alendronate (9.75 mg/kg)-treatment group (ALN). These doses were derived from the human equivalent clinical dose by conversion using body surface area normalization factors. All drug treatments were dissolved in normal saline and given orally for 4 weeks, and the dosages were derived from an equivalent conversion between animals and humans by body surface area based on the recommended daily human dosage. Additionally, mice in the normal and model groups were given an equal volume of distilled water daily. For the confirmation of diabetic osteoporosis, the bone trabecular damage was evaluated by hematoxylin and eosin (H&amp;E) staining. The body weight of mice was recorded at regular time intervals, and blood glucose level was monitored weekly until the end of the animal experiment. The serum lipid profile, including total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL), was assessed across all study groups. After 4 weeks of treatment, the mice were euthanized using isoflurane anesthesia followed by cervical dislocation, in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines and the ARRIVE guidelines, to ensure humane treatment and confirm death.</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)</h3>
<p id="Par10">After 4 weeks treatment with JGSQP by gavage administration, the OGTT was performed using as previously described<sup><a href="#CR22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR22">22</a></sup>. Briefly, after an overnight fast of 10 h, all mice were loaded with 2 g/kg body weight D-glucose (10% D glucose solution; Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) by oral administration. Blood was sampled from the tail vein at 0 (baseline), 30, 60, 90, and 120 min to detect glucose levels using a glucometer. The area under the curves (AUC; units: mmol/L·H) was used to calculate the glucose tolerance.</p></section><section id="Sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&amp;E) staining</h3>
<p id="Par11">Following harvesting of left femoral tissues, the samples were decalcified in 10% tetracyclin EDTA solution(0.5 M, pH = 8.0)(Servicebio༉ at room temperature for 2 weeks. Next, all samples were dehydrated through a gradient series of ethanol in a dehydrator (Leica, ASP300s) and then embedded in paraffin wax (Leica, 1150). After trimming the paraffin blocks, 4-µm-thick bone tissue slices were prepared using a paraffin tissue slicer (Leica, RM2245). The sections were stained with the H&amp;E staining solution. The sections were imaged using an Olympus BX53 light microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). The histological analyses, including bone volume per tissue volume (BV/TV), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), and trabecular thickness (Tb.Th) were performed by Image Pro Plus 6.0 software.</p></section><section id="Sec7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)</h3>
<p id="Par12">On the last day of the animal experiment, the mice were anesthetized with isoflurane and blood samples were collected from the orbital veins plexus and placed in EDTA tubes. The serum was collected after centrifugation and stored in a refrigerator at −80℃ for subsequent assays. The serum concentrations of β-C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen (β-CTX), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and procollagen type 1-N-terminal propeptide (PINP) were measured using mouse ELISA kits (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The optical density (OD) values were determined using a microplate reader (EnVision, PerkinElmer, USA) at 450 nm.</p></section><section id="Sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Microcomputed tomography (Micro-CT) imaging and analysis<sup><a href="#CR23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR23">23</a></sup>
</h3>
<p id="Par13">After harvesting, for Micro-CT analysis, the right femurs of mice were quickly put into 4% paraformaldehyde solution and fixed for 24 h (4℃). Subsequently, the femurs were analyzed using a micro-CT imaging system (Quantum GX, PerkinEImer, Singapore) under the following settings: 80 kV voltage, current of 100 µA, rotation step of 0.4°, slice thickness of 5 μm, and a scanning duration of 14 min. Following the scan, the two-dimensional (2D) image sequence was automatically reconstructed into a three-dimensional (3D) volume with an isotropic voxel size of 15.9 μm, using Skyscan NRecon software and the Feldkamp-Davis-Kress algorithm. The trabecular microstructure of the femur was analyzed by selecting a 1.0 mm volume of interest, starting 0.45 mm below the proximal tibial growth plate and extending 1.0 mm distally. The structural parameters used to evaluate trabecular bone included trabecular number (Tb.N), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), bone volume (BV), percentage of bone volume per tissue volume (BV/TV), trabecular volume (TV), and bone mineral density (BMD). Additionally, the structure model index (SMI) was measured to assess cortical bone parameters.</p></section><section id="Sec9"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Preparation of JGSQP-medicated serum</h3>
<p id="Par14">The medicated serum approach was employed to simulate the in vivo metabolic processing of JGSQP. This method ensures that the cells are exposed to the bioavailable metabolites of the formula, thereby providing a more physiologically relevant model than the direct application of the herbal extract, which can be confounded by issues of solubility, osmolarity, and non-bioavailable components. Because cellular medications were needed for in vitro experiments, JGSQP - medicated serum was prepared as in previous studies<sup><a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a>,<a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">25</a></sup>. After 7 days of adaptive feeding, ten Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (250 g) were randomly divided into the JGSQP and blank groups (5 rats per group). The rats in JGSQP group were administered at 6.5 g/kg/d (converted for the adult daily dose), and the blank group was given an equal volume of distilled water at the same time every day. Rats were gavaged twice a day for 5 days. The blood was obtained from the abdominal aortic 2 h after the final gavage of anesthesia, then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min at 4◦C. The supernatants were calefied in a water bath at 56℃ for 30 min and following of filtration using a 0.22 µM fine-pore filter, then finally packed and stored at −80 °C for future use.</p></section><section id="Sec10"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Cell culture and in vitro differentiation</h3>
<p id="Par15">The mouse pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 subclone 14 cell line was purchased from the Procell Life Science &amp; Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). The MC3T3-E1 was cultured in α-minimum essential medium (α-MEM, Solarbio, Beijing, China) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Hycclone, Logan USA), and 1% (v/v) penicillin/streptomycin (P/S, Procell Life Science &amp;Technology Co., China), All cells were maintained at 37 °C in 5% CO<sub>2</sub>. For the in vitro differentiation experiments, once the MC3T3-E1 cells reached confluence, they were seeded at a density of 1 × 10<sup>4</sup> cells/well in 6-well plates and cultured in complete cell culture medium supplemented with differentiation medium. The differentiation medium contained 10 mM β-Glycerol phosphate disodium salt pentahydrate, 5 µmol/L L-Ascorbic acid, and 1 mmol/L Dexamethasone (Sigma-Aldrich Trading Co.Ltd, Darmstadt, Germany), while the fresh differentiation medium was replaced every 2–3 days.</p>
<p id="Par16">Subsequently, cells were cultured in various groups, including control medium (α-MEM), HG medium (25 mM glucose to mimic diabetic conditions), HG + serum medium (with the 25 mM glucose and blank medicated) and HG + drug serum (with the 25 mM glucose and JGSQP medicated) for 48 h to investigate the potential mechanism of JGSQP in osteoblasts.</p></section><section id="Sec11"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay</h3>
<p id="Par17">For cell viability assay, MC3T3- E1 cells were seeded into a 96-well plate with 3 × 10<sup>3</sup> well cell density, after culturing with complete α-MEM for 12 h, the cells were treated with increasing concentrations of JGSQP (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30%) for 48 h under HG (25 mM glucose)conditions and incubated with CCK8 solution (TransGen Bioech, Beijing, China)) at 37 °C for 1.5 h, at the end of the incubation period, the OD value was evaluated at 450 nm under a microplate reader (EnVision, PerkinElmer, USA), and the cell viability was calculated using the following formula: (OD <sub>treated cells</sub> – OD <sub>blank</sub>)/(OD <sub>control</sub> – OD <sub>blank</sub>) × 100%.</p></section><section id="Sec12"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Western blotting</h3>
<p id="Par18">Protein samples from the cells were extracted using a lysis buffer (KeyGen Biotech. Co. Ltd, Nanjing, China) containing a cocktail of protease and phosphatase inhibitors for 30 min on ice. After determining protein concentration using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Keygen Biotech, Nanjing, China), equal amounts of protein samples (30 µg) were loaded onto sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were then blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature (RT). The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Next day, after three washes with Tris-buffered saline, 0.2% Tween 20 (TBST), the membranes were incubated with appropriate horseradish appropriate (HRP) conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at RT. Finally, following washing three times with TBST, the enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kits (Advansta, Menlo Park, CA, USA) were used to develop protein bands. The sensitive chemiluminescence images were quantified with the Bio-Rad gel imaging system and ImageJ software, and the relative expression of proteins were normalized to β-actin.</p></section><section id="Sec13"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">ALP staining and activity assay<sup><a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a></sup>
</h3>
<p id="Par19">For the ALP activity assay in MC3T3-E1 cells, ALP staining was conducted using a BCIP/NBT alkaline phosphatase color development kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions. In brief, the medium was removed, and the cells were washed with PBS and fixed in 4% PFA for 15 min and then exposed to 1% ALP staining buffer in the dark for 20 min, and then observed under a light microscope.</p></section><section id="Sec14"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Alizarin red S staining<sup><a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a></sup>

<p id="Par21">First, the bioactive ingredients of JGSQP were identified using The Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology database (TCMSP, <a href="http://tcmspw.com" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://tcmspw.com</a>), according to the filtering parameters of which was oral bioavailability (OB) &gt;30%, and drug-likeness (DL) &gt;0.18, as these are well-established benchmarks for identifying compounds with a high probability of adequate oral absorption. Then, we used ‘type 2 diabetes mellitus, osteoporosis’ as search terms in a cascade of databases including Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM, <a href="https://omim.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://omim.org/</a>), GeneCards database (<a href="https://www.genecards.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.genecards.org/</a>), PharmGkb (<a href="https://www.pharmgkb.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.pharmgkb.org/</a>), Therapeutic Target database (TTD, <a href="http://db.idrblab.net/ttd/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://db.idrblab.net/ttd/</a>) and DrugBank database (<a href="https://www.drugbank.com/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.drugbank.com/</a>), then we combined the results from databases to improve the accuracy of prediction, and screened potential disease targets related to DOP. Take the intersection of JGSQP targets and disease targets to ultimately obtain common targets, which are defined as the predicted targets of JGSQP for treating the DOP. Next, all intersecting targets were imported into universal Protein Resource database (UniProt, <a href="https://www.uniprot.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.uniprot.org/</a>) to standardize the names into gene symbols.</p>
<p id="Par22">To illustrate the roles of potential therapeutic targets, genes related to the targets of JGSQP in the treatment of DOP were obtained and uploaded into the STRING database (<a href="http://www.string-db.org" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://www.string-db.org</a>) to identify human target proteins interaction. Additionally, herbs, bioactive ingredients and potential targets were imported into the Cytoscape software(National Institute of General Medical Sciences, USA) to construct the visual protein-protein interaction (PPI) network diagram. Afterwards, the Metascape database (<a href="http://metascape.Org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://metascape.Org/</a>) was utilized to conduct the ontology (GO) functional and KEGG enrichment analysis<sup><a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a>–<a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a></sup>.</p></section><section id="Sec16"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Metabolomics analysis and data acquisition</h3>
<p id="Par23">Mice were fasted for 8 h and blood samples were collected from the ocular vein in the morning. Blood samples were collected in a 2 mL vacuum blood collection tube containing the chelating agent ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA). The samples were centrifuged for 15 min (1500 rpm, 4 °C) to obtain plasma. Each equal sample (150 µL) of the plasma sample was quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at-80 °C for subsequent testing. Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed using UHPLC (1290 Infinity LC, Agilent Technologies, CA, USA) coupled to an electrospray ionization source-linked quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (Q-TOF-MS, AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600, Framingham, MA, USA), provided by Shanghai Applied Protein Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).</p></section><section id="Sec17"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Integration analysis of network pharmacology and metabolomics<sup><a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a></sup>
</h3>
<p id="Par24">By comparing the potential pathways enriched by the network pharmacological targets with the metabolic pathways enriched by differential metabolites, the common metabolic pathways which were regarded as the key pathways were screened out. The KEGG database was used to obtain the proteins contained in the key metabolic pathways to screen the key targets from the potential targets of network pharmacology.</p></section><section id="Sec178"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Immunofluorescence staining</h3>
<p id="Par2643">For immunofluorescence staining, MC3T3-E1 (5 × 10<sup>5</sup>/mL) were seeded on coverslips and culture ed with differentiation medium for 7–14 days, treated with JGSQP or HG for 24 h. <p id="Par27">With the exception of the analysis software used for annotation, all data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). <section id="Sec20"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Determination of the main active components in JGSQP</h3>
<p id="Par28">To control the quality of JGSQP, the active chemical components of JGSQP were first analyzed using UPLC-Q-TOF-MS. Besides, the chemical composition of all compounds in JGSQP samples was identified based on the total ion chromatogram (TIC) in positive and negative modes (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>). The top 30 components of JGSQP were listed in Table <a href="#Tab1" class="usa-link">1</a>. Wolf, Paeonia delavayi, Cinnamomum cassia, and Aconitum carmichaelii Debx, respectively.</p>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="table/Tab1/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div></section></section><section id="Sec21"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">JGSQP treatment mitigated the progression of DOP in mice</h3>
<p id="Par31">It has been revealed in accumulating evidence that JGSQP can reduce the glucose level in patients with T2DM. Therefore, this study was conducted to evaluate the therapeutic potential of JGSQP in alleviating the progression of DOP in a mouse model. The DOP mouse model was established by the HFD-feeding of MKR mice for 8 weeks, and the tissues were harvested at week 12 after the challenge (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>A). As expected, a significant improvement in the body weight was observed in HFD-challenged mice that treated with the administration of low-, medium-, and high-dose JGSQP for 4 weeks compared with model mice (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>B). Of note, the JGSQP treatment significantly decreased the level of fasting blood glucose (FBG) compared with model mice (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>C). Both elevated blood glucose and weight loss were typical symptoms of T2DM and relevant complications. Therefore, this result suggested that JGSQP could reduce the progression of DOP. Next, OGTT, a standard assay for evaluating apparent insulin resistance and glucose tolerability, was detected 2 h before the sacrifice. The OGTT results showed the blood glucose level rapidly increased in each group after the challenge with a bolus of glucose, and the peak value was reached after 30 min (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>D). Encouragingly, compared with the model group, the glucose level significantly decreased to the normal level in both the JGSQP treatment and normal groups between 60 and 120 min (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>D). However, the increased glucose level of mice in the model group also decreased but did not reach the normal level. In addition, the OGTT AUC was significantly higher in DOP mice compared with the normal group, but JGSQP treatment significantly reduced the OGTT AUC in DOP mice compared with the model group (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>E). To further characterize our mouse model and investigate the role of lipid metabolism in bone health, we analyzed serum lipid profiles. Compared to the controls, mice with DOP displayed a pronounced dyslipidemia, characterized by significantly elevated TG and LDL, alongside a marked reduction in HDL. This profile confirms that the model recapitulates key metabolic features of T2DM. Following JGSQP intervention, the model mice exhibited a significantly improved lipid profile, with reductions in TC, TG, and LDL, and an increase in HDL (Fig. S3). Overall, the results indicated that JGSQP could alleviate DM, reducing the DM-induced weight loss, hyperglycemia, and glucose tolerance, while also improving lipid metabolism.</p>

<p id="Par36">Previous animal experiments have confirmed that JGSQP can effectively improve bone mass loss and bone microstructure injury. To explore the role of JGSQP in osteogenesis, an in vitro high-glucose (HG, 25 mM) model of the osteoblast cell line MC3T3-E1 was established and treated with different concentrations of JGSQP, which was added to the osteogenic medium of MC3T3-E1. The CCK-8 assay results revealed that the viability of HG-treated MC3T3-E1 cells was reduced 48 h after treatment (Fig. <a href="#Fig4" class="usa-link">4</a>A), but this change was not observed in the control group, which might result in hyperglycemic cell damage. Remarkably, the treatment with 15% JGSQP-medicated serum (v/v) for 48 h significantly reduced the death of HG-induced cells. Besides, the effects of JGSQP on the relative expression of OPG, RUNX2, and RANKL in MC3T3-E1 cells were analyzed by the western blotting assay (Figs. <a href="#Fig4" class="usa-link">4</a>B,C). In contrast, 48 h after JGSQP-drug serum treatment, the expression of OPG and RUNX2 was up-regulated, and that of RANKL was down-regulated both on days 14 and 21. At the same time, there was no significant difference in the blank-serum group, which corroborated that JGSQP could counteract HG-induced cell damage in MC3T3-E1 cells. Furthermore, ALP staining and Alizarin Red were performed to detect the osteoblast differentiation and mineralization of MC3T3-E1 cells treated with JGSQP under an HG condition (Figs. <a href="#Fig4" class="usa-link">4</a>D,E). The ALP staining results demonstrated that HG treatment decreased the percentage of ALP-positive areas compared with the control group, which was improved in the HG + drug serum (with the 25 mM glucose and JGSQP medicated) group on days 7 and 14. Notably, the number of calcium nodus decreased significantly in MC3T3-E1 cells treated with HG on days 14 and 21, which was restored after JGSQP treatment. In summary, hyperglycemia greatly inhibited the osteogenic capacity of MC3T3-E1 cells, while JGSQP promoted the osteoblast differentiation and mineralization of these cells under HG in vitro.</p>

<p id="Par38">A network pharmacology analysis was performed to clarify the related potential targets and pathways between JGSQP and anti-DOP activity from a comprehensive perspective. Compared with the HG group, the level of IL-1β and IL-6 decreased significantly in the JGSQP group (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.01) (Figs. <a href="#Fig8" class="usa-link">8</a>C–E). The results indicated that JGSQP can down-regulate the expression of AGEs, NF-κB, and inflammatory factors in HG-damaged MC3T3-E1 cells.</p>

<p id="Par52">In recent decades, DOP has emerged as a critically significant skeletal-muscular complication of T2DM, imposing a substantial burden on global public health<sup><a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a></sup>.The pathological mechanisms and treatment strategies for DOP require further exploration. The unique diabetic microenvironment associated with T2DM leads to abnormally elevated levels of extracellular metabolites, which disrupt the balance of bone metabolism. This disruption results in the deterioration of trabecular bone structures and increases the incidence of fractures in DOP patients<sup><a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a>,<a href="#CR32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR32">32</a></sup>. Although several medications are available that inhibit osteoclastic activity and promote bone formation, they do not effectively address the progression of DOP within the diabetic context and are often associated with side effects such as hypercalcemia and gastrointestinal disturbances. As such, there is a pressing need for the continuous development of new therapeutic agents.</p>
<p id="Par53">JGSQP is a traditional Chinese medicine formula that has been used for centuries. It is well-regarded for its effects in regulating angiogenesis, repairing tissues, and inhibiting apoptosis, thereby helping to prevent serious chronic diseases<sup><a href="#CR33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR33">33</a></sup>. In clinical studies, JGSQP was used for treating T2DM and osteoporosis with high efficacy and safety<sup><a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a></sup>.Qi Shang’s study revealed that JGSQP could treat osteoporosis by regulating bone lipid homeostasis<sup><a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a></sup>.In the present study, the protective effects and regulatory mechanisms of JGSQP were investigated using both in vivo and in vitro models of DOP. Given the complexity of TCM formulas, which contain multiple compounds and targets, this study utilized various techniques to elucidate the mechanisms underlying JGSQP’s treatment of DOP<sup><a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>,<a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a></sup>. while the investigation encompassed drug composition, target identification, pathways of action, and effects on metabolites, employing a novel approach that combines network pharmacology with metabolomics for the first time.</p>
<p id="Par54">In order to better explore the potential mechanism of JGSQP in DOP, a DOP mouse model was established using high-fat diet-induced MKR mice. MKR mice are a transgenic model of non-obese type 2 diabetes, characterized by a skeletal muscle-specific insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor deficiency and a mixed insulin growth factor 1 and insulin receptor mutant profile. The experimental mice exhibited hyperglycemia, an imbalance of glucolipid metabolism, peripheral tissue insulin resistance, and a chronic inflammatory state. After 8 weeks of high-fat diet feeding, the mice in the DOP model group exhibited typical T2DM symptoms, such as weight loss, fasting glucose level &gt; 11.1 mmol/L, and significantly abnormal OGTT, compared with that in the normal group. Certain serum biomarkers are used to diagnose and assess the balance of bone metabolism in the process of DOP. These include markers for bone turnover such as ALP and PINP for bone formation, and β-CTX for bone resorption<sup><a href="#CR37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR37">37</a></sup>. ALP is a specific enzyme produced by osteoblasts, and its upregulation indicates an accelerated osteogenic process. Meanwhile, PINP expression levels are up-regulated by its agonist to promote osteoporosis-induced bone healing. Moreover, a positive correlation exists between drug concentration and PINP concentration<sup><a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a></sup>. β-CTX is representative of osteoclast activity that affects the degradation of bone matrix and can be caused by diabetes or different treatment durations of diabetic drugs. DOP can be treated by decreasing the expression of β-CTX<sup><a href="#CR38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR38">38</a></sup>. In the present study, the serum level of β-CTX was elevated, while the expression of ALP and PINP was reduced in the model group. Such findings indicate accelerated bone turnover and a sustained state of bone metabolism disorder in mice with DOP. In contrast, treatment with high, medium, and low concentrations of JGSQP led to a reduction in serum β-CTX levels, while ALP and PINP levels increased to varying degrees. These changes indicate that JGSQP reduced bone turnover in mice with DOP and exerted a therapeutic effect by inhibiting the pathological progression of DOP. Consequently, the symptoms associated with DOP were alleviated and restored following treatment with JGSQP. Moreover, JGSQP improved the microstructural changes of the femur in this model. The results of H&amp;E staining and micro-CT clearly indicate that JGSQP regulated the changes in the trabecular index of the femur, such as reducing Tb.Sp, preventing and controlling the reduction of TV, increasing bone density, and restoring the femur microstructure to improve the performance of osteoporosis in mice with DOP. Consistent with these findings, the analysis of H&amp;E staining and micro-CT imaging reveals not only a significant decrease in bone mass but also severe damage to bone microarchitecture in the model group. These observations confirm that the model accurately reflects the pathogenesis of DOP. Hence, high-fat-diet-fed MKR mice are validated as an ideal model for studying T2DM-related osteoporosis. The results demonstrate that JGSQP provided protection against bone trabecular injury in mice with DOP by mitigating bone metabolism disorders. Additionally, untargeted metabolomics analysis was employed to compare changes in metabolites in mice following JGSQP treatment.</p>
<p id="Par55">The hyperglycemia microenvironment induced by type 2 diabetes aggravates the damage of osteoblasts, making bone tissue more susceptible to the accumulation of microdamage, which leads to increased bone fragility and diabetic osteoporosis<sup><a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a></sup>. In the present study, 25 mmol of glucose was used to simulate the diabetic environment of bone cells in vitro. MC3T3-E1 cells, derived from a mouse skull osteoblast precursor cell line, are well-suited for in vitro studies on osteoblast differentiation<sup><a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a>,<a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a></sup>. Alizarin red staining is used to detect calcium deposits in mineralized nodule matrices, serving as a method to assess bone mineralization in these experiments<sup><a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a>,<a href="#CR40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR40">40</a></sup>. As an indicator of osteoblast differentiation, calcified nodules can be directly used to identify the role of osteoblast differentiation. At the same time, high ALP activity is an early marker of osteoblast differentiation and maturation<sup><a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a></sup>.Increasing ALP activity promotes bone formation and enhances the mineralization of the bone matrix<sup><a href="#CR41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR41">41</a></sup>. In contrast, during osteoblast differentiation, calcium salt deposits on the cell surface to form calcium nodules<sup><a href="#CR42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR42">42</a></sup>. Compared with the model group, the number of mineralized nodules in the JGSQP group increased. Such findings emphasize that JGSQP significantly increased the mineralization ability and ALP activity of MC3T3-E1 cells under high glucose inhibition. To further elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the protective effects of JGSQP against bone loss in DOP, the expression levels of several proteins associated with bone metabolism were analyzed using Western blotting. RUNX2 and OPG are recognized as key positive regulators of osteogenic differentiation and bone formation. RUNX2 serves as a primary transcription factor for osteogenesis<sup><a href="#CR43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR43">43</a>,<a href="#CR44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR44">44</a></sup>. Previous studies have demonstrated that high glucose levels inhibit osteogenic differentiation by disrupting the BMP signaling pathway, which involves RUNX2, thus impairing osteoblast differentiation<sup><a href="#CR45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR45">45</a></sup>. OPG, on the other hand, is a cytokine produced by osteoblasts that plays a critical role in bone metabolism by inhibiting osteoclast differentiation and activity, thereby promoting the maintenance of bone mass. RANKL is a critical factor regulating osteoclast differentiation, promoting osteoclast formation while inhibiting their proliferation<sup><a href="#CR46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR46">46</a></sup>. Shanshan Qi et al. have demonstrated that black rice can reduce RANKL levels in a dose-dependent manner, thereby preventing DOP<sup><a href="#CR47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR47">47</a></sup>. In the present study, compared to the model group, the expression of RUNX2 and OPG was significantly upregulated, while RANKL expression was downregulated in the JGSQP-treated group. Further, JGSQP was shown to mitigate the increase in RANKL levels induced by high glucose in MC3T3-E1 cells. Such results suggest that JGSQP enhances the viability, differentiation, and mineralization of MC3T3-E1 cells under high glucose conditions, contributing to improved osteoporosis and providing protective effects on bone health in vivo.</p>
<p id="Par56">In order to further explore the anti-DOP mechanism of JGSQP, UPLC-Q-Exactive-Orbitrap-MS analysis combined with network pharmacology was utilized. This approach highlighted the multi-component nature of JGSQP and demonstrated its efficacy through the multifunctional roles of its primary active components, which regulate multiple targets and pathways. The PPI analysis identified key targets of JGSQP in the treatment of DOP, including TP53, AKT1, JUN, TNF, IL-1β, and NFKBIA. KEGG pathway analysis revealed several beneficial targets and signaling pathways, such as lipid and atherosclerosis, AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications, TNF signaling pathway, endocrine resistance, IL-17 signaling pathway, osteoclast differentiation, apoptosis, Th17 cell differentiation, NF-κB signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway. These findings suggest that the therapeutic effects of JGSQP in alleviating DOP are closely associated with its regulation of inflammation, immune response, cell signal transduction, and metabolic processes.</p>
<p id="Par57">Previous studies have shown that the homeostasis of glucose and lipid metabolism is essential for diabetes and its complications, and the occurrence of DOP is closely related to systemic diseases of glucose and lipid metabolism. At the same time, numerous studies have demonstrated that metabolomics is an effective approach for understanding the pathological changes associated with TCM symptoms and elucidating the mechanisms of TCM therapies<sup><a href="#CR48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR48">48</a>,<a href="#CR49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR49">49</a></sup>. Therefore, non-targeted metabolomics analysis was conducted on mouse plasma samples from the normal, model and JGSQP-treated groups to uncover the changes in DOP-related metabolites following JGSQP treatment and to further elucidate the potential molecular mechanisms underlying DOP pathology. The results of enrichment analysis of differential metabolites showed that 32.7% of the metabolites changed were lipids and lipid molecules, 20.9% were organic acids and their derivatives, and 7.3% of the significantly different metabolites were nucleosides, nucleotides and analogues. Lipids and lipid molecules are crucial for various physiological functions, including membrane protein binding, signal transduction, energy storage, and regulation<sup><a href="#CR50" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR50">50</a>–<a href="#CR52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR52">52</a></sup>. Disturbances in lipid metabolism can lead to the production of abnormal lipid metabolites and induce lipid toxicity. Additionally, metabolic disorders involving organic acids and their derivatives can impact the digestion and absorption of nutrients by cells. Nucleosides and analogues can act as inhibitors of glycosyltransferases, phosphoglycosyltransferases and sugar-modifying enzymes<sup><a href="#CR50" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR50">50</a></sup> Disorders in the expression of these metabolites have been consistently identified as risk factors for T2DM and its complications<sup><a href="#CR53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR53">53</a></sup>. Disorders in the expression of these metabolites have been consistently identified as risk factors for T2DM and its complications. Notably, the comparison between the model group and the JGSQP group revealed that, in mice with DOP, the levels of certain lipid molecules (for example, 2r-3-hydroxyisovalerylcarnitine), organic acids and their derivatives (for example, DL-cysteine, 2-oxo-5-(3,3-dimethylguanidin-1-yl) valeric acid, and L-pyrazinic acid), and nucleosides, nucleotides, and analogues (for example, S-methyl-5’-thioadenosine) were significantly downregulated after 4 weeks of JGSQP treatment. These findings align with previous studies on the development of T2DM and suggest that these metabolites play a critical role in DOP by affecting glucose and lipid metabolism<sup><a href="#CR54" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR54">54</a></sup>. JGSQP appears to regulate these metabolic pathways, mitigating pathological damage to trabecular bone by addressing glucose and lipid metabolism disorders, and reducing lipid toxicity, thereby alleviating DOP.</p>
<p id="Par58">The complex, multi-component nature of natural products presents a significant challenge in elucidating their precise mechanisms of action. Our integrative strategy—employing a cycle of network pharmacology prediction followed by experimental validation—is specifically designed to address this complexity. As demonstrated in the cited literature<sup><a href="#CR55" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR55">55</a></sup>, this paradigm allows for the systematic generation of holistic hypotheses regarding multi-target mechanisms, which are then rigorously tested. In our work, this involved using target prediction to identify key pathways (e.g., AGE-RAGE and NF-κB), which we subsequently confirmed through western blot analysis and immunofluorescence staining. This iterative process bridges the gap between in silico prediction and biological confirmation, thereby enhancing the credibility and comprehensiveness of our findings on natural product polypharmacology.</p>
<p id="Par59">To further elucidate the mechanism of JGSQP in treating DOP, an analysis was conducted to explore the interactions between differential genes identified through network pharmacology and differential metabolites. A network was constructed encompassing compound-reaction-enzyme-gene relationships by enriching key metabolic pathways such as amino sugar metabolism, which helped reveal their interconnections. Moreover, it was observed that JGSQP significantly downregulated the expression levels of IL-6 and IL-1β in vitro, effectively reducing pro-inflammatory factors. These cytokines play a crucial role in the AGE pathway associated with DOP. Collectively, the results demonstrate that JGSQP likely mitigates the risk of DOP through the modulation of the NF-κB signaling pathway and related cytokine levels. This consistency with network pharmacology predictions offers robust and coherent support for the proposed mechanism of action of JGSQP.</p>
<p id="Par60">Although the integration of network pharmacology with experimental validation provided a robust framework for this study, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the predictive accuracy is contingent upon the completeness of the underlying databases, which may introduce bias. Second, the experimental validation was intentionally focused on key network hubs and thus may not reflect the full complexity of the predicted interactions. To mitigate these limitations, we applied stringent criteria for target prediction and prioritized central hypotheses for experimental confirmation. The strong concordance between our computational and experimental findings substantiates the robustness of our conclusions, while also highlighting the need for more comprehensive investigations in the future.</p>
<section id="Sec27"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Study limitations and future perspectives</h3>
<p id="Par61">We acknowledge several limitations of the present study, which also delineate clear avenues for future research.</p>
<ol class="list" style="list-style-type:decimal">
<li><p id="Par63">Model Validation Scope: The study combines an in vitro cellular model with computational multi-omics. While the high-glucose (25 mmol/L) treatment is a well-established, non-cytotoxic stressor in the literature, the absence of our own viability data under the precise experimental conditions remains a methodological constraint. Future work will include rigorous viability and dose-response assessments to strengthen the model foundation.</p></li>
<li><p id="Par64">Mechanistic Causality: The integrative omics predictions, while insightful, require targeted experimental validation (e.g., via genetic or pharmacological modulation of key targets like TP53/AKT1) to establish definitive causal relationships. This constitutes a core focus for our immediate follow-up studies.</p></li>
<li><p id="Par65">Analytical Methodology: The field of multi-omics integration is advancing rapidly. Our current analytical pipeline, while effective, can be enhanced by employing more sophisticated algorithms (e.g., network propagation, multi-omics factor analysis) to improve quantitative prioritization and biological discovery in future analyses.</p></li>
<li><p id="Par66">Translational Generalizability: Findings from a specific cellular model require validation in more physiologically relevant systems. Subsequent research will extend to primary cells, animal models, and ultimately clinical correlative studies to confirm the pathophysiological and therapeutic relevance of the identified mechanisms.</p></li>
</ol>
<p id="Par67">These limitations, often inherent to exploratory integrative studies, are recognized. They do not undermine the validity of our core findings but provide a structured roadmap for deepening the mechanistic understanding and translational potential of this research.</p></section></section><section id="Sec28"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusions</h2>
<p id="Par68">In conclusion, the results of the present study confirm that JGSQP is an effective prescription for improving DOP in vitro and in vivo, and its potential mechanism was explored. UPLC-Q-Exactive-Orbitrap technology, network pharmacology, non-target metabolomics and molecular biology methods were employed to verify that JGSQP alleviates damage to bone trabeculae caused by various metabolic disorders in both the DOP mouse model and high glucose-induced model. The findings suggest that the mechanism of JGSQP’s action may be indirectly related to its impact on bone metabolism. In general, a new mechanism was identified for JGSQP in reducing DOP-induced inflammatory responses, highlighting its therapeutic potential. The study also demonstrates that JGSQP’s therapeutic effect on DOP is achieved through multi-target and multi-pathway synergy, offering new insights for its clinical application in the treatment of DOP.</p></section><section id="Sec29"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Supplementary Information</h2>
