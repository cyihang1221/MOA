</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="ab0005"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Foxtail millet has been cultivated in Gansu Province for centuries, with several dominant cultivars; however, the metabolic differences among these varieties remain unclear. Therefore, the volatile and non-volatile metabolites of five different millets cultivars were analyzed using GC–MS and LC–MS techniques. A total of 64 volatile compounds were detected by GC–MS, among which aldehydes were the main contributors to foxtail millet flavor. A total of 1512 metabolites were detected by LC–MS. Comparison of HMDB (Human Metabolome Database) showed that the proportion of lipids and lipid-like molecules among the metabolites of JG21, ZZ13, and JF107 was the highest, while those of HM and JG1 predominantly consisted of organic acids and their derivatives. Additionally, 16 differential metabolites of polyphenols were screened and the biosynthetic pathways of flavonoids such as apigenin, luteolin, and phloretin were identified through KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) annotation. This study can provide a reference for the processing and quality control of downstream products of foxtail millet.</p>
<section id="ks0005" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> <em>Setaria italica</em>, Flavor substances, Polyphenol compounds</p></section></section><section class="abstract" id="ab0010"><h2>Highlights</h2>
<ul id="l0005" class="list" style="list-style-type:none">
<li id="li0005">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0005">For the first time, volatile and non-volatile metabolites were characterized in Qingyang millet.</p>
</li>
<li id="li0010">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0010">Aldehydes identified by GC–MS are the main contributors to millet flavor.</p>
</li>
<li id="li0015">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0015">1512 metabolites detected by LC–MS vary across five millet varieties.</p>
</li>
<li id="li0020">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0020">30 flavor-related compounds include in differential metabolites.</p>
</li>
<li id="li0025">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0025">16 differential polyphenolic metabolites are screened.</p>
</li>
</ul></section><section id="s0005"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">1. Introduction</h2>
<p id="p0035">Foxtail millet (<em>Setaria italica</em> (L.) Beauv) is one of the oldest crops in the world, closely related to the development of agricultural civilization in Asia. It originated in northern China and has a long history of cultivation, mostly grown in arid and semi-arid regions (<a href="#bb0115" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0115">Liang &amp; Han, 2024</a>; <a href="#bb0180" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0180">Sharma &amp; Niranjan, 2018</a>). Compared with other cereals, millet is high in nutrients such as protein, vitamins, essential amino acids, and mineral elements, which have a variety of functional properties such as lowering blood lipids and treating colon cancer (<a href="#bb0180" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0180">Sharma &amp; Niranjan, 2018</a>). It can be used as a therapeutic food for diabetics (<a href="#bb0140" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0140">Parameswaran &amp; Sadasivam, 1994</a>), and it can be used as a raw material for producing other foods, such as millet biscuits, millet pots, millet shaqima (a class of caramel snacks), millet flour steamed buns, millet nutritional powder, millet yogurt, millet beer, millet vinegar, and millet sausages (<a href="#bb0030" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0030">Bi et al., 2021</a>; <a href="#bb0050" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0050">Fan et al., 2022</a>; <a href="#bb0240" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0240">Yang &amp; Gao, 2022</a>). Therefore, the quality research of millet is particularly important.</p>
<p id="p0040">The flavor of foxtail millet, an important raw material for a variety of foods, may affect the quality of downstream products and thus affect their acceptance by consumers (<a href="#bb0085" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0085">Jin, Cai, Zhao, Gao, &amp; Jiang, 2023</a>). The flavor of millet is mainly affected by volatile and non-volatile metabolites (<a href="#bb0265" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0265">Zhang et al., 2018</a>). In recent years, research on millet flavor has mainly focused on volatile substances, while there are few studies on non-volatile flavor-presenting substances. The main volatile compounds of different varieties of Longjiang millet in Qiqihar area are aldehydes, alcohols and ketones, their content has certain differences (<a href="#bb0085" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0085">Jin et al., 2023</a>). Some scholars have identified the volatile compounds of the elite millet variety Jingu 21 and other control varieties (Jingu 36, Daqinggu, and Zhishenggu), and their results show that Jingu 21 is the most abundant in volatile substances (<a href="#bb0265" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0265">Zhang et al., 2018</a>). Additionally, a study on non-volatile metabolites can further analyze the quality of different varieties of millet. Some studies have suggested that the difference in polyphenols in the metabolites of four different colors of millets such as Yidianhong leads to differences in the antioxidant capacity of different millets (<a href="#bb0110" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0110">Li et al., 2021</a>). Simultaneously, in a study of metabolites of grains such as millet and sorghum, some scholars have found that their main metabolites are phenolic acids, flavonoids, fatty acyls, and organic acids, revealing the potential of super-grains as a source of polyphenols (<a href="#bb0160" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0160">Ribeiro da Silva Lima, Barros Santos, Gomes, Fernández-Ochoa, &amp; Simões Larraz Ferreira, 2024</a>).</p>
<p id="p0045">Although the metabolomic profile of millet has been previously reported, the characterization of cultivars from the Qingyang area in Gansu province remains unclear. Therefore, we determined the differences in metabolites of different main varieties of millet to comprehensively compare the quality of the five millet varieties. Simultaneously, through the comparison of metabolites, the differences in the overall flavor and polyphenols of the five millets were analyzed to provide a theoretical basis for the processing and production, quality control, and mining of functional components of downstream millet products in Qingyang, Gansu Province.</p></section><section id="s0010"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. Materialand methods</h2>
<section id="s0015"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1. Materials</h3>
<p id="p0050">Five kinds of foxtail millet were collected from Xifeng District, Qingyang, Gansu Province: Huangmaogu (HM), Jingu 1 (JG1), Jingu 21 (JG21), Zhangza 13 (ZZ13), and Jinfen 107 (JF107). We removed incomplete grains, including unripe, insect-infested, and moldy grains, from the sample. The samples were then washed to eliminate impurities and dried in an oven at 50 ± 2 °C. We then stored them at 4 °C and used them for the determination of subsequent indicators.</p></section><section id="s0020"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2. Determination of physical–chemical indicators</h3>
<section id="s0025"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.1. Color</h4>
<p id="p0055">We used a colorimeter (CR-400, Konica Minolta, Japan) to determine the color of the different millets. Specify the color parameters measured using the CIE system: L* (lightness), a* (red-green scale, where +a* indicates redness and -a* indicates greenness), and b* (yellow-blue scale, where +b* indicates yellowness and -b* indicates blueness).</p></section><section id="s0030"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.2. Protein, fat, starch, and amylose analysis</h4>
<p id="p0060">The millet samples were ground and sieved through an 80-mesh, and then the protein, fat, starch, and amylose contents were determined following the Chinese National Standard. Specifically, determination of protein content was done through the Kjeldahl method (GB 5009.5-2016), the weighing amount for the determination is 2.0 g. The coefficient used to convert nitrogen compounds to proteins is 5.70. Fat content was determined by Soxhlet extraction method (GB 5009.6-2016), the weighing amount of millet for the determination is 3.0 g. Starch content was determined by acid hydrolysis method (GB 5009.9-2016), the weighing amount for the determination of starch content in millet is 2.0 g. Amylose content was determined spectrophotometrically (GB/T 15683-2008), the weighing amount for the determination of amylose content in millet is 0.2 g. These standards can be found online at Foodmate (<a href="http://down.foodmate.net/standard/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://down.foodmate.net/standard/</a>). All data were obtained by three sets of parallel trials, and the results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation.</p></section></section><section id="s0035"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.3. Volatile-metabolite analysis</h3>
<p id="p0065">Volatile metabolites were extracted by headspace solid-phase microextraction (<a href="#bib291" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib291">Zhao, 2022</a>). Referring to previous studies (<a href="#bb0145" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0145">Pexová Kalinová, Tříska, &amp; Hořejší, 2023</a>), we analyzed the volatile substances using a GC–MS system (5977C GC/MSD, Agilent, USA).</p>
<p id="p0070">The system conditions were as follows: helium as the carrier gas, a constant flow rate of 0.7 mL/min, and a no-shunt mode. The column box was warmed up to 45 °C for 1.5 min, 6 °C/min to 85 °C without hold, and then 4 °C/min to 225 °C for 15 min. The inlet temperature was 230 °C. The filament emission current was 200 μA, the ion source temperature was 200 °C, and the interface temperature was 250 °C. The electron energy was 70 eV and the ionization mode was electron ionization.</p>
<p id="p0075">The detection results were retrieved using the NIST library. Substances with a similarity greater than 80 % were selected, and the various substances were qualitatively compared with the MS spectrum. The relative percentage content (%) of each volatile substance was calculated by chromatographic peak area normalization method. The peak area of each individual peak in the chromatogram is measured using chromatographic software, and the total peak area is obtained by summing the peak areas of all components. The relative content of each component is calculated by dividing its individual peak area by the total peak area. The formula is as follows:</p>

Thresholds can be queried in the compilation of compound aroma thresholds (<a href="#bb0195" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0195">Van, 2015</a>).</p></section><section id="s0040"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4. Non-volatile-metabolite analysis</h3>
<p id="p0085">Transfer 100 mg of ground millet into a 1.5 mL EP tube, followed by addition of 500 μL ice-cold 8 % (v/v) methanol aqueous solution. Vortex-mix thoroughly for 1 min and incubate on ice for 5 min. Centrifuge the mixture at 15,000 ×<em>g</em> for 20 min at 4 °C to ensure complete particulate removal. Collect the supernatant, and inject it into LC–MS for analysis. (<a href="#bb0205" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0205">Want et al., 2013</a>). Each of the five test samples of the same volume was mixed as a quality control (QC) sample. A 53 % methanol solution in water was used as a blank sample.</p>
<p id="p0090">The samples were analyzed on an LC–MS/MS system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) (LC: Vanquish UHPL. MS: QExactive™ HF/Q Exactive™ HF). The metabolites were separated by an Hypersil gold column (C18, Thermo Fisher Scientific, America).The column temperature was 40 °C, and the flow rate was 0.2 mL /min; mobile phase A, formic acid; mobile phase B, methanol. Gradient elution program settings for mobile-phase volumes: 0–1.5 min, 98 % A, 2 % B; 3 min, 15 % A, 85 % B; 10 min, 0 % A, 100 % B; 10.1–12 min, 98 % A, 2 % B. The water used in the experiments was LC–MS grade from MERCK. The mass spectrometry scanning range was selected for <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> 100–1500, and the electrospray ionization source was set as follows: 3.5 kV spray voltage, 35 psi sheath velocity, 10 L/min auxiliary air velocity, 320 °C ion-transfer-tube temperature, iontophoresis RF level at 60, 350 °C auxiliary gas heater temperature, positive and negative polarities, and data-dependent MS/MS secondary scans. The non-volatile metabolites of the different millets were qualitatively and quantitatively analyzed by mzCloud (<a href="https://www.mzcloud.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.mzcloud.org/</a>), mzVault, and Mass list databases. The metabolites identified in the five millets were compared with HMDB. The Pearson correlation coefficient between QC samples was calculated based on the relative quantification of metabolites (<a href="#bb0155" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0155">Rao, Sui, &amp; Zhang, 2016</a>). The top 20 metabolic pathways with differential metabolites screened in the different millets were mapped and functionally annotated using the KEGG database.</p></section><section id="s0045"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.5. Statistical analysis</h3>
<p id="p0095">One-way ANOVA was performed on the data using SPSS 27.0. All data were obtained by three sets of parallel trials, and the results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using Origin 2021. Orthogonal partial-least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), cluster analysis, and comparison of metabolites between multiple groups were carried out using the Wekemo platform (<a href="https://www.bioincloud.tech/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.bioincloud.tech/</a>). Significant differences in non-volatile metabolites were analyzed based on predicted variable importance (VIP &gt; 1.0) and statistical significance (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05 and fold change ∣FC∣ ≥1.0).</p></section></section><section id="s0050"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">3. Results and discussion</h2>
<section id="s0055"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.1. Physicochemical analysis</h3>
<p id="p0100">Beige color is an important trait of the millet's appearance quality, and yellow is the color of majority in the market. The chromaticity of the five millets is shown in <a href="#t0005" class="usa-link">Table 1</a>. There was no significant difference in the <em>L</em>* value and <em>a</em>* value between the five varieties of millet (<em>P</em> &gt; 0.05). The b* value, representing yellowness, varies significantly between 33.99 and 59.82. Among the samples, JG1 exhibits the highest b* value (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05). However, the yellowness of the five millet cultivars analyzed is lower than that of the nine Jingu varieties previously reported by <a href="#bb0235" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0235">Yang et al. (2020)</a>. Studies have shown that the yellowness value of millets is related to the content of carotenoids (<a href="#bb0275" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0275">Zhang et al., 2021</a>).</p>

<div class="tw-foot p"><div class="fn" id="fn1"><p>Note: The data are presented as mean ± standard deviation from three independent biological replicates. Lowercase letters indicate significant differences among the horizontal groups(<em>P</em>&lt;0.05); HM: Huangmaogu, JG1: Jingu 1, JG21: Jingu 21, ZZ13: Zhangza 13, and JF107: Jinfen 107.</p></div></div></section><p id="p0105">Protein is the material basis of all life activities, and it plays an important role in human physiology and metabolism (<a href="#bb0200" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0200">Wang &amp; Sun, 2023</a>). The results indicate that the protein content of the millet varieties ranged from 9.90 g/100 g to 12.30 g/100 g (<a href="#t0005" class="usa-link">Table 1</a>). Among them, ZZ13 had the highest protein content, significantly exceeding that of the other varieties (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05), while JG1 had the lowest. This is consistent with the protein content of Foxtail, Proso, and Finger millets in the earlier study (<a href="#bb0015" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0015">Amadou, Gounga, &amp; Le, 2013</a>).</p>
<p id="p0110">The fat content of the different millets varied between 2.60 g/100 g and 2.93 g/100 g, but there was no significant difference in the fat content of each variety (<em>P</em> &gt; 0.05). The fat content of millet was generally lower than that of the Jigu and Henggu varieties grown in Hebei (<a href="#bb0105" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0105">Li et al., 2021</a>), possibly due to differences in the planting environment.</p>
<p id="p0115">Starch is one of the most important components of millet (<a href="#bb0290" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0290">Zhu, 2014</a>). The starch content of the different millets ranged from 68.00 g/100 g to 73.43 g/100 g, and the starch content of JG1 was significantly higher than that of ZZ13 (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05). The starch content in this study was much higher than that of Sava, Kutki, and other varieties (<a href="#bb0150" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0150">Rana et al., 2023</a>). The genetic differences between different varieties directly affect the activity of enzymes related to starch synthesis, resulting in different rates and total amounts of starch accumulation (<a href="#bb0045" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0045">Dhaka, Muthamilarasan, &amp; Prasad, 2021</a>).</p>
<p id="p0120">Amylose content is related to the waxiness of millets (<a href="#bb0025" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0025">Bangar et al., 2021</a>). The results showed that the amylose content of the different millet varieties was between 12.23 g/100 g and 17.03 g/100 g (<a href="#t0005" class="usa-link">Table 1</a>). HM and JG1 had the highest amylose content, 16.40 g/100 g and 17.03 g/100 g, respectively, and the lowest amylose contents were for ZZ13 and JF107, which were 12.23 g/100 g and 12.50 g/100 g, respectively. Amylose content in millet is one of the important factors affecting the quality of millet-based foods (<a href="#bb0170" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0170">Sandhyarani &amp; Bhattacharya, 1989</a>). For example, pasta with high amylose content exhibited high water absorption capacity and required longer cooking time, while those with lower amylose content showed lower and chewiness and higher adhesiveness (<a href="#bb0035" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0035">Cordelino, Tyl, Inamdar, Vickers, &amp; Marti, 2019</a>).</p></section><section id="s0060"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.2. Volatile-metabolite analysis</h3>
<p id="p0125">We detected 64 volatile compounds in the five millets by GC–MS (Table S1 &amp; <a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Fig. 1</a>A), including 12 aldehydes, 13 alcohols, 6 esters, 10 ketones, 6 acids, 8 hydrocarbons, 4 aromatics, and 5 furans, which were consistent with the types of volatile compounds in Jigu 42 and Henggu 13 (<a href="#bb0105" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0105">Li, Zhao, et al., 2021</a>). Additionally, the proportion of aldehydes in the different varieties of millet was the highest, ranging from 22.56 % to 42.90 %. The proportions of acids and esters in HM were 21.47 % and 22.22 %, respectively, which were higher than those of other varieties. The proportion of alcohols in JG21 was 20.68 %, which was also higher than that of other varieties. The greatest PCA separation occurs along the PC1 axis (31.5 %), which distinguishes a large group consisting of JG1, JG21, and JF107, characterized by a volatile metabolite composition that contrasts with HM. Positioned at the center, ZZ13 aligns with point 0 on PC1, indicating its intermediate similarity to both groups(<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Fig. 1</a>B).In order to further analyze the differences in volatile compounds between samples, ROAV was used to evaluate the contribution of volatile compounds to the flavor of millet. It was generally believed that the larger is the ROAV, the greater is the contribution to the flavor of the samples, and that substances with ROAV greater than 0.1 have an important contribution to the samples. The 18 volatile compounds with ROAV &gt;0.1 were screened as shown in Table S2 and <a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Fig. 1</a>C. 1-Octen-3-ol is associated with mushroom odor, 1-octanol with fruity odor, and 1-penten-3-ol with green notes (<a href="#bb0020" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0020">Amanpour, Zannou, Kelebek, &amp; Selli, 2019</a>; <a href="#bb0210" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0210">Wei et al., 2022</a>). (<em>E</em>)-2-Octenal, (<em>E</em>)-2-nonenal, (<em>E</em>,<em>E</em>)-2,4-nonadienal, (<em>E</em>,<em>E</em>)-2,4-decadienal, and hexanal have fat and meat odors, while nonanal and decanal have floral and citrus aromas (<a href="#bb0040" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0040">Cui et al., 2021</a>; <a href="#bb0090" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0090">Kim, Sowndhararajan, Choi, Park, &amp; Kim, 2019</a>). 2-Pentyl-furan has a fruity flavor; 2-<em>n</em>-butyl furan has the sweet and spicy flavor of fruit wine; while 3-octen-2-one and 1-penten-3-one have earthy, mushroom, and spicy flavors (<a href="#bb0120" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0120">Lin, 2007</a>).</p>

As a raw material in food processing, the flavor compounds of foxtail millet can significantly affect the flavor quality of final products. For instance, <a href="#bb0185" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0185">Shi et al. (2024)</a> found that rice wines produced from different varieties of foxtail millet exhibited distinct flavor profiles.</p></section><section id="s0065"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3. Non-volatile-metabolite analysis</h3>
<section id="s0070"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.1. Stability survey</h4>
<p id="p0135">As shown in <a href="#t0010" class="usa-link">Table 2</a>, for the Positive and Negative Ion Mode (POS: Positive. NEG: Negative), |r| between QC samples detected was close to 1, indicating that the correlation between samples was good, the whole detection process was stable, and the data quality was high.</p>

<div class="tw-foot p"><div class="fn" id="fn2"><p>Note: QC is quality control.</p></div></div></section></section><section id="s0075"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.2. Differential analysis of non-volatile metabolites</h4>
<p id="p0140">711 metabolites were identified in the five millets and perform superclasses (<a href="#f0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a>A). The results showed that the contents of lipids and lipid-like molecules in the different millets ranged from 23.79 % to 40.12 %. The content of organic acids and their derivatives was between 17.61 % and 27.12 %, and the benzene content was between 16.68 % and 25.75 %. Organic acids and their derivatives were the most abundant in HM and JG1, while JG21, ZZ13, and JF107 consisted mainly of lipids and lipid-like molecules. Additionally, the proportion of metabolites such as organic oxygen compounds, alkaloids, and their derivatives in the pie chart is relatively low. The main metabolite species of the five millets were similar to those of Yidianhong, Niandiaoya, Dahuilang, and Qishafeng millets (<a href="#bb0110" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0110">Li, Wen, et al., 2021</a>). PCA reduces the dimensionality of metabolite variables by applying a weighted linear combination to generate new characteristic variables. These principal components enable classification based on similarity, effectively capturing overall metabolic differences between groups while also reflecting variability within each group. HM is similar to JG21 and ZZ13 along the PC2 axis, while it shares similarity with JF107 along the PC1 axis (<a href="#f0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a>B).</p>
<figure class="fig xbox font-sm" id="f0010" title="Fig. 2"><h5 class="obj_head">Fig. 2.</h5>
<p class="img-box line-height-none margin-x-neg-2 tablet:margin-x-0 text-center"><a class="tileshop" target="_blank" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/core/lw/2.0/html/tileshop_pmc/tileshop_pmc_inline.html?title=Click%20on%20image%20to%20zoom&amp;p=PMC3&amp;id=12051117_gr2.jpg"><img class="graphic zoom-in" src="https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/304d/12051117/6d612c1621eb/gr2.jpg" loading="lazy" id="lk0010" height="575" width="789" alt="Fig. 2"></a></p>
<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="figure/f0010/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div>
<figcaption><p>Analysis of non-volatile metabolites of five millets. (A) Classification of non-volatile metabolites and the proportion of various substances compared with HMDB. (B) Principal Components Analysis (PCA) score plot. (C) Volcano plot for differential metabolites analysis among multi-groups. (D) Hierarchical clustering heatmap of the top 30 differentially abundant metabolites. (OPLS-DA used to screen differential metabolites is shown in Fig. S2).</p></figcaption></figure><p id="p0145">The metabolites were grouped and compared with FC (fold change) &gt; 2, with <em>P</em> &lt; 0.05 as the threshold. The scatter plots of multiple groups of differential metabolites were plotted using log 2 (FC) values (<a href="#f0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a>C). A total of 286 differential metabolites were found between HM and JG1, of which 72 were significantly up-regulated and 214 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 372 differential metabolites were found between HM and JG21, of which 141 were significantly up-regulated and 231 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 466 differential metabolites were found between HM and ZZ13, of which 227 were significantly up-regulated and 239 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 410 differential metabolites were found between HM and JF107, of which 176 were significantly up-regulated and 234 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 257 differential metabolites were found between JG1 and JG21, of which 155 were significantly up-regulated and 102 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 450 differential metabolites were found between JG1 and ZZ13, of which 269 were significantly up-regulated and 181 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 218 differential metabolites were found between JG1 and JF107, of which 136 were significantly up-regulated and 82 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 194 differential metabolites were found between JG21 and ZZ13, of which 125 were significantly up-regulated and 69 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 294 differential metabolites were found between JG21 and JF107, of which 157 were significantly up-regulated and 137 were significantly down-regulated. A total of 372 differential metabolites were found between ZZ13 and JF107, of which 154 were significantly up-regulated and 218 were significantly down-regulated. In summary, the metabolite differences between HM and ZZ13 were the largest, and the metabolite differences between JG21 and ZZ13 were the smallest. Among the top 30 differentially metabolites, 6 were identified as phospholipids. Phospholipids may influence the lipid composition of millet, potentially correlating with its antioxidant properties and storage stability (<a href="#bb0230" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0230">Ya, Tong, &amp; Bao, 2018</a>). During storage, phospholipids are prone to oxidation and hydrolysis, leading to a gradual decline in lipid and unsaturated fatty acid content (<a href="#bb0070" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0070">Hao, Narciso, &amp; Nyström, 2021</a>). This process accelerates the aging (deterioration) of grains (<a href="#bb0125" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0125">Liu, Waters, Rose, Bao, &amp; King, 2013</a>). The top 30 differentially metabolites also include chlorogenic acid methyl ester, eupatilin, and sedanolide. Chlorogenic acid methyl ester，a phenolic acid detected in rice, exhibits anti-inflammatory activity(<a href="#bb0280" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0280">Zhang et al., 2018</a>). Eupatilin was identified in purple glutinous rice and associated with its antioxidant properties (<a href="#bb0225" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0225">Xiong et al., 2022</a>). Sedanolide was found in the seeds and leaves of certain plants, contributing to antimicrobial effects (<a href="#bb0005" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0005">Adam et al., 2021</a>; <a href="#bb0010" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0010">Ahmed et al., 2021</a>).</p>
<p id="p0150">Simultaneously, differential metabolites were screened by OPLS-DA (Fig. S2). A total of 504 differential metabolites were screened in different millets with VIP &gt; 1 and <em>P</em> &lt; 0.05 as the threshold, and the first 30 differentially abundant metabolites were clustered as shown in <a href="#f0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a>D. Through cluster analysis, different millets were divided into two categories: HM, JG1, and JF107 were clustered into one category, and JG21 and ZZ13 were clustered into another category.</p>
<p id="p0155">Among the top 30 differentially abundant metabolites, jionoside A1 and heptamethoxyflavone of HM were significantly up-regulated compared with the differential metabolites of other millet varieties. Phenylethanolosides are water-soluble phenolic compounds that are present in many medicinal plants and have antibacterial, anticancer, antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, anti-obesity, antioxidant, antiviral, and neuroprotective properties (<a href="#bb0220" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0220">Wu et al., 2020</a>). Heptamethoxyflavone can effectively prevent obesity and hyperlipidemia by regulating the expression of lipid-metabolism-related genes and inflammatory-response-related genes (<a href="#bb0055" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0055">Feng et al., 2019</a>). Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC) and lysophosphatidylglycerol (LPG) are lipid macromolecules in which LPC is involved in important signal transduction and immunoregulation (<a href="#bb0135" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0135">Papangelis &amp; Ulven, 2022</a>). Compared with other millet varieties, <em>N</em>-scetyl-L-tyrosine, luteolin, glycerol-3-phosphate, epigallocatechin, and LPG of JG1 were up-regulated. Chlorogenic acid methyl ester exerts strong anti-inflammatory effects via inhibiting the COX-2/NLRP3/NF-κB pathway (<a href="#bb0280" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0280">Zhang, Yang, et al., 2018</a>). Eupatilin inhibits pulmonary fibrosis by activating Sestrin2/PI3K/Akt/mTOR dependent autophagy pathway (<a href="#bb0065" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0065">Gong et al., 2023</a>). Compared with the other three millets, chlorogenic acid methyl ester, tricin-4′-<em>O</em>-(<em>β</em>-guaiacylglyceryl) ether, <em>C</em>-hexosyl-apigenin-<em>O</em>-feruloylhexoside, <em>C</em>-hexosyl-chrysoeriol-<em>O</em>-feruloylhexoside, and eupatilin of HM and JF107 were up-regulated, while <em>N</em>-acetyl-L-tyrosine, luteolin, sedanolide, catalpol, hexadecanedioic acid, and 6-<em>β</em>-prostaglandin were down-regulated. Raffinose positively regulates maize drought tolerance by reducing leaf transpiration (<a href="#bb0130" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0130">Liu et al., 2023</a>). Rosmarinic acid is known for its therapeutic potentials against cancer, inflammatory and neuronal diseases such as the treatment of neurofibromatosis or prevention from Alzheimer's diseases (<a href="#bb0165" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0165">Sally et al., 2022</a>). Additionally, the metabolites of HM, JG1, and JF107, such as raffinose, rosmarinic acid, 2,6-dihydroxypurine, and LPC, were also up-regulated compared with JG21 and ZZ13. Overall, HM, JG21, and ZZ13 had the most abundant of the top 30 differential metabolites.</p></section><section id="s0080"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.3. KEGG enrichment analysis</h4>
<p id="p0160">The metabolic pathways of the five millets were distributed in 18 metabolic pathways, including amino acid metabolism and flavonoid biosynthesis. As shown in <a href="#f0015" class="usa-link">Fig. 3</a>, HM vs. ZZ13 and HM vs. JF107 exhibited the highest number of annotated metabolic pathways (9). In contrast, JG21 vs. JF107 had the fewest, with only three annotated pathways. Among them, glycerophospholipid metabolism was only annotated in the differential metabolites of HM vs. ZZ13; TCA cycle and glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism were annotated only in the differential metabolites of JG1 vs. JG21; and sphingolipid metabolism and galactose metabolism were only annotated in the differential metabolites of ZZ13 vs. JF107.</p>

<p id="p0165">The non-volatile metabolites with flavor characteristics were screened from 504 differential metabolites, as shown in <a href="#f0020" class="usa-link">Fig. 4</a>A and Table S3. The sour metabolites in the flavor substances were salicylic acid, nicotinic acid, and gluconic acid, and the content of sour substances in ZZ13 was significantly higher than that of other millets (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05). The most abundant types of sweeteners, including metabolites such as oligosaccharides, serine, and methionine, were the most abundant, and the content of sweeteners in HM was significantly higher than that of other millets. The five millets had low levels of bitter compounds. Some scholars have identified polyphenolic compounds as substances responsible for the bitter and astringent taste of quinoa (<a href="#bb0190" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0190">Song et al., 2024</a>). The bitter substances screened in this study included four alkaloids and some polyphenolic compounds. There was only one spicy substance and umami substance, which were capsaicin and phosphocholine, respectively. In summary, the flavor substances of the five millets were significantly different.</p>

Among the 30 metabolites, the expression trends of HM and ZZ13 were reversed, and 16 differential substances such as diosmetin and phloretin of HM were down-regulated, while metabolites such as phosphocholine, uridine, and apigenin were up-regulated. Only three metabolites in JF107, namely, gluconic acid, rosmarinic acid, and salicylic acid, were up-regulated; studies have shown that gluconic acid can enhance the taste of tea and other beverages, while rosmarinic acid and salicylic acid, which are natural phenolic acids, have a wide range of biological activities such as antioxidation (<a href="#bb0100" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0100">Li et al., 2022</a>; <a href="#bb0175" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0175">Sehrawat et al., 2022</a>), which can also be applied to dairy products, oil-rich foods, and various flavors to increase the appropriate bitterness and sourness (<a href="#bb0060" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0060">García, Prieto, Guevara, Malagon, &amp; Osorio, 2016</a>; <a href="#bb0215" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0215">Wood, Baxter, Thies, Kyle, &amp; Duthie, 2011</a>).</p></section><section id="s0090"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3.5. Differential analysis of polyphenols</h4>
<p id="p0175">A total of 16 polyphenols including 4 phenolic acids and 11 flavonoids were screened (Table S4), among which the flavonoids were mainly isoflavones, flavanols, and flavonols. Polyphenolic compounds have liver protection, liver detoxification, and antifungal activities and are used as treatment of acute and chronic hepatitis and liver cirrhosis (<a href="#bb0185" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0185">Shi et al., 2024</a>). They also have anti-free-radical and antioxidant effects (<a href="#bb0095" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0095">Li et al., 2024</a>). As shown in <a href="#f0025" class="usa-link">Fig. 5</a>A, JF107 had significantly lower polyphenol and flavonoid contents compared to the other millet varieties. However, its phenolic acid content was significantly higher (<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05). Previous studies have detected phenolic acids such as ferulic acid, chlorogenic acid, caffeic acid, coumaric acid, syringic acid, and others in Jingu 28 and Jingu 34 (<a href="#bb0270" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0270">Zhang &amp; Liu, 2015</a>). Based on an ultra-performance liquid chromatography electrospray ionization-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC–ESI-MS/MS) system, <a href="#bb0285" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0285">Zhang et al. (2021)</a> identified 116 flavonoid metabolites from five foxtail millet varieties: Jingu 21, Qinzhouhuang, Yugu 1, Daobaqian, and Niumaobai.</p>

Conclusion</h2>
<p id="p0185">For the first time, volatile and non-volatile metabolites were characterized in Qingyang millet. The research results indicate that the five main cultivated millet varieties exhibit significant differences in both volatile flavor compounds and non-volatile metabolites. Different varieties of millet possess distinct characteristic flavor compounds.The taste variations among different millet varieties are primarily attributed to differences in sweet-tasting substances, while the levels of polyphenolic compounds may contribute to the disparity in antioxidant activity observed among various millets. This study provides theoretical foundations for the processing and quality regulation of downstream millet products.</p></section><section id="s0100"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">CRediT authorship contribution statement</h2>
