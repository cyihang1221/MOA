========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: FFT-based
sample_description: The study used publicly available genome-wide histone modification data of H3K4me3 obtained from the Zenodo repository, originally published by Sadeh et al. This dataset was generated using cfChIP-Seq technology to profile histone modifications in cell-free plasma samples from 88 healthy donors and 128 colorectal cancer (CRC) patients. All human subject data were de-identified, and the study complied with the human ethics protocol (#ET000631) approved by the University of Western Australia’s Human Ethics Committee; no informed consent was required due to the use of anonymized public data. Genomic signal processing was performed at single-nucleotide resolution across multiple genomic region sizes: {300 kb, 600 kb, 1 Mb, 5 Mb, 10 Mb, 50 Mb}, with each region analyzed for H3K4me3 signal intensity defined as a vector x[n] of length L, where positions within experimentally identified peaks P = {(s,e,v)} — start s, end e, and intensity v — inherit v, and all other positions are assigned zero intensity.
complete_workflow_description: The computational pipeline was implemented in Python 3.11.10 using PyTorch 2.5.1 for both FFT and autoencoder modules, scikit-learn 1.5.2 for performance metrics (MSE, MAE, COS, ROC-AUC, AP), and XGBoost 2.0.3 (GPU-accelerated) for downstream classification. FFT signal processing involved: (1) constructing single-nucleotide-resolution signal vectors from H3K4me3 peak annotations; (2) applying torch.fft.rfft; (3) computing magnitudes and selecting top-m components by absolute magnitude; (4) zero-padding and applying torch.fft.irfft for reconstruction. Autoencoder comparison used convolutional autoencoders with architecture varying by compression ratio (Table 1): encoder/decoder paths with 4 Conv1D/ConvTranspose1D layers, BatchNorm, ReLU (except final layer), trained with Adam (lr=0.0001), ReduceLROnPlateau (factor=0.1, patience=10), batch size=64, max 1000 epochs, early stopping (patience=50), and 80/20 train/validation split across 100 distinct 300-kb genomic regions. Classification used XGBoost (500 estimators, max_depth=50, learning_rate=0.1, 5-fold CV, stratified 80/20 train/test) on three feature sets: original H3K4me3 signals, FFT-compressed features (top-m magnitudes), and autoencoder latent features (encoding_dim), all extracted from gene bodies + 2 kb upstream TSS. All GPU computations ran on the Kaya supercomputing cluster (UWA); CPU benchmarks used single-core execution.
