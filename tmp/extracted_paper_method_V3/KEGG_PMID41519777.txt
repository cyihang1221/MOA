>Methods   Participants   Participants were recruited from the National Institute of Sports Medicine in China. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai University of Sport (No. 102772022RT041). This study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200061779) on 02/07/2022. Before participation, all participants were introduced to detailed procedures and signed the informed consent form. Inclusion criteria for the study were: (1) aged 18–60 years, (2) unilateral ACL injury with/without meniscus damage determined by clinical exam (positive anterior drawer test, Lachman test and pivot shift test) that was corroborated using magnetic resonance imaging, (3) underwent ACLR surgery with semitendinosus and gracilis hamstrings tendon (HT) autograft, (4) no history of trauma or surgery on the contralateral knee joint. Exclusion criteria were as follows:1) previous knee surgery, 2) concomitant ligamentous injuries (such as posterior cruciate ligament injury, medial or lateral collateral ligament injury) and chondral damage, 3) a clinical history of any inflammatory disorder, 4) patients complicated with metabolic disease, cerebrovascular disease, neurodegenerative disorders and urinary system dysfunction, (5) history of drug or alcohol abuse, (6) pregnant or nursing (lactating) women. Study design   ACL reconstruction was performed with an HT autograft by one of two experienced knee surgeons at the National Institute of Sports Medicine in China. There was no heterogeneity existed in graft selection during surgery. After surgery, all participants followed the same standardized rehabilitation protocol offered by the clinic. Progressive knee range-of-motion exercises and partial weight-bearing with crutches were initiated as tolerated the day after ACLR. In the first two weeks following surgery, patients were instructed to reduce knee swelling, bear weight as tolerated, and perform range-of-motion exercises for the knee. Over the course of three weeks, wall squats, a stationary bike, and straight-leg raises were introduced, followed by progressive blocks of strength, power, and plyometric exercises at approximately 5 weeks. After 3 months, the rehabilitation progressed to jogging once there was no evidence of swelling. The range of motion, lower muscle strength, and proprioceptive sensation should be continuously improved. After 6 months, patients were allowed to return to sports, including kicking, dribbling, and changing direction. Patients were encouraged to increase the intensity of their training gradually. For this study, clinical assessments and urine collection were conducted approximately 9 months after ACLR. In this study, the criteria for RTS include being greater than 9 months postoperatively, recovering to a preinjury level of Tegner, and an SLH test LSI of 85% or greater. Patients were grouped according to whether they returned to sport (RTS group) or not (Non-RTS group). Clinical data acquisition   Urine samples were collected from each participant at the time of study inclusion. Then, participants underwent clinical tests, including subjective evaluations (International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC), Lysholm, and Tegner), KT-2000 measurement, limb circumference measurement, isokinetic strength testing, and the SLH test.   Limb circumference   Bilateral lower limb circumferences were measured with a flexible ruler without elasticity. Limb circumference was measured at 15 cm, 10 cm, and 5 cm from the patella upper margin, the knee joint, and 15 cm below the lower edge of the patella, respectively. Index of atrophy = (unaffected limb circumference - affected limb circumference)/unaffected limb circumference. Isokinetic muscle strength   Measurements of concentric isokinetic strength of the knee extensor and flexor muscles were performed at a speed of 60°/s (5 repetitions) and 180°/s (10 repetitions) from a range of 0° to 90° of knee flexion of both limbs with a isokinetic dynamometer (Biodex Medical System, New York, USA). The peak torque (PT), the relative peak torque, and total power (TW) were recorded. The relative peak torque was calculated by normalizing the peak torque to body mass (PT/BW). LSI was calculated for both quadriceps and hamstrings PT by expressing the injured limb relative to the unaffected limb. The hamstrings: quadriceps ratio (H/Q) was calculated by expressing hamstrings torque relative to quadriceps torque. Single-Leg hop for distance   The patient stood on one foot, jumped forward as far as possible, and landed on the same foot. If the landing resulted in loss of balance, the trial was repeated until the landing was controlled. The hop distance was measured from the starting line (with the toe touching the line) to the toe of the foot on landing and recorded using a tape measure. All participants were given two practice trials and then performed three measured trials on each leg, with the non-operated leg first. The averaged value for each leg was recorded. For hop distance, an LSI was calculated as the operated leg/the non-operated leg × 100%. Urine proteomics   Urine was collected from each participant at the time of study inclusion. Subsequently, urine samples were collected, aliquoted, and stored at −80 °C until used. Frozen samples were thawed at room temperature, and 10 mL of each patient’s urine was taken for proteomic analysis. The urine samples were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C, and the supernatants were collected. Then, the protein was precipitated with three times the volume of pre-cooled acetone for 4 h at −20 °C. The precipitates were solubilized in 8 M urea and sonicated. Protein was digested using standard filter-aided sample preparation. Enzymatic digestion with trypsin was performed, and the resulting peptides were analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). LC analysis was performed using the Ultimate 3000 (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), with one injection per sample for all tested samples. The resulting peptides were analyzed by an Orbitrap Exploris 480 Mass Spectrometer (Thermo Scientific). Protein identification and quantification were performed using Proteome Discoverer (PD, version 2.5, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany). Then, data processing and analysis were performed in an R environment. Biomarker selection and validation   Differentially expressed proteins between the RTS and Non-RTS groups were first identified using a two-sample t-test (|Log₂ fold-change (FC)|> 1,  P  < 0.05). 20 of the highest significantly up-regulated and 20 of the highest significantly down-regulated proteins were selected for subsequent modeling. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was then applied to these candidate proteins using the R package glmnet (v4.1–4). A 10-fold cross-validation procedure was used to determine the optimal regularization parameter (λ) that minimized the mean cross-validated error. Proteins with non-zero regression coefficients at this optimal λ were regarded as LASSO-selected feature proteins. Subsequently, Spearman correlation analyses were performed between the relative abundance of proteins (The top 20 up- and top 20 down-regulated proteins) and differential clinical indexes. Proteins that exhibited significant correlations ( P  < 0.05) with at least one clinical index were intersected with the LASSO-selected features to obtain a subset of clinically relevant proteins. The discriminative ability of each intersected protein was further evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. Proteins with area under the curve (AUC) > 0.8 were defined as candidate biomarkers with clinical relevance [ 17 ]. Statistical analysis   The proteomic raw data files were analyzed using Proteome Discoverer (PD, Thermo Fisher Scientific, version 2.5) for protein identification and quantification; with the Mascot algorithm. The database search was performed against the human UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot FASTA database (released in 2023, containing 20,423 entries). Trypsin and Lys-C were selected as specific proteolytic enzymes, and up to two missed cleavages were allowed. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine was set as a fixed modification, whereas methionine oxidation and protein N-terminal acetylation were defined as variable modifications. Both the peptide-spectrum match (PSM) false discovery rate (FDR) and the protein-level FDR were controlled below 1%.   Quantitative results were exported from PD and imported into Perseus (version 1.6.14.0) for downstream statistical analysis. Label-free quantification intensity values were Log₂-transformed, and missing values were imputed from a normal distribution (width = 0.3, downshift = 1.8) to simulate signals from low-abundance proteins near the detection limit [ 18 ]. The significantly different proteins were determined by upregulated fold-change (FC) ≥ 2 or downregulated FC ≤ 0.5, and the  P  < 0.05 of an independent-sample t-test.   The UniProt ( https://www.uniprot.org/ ) was used for protein identification. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis was performed using DAVID ( https://david.ncifcrf.gov/ ), and Gene Ontology (GO) annotations ( http://geneontology.org/ ) were used for the functional annotation of proteins. Differences in continuous clinical variables with a normal distribution were evaluated using the independent sample t-test. In contrast, differences in variables with non-normal distributions were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Comparison between the two groups’ nominal variables was tested with the Chi-squared test. The means ± standard deviations or count (%) of clinical variables were calculated. Correlation analyses were conducted between urine proteomic data and clinical measures. All data were analyzed using SPSS 23.0 (Chicago, IL, USA), with a statistical significance level set at  P  < 0.05. These findings collectively suggested that patients who successfully returned to sport exhibited more complete recovery of quadriceps strength, endurance, and limb symmetry following ACL reconstruction.
