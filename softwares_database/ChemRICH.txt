sample_description: The study used venous blood samples collected from 69 pairs of type 2 diabetic patients with diabetic retinopathy (DR) and without DR (DM), matched via propensity score matching (PSM) based on age, gender, BMI, and HbA1c. All participants fasted for over 8 hours prior to blood collection. Serum was separated by centrifugation at 2000 rpm, 4°C, for 10 minutes, and 1.5 mL aliquots were stored in sterile tubes at −86°C for metabolomic analysis. A total of 613 metabolites were detected using UPLC-MS/MS (318 in positive ion mode, 295 in negative ion mode); after preprocessing—including removal of metabolites with CV >30% in QC samples, missing ratio >20% in either group, and imputation of missing values (<20% missing) by half the lowest detected peak area—461 metabolites remained for downstream analysis. Log transformation and Pareto scaling were applied to improve data normality and comparability.
software_configuration_description: ChemRICH (Chemical Similarity Enrichment Analysis) was applied as a threshold-free pathway mapping method based on chemical similarity to complement traditional MetPA. It used the PubChem compound database as the background set—the largest publicly available free compound repository. ChemRICH enriched all PubChem-identified metabolites and identified DR-altered metabolic clusters; significance was determined using FDR-adjusted q-value < 0.01. The analysis specifically screened for key metabolites within perturbed clusters and assessed their associations with DR using restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression, adjusting for SBP, duration of diabetes, and insulin treatment history. Eight significantly associated metabolic clusters were reported: unsaturated fatty acids (key compound: linoleic acid), disaccharides (trehalose), ethanolamines (choline), HETE (12-HETE), dipeptides (phenylacetylglutamine), indoleacetic acids (indoleacetamide), saturated fatty acids (hexadecanoic acid), and amino acids, sulfur (sulfocysteine).

sample_description: The study recruited 211 cirrhotic outpatients prospectively from GI and Hepatology Clinics at Virginia Commonwealth University and McGuire VA Medical Centers between October 2015 and November 2016. software_configuration_description: ChemRICH (Chemical Similarity Enrichment Analysis) was employed to identify chemically similar compound classes significantly altered in patients who developed a specific outcome compared to those who did not. It performed enrichment analysis based on chemical structures rather than predefined metabolic pathways, relying on background databases. The statistical significance of enriched clusters was determined using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, with a p-value threshold of <0.05 considered statistically significant. ChemRICH output included cluster p-values, compound significance, effect sizes, and direction of alteration (increase/decrease), which were further visualized using MetaMapp. The analysis was applied separately to serum and urine metabolite datasets for each outcome (hospitalization, overt HE, transplant, death), following multivariate logistic regression and VIP (Variable Importance in Projection) scoring to select top 20 metabolites per outcome for focused enrichment.
