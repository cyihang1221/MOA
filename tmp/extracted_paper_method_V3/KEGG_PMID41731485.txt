>Methods   Population   Prior to the study, all participants allowed the use of data and samples for scientific purposes and signed written informed consent form. From July 2020 to June 2021, 46 patients diagnosed with moderate-to-severe AV, matched along with 20 healthy controls (the HC group) were enrolled in the study from the dermatology department of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University. Disease severity was determined by the Global Acne Grading System (GAGS) score, with GAGS ≥ 19 defined as moderate to severe AV [ 12 ]. Beyond adhering to the criteria established in prior study [ 13 ], the participants who had already received intervention of isotretinoin, antibiotics, glucocorticoids, ω-3 fatty acids, immunosuppressive drugs, or herbal treatments within the past 6 months, or were unwilling to sign the informed consent form, or were known to be allergic to the intervention were all excluded. Moreover, daily lifestyle changes were recommended during the treatment, including less sweet intake, a high-fat diet, and dairy products. The Ethics Committee had given approval for the use of human subjects in this study (No. KY2020115). Clinical trial design   The patients were randomized to receive orally taken isotretinoin treatment (Huabang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. Chongqing, China), either combined with (the AVW group) 2400 mg/day ω-3 fatty acids (Nature Made, LA, USA) or monotonously (the AVP group) for 6 months. When the medicines were administered to patients, identifiable packaging was removed so that the patients could not know the name of the medicine they were taking. The isotretinoin dosage was adjusted based on drug tolerance and treatment efficacy. Therapeutic effects were evaluated using the GAGS score. When GAGS score was between 19 and 30, the isotretinoin dose was set at 0.5 mg/ kg/day; when the score exceeded 30, the dose was applied to 1.0 mg/ kg/day. Demographic data of the participants, including sex, age, body mass index (BMI), and disease duration were recorded at enrollment. GAGS score evaluation, skin analysis by vital imaging system for aesthetic analysis (VISIA), and blood serum sample were collectted at baseline and at the end of treatment. The study used the CONSORT reporting guidelines to draft this manuscript and the CONSORT reporting checklist for editing [ 14 ]. Efficacy evaluation criteria and VISIA   GAGS divides acne-prone areas into the nose and chin area (1 point), forehead (2 points), left cheek (2 points), right cheek (2 points), and chest and upper back (3 points) regions, based on the approximate area and density of the pilosebaceous units. Different zones correspond to different weight scores, 1–18 points for mild, 19–30 points for moderate, 31–38 points for severe, and ≥ 39 points for extremely severe. Only moderate to severe cases were enrolled in this study.   A VISIA-CR™ imaging system (Canfield Scientific, Inc., USA) was used to evaluate the average percentage of facial red and purple areas of the participants before and after treatment. The red area index reflects the condition of capillaries in patients with AV, whose blood vessels and hemoglobin in the dermal papilla layer of the skin appear reddish. On the other hand, the purple area index measures porphyrins, which are metabolites of  Propionibacterium acnes  at the hair follicle opening and appear as brick-red highlights. A higher percentage of VISIA indices indicates milder disease severity. Sample collection and detection   Serum were collected from 2 mL of venous blood at fasting in non-anticoagulant tubes at the beginning of enrollment and the end of their 6-month treatment; samples were centrifuged after collection at 3,000 rpm for 15 min after 30 min of resting at 20–25 ℃, and subsequently stored in a -80 ℃ refrigerator. An automated biochemical detector (Synchron LX-20; Beckman Coulter, USA) was used to determine the blood glucose (Glu), TC, TG, LDL-C, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels. Insulin resistance was assessed by homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR). Fasting insulin (INS) levels were measured by the ELISA kit (Promega, USA). Serum sample processing for lipidomics analysis   For lipid extraction, 200 µl of serum samples, 80 µl of methanol (Cat: M742213, Macklin, Shanghai, China) and 400 µl of methyl-tert-butyl ether (MTBE, Cat: B802533, Macklin, Shanghai, China) were mixed; the mixtures were vortexed for 30 s and then ultrasonically extracted for 30 min on 40 kHz power at 5 ℃. The samples were settled at -20 ℃ for 30 min then centrifuged (13000 rpm, 4 ℃) for 15 min. Next, supernatant was drawn out and dried in a vacuum concentrator (Cat: Y-TN- C19819 , Yuyan Machinery Co., LTD, Shanghai, China), and 100 µl of isopropanol (Cat: I766839, Macklin, Shanghai, China): acetonitrile (1:1, v/v, Cat: A801556, Macklin, Shanghai, China) solution was added for re-dissolution. After 30 s of vortexing, the mixtures were ultrasonically extracted in ice water bath and centrifuged (13000 rpm, 4 ℃) for 5 min then transfer 80 µl of supernatant to UPLC-Q Exactive System (Thermo, CA). Acquisition of LC-MS/MS data   The study obtained metabolites were processed by an Ultra High Performance Liquid Chromatography Tandem Fourier Transform Mass Spectrometry UPLC-Q Exactive system (Thermo Scientific™ Vanquish™ UHPLC, CA). Two microliters of the sample were separated by a BEH C18 column (0.1 cm × 0.0021 cm i.d., 1.7 μm) and then operated by mass spectrometry. Solvent A, which contained 0.1% formic acid (Catalog: F809715, Macklin, Shanghai, China) dissolved in 50% acetonitrile, 10 mmol/L ammonium acetate solution; solvent B, which consisted of 2 mmol/L ammonium and 0.02% formic acid in acetate solution mixed with isopropanol, acetonitrile and water in a volumetric ratio of 10:88:2, these two distinct solvent systems in which the mobile phase was composed. The solvent gradient elution protocol was established as follows: an initial composition of 65% solvent A and 35% solvent B was maintained from 0 to 4 min, transitioning to 40% A and 60% B. Subsequently, from 4 to 12 min, the gradient shifted from 40% A:60% B to 15% A:85% B. Between 12 and 21 min, the composition was further adjusted from 15% A:85% B to 0% A:100% B. A rapid re-equilibration step occurred from 24.0 to 24.1 min, returning the mobile phase to 65% A and 35% B. Finally, from 24.1 to 28 min, the system was held isocratically at 65% solvent A and 35% solvent B to ensure column reconditioning. Chromatographic separation was performed with a constant flow rate of 0.40 mL/min and a column temperature maintained at 40 °C. For mass spectrometric detection, signals were acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes simultaneously. The operational parameters were configured under mass scan range (m/z), 200–2000, spray voltage, 3.00 kV or both modes; ion source temperature, 370 °C, collision energy, 20–60 V. Data process and statistical analysis   The raw LC-MS data were imported into LipidSearch (Thermo Fisher Scientific) software to obtain a data matrix for subsequent analysis. Multivariate statistical analyses were applied from the Majorbio Cloud website (https://cloud.majorbio.com) utilizing the R programming environment (version 4.1.1). Partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) or orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was employed to provide a comprehensive view of the metabolic dataset, facilitating the assessment of overall clustering patterns, discernible trends, and identification of potential outliers. Volcano plot analysis of differential expression provided dual validation (P-value+ FC), emphasizing statistical significance, magnitude of biological change, and the direction of changes before and after treatment. The metabolites of significantly difference were selected out on the basis of the variable weight value (VIP) ≥ 1, fold change (FC) > 1/ FC < 1 and P value < 0.05. The biochemical pathways and associated metabolic processes were identified through metabolic enrichment analysis and pathway mapping by the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (kegg_v2021. 09. 18) and Scipy. stats (Python package) (https://docs.scipy. org/doc/scipy/) to identify those significantly enriched pathways. Statistical analysis用means ± standard deviation   All statistical analyses were conducted by the SPSS (version 25.0). Quantitative data are presented as means ± standard deviation. This study employed the Mann-Whitney U test to assess the differences in the data both between and within the two groups, the Wilcoxon test to appraise the intervention before and after within the same group, the Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient to estimate the monotonic correlation between variables. Statistical significance standard was set at  P  < 0.05. GraphPad Prism (version 8.0) was employed for data visualization. Results   Background and efficacy of combination therapy in AV   Three patients withdrew from the AVP group due to nosebleeds, generalized eczema, and severely chapped lips, and three were lost to follow-up in the AVW group. Twenty patients were eventually included in each group for the clinical analysis (Supplement. 1). The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are exhibited in Table  1 . The differences in baseline data among the three groups in terms of gender, BMI, HOMA-IR, INS, HDL-C, LDL-C, TG, and TC were not statistically significant ( P 1  > 0.05). While there was an age difference existed in the HC group and the patient group ( P 1  < 0.001), all individuals enrolled in the study were within the 18–30 age range specified by the inclusion criteria. Regardless of the application of ω-3 fatty acids, the intervention group showed varying degrees of improvement in facial GAGS scores ( P 2  < 0.001,  P 3  < 0.001), sclererythrin levels ( P 2  < 0.001,  P 3  < 0.001) and percentage of red areas ( P 2  < 0.001,  P 3  = 0.006) after treatment. More importantly, the AVW group achieved a significantly greater improvement in GAGS scores ( P 4  = 0.023 for AVP1 vs. AVW1) and TG levels ( P 4  = 0.01 for AVP1 vs. AVW1) than the AVP group. Due to lipid extraction failure, a total of five samples were excluded, two from the HC group, two from the AVP group, and one from the AVW group, and 258 lipid metabolites were finally measured after removing all false positives and noise data. A PLS-DA model was applied on the complete dataset to estimat the clustering tendency from the patients before and after treatment and the HC group. As shown in Fig.  1 A, the separation trend of metabolite compositions across the HC group and the patients in both AVP0 and AVW0 groups was clearly observed at baseline, especially in the cation model (Fig.  1 A), suggesting that the lipid metabolite structures in AV patients differ from those in HC. Following intervention, the lipid metabolism profiles of both AVW1 and AVP1 groups drifted closer to that of the HC group (Fig.  1 C, D). However, the drift was more pronounced in AVW1 group, rather than AVP1 group, a disparity more evident in the cation model (Fig.  1 C). Combination therapy altered serum lipid metabolites of the patients with AV   Volcano plots were used to examine the composition of differential lipid metabolites. This provided double validation of the previous results and emphasized the direction of variation of the metabolites after ω-3 fatty acid treatment. As shown in Fig.  2 A, B, the metabolites formed two independent clusters; 79 and 54 upregulated metabolites and 83 and 29 downregulated metabolites were observed in the cation and anion models, respectively.
