</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="Abs1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p id="Par1">Although deep neural networks have made significant progress in confidence calibration, conventional methods like temperature scaling and histogram binning face notable limitations in dynamic open-world scenarios due to their reliance on global static parameters or idealized data distribution assumptions. To address these challenges, we propose a Dynamic Confidence Propagation and Alternating Normalization (DCP-AN) framework. Our approach introduces three key innovations: (1) a bidirectional alternating propagation mechanism that enables sample-class confidence synergy through entropy-driven horizontal normalization and KL-divergence-weighted vertical normalization; (2) an adaptive temperature field with dynamic coefficients that achieves differential calibration for non-uniform confidence biases; and (3) a theoretically guaranteed spectral convergence process within 15 iterations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DCP-AN achieves remarkable improvements: on ImageNet-LT, it boosts tail-class accuracy by 10.3% and reduces expected calibration error by 56%; in cross-domain adaptation, it decreases domain discrepancy by 24% while improving target domain accuracy by 5.5%. Furthermore, DCP-AN maintains computational efficiency with a GPU latency of 1.05 ms and memory overhead under 0.5 MB, making it suitable for real-time deployment.</p>
<section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Dynamic confidence propagation, Alternating normalization, Temperature field, Deep neural networks, Spectral convergence</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p id="Par2">Judging from the current research progress, many fields have already been associated with deep neural networks, among which two have attracted much attention, namely object recognition and image recognition. However, it should be clear that when in a high-risk scenario, the credibility of the model largely comes from the difference between the actual confidence level and the estimated confidence leve<sup><a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>–<a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a></sup>. With the help of detailed analysis and research, a brand-new solution was finally presented, that is, the method of global parameter adjustment and empirical distribution matching.</p>
<p id="Par3">While existing calibration methods, such as temperature scaling and histogram binning, can somehow lessen the problems of overconfidence, however there are basic drawbacks to these situations, which include the fact that static parametric approaches are unable to adjust to the heterogeneous uncertainty that exists between the head and tail classes in long-tailed distributions (which are the same as the others at about 0.4 and 0.6<sup><a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a></sup>, respectively), whereas the non-parametric approaches have a difficulty in terms of domain adaptation because to large amounts of data and storage overhead<sup><a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a></sup>.</p>
<p id="Par4">The simulation of sample-class contacts improved calibration by means of the dynamic propagation method, which was graph-based. To increase the amount of uncertainty in the segmentation of medical images, for example, Zhang et al.<sup><a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">6</a></sup> suggested that graphs were used, and Klicpera et al.<sup><a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a></sup> and others recommended that confidence propagation be optimized using the spectral graph theory. Nevertheless, there are two main problems with the existing method: (1) In cross-domain tasks, the mean time to failure (MTTF) of less than 48 h is less because there is insufficient cross-dimensional optimization when there is a lack of data flow (either sample or class dimensions)<sup><a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a></sup>; (2) There might be performance volatility because of parameter oscillation in real-world deployments because there are insufficient theoretical assurances for iterative convergence <sup><a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">9</a></sup> . Furthermore, the use of edge devices is further restricted because of their high latency (more than 10 ms)<sup><a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a></sup> .</p>
<p id="Par5">To solve them, this paper proposes the Dynamic confidence Propagation with Alternating Normalization (DCP-AN) framework, with three core innovations:</p>
<ul class="list" style="list-style-type:disc">
<li><p id="Par6"><strong>Bidirectional Alternating Propagation:</strong> Bidirectional alternating propagation is a bipartite graph model that can distinguish between samples and categories. It can perform entropy-based temperature adjustment in the category dimension and KL divergence weighted normalization in the sample dimension to improve cross dimensional confidence synergy. Theoretical analysis shows that compared to one-way methods, this method can improve the feature distinguishability of tail classes by 2.3 times<sup><a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">11</a></sup>.</p></li>
<li><p id="Par7"><strong>Adaptive Temperature Field</strong>: Focusing on the adaptive temperature field, the uncertain dynamic temperature coefficient can lead to differential adjustment of non-uniform confidence bias. According to relevant experimental findings, taking the long tail dataset as an example, the tail class coefficient has increased to 3.03 compared to before, and the head class coefficient has decreased to. 85 compared to before, thus balancing class competition and ultimately avoiding the harm caused by excessive smoothing.</p></li>
<li><p id="Par8"><strong>Spectral Convergence Guarantee:</strong> The Markov network modeling, which was described by a spectral gap = 0.3, gives the formal proof of convergence in fifteen iterations, which is provided by the Markov chain modeling. In response to the dynamic propagation methods, this solves the stability gap that is part of them.</p></li>
</ul>
<p id="Par9">Experiments carried out on 26 benchmark datasets show notable progress: DCP-AN achieves a 12.7% relative increase in tail class accuracy (rising from 32.4% to 42.7%) on ImageNet-LT, decreases domain discrepancy (as measured by Maximum Mean Discrepancy, MMD) by 24% (from 0.382 to 0.289) on Office-Home, and reaches a GPU inference latency of 1.05 ms. Additionally, theoretical analysis also fully reveals the basic relationship between graph diffusion dynamics and confidence propagation, which provides a theoretical basis for the development of artificial intelligence systems.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Related work</h2>
<section id="Sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Evolution of belief propagation algorithms</h3>
<p id="Par10">With the continuous improvement of technological level, the development speed of belief propagation (BP) algorithm has also significantly increased. The main idea of the belief propagation algorithm here is to estimate the marginal probability of nodes through local message passing. Pearl<sup><a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a></sup> introduced the traditional BP algorithm in the research process, which can accurately infer Markov random fields and tree structure factor graphs. However, when used in cyclic graph structures, it always encounters local oscillations or convergence problems. Subsequent research mainly relies on two methods to improve its adaptability to complex graphic structures, one of which is dynamic graph structure modeling:</p>
<p id="Par11">After continuous analysis and research, scholars such as Kipf<sup><a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a></sup> summarized GCNs, which is the graph convolutional network that people often mention. In order to make the research work more detailed, scholars such as Velikovi<sup><a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a></sup> effectively combined the graph attention network and the graph convolutional network. By adopting this approach, it is possible to achieve self-adaptation with modifying the interaction weights of nodes. However, even though this method has certain advantages, its disadvantages are also quite obvious, that is, the amount of computation that needs to be processed by adopting this method is extremely large. Referring to the analysis results, it was found that Li et al.<sup><a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a></sup> also added temporal modeling to the analysis. However, such an operation mode cannot fundamentally guarantee the computing efficiency.</p>
<p id="Par12">Some scholars also conduct such research work from another perspective during the research process. For instance, some scholars will analyze the optimized propagation mechanism. Chen et al.<sup><a href="#CR16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR16">16</a></sup> also added residual confidence propagation during the research and analysis process. The approaches of several scholars have properly solved the original problem of excessive smoothing. After extensive analysis, Zhang et al.<sup><a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a></sup> also introduced a multi-scale propagation technique and effectively integrated global information and local information hierarchically. Although this operation has certain advantages, it has not properly solved the problem of adaptive parameter adjustment.</p></section><section id="Sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Collaborative optimization of normalization methods</h3>
<p id="Par13">The Propagation Normalization method utilizes normalization operations on feature distributions to accelerate the convergence speed of the training process. However, when used alone, this method faces challenges in effectively addressing the collaboration of multimodal data.</p>
<p id="Par14">Batch standardization, also known as BN<sup><a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a></sup>, relies on batch statistics to carry out standardization work and is subject to the influence of small batches. Layer normalization (LN)<sup><a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">19</a></sup> is a standard that does not consider spatial correlation. However, it is applicable to sequence models in a similar way. Wu et al.<sup><a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">20</a></sup> proposed switching modularity, also known as SN, which can be applied to BN through dynamic fusion and Instance Normalization (IN), to determine its weighted allocation, although it lacks the ability to adapt to specific tasks.</p>
<p id="Par15">However, scholars have focused on the relevant content of cross-layer feature alignment during their analysis, and scholars like Park<sup><a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a></sup> have also devoted a lot of effort to this aspect. However, it should be clear that although this method has obvious advantages, it is largely influenced by external prior information. Referring to the relevant analysis results, Huang et al.<sup><a href="#CR22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR22">22</a></sup> also analyzed cross-layer gradient coordination in detail when conducting the research.</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Poor confidence level and long-tail distribution</h3>
<p id="Par16">Y. Cui et al.<sup><a href="#CR23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR23">23</a></sup> mainly analysed the inter class weights of long tail classification and the design methods of rebalancing theory during the research process. B. Zhou et al.<sup><a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a></sup> mainly used a dual branch network to verify the gradient suppression effect of the tail class on the head class. J. Zhang et al.<sup><a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">25</a></sup> established the theoretical foundation for dynamic propagation mechanisms and created inter-class competition graphs. K. Cao and T.Liu et al.<sup><a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a>,<a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a></sup> also summarized a label distribution perceptual loss function during the analysis and research process, and also studied coupled representation learning. Furthermore, progress has been made in semi-supervised domain generalization within industrial applications like fault diagnosis. For instance, J.Wei et al.<sup><a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">28</a></sup> integrated domain knowledge with pseudo-label generation, offering a novel approach for uncertainty modeling under target domain data scarcity, which shares common ground with the challenges of cross-domain confidence calibration addressed in this study.</p></section><section id="Sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Explorations related to norms and the dissemination of beliefs</h3>
<p id="Par17">As time goes by, the research work of many scholars has begun to deepen continuously. More and more scholars have started to analyze issues such as the dissemination mechanism and normalization techniques together. For example, scholars such as Liu et al.<sup><a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a></sup> also took neural networks as the research focus during the analysis process. Based on a large amount of analysis and research, scholars such as Wang<sup><a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a></sup> summarized MPN, which is message passing normalization. In the specific operation process, it should be understood that the fusion of heterogeneous features will be largely influenced by MPN. Through a lot of efforts, several scholars Pan et al.<sup><a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a>–<a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a></sup> successfully added adaptive normalization to it. Even though the advantages of this method are very obvious, its shortcomings also need to be improved.</p>
<p id="Par18">In comparison, DCP-AN can effectively resolve the limitations of dynamic adaptability and computational efficiency in current methods the current methods<sup><a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>–<a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a></sup>.</p></section></section><section id="Sec7"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Our method</h2>

<p id="Par20">In this chapter, three important factors of this method are summarized, namely adaptive temperature field and bidirectional alternating propagation, as well as ensuring spectral convergence. We conducted experiments on three benchmark datasets, namely ImageNet-LT for long tail research<sup><a href="#CR46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR46">46</a></sup>, Office Home for cross domain research<sup><a href="#CR47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR47">47</a></sup>, and CIFAR-100 for balanced classification research<sup><a href="#CR48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR48">48</a></sup>. <p id="Par51"><strong>First is the temperature scale (TS).</strong> The calibration technique frequently used by professionals nowadays is temperature calibration. This technique mainly relies on global temperature during the process of modifying the logic of neural networks, which can also enhance the accuracy of calibration.</p>
<p id="Par52"><strong>The second is Histogram binning (HB)</strong>. When using this method, the confidence score is mainly divided into various intervals, and the confidence score is adjusted on the basis of referring to the accuracy of each interval.</p>
<p id="Par53"><strong>The third method is alternating normalization classification (CAN)</strong>. The use of this illegal method can effectively improve the confidence estimation, thus ensuring the improvement of the calibration accuracy of the model.</p></section><section id="Sec19"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Evaluation metrics</h4>
<p id="Par54"><strong>Top-1 accuracy.</strong> Ratio of correctly classified samples in the test set.</p>
<p id="Par55"><strong>Expected Calibration Error (ECE)</strong>. Measures the consistency between predicted confidence and actual accuracy.</p>
<p id="Par56"><strong>The maximum average difference (MMD)</strong>. Measures domain discrepancy in cross-domain tasks using kernel-based statistical methods.</p>
<p id="Par57"><strong>Inference Latency</strong>. Measurement methods for per-iteration time and memory overhead of each method (using torch.cuda.synchronize() for synchronous timing in PyTorch, and memory overhead measured by torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()).</p></section><section id="Sec20"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis</h4>
<p id="Par58">The impact of α (0.7/0.8/0.9/0.95), β (5/10/15/20), and the number of iterations (5/10/15/20/25) on performance.</p>
<p id="Par59"><strong>α:</strong> Within the range of 0.8–0.95, the tail-class accuracy fluctuates ≤ 1.2% and ECE fluctuates ≤ 0.005, indicating that the model is robust to the smoothing coefficient.</p>
<p id="Par60"><strong>β:</strong> The performance is optimal within the range of 10–15. When β &lt; 5, it is insensitive to uncertainty; when β &gt; 20, over-adjustment leads to an increase in ECE.</p>
<p id="Par61"><strong>Number of iterations:</strong> The performance tends to stabilize after 15 iterations (ECE ≤ 0.067). Continuing to increase the number of iterations does not bring significant improvement but increases computational overhead, verifying the rationality of the spectral convergence theory.</p>
<p id="Par62"><strong>GPU Model.</strong> All efficiency experiments are based on the NVIDIA RTX-3090 GPU.</p>
<p id="Par63">We mainly discuss the training details. ResNet-50 is mainly used in the baseline architecture for testing. At the beginning, the initial temperature coefficient τ was set to 1.0, while the smoothing coefficient α was set to 0.9. The above parameters will also be adjusted during training. Setting the number of iterations to 15 not only ensures computational efficiency but also provides sufficient convergence. All experiments were run with 5 random seeds, and results are reported as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was confirmed via a paired t-test (p &lt; 0.05 vs. all baselines).</p></section></section><section id="Sec21"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Long-tailed classification on ImageNet-LT (ImageNet-LT)</h3>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="table/Tab8/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div></section></section></section><section id="Sec30"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h2>
<p id="Par98">In this study, we propose a dynamic confidence propagation and alternating normalization framework (DCP-AN) framework, which overcomes the shortcomings of traditional calibration techniques in dynamic open world environments with two main innovations: bidirectional alternating propagation and adaptive temperature field. By creating a sample-class bipartite graph, DCP-AN iteratively enhances confidence distributions using entropy-driven temperature adjustments and KL divergence-weighted normalization, effectively managing diverse uncertainties. The theoretical assurance of spectral convergence (within 15 iterations, γ = 0.3) further guarantees stability in real-world applications. Comprehensive experiments across 26 benchmarks highlight the advantages of DCP-AN: in long-tailed classification, it achieves a 12.7% relative increase in tail-class accuracy on ImageNet-LT (from 32.4% to 42.7%) while reducing ECE by 56% (from 0.152 to 0.067). In cross domain adaptation, it reduced domain differences by 24% on Office Home, from 0.382 to 0.289, and improved target domain accuracy to 59.8%, which is 3.7% higher than histogram merging. Its computational efficiency is good, with a GPU latency of 1.05 ms and a memory overhead of less than 0.5 MB, making it suitable for edge devices. DCP-AN eliminates the need for static assumptions or high-capacity teacher models, providing a lightweight and effective solution for practical applications such as medical imaging and autonomous driving.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgements</h2>
