<p>The effects of dried and fresh perilla leave water extract (PLWE) marination on physicochemical properties and flavor of grass carp cubes (GCC) were evaluated. The results demonstrated that GCC marinated with fresh PLWE exhibited low centrifugation loss, cooking loss, fishy odor, and high chewiness, pH (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05). GC–MS and GC-IMS results revealed the difference in volatile organic components (VOCs) among the GCC samples, and 14 components were identified as key differential VOCs. Based on untargeted metabolomics analysis, lipids and lipid-like molecules, organic acids and derivatives, organic oxygen compounds, nucleosides, nucleotides, analogues, phenylpropanoids, polyketides, and benzenoids were identified as differential metabolites among the 751 metabolites. Furthermore, 5 KEGG pathways were identified as key differential metabolic pathways. PLWE might mainly improve the flavor of GCC by inhibiting the production of fishy substances by suppressing the lipid, amino acid, and creatine/choline metabolism. It would provide theoretical guidance for the application of PLWE in enhancing flavor characteristics of freshwater fish ready-to-eat products.</p>

<p id="p0030">As the world's leading producer of freshwater aquatic products, China accounts for over 90% of global freshwater fish production. Concurrently, the ready-to-eat (RTE) food industry has experienced exponential growth in China, reaching a market size of USD 69.5 billion in 2023, of which aquatic RTE products alone contributed USD 20 billion. Projections indicated that the overall RTE market will exceed USD 144.8 billion by 2026, signaling enormous commercial potential (<a href="#bb0005" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0005">Bo &amp; Xu, 2023</a>). Fresh-processed fish products are poised to become the primary consumption form for freshwater fish. According to China fishery year book 2025, the harvest of grass carp (<em>Ctenopharyngodon idella</em>) in China reached 6.1 million tons in 2024, ranking first in freshwater aquaculture output. However, due to their growth environment and inherent physicochemical properties, grass carp often exhibit a pronounced fishy odor, which diminishes consumer acceptance and severely constrains industry development. Consequently, eliminating or mitigating this odor has emerged as a critical technological challenge for the grass carp and even freshwater fish RTE industry.</p>
<p id="p0035">Several methods are currently employed to mitigate undesirable odors in freshwater aquatic products, including acid/alkali treatment, adsorption techniques, steam distillation, and high-pressure processing. For instance, Phetsang et al. demonstrated that an alkali-aided process effectively removed off-odors (e.g., geosmin, 2-methylisoborneol, hexanal, and (E)-2-nonenal) from farm-raised hybrid catfish (<a href="#bb0125" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0125">Phetsang et al., 2021</a>). Similarly, <a href="#bb0185" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0185">Zhang et al. (2020)</a> reported that yeast β-glucan adsorption reduced key off-odor compounds (1-octen-3-ol, hexanal, and nonanal) in silver carp mince compared to untreated samples. However, these methods have notable drawbacks, including protein denaturation, nutrient loss, and potential health risks (<a href="#bb0180" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0180">Wu, Wang, Wang, Tian, &amp; Zhan, 2022</a>). Consequently, natural extracts have gained increasing attention in the food industry due to their low safety risks, eco-friendliness, and minimal likelihood of inducing antimicrobial resistance.</p>
<p id="p0040">Perilla (<em>Perilla frutescens</em> L. Britt.), a widely cultivated aromatic plant in China, has traditionally been used as a natural condiment in freshwater fish cuisine to suppress off-odors. However, its large-scale industrial application remains limited, hindering its potential to meet the growing demands of the freshwater aquatic RTE market. Perilla contains numerous bioactive compounds, including polyphenols, flavonoids, and polysaccharides, which exhibit strong antioxidant and antibacterial properties (<a href="#bib206" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib206">Yi et al., 2025</a>). Recent studies have primarily explored its role in extending shelf life and enhancing flavor profiles in freshwater fish products. For example, perilla polyphenol extract was found to significantly improve the flavor of green fin horse-faced file fish, reducing undesirable notes (e.g., fishy, oily/fatty, and marine odors), while enhancing freshness, herbal aromas, and overall acceptability (<a href="#bb0170" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0170">Wang et al., 2024</a>). Similarly, <a href="#bb0040" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0040">Dang, Li, You, Xiong, and An (2024)</a> demonstrated that perilla juice effectively suppressed warmed-over flavor (WOF) in surimi gels by inhibiting WOF compound formation and masking off-flavors with its distinctive aroma. Despite these advances, the mechanisms underlying perilla extract's flavor enhancement in freshwater fish remain unclear. Current research on perilla leaf extract (PLE) primarily utilized dried leaves. However, dehydration might lead to significant losses of volatile oils and other bioactive compounds due to thermal degradation and evaporation, consequently reducing the bioactive potential of PLE (<a href="#bb0095" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0095">Loh &amp; Lim, 2018</a>). Additionally, no comparative studies have evaluated the efficacy of fresh versus dried PLE in flavor improvement. These unexplored areas present significant opportunities for future research, particularly in optimizing perilla-based applications for the aquatic food industry.</p>
<p id="p0045">Flavoromics was first proposed by Reineccius in 2008, which established correlations between volatile components in food and their sensory attributes. This approach employed advanced separation techniques to comprehensively capture flavor-related compounds, followed by data-driven modeling to identify key components associated with sensory perception. By integrating subjective sensory evaluation with objective instrumental analysis, flavoromics provides a systematic framework for elucidating the interplay among food flavor profiles, chemical composition, and human sensory responses. This methodology proved instrumental in deciphering aroma formation mechanisms and characterizing flavor-active compounds (<a href="#bb0010" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0010">Cai, Li, Liu, Wen, &amp; Qu, 2024</a>). Complementarily, untargeted metabolomics focuses on the comprehensive detection of endogenous small molecules (&lt; 1000 Da) within biological matrices. Its application in food science enables holistic investigation of dynamic flavor compound networks, offering novel insights into flavor biosynthesis and transformation pathways during food processing or storage (<a href="#bb0165" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0165">Wang et al., 2023</a>; <a href="#bb0160" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0160">Wang et al., 2023</a>).</p>
<p id="p0050">Therefore, this study systematically compared the effects of fresh and dried PLWE on the physicochemical properties of grass carp cubes (GCCs). Utilizing flavoromics combined with untargeted metabolomics, we further investigated their differential impacts on volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and metabolite profiles after marination. This comprehensive approach elucidated the underlying mechanisms of flavor modification, providing both theoretical foundations and practical technical references for optimizing PLWE application in enhancing flavor characteristics and the overall quality of freshwater fish RTE products.</p></section><section id="s0010"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. Materials and methods</h2>
<section id="s0015"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1. Sample preparation</h3>
<p id="p0055">The C<sub>8</sub><sub>–</sub><sub>40</sub> n-alkane mixture (used for retention index calibration) was obtained from Tanmo Quality Control Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). All other chemicals and solvents were of analytical grade and purchased from Beijing Solarbio Science &amp; Technology Co., Ltd. (China).</p>
<p id="p0060">Fresh perilla leaf was supplied by Jiangxi Sunshine Agricultural Technology Co., Ltd. (Jiujiang, China). For fresh leaf water extract, 40 g of leaves were homogenized with ultrapure water (1:25, <em>w</em>/<em>v</em>) and extracted under ultrasonic-assisted conditions (350 W, 55 °C, 2 h). For dried leaf water extract, an equivalent quantity of fresh leaves was dehydrated via hot-air drying (65 °C) to constant weight prior to identical extraction. Both extracts were subsequently filtered (0.45 μm) and stored at −80 °C until analysis.</p>
<p id="p0065">Fresh grass carp (2.5 ± 0.5 kg) were procured live from Baijiahui Market (Nanchang, China), which were maintained in a tank with clean water during transportation to the laboratory. Upon arrival, specimens were immediately euthanized by electrical stunning, <strong>following ethical guidelines for animal handling.</strong> Subsequently, the fish were decapitated, eviscerated, and deboned. Only dorsal muscle tissue was collected for analysis, which was uniformly sectioned into 2 cm × 2 cm × 3 cm cubes. For experimental groups, GCCs were fully immersed in either fresh or dried perilla leave extracts (4 °C, 5 h), namely FPLWE and DPLWE groups, respectively. The samples not underwent pickling treatment were defined as the Blank group, and the group marinated with deionized water was the Control group. All procedures complied with national animal welfare regulations and institutional ethical guidelines for experimental animal use.</p></section><section id="s0020"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2. Physicochemical attributes evaluation</h3>
<section id="s0025"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.1. pH and color</h4>
<p id="p0070">The pH was detected through a digital pH meter (S220, Mettler Toledo, Switzerland) after a 5 g sample homogenizing with 50 mL deionized water. Color values were determined through a portable colorimeter (CR-400, Konica minolta, Japan).</p></section><section id="s0030"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.2. Cooking loss and centrifugation loss</h4>
<p id="p0075">Cooking loss was conducted by the method of <a href="#bb0045" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0045">Fan et al. (2022)</a> with a slight modification. The initial weight of GCC was defined as W<sub>1</sub>, and then the GCC was sealed in a retort pouch and boiled at 100 °C for 5 min, then re-weighted as W<sub>2</sub>. The cooking loss was calculated as follows:</p>

<p id="p0085">Centrifugation loss was assessed according to <a href="#bb0205" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0205">Zhuang et al. (2023)</a>, the GCC wrapped in double-layer filter paper was centrifuged at 4000 r/min at 4 °C for 10 min, and the calculation of centrifugation loss was consistent with the cooking loss.</p></section><section id="s0035"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.3. Thiobarbituric acid reactants (TBA)</h4>
<p id="p0090">The determination of TBA was according to <a href="#bb0055" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0055">Hu, Jiang, Wang, Li, and Tu (2023)</a> with a slight modification. In brief, 1 g ground sample was mixed with 15 mL 7.5% trichloroacetic acid (containing 0.1% EDTA), the mixture was then centrifuged at 8000 r/min at 4 °C for 10 min. The supernatant of 3 mL was aspirated and mixed with 3 mL of 0.02 M TBA solution and then heated at 100 °C for 15 min in a water bath. The absorbance was measured at 532 nm, and the results were expressed as mg of malondialdehyde (MDA) per kg of sample.</p></section><section id="s0040"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.4. Texture properties</h4>
<p id="p0095">The texture properties of samples were analyzed through a TA-XT Plus Texture Analyzer (UK). The samples were sectioned into cubes with a slide length of 1 cm and tested with a P/36R probe. Each sample was tested with the following standardized settings: pre-test speed 1 mm/s, test speed 1 mm/s, post-test speed 1 mm/s, compression ratio (40.00%), and trigger force (5.0 g) (<a href="#bb0205" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0205">Zhuang et al., 2023</a>).</p></section></section><section id="s0045"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.3. Sensory assessment</h3>
<p id="p0100">The sensory assessment was operated by ten master assessors (six female and four male), referred to <a href="#bb0140" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0140">Shen et al. (2023)</a>. Seven attributes (metallic, grassy, mushroom, fishy, perilla, fish flavor, and comprehensive) and a ten-point interval scale (0–9 points) were used to describe the sensory profile of the sample, where 0 represented imperceptible, 1 represented weak, 5 represented significant, and 9 represented extremely strong. The study adhered strictly to pertinent laws and regulations, ensuring the complete confidentiality and privacy of all participants, and all the evaluators agreed to participate in the assessment. Furthermore, the experimental protocol received ethical clearance and was endorsed by the academic committee of the college, affirming its compliance with ethical standards.</p></section><section id="s0050"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4. Identification and quantification of flavor characteristic</h3>
<section id="s0055"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4.1. GC-IMS analysis</h4>
<p id="p0105">GC-IMS analysis by using a GC-IMS flavor device (FlavourSpec® G.A.S. Dortmund Co., Germany) following the methodology described by a previous study (<a href="#bb0075" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0075">Li et al., 2025</a>), and the detection conditions were as follows: sample of 2 g was incubated at 50 °C for 15 min, then 500 μL volatile gas derived from the sample was injected into an MXT-5 capillary column (15 m × 0.53 mm × 1 μm), and the injector temperature was set at 85 °C. The flow rate of drift gas (nitrogen gas) was 150 mL/min, with nitrogen as a carrier gas, and the program was as follow: the initial flow rate was 10 mL/ min, and then increased to 100 mL/ min for 10 min, finally stopped. The NIST database and IMS database were combined for VOC quantification.</p></section><section id="s0060"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4.2. HS-SPME-GC–MS analysis</h4>
<p id="p0110">Headspace solid-phase microextraction gas chromatography (HS-SPME-GC–MS) was employed to detect the VOCs in GCC. The determination was according to our previous study (<a href="#bb0055" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0055">Hu et al., 2023</a>), 5 g ground sample was mixed with 2 mL saturated sodium chloride solution and 10 μL 1.306 μg/mL 1,2-dichlorobenzene (added as an internal standard) in a 30 mL headspace vial. The sample was heated at 55 °C for 30 min in a water bath and then extracted using 50/30 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS SPEM fiber (Suplco, USA) for 30 min, subsequently desorbed at 250 °C for 10 min using an 8890/5977 B GC–MS instrument (Agilent Technologies Inc., USA) for analysis. A DB-WAX column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent, USA) was used for analysis and helium was used as carrier gas at a flow rate of 1 mL/min. The temperature program was set as follows: the oven temperature was held at 40 °C for 3 min, increased to 240 °C by 5 °C/min, and held for 15 min. The mass spectrometer was operated in electron ionization (EI) mode at 70 eV with a scan range of <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> 50–550. The VOCs were quantified by comparing them with the NIST20 library coupled with their retention indices (LRI). LRIs were calculated as follows:</p>

Metabolite determination</h3>
<p id="p0135">A sample of 50 mg was mixed with 400 μL extraction (methanol: water = 1:1, <em>v</em>/v, containing 0.02 M 2-chloro-<em>L</em>-phenylalanine) and then ground at 50 KHz, −10 °C for 6 min, followed by ultrasound extraction for 30 min (5 °C, 40 KHz) and then placed at −20 °C for 30 min. Subsequently, the mixture was centrifuged at 1300<em>g</em>, 4 °C for 15 min, and the supernatant was used for LC-MS analysis. LC-MS detection was operated by a Thermo Scientific UHPLC-Exploris 240 system combined with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm × 1.8 μm; Waters, (Milford, USA). The mobile phase A was 95% water and 5% acetonitrile (containing 0.1% formic acid), and the mobile phase B was 47.5% acetonitrile and 47.5% isopropanol and 5% water (containing 0.1% formic acid). A precisely measured 3 μL sample was subjected to electrospray ionization, the column temperature was 40 °C, and mass spectrometric signals were collected using both positive and negative ion scanning modes. The specific parameters were as follows: sheath gas and aux gas flow rates were 60 and 20 arb, respectively. Heater temperature for 350 °C and capillary temperature for 320 °C, spray voltage was 3400 V (ESI) or − 3000 V (ESI-), ion source temperature was 100 °C, dissolvent temperature was 500 °C, S-Lens RF Level for 70, normalized collision energy were 20%, 40%, and 60%, and scan type range was 70–1050 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em>. The original data collected were performed by Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA) for peak alignment, peak recognition, and other data handling operations. All experiments were performed in triplicate and the data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The variance and significance were analyzed by using SPSS 16.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Other data analysis and image production were accomplished by GraphPad Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, USA) and Metware Cloud platform (<a href="https://cloud.metware.cn" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://cloud.metware.cn</a>).</p></section><section id="s0075"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.6. Statistical analysis</h3>
<p id="p0140">All experimental procedures were performed in triplicate, with data expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical analyses, including ANOVA and significance testing, were conducted using SPSS 16.0 (SPSS Inc., USA), while data visualization was performed with GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). Spearman correlation analysis was performed by Origin 2024 (Origin Lab Inc., USA), and multivariate analysis (PCA and OPLS-DA) were implemented through the Metware platform (<a href="https://www.metware.cn/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.metware.cn/</a>).</p></section></section><section id="s0080"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">3. Results</h2>

Compared to the Control, both DPLWE and FPLWE showed a relatively stronger capacity to stabilize pH.</p>

Sensory evaluation analysis</h3>
<p id="p0165"><a href="#f0020" class="usa-link">Fig. 4</a>B shows the sensory evaluation analysis results of GCC marinated with PLWE. Both dried PLWE (0.75) and fresh PLWE (0.55) effectively attenuated the fishy odor compared to the Control (3.5) and Blank (4.75). All treated groups showed significantly lower metallic notes (DPLWE: 0.67; FPLWE: 1.0; Control: 1.25) than the Blank (3.25). While mushroom-like flavors were reduced in DPLWE (1.67), FPLWE (3.25) and Control (1.50) relative to Blank (4.00), PLWE treatments notably enhanced grassy notes (DPLWE: 4.33; FPLWE: 3.75 vs Control: 2.25; Blank: 3.00). Notably, FPLWE achieved the highest overall acceptability score (5.75), followed by DPLWE (5.00), both significantly surpassing the Control (3.00) and Blank (2.50), indicating fresh PLWE's superior flavor-enhancing potential for GCC. The sensory evaluation results demonstrated significant differences among treatment groups, revealing that PLWE marination substantially modified the flavor profile of GCC.</p>

Discussion</h2>
<section id="s0145"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.1. Perilla leaf water extract marination improved physicochemical properties</h3>
<p id="p0240">After harvest, certain acids in GCC might originate from glycolysis and ATP decomposition (<a href="#bb0120" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0120">Nie et al., 2022</a>), and marination treatment could remove these acidic substances, resulting in an increased pH of Control compared to Blank. However, acid compounds in PLWE might also penetrate GCC tissues, leading to significant pH differences among groups (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05), which could be verified from the pH value of the PLWE (Table S1, pH of dried PLWE was 6.17, pH of fresh PLWE was 6.29) (<a href="#bb0070" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0070">Lee et al., 2017</a>; <a href="#bb0065" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0065">Lee et al., 2017</a>). Compared to the Control group, marination with PLWE significantly increased the a*, and b* values (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05), which was closely related to the yellowish-brown color of the PLWE (Fig. S1). A similar phenomenon was observed, the a* and b* values of container-cultured snakehead treated with grape seed extract were significantly higher than those of the control group (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05). This could be attributed to the rich content of red proanthocyanidins and yellow phenolic compounds in the grape seed extract solution, which caused discoloration of the samples (<a href="#bb0085" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0085">Li et al., 2020</a>). Compared to the Control group, GCC marinated with PLWE exhibited reduced centrifugation loss, lower cooking loss, and improved texture properties. It was likely due to phenolic-protein interactions promoting the formation of a stable, dense network structure (<a href="#bb0115" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0115">Mi et al., 2017</a>). Analogously, surimi fish balls treated with 0.01% and 0.03% PLWE showed improved texture compared to the control during cold storage (<a href="#bb0195" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0195">Zhao, Kong, Zhang, Hu, &amp; Wang, 2019</a>). Similarly, the low TBARs values observed in both DPLWE and FPLWE groups were closely associated with the presence of polyphenols in PLWE. Studies have shown that polyphenols possess potent antioxidant activity, effectively inhibiting lipid oxidation in meat products (<a href="#bb0120" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0120">Nie et al., 2022</a>).</p>
<p id="p0245">Compared to the DPLWE group, the FPLWE group exhibited significantly higher total phenolic and flavonoid content, along with superior DPPH radical-scavenging capacity (a key antioxidant indicator, Table S6). These properties likely contributed to the observed increase in chewiness and the reduction in centrifugation and cooking losses in the FPLWE groups. The elevated polyphenol levels more effectively inhibited lipid oxidation, as reflected by lower TBARs values, while simultaneously promoting the formation of a denser protein network through polyphenol-protein interactions. This enhanced structural integrity better retained moisture and preserved the textural properties of GCC.</p>
<p id="p0250">Overall, GCC marinated with PLWE demonstrated significantly lower pH, centrifugation loss, cooking loss, and TBARs values, along with higher a* and b* values and improved resilience compared to the control group. Moreover, the FPLWE group demonstrated superior moisture retention ability compared to the DPLWE group. These findings underscored the potential of PLWE, particularly fresh PLWE, in enhancing the quality of GCC.</p></section><section id="s0150"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.2. Perilla leaf water extract marination enhanced the flavor characteristic of GCC</h3>
<p id="p0255">The application of PLWE as a flavor enhancer in aquatic products has been documented, particularly for its remarkable effect in reducing fishy odors. For instance, <a href="#bb0170" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0170">Wang et al. (2024)</a> found that adding 0.3% PLWE reduced off-flavors (fishy, oily/fatty, and marine notes) while enhancing freshness, herbal aromas, and overall acceptability in boiled greenfin horse-faced filefish. Flavor is a critical index for evaluating freshwater fish quality, influenced by the interaction of various volatile components. Previous studies have summarized fishy odor substances in freshwater fish (<a href="#bb0035" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0035">Dai et al., 2024</a>). In this study, hexanal, nonanal, (E)-2-octenal, (E)-2-nonenal, (E)-2-decenal, decanal, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, 1-octen-3-ol, and TMA (OAV &gt; 1) were identified as typical fishy odor compounds. Compared to the Blank and Control groups, their contents in DPLWE and FPLWE were significantly reduced (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05). <a href="#bb0040" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0040">Dang et al. (2024)</a> found that perilla juice addition inhibited lipid and protein oxidation, and suppressed the formation of warmed-over flavor compounds, including heptanal, (E)-2-octenal, (E)-2-nonenal, decanal, etc., and improved the sensory quality of surimi gels. In addition, (<em>S</em>)-(−)-perillaldehyde (imparting perilla and cinnamaldehyde notes) and linalool (with floral and woody scents) derived from PLWE (<a href="#bb0110" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0110">Meng et al., 2025</a>; <a href="#bb0170" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0170">Wang et al., 2024</a>). They might mask unpleasant odors in GCC and contribute to its distinctive flavor.</p>
<p id="p0260">In this study, the FPLWE group exhibited significantly lower concentrations of hexanal, (E)-2-decenal, and TMA than the DPLWE group. With the exception of (E)-2-octenal, most other fishy odor compounds did not differ significantly between the two groups, the net decrease in key odorants resulted in an overall lower fishy odor intensity in the FPLWE group. This reduction could be attributed to the absence of a drying step in FPLWE preparation, which better preserved heat-sensitive bioactive constituents. This enhanced preservation endowed FPLWE with a superior capacity to suppress lipid and protein oxidation relative to DPLWE. Furthermore, (<em>S</em>)-(−)-perillaldehyde and linalool were more effectively retained in FPLWE, thereby contributing to its more desirable flavor profile.</p></section><section id="s0155"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.3. Perilla leaf water extract marination regulated metabolic composition and KEGG metabolic pathway</h3>
<p id="p0265">As the majority of differential metabolites, lipids might play critical roles in flavor formation via the following mechanisms (<a href="#bb0200" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0200">Zhou et al., 2022</a>): (1) Serving as aromatic intermediates in further reactions; (2) Hydrolyzing into free fatty acids, which were then converted to aldehydes, ketones, alcohols, esters, and other VOCs through hydrolysis, autoxidation, decarboxylation, and dehydration. Lipids and lipid-like molecules could participate in fatty acid oxidation, degradation, and glycerophospholipid metabolism, which promoted flavor compound generation (<a href="#bb0190" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0190">Zhang, Li, Nie, Wu, &amp; Huang, 2025</a>). However, metabolites accumulated abundantly from these pathways might also be associated with aquatic product spoilage (<a href="#bb0175" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0175">Wang et al., 2025</a>). GCC, being rich in fatty acids, might undergo profound changes in lipid metabolism upon PLWE marination, thereby influencing VOC formation. It was consistent with a finding from Liang et al. (<a href="#bb0090" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0090">Liang et al., 2025</a>), where rosemary extract inhibited phospholipid degradation and reduced off-flavor production in pork snacks. Compared with the Blank and Control groups, a large number of lipids and lipid-like molecules were found to be simultaneously down-regulated in the FPLWE group, including Lpe(20:0), (8<em>Z</em>,11<em>Z</em>)-henicosa-8,11-dienoylcarnitine, fasciculol C, taurodeoxycholic acid 3-glucuronide, ricinoleic acid, cortisol, Lpe(18:3), platelet-activating factor, Lpe(18:1), Lpe(22:5), Lpe(18:2), Lpe(22:6), Lpe(16:1), Lpa(18:2), ganolucidic acid A. The VIP of these metabolites were all higher than 1.5. In addition, avocadene, 12-methyltridecanoic acid, androsta-1,4,6-triene-3,17-dione, 3-hydroxypropanoyl-coa, and other metabolites were also identified as significantly down-regulated lipids and lipid-like molecules in the FPLWE. On the contrary, 10-acetoxyligustroside, quercilicoside A, corchoionol C 9-glucoside, quercetin 7-glucuronide, 3-rhamnoside, 20, 22-dihydrodigoxigenin, ciprostene, 10-hdohe, Pe-nme2(9D5/9D3), Pe(22:5/22:6), Gpetn(22:2/18:4), and other lipids and lipid-like molecules were up-regulated significantly in FPLWE. As the primary precursors for flavor components, lipid metabolism exhibited high activity in this study. These findings suggested that PLWE might modulate lipid metabolic pathways, thereby significantly influencing the formation of flavor substances in GCC. As important precursor lipids for flavor substance formation, the contents of various triglycerides, such as TG(16:0/18:1/20:4), TG(15:0/18:1/20:5), TG(18:1/18:2/20:3), in FPLWE were lower than those in DPLWE, which might have reduced the production of certain fishy odor compounds.</p>
<p id="p0270">Phenylpropanoids, polyketides, and benzenoids might originate from phenylalanine metabolism. In this study, FPLWE showed up-regulated levels of these metabolites compared to the Blank group. It was potentially contributing to floral and fruity aromas in GCC. PLWE showed significantly higher contents of phenylpropanoids, polyketides, and benzenoids than both the control and blank groups, with the FPLWE group displaying a further increase relative to DPLWE.</p>
<p id="p0275">Organic acids and their derivatives were another category of key differential metabolites in this study, with amino acids (AAs), peptides, and analogues constituting the majority. These compounds served as important precursors of VOCs. <a href="#bb0015" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0015">Chen et al. (2023)</a> used integrated volatolomics and metabolomics to analyze Chouguiyu (a traditional fermented mandarin fish) and found that umami amino acids contributed to its characteristic taste, while tryptophan metabolism was a key pathway for flavor compound formation. Alanine, aspartate, and glutamate metabolism typically play a crucial role in flavor formation in meat products. Notably, these AAs were also important flavor-active components: aspartate and glutamate enhance freshness, while alanine was associated with sweetness (<a href="#bb0025" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0025">Cheng, Mei, &amp; Xie, 2023</a>). Additionally, AAs, peptides, and analogues were more prone to down-regulation than up-regulation, suggesting that PLWE marination might inhibit protein oxidation and degradation into these metabolites. AAs also serve as key precursors for flavor substance formation. In the present study, numerous AAs, peptides, and analogues exhibited significant down-regulation, containing N-lauroylsarcosine, hexanoylglycine, L-aspartic acid, leucylproline, myristoylglycine, leucylproline etc. In contrast, up-regulated metabolites contained only a minor proportion of these compounds. While creatine was found to be down-regulated significantly. These findings suggested that PLWE may inhibit the synthesis of specific GCC flavor compounds by suppressing AA metabolism.</p>
<p id="p0280">In this study, organic oxygen compound metabolism was highly active, predominantly exhibiting an upward trend. Notably, compounds such as <em>L</em>-tartaric acid, protocatechuic aldehyde, and Nnal-N-glucuronide displayed VIP values exceeding 1.4. Among the up-regulated organic oxygen compounds, carbohydrates and carbohydrate conjugates constituted the majority, including key glycometabolism intermediates like succinic acid, galactaric acid, L-malic acid, fumaric acid, and <span class="font-variant-small-caps">d</span>-glucose 6-phosphate. These results suggested that PLWE marination might enhance glycometabolism activity.</p>
<p id="p0285">As key flavor-active components, nucleotides influenced odor development through interactions with reducing sugars or synergized with umami AAs to enhance umami. For example, 5′-nucleotides (e.g., 5′-guanosine monophosphate, 5′-adenosine monophosphate, and 5′-inosine monophosphate) have been shown to interact with AAs, modulating flavor and significantly contributing to freshness (<a href="#bb0100" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0100">Manninen, Pukkila, Aisala, Hopia, &amp; Laaksonen, 2018</a>).</p>
<p id="p0290">The TCA cycle serves as the central hub for the metabolism of carbohydrates, fats, and AAs, playing a crucial role in the metabolic pathways of meat products. First, the citric acid cycle generates oxaloacetate, which acts as a precursor for the gluconeogenesis pathway (converted to phosphoenolpyruvate by phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase). Phosphoenolpyruvate was then transformed to pyruvate via phosphoenolpyruvate carboxylase, followed by conversion to lactic acid through lactate dehydrogenase catalysis. In this study, glucose was converted to pyruvate and acetyl-CoA via glycolysis, with acetyl-CoA entering the TCA cycle to produce organic acids. The accumulation of organic acids lowered the pH, inhibiting harmful microorganism growth and enhancing flavor (<a href="#bb0080" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0080">Li et al., 2024</a>). Pyruvate metabolism has been identified as the primary pathway for generating organic acids (e.g., malic, fumaric, and succinic acids) (<a href="#bb0020" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0020">Chen et al., 2025</a>).</p>
<p id="p0295">Among the key pathways, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism was the most likely initial and core regulatory target. However, future research still requires further validation through targeted metabolomics techniques, such as performing absolute quantification of key intermediate metabolites (e.g., glutamate, aspartate, alanine) within the proposed core pathway to directly confirm PLWE's regulatory effect on their levels. Additionally, molecular biology techniques can serve as a complementary means for cross-validation, for instance, by measuring the activity of key rate-limiting enzymes (e.g., glutamate dehydrogenase, transaminases) or using qPCR to detect changes in their gene expression levels. This will provide direct evidence for the altered metabolic flux at the enzymatic and transcriptional levels.</p></section><section id="s0160"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.4. The potential mechanism of perilla leaf water extract marination on the metabolic pathway between differential metabolites and key differential VOCs of GCC</h3>
<p id="p0300">Based on the KEGG database and prior literature, the potential impact of PLWE marination on the metabolic pathways linking key differential metabolites to key differential VOCs in GCC is illustrated in <a href="#f0040" class="usa-link">Fig. 8</a>. Unsaturated fatty acids (e.g., stearidonic acid, palmitoleic acid, and oleic acid) underwent β-oxidation to form hydroperoxides, and then be converted into some aldehydes, such as nonanal, decanal, hexanal, (E)-2-octenal, (E)-2-decenal, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, (E,Z)-2,4-decadienal, under the action of hydroperoxidelase (<a href="#bb0060" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0060">Jia et al., 2025</a>; <a href="#bb0150" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0150">Tang &amp; Peng, 2024</a>; <a href="#bb0165" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0165">Wang, Dai, et al., 2023</a>; <a href="#bb0160" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0160">Wang, Wang, et al., 2023</a>). In addition, protein could initially degrade to branched-chain AAs such as leucine, isoleucine, and valine via the action of proteases and peptidases, which was further metabolized to corresponding α-keto acids under aminotransferases. These α-keto acids were then converted to hexanal, nonanal, and decanal under the decarboxylase. Through dehydrogenase-mediated reactions, certain aldehydes were converted to alcohols (e.g., 1-hexanol and 1-heptanol) (<a href="#bb0060" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0060">Jia et al., 2025</a>). Saturated fatty acids underwent conversion to keto acyl-CoA through β-oxidation, followed by transformation to β-keto acids and subsequent metabolism to 2-undecanone (<a href="#bb0060" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0060">Jia et al., 2025</a>). γ-Decalactone was derived from ricinoleic acid degradation (<a href="#bb0145" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0145">Singh et al., 2023</a>). Ricinoleic acid, a significantly down-regulated fatty acid in FPLWE, exhibited a VIP value exceeding 1.5 compared to both the Control and Blank groups. This observation might be attributed to the low concentration of γ-decalactone in FPLWE. Pyruvate could be converted into AAs through multiple reactions, thereby generating VOCs such as aldehydes. On the other side, pyruvate could be converted into acetyl CoA through pyruvate metabolism, and then transformed into fatty acids through multiple reactions, further producing aldehydes and other volatile components (<a href="#bb0190" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0190">Zhang et al., 2025</a>). Additionally, creatine and choline could be converted to trimethylamine N-oxide through the creatine pathway and glycine metabolism, respectively. Trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) then be catalyzed by TMAO reductase with H<sub>2</sub>O and NADP to produce TMA (<a href="#bb0015" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0015">Chen et al., 2023</a>). Therefore, PLWE might suppress lipid metabolism, AA metabolism, and creatine/choline metabolism, thereby reducing fishy odor.</p>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="figure/f0040/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div>
<figcaption><p>The potential effect of PLWE marination on the metabolic pathway between key differential VOCs and metabolites.</p></figcaption></figure><p id="p0305">Based on the comparative analysis of common marination agents in aquatic products, each category presents distinct advantages and disadvantages in modifying physicochemical properties and flavors (Table S7). Salt remains fundamental for preservation and texture enhancement but poses health risks and can suppress or mask the inherent flavor profile of fish at high concentrations. Sugar effectively balances salinity and improves color, yet its overuse can lead to textural softening and health concerns. While spices contribute to deodorization and diverse flavor profiles, they may overpower the natural taste of aquatic products. Fermentation agents generate unique flavors but require precise process control. Furthermore, chemical additives like phosphates and nitrites excel in texture modification and color fixation, yet face consumer skepticism due to potential health risks.</p>
<p id="p0310">In this context, FPLWE emerges as a promising natural alternative. Its key advantages include effective deodorization by targeting fishy aldehydes, natural antimicrobial and antioxidant properties that extend shelf life, and the imposition of a unique herbal aroma without significantly altering the product's color. Moreover, using fresh leaves avoids the drying step, thereby lowering production costs and better preserving compounds that are sensitive to heat. However, its limitations must also be considered. The distinct herbal flavor may not align with all consumer preferences, and improper application can introduce slight bitterness. Furthermore, its efficacy is highly dependent on optimized processing parameters such as concentration, temperature, and immersion time, requiring more precise control than traditional curing agents. It is also critical to note that storage stability and processing stability (during marination) of the extract are crucial determinants of its practical value. Multiple studies (<a href="#bb0105" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0105">Marpaung, Andarwulan, Hariyadi, &amp; Faridah, 2017</a>; <a href="#bb0135" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0135">Rashid et al., 2021</a>) have confirmed that storing active component extracts in the dark at an appropriate pH under refrigeration or freezing conditions can maximize their stability. Therefore, establishing defined storage and handling protocols is essential to maintain the stability of these active component extracts. The current study is limited to laboratory-scale results, while research on the efficiency and stability during scaled-up production remains insufficient and will be explored in future work.</p></section></section><section id="s0165"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">5. Conclusion</h2>
<p id="p0315">In this study, marination with PLWE significantly reduced centrifugation loss, cooking loss, and TBARs values, while maintaining the stability of pH, a* and b* values, and resilience compared to the Control group. Furthermore, compared to the DPLWE treatment, the FPLWE treatment demonstrated higher contents of polyphenols and flavonoids, enhanced antioxidant capacity, and superior water-holding capacity. A total of 91 and 37 VOCs were detected by GC–MS and GC-IMS, respectively. Key differential VOCs were identified via OAV analysis included hexanal, nonanal, (E)-2-octenal, decanal, (E)-2-decenal, (E,Z)-2,4-decadienal, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, (<em>S</em>)-(−)-perillaldehyde, 1-hexanol, linalool, 1-heptanol, 2-undecanone, γ-decalactone, and TMA. Compared to the Control group, the PLWE treatment significantly reduced the content of fishy compounds in GCC, including hexanal, nonanal, (E)-2-octenal, (E)-2-nonenal, (E)-2-decenal, decanal, (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, 1-octen-3-ol, and TMA. In addition, the PLWE treatment introduced higher levels of (<em>S</em>)-(−)-perillaldehyde and linalool, imparting a unique aroma to GCC. Even when compared to the DPLWE treatment, the FPLWE treatment still significantly reduced the content of hexanal, (E)-2-decenal, and TMA, demonstrating a stronger ability to mitigate fishy odors. Furthermore, compared to DPLWE, FPLWE does not require a drying process, thereby reducing production costs and maximizing the preservation of heat-sensitive active components, making it more valuable for large-scale industrial development. Untargeted metabolomics analysis identified differential metabolites: lipids and lipid-like molecules, organic acids and derivatives, organic oxygen compounds, nucleosides, nucleotides, and analogues, phenylpropanoids and polyketides, and benzenoids. Key differential metabolic pathways included alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, pyruvate metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, TCA cycle, and propanoate metabolism. Results showed that PLWE might suppress fishy odor by inhibiting lipid, AA, and creatine/choline metabolism, providing new insights and a scientific basis for quality improvement in freshwater fish RTE products.</p></section><section id="s0170"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">CRediT authorship contribution statement</h2>
