sample_description: The sample consisted of a cohort of 28 patients diagnosed with Parkinson's disease (PD), recruited from the FallRiskPD study (DRKS-ID: DRKS00015085). Inclusion criteria required: (1) a confirmed diagnosis of Parkinson’s syndrome according to the German Society for Neurology guidelines; (2) Hoehn and Yahr staging I–III; (3) ability to walk 4×10 meters without physical support; (4) predominantly ambulatory status without reliance on walking aids in daily life (none of the 28 participants used walking aids); and (5) sufficient cognitive and visual capacity to independently read, understand, and operate a wearable sensor-based gait analysis system during a two-week unsupervised free-living phase. Exclusion criteria included: (1) maximum walking distance <100 meters; (2) decompensated cardiopulmonary limitations; and (3) other severe musculoskeletal disorders significantly impairing mobility or walking ability. Patient characteristics (N=28) were as follows: gender distribution 7 female / 21 male; mean age 63.7 ± 7.2 years; mean height 174.6 ± 8.8 cm; mean weight 77.1 ± 15.3 kg; mean UPDRS-III score 17.0 ± 8.1; mean Hoehn & Yahr stage 2.5 ± 0.6. The study was approved by the local ethics committee (Re-No. 165_18B, Friedrich-Alexander-University Erlangen-Nuremberg, Germany), and all participants provided written, informed consent prior to data collection. Data collection comprised two subsets: an 'at-lab dataset' (explicitly named but not further described in the provided text) and an unsupervised free-living dataset collected over two weeks using wearable sensors.

sample_description: The study utilized a diverse collection of fungal strains, including standard reference strains and clinical isolates. Standard strains comprised 24 species across multiple genera: Aspergillus (A. flavus, A. fumigatus, A. terreus), Candida (C. albicans, C. dubliniensis, C. glabrata, C. orthopsilosis, C. parapsilosis, C. tropicalis, C. zeylanoides), Clavispora lusitaniae, Cryptococcus (C. gattii, C. neoformans), Debaryomyces hansenii, Meyerozyma guilliermondii, Pichia kudriavzevii, Rhodotorula mucilaginosa, and Talaromyces marneffei. Strains were sourced from multiple repositories: Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (CAMS), Sun Yat-Sen Memorial Hospital (SYMH), Agricultural Research Service (ARS/NRRL), Medstar Washington Hospital Center (MWHC), Yeast Genetics Stock Center (YGSC), and ATCC. Clinical isolates included oral and vaginal yeast isolates from HIV-positive and HIV-negative women, previously described in reference [22]. Species identity for all strains was verified by partial sequencing of the ITS or 25S rDNA region. For DNA extraction, yeast strains were cultured in 2 mL YPD medium (1% yeast extract, 2% Bacto Peptone, 2% glucose) for ~24 h at 28°C; molds were cultured for 48–72 h. Yeast cells were pelleted by centrifugation; mycelia were collected using Spin-X centrifuge tube filters. Cells were washed with 1 mL H₂O, suspended in 1 mL lysis buffer (500 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 50 mM EDTA, 4% SDS), and processed via the 'repeated bead beating plus column' method (Yu & Morrison [23]), with genomic DNA purified using the Isolate II Genomic DNA Kit (Bioline). DNA concentration was quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. Additionally, for high-throughput HRM analysis, crude DNA preparations were generated from clinical isolates and standard strains: isolates were grown in 150 μL YPD in 96-well plates for 24 h at 28°C, pelleted, washed twice with 100 μL 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.5), resuspended in 100 μL Tris buffer, subjected to thermal cycling (95°C for 15 min, −80°C freeze for 15 min, then room-temperature thaw), and centrifuged to collect supernatant containing crude DNA.
