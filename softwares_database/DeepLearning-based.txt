sample_description: The study used a dataset of 38,472 distinct chemical compounds with reference MS/MS spectra, sourced from NIST20, MSDIAL, GNPS, and Agilent METLIN databases. Compounds were restricted to [M+H]+ adducts (positive ionization) and [M−H]− adducts (negative ionization). SMILES strings were retrieved via PubChem API (for NIST20 and METLIN entries with missing structural identifiers) and RDKit (to standardize or infer missing molecular representations); stereochemistry was ignored by truncating InChIKeys to their first 14 characters. Spectra were preprocessed to remove peaks >0.5 Da above precursor mass, filter fragments below 1% relative intensity, bin m/z values at 0.01 Da resolution up to m/z 700 (yielding 70,000-bin vectors), and binarize intensities. Two spectral input formats were used: (i) 'Individual spectra' — 527,236 spectra (80% train, 10% val, 10% test), each corresponding to a single collision energy acquisition per compound; and (ii) 'Merged spectra' — 30,775 merged spectra (80% train, 10% val, 10% test), generated by taking the union of fragment bins across all available collision energies per compound, plus calculated neutral losses (mapped to same 0.01 Da bins and concatenated), then binarized.
software_configuration_description: A custom convolutional neural network (CNN) implemented in PyTorch was trained on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU to map binned, binarized MS/MS spectra (70,000-dimensional input vectors) to 300-dimensional Mol2vec embeddings. The architecture consisted of six convolutional layers followed by six max-pooling layers, then a flattening layer and two fully connected layers, with the final layer outputting the 300-D Mol2vec embedding. Mol2vec embeddings were precomputed using the original Mol2vec model (300-D, skip-gram architecture trained on ChEMBL molecular graphs) and served as fixed, continuous structural targets during training. Input spectra were either individual collision-energy spectra or merged spectra incorporating neutral losses.
