========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: MolNetEnhancer
sample_description: The study used 10 sponge samples (3–5 mm³ each) of two marine sponge species: Aplysina caissara Pinheiro and Hajdu, 2001 and Aplysina fulva Pallas, 1766 (Verongiida, Aplysinidae), collected by scuba diving in July and October 2018 at two locations in São Sebastião, São Paulo, Brazil—Ponta Recife (23°49’30.36”S 45°24’47.879”W) and southern rock shores of Praia do Guaecá (23°49’22.8”S 45°28’19.2”W)—at depths of 3–8 m. Samples were placed in sterile plastic bags with natural seawater, kept in a cooler during transport, and processed immediately upon arrival at the laboratory. For bacterial isolation, 2.5 g of choanosome tissue from each sponge was macerated in 25 mL calcium/magnesium-free artificial seawater (ASW); supernatants were serially diluted (10⁻² to 10⁻⁶) and plated on multiple media: enriched (BHI), oligotrophic (MB, R2A), and selective (AIA for Actinobacteriota; SNAX for Cyanobacteria). Pure isolates were grown in corresponding broths (BHI, R2A, MB, or selective formulations) for 18 h at room temperature (20–25 °C, 200 rpm), then stored in 20% glycerol at −80 °C. Genomic DNA was extracted using the Wizard Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega), and the 16S rRNA gene was amplified using primers 27F-1492R and sequenced via Sanger sequencing on an ABI 3500. Additionally, metabarcoding data from 15 previously sequenced sponge samples (10 A. caissara and 5 A. fulva; Bioproject IDs PRJNA665805 and PRJNA673577) were re-analyzed for comparative ASV-based community profiling. For secondary metabolite analysis, bacterial strains positive for type I/II PKS or NRPS genes were cultured on solid BHI and MB media, harvested at stationary phase (~72 h), and extracted sequentially with ethyl acetate (30 min ultrasonication) and methanol; extracts were dried via rotary evaporation and vacuum concentration. Crude extracts (5.0 mg) were cleaned up using C18 solid-phase extraction cartridges (Chromabond, 45 μm, 500.0 mg, 6.0 mL), pre-activated with 3.0 mL MeOH and equilibrated with 3.0 mL MeOH/H₂O (8:2, v/v), then eluted with 1.5 mL of the same solvent mixture and reconstituted in MeOH to 2.0 mg/mL for LC-MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: LC-MS/MS raw data files were first converted to mzML format using MSConvert (ProteoWizard) with Peak Picking filter and vendor-checked algorithm. Data were uploaded to the Global Natural Products Social (GNPS) platform (https://gnps.ucsd.edu) and processed through the online molecular networking workflow. Key parameters included: removal of all MS/MS fragment ions within ±17 Da of the precursor m/z; window filtering selecting only the top six fragment ions within a ±50 Da window; precursor ion mass tolerance = 0.02 Da; MS/MS fragment ion tolerance = 0.05 Da; cosine score threshold ≥0.7; minimum matched peaks = 4; maximum molecular family size = 100 (with low-scoring edges pruned until family size <100). Spectral library matching against GNPS libraries applied identical filtering criteria (score >0.7, ≥4 matched peaks). For unannotated signals, manual annotation was performed using exact mass-derived chemical formulas and cross-referencing with Dictionary of Natural Products, The Natural Products Atlas, and literature. MS2LDA (http://ms2lda.org/) and NAP workflows were run in parallel. Dereplicator was executed with the following settings: precursor ion mass tolerance = 0.02 Da, MS/MS fragment ion tolerance = 0.05 Da, VarQuest algorithm, PNP database, max charge = 2, min AA = 5, max modification mass = 150 Da, min matched peaks = 5. ClassyFire (https://classyfire.wishartlab.com) was used for chemical ontology-based class annotations. All networks were visualized using Cytoscape v.3.8.2. Analytical blanks (MeOH only) were analyzed every 10 samples and their features removed from the final feature table.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: MolNetEnhancer
sample_description: The study used four bacterial isolates belonging to the Bacillus genus: B. licheniformis M017, B. licheniformis 1001, B. amyloliquefaciens, and B. laterosporus; and a commercial microbial consortium named Bacstim® 100, containing equal volumes of the above four Bacillus strains. Bacterial cultures were grown in Lysogeny broth (LB) medium at 28°C and 140 rpm for 36 h under sterile conditions. Growth was monitored via OD600 measurements every 3 h, and cells were harvested at four defined time points corresponding to lag phase (3 h), exponential phase (7.5 h), and two stationary phase time points (24 h and 31.5 h). Harvesting involved centrifugation at 5,000 rpm for 15 min at 28°C; supernatants were decanted and pellets (wet weight measured) were stored at −80°C. Intracellular metabolites were extracted from pellets using 100% LC-MS grade methanol (15 mg pellet per 320 μL solvent), followed by vortexing, probe sonication at 55% amplitude for 1 min, and centrifugation at 3,700 rpm for 30 min at 28°C. Supernatants were filtered through 0.22 μm nylon filters into pre-labeled HPLC glass vials with 500 μL inserts.
complete_workflow_description: The process procedure involved multiple integrated bioinformatics and cheminformatics software tools. First, raw .raw Waters mass spectrometry data files were converted to ABF format using Reify's ABF converter software. Then, ABF files were processed in MS-DIAL (version not specified) with the following parameters: MS1 and MS2 mass accuracy tolerance = 0.05 Da, minimum peak height = 10 amplitude, mass slice width = 0.1 Da, sigma window = 0.5, retention time tolerance = 0.2 min. Peak features were exported as GNPS-compatible MGF files and feature quantification tables. These were uploaded to GNPS via WinSCP server. Feature-based molecular networks (FBMN) were generated using GNPS workflow with precursor and fragment ion mass tolerance = 0.5 Da, cosine score threshold ≥ 0.6, and minimum matched fragment ions = 4. Molecular networks were enhanced using MolNetEnhancer, which orchestrated MS2LDA (via ms2lda.org interface), DEREPLICATOR, and NAP. Manual validation involved molecular formula calculation using MassLynx software, database searching (ChemSpider, KEGG), fragmentation pattern inspection (MS1 and MSE spectra), and literature comparison. Metabolite annotations followed Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) levels 2 and 3. Visualization of networks was performed using Cytoscape (version 3.8.2).

========== paper_workflow entry 3 ==========
related_tool: MolNetEnhancer
sample_description: The study used dried blood spot (DBS) samples collected from newborns aged 3–10 days (before 2009), blotted onto Ahlstrom #226 filter paper and air-dried for at least 3 hours at room temperature before being mailed at ambient temperature to the Department of Congenital Disorders at Statens Serum Institut in Copenhagen. After use in the Danish newborn screening program, the DBS samples were biobanked in the Danish National Biobank (www.nationalbiobank.dk) at −20 °C until retrieval for research. The study included 37 children diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder (ASD; ICD-10 codes F84.0, F84.1, F84.5, F84.8, F84.9) born in 2005 and identified via the iPSYCH case-cohort sample, along with 37 gender- and birth-date-matched controls without psychiatric diagnoses. Additional metadata included gestational age, birth weight, age at sampling, month of birth, mother’s age at birth, and date of diagnosis, all extracted from iPSYCH and the Danish newborn screening database.
complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow comprised multiple software tools and configurable steps: (1) Raw LC-MS/MS data (.raw files) were converted to centroided .mzML using MSConvertGUI v3.0 (ProteoWizard) with default settings. (2) Preprocessing was performed in MZmine v2.41 using a batch.xml configuration (Online Resource 2): data cropping to RT 0.27–9.80 min; MS1 peak detection with noise threshold 10,000; ADAP-based chromatogram building (min 7 scans/peak, group intensity threshold 10,000, min highest intensity 150,000, m/z tolerance 0.001 Da or 5 ppm); Wavelets (ADAP) deconvolution (m/z center by median, MS2 pairing ranges: 0.01 Da and 0.4 min); isotope grouping (m/z tolerance 0.001 Da or 5 ppm, RT tolerance 0.1 min); peak alignment (m/z tolerance 0.001 Da or 5 ppm, RT tolerance 0.1 min, 75% m/z / 25% RT weighting); and peak filtering (min 15 consecutive peaks, same RT/peak duration constraints). Output: feature quantification table (.csv) and aggregated MS2 mass list (.mgf). (3) Feature-based molecular networking was conducted on GNPS using the .mgf file with parameters: precursor m/z tolerance 0.02 Da, MS/MS fragment ion tolerance 0.02 Da, cosine score threshold ≥0.7, minimum 4 matched peaks, mutual top-10 node similarity enforcement, max molecular family size 100, and edge pruning to meet size constraint. Spectral library matching against GNPS libraries required score >0.7 and ≥4 matched peaks. (4) Compound Discoverer 2.1 SP1 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used in parallel for complementary annotation, with settings detailed in Online Resource 3; annotations were accepted at ≥80% confidence (level 2, putative) per mzCloud spectral library. (5) MASST and SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID were applied for additional structural elucidation when GNPS/Compound Discoverer yielded no hits: MASST queried single MS/MS spectra across all public GNPS datasets; SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID employed deep learning to predict molecular formula and structure from MS/MS spectra. (6) Contamination filtering used a Kendrick Mass Filter (da Silva et al. 2019) in Python/Jupyter (parameters: number of observed signals = 5, Kendrick mass defect = 0.01, fraction base = 1) to remove PEG and PPG-related features. (7) Statistical analysis used R v4.0.3 with packages ggplot2, ggfortify, ggsci, rlang, viridis, vegan (for PERMANOVA), and MetaboAnalystR3.0: missing values imputed as half the minimum positive value; glog transformation and Pareto scaling applied; fold change threshold = 2; t-tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with FDR correction; PCA including pooled samples; all scripts publicly available on GitHub.

complete_workflow_description: The MolNetEnhancer workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data processed either (i) directly into consensus MS2 spectra using MS-Cluster, or (ii) via MZmine2 for MS1 feature detection and MS2 spectrum association. These spectra are uploaded to GNPS for molecular networking and optional in silico annotation (e.g., NAP or DEREPLICATOR). Separately, the same MS2 spectra are analyzed with MS2LDA to extract Mass2Motifs. All resulting outputs — GNPS molecular network (in .graphml or compatible format), MS2LDA motif table (.csv/.tsv), and in silico annotation results (e.g., structure candidates with scores) — are fed into MolNetEnhancer. MolNetEnhancer then: (a) overlays Mass2Motif presence on network nodes as pie charts using overlap scores; (b) adds inter-node edges for shared motifs; (c) submits all candidate structures from GNPS and in silico tools to ClassyFire to obtain five-level chemical taxonomy annotations; and (d) computes per-family chemical class consistency scores at each taxonomic level. Final enhanced networks are exported in GraphML format for visualization in Cytoscape. Interactive Jupyter notebooks and step-by-step tutorials accompany the GitHub repositories.
source_paper: Madeleine et al. (2019), Metabolites, DOI: 10.3390/metabo9070144, PMID: 31315242

complete_workflow_description: The global workflow described in the paper is an end-to-end integrative bioinformatics pipeline for enhanced molecular networking. It starts from raw LC-MS/MS data and proceeds through three parallel preprocessing tracks: (1) GNPS-based molecular networking (with optional NAP or DEREPLICATOR annotation); (2) MS2LDA-based unsupervised substructure mining; and (3) submission of candidate structures (from GNPS library matching and/or in silico tools like SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID) to ClassyFire for automated chemical classification. MolNetEnhancer serves as the integration layer that fuses these outputs: it maps Mass2Motifs onto network nodes with quantitative overlap scoring, creates motif-sharing edges, computes hierarchical chemical class consensus scores per molecular family (based on ClassyFire annotations), and exports enriched networks in GraphML format. The workflow supports two upstream data preprocessing routes — MS-Cluster–based consensus spectra or MZmine2–based feature-centric spectra — and is fully documented via GitHub repositories, Jupyter notebooks, and tutorial guides. This constitutes a reproducible, modular, and tool-agnostic template for integrating orthogonal structural insights in untargeted metabolomics.

related_tool: MolNetEnhancer
sample_description: Ten adult individuals of Stichopus cf. horrens (approximately 1 kg each) were obtained from aquaculture at the Bolinao Marine Laboratory, University of the Philippines Marine Science Institute. Samples were euthanized by freezing, transported on ice, and stored at -80°C. Body wall (50 g per individual) and viscera (whole organ per individual) were separately extracted with absolute ethanol (1:20 g/mL) for 48 hours. Extracts were filtered, dried in vacuo, and resuspended in absolute ethanol to 10 mg/mL for LC-MS/MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow began with sample collection (10 cultured S. cf. horrens individuals), followed by anatomical dissection into body wall and viscera, and separate ethanol extraction (1:20 w/v, 48 h). Extracts were dried and resuspended to 10 mg/mL. LC-MS/MS analysis was performed on a Waters Acquity UPLC coupled to a Xevo G2-XS QTOF mass spectrometer using a BEH Fluoro-Phenyl column and gradient elution (5–100% acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid) in both positive and negative ion modes. Data-dependent acquisition (DDA) was applied with intensity threshold of 3.0 × 10⁵ counts and top-6 most intense precursors per scan; CID energies ranged from 15–60 eV (for <1 kDa) or 60–90 eV (for ≥1 kDa). Raw .RAW files were converted to mzXML using ProteoWizard’s MSConvert. Preprocessing was performed in MZmine 2.52 (deisotoping, deconvolution, gap-filling; parameters in S9 Table), followed by export of .csv and .mgf files. These were uploaded to GNPS for Feature-Based Molecular Networking (FBMN), using precursor and product ion tolerances of m/z 0.02 and m/z 0.05, cosine similarity threshold ≥0.70, and ion correlation thresholds (Pearson shape/height) of 85% and 50%. Ion-identity networks collapsed adducts; merged polarity networks combined positive/negative mode results. Library search against GNPS public spectral libraries used same mass tolerances and required ≥8 matched peaks and cosine score >0.70. MS2LDA was run alongside MolNetEnhancer to extract Mass2Motifs with overlap and probability thresholds >0.30; motif annotations were grounded in diagnostic ions and literature-supported fragmentation pathways (e.g., m/z 168.044 for phosphocholine, neutral loss of 60 Da, m/z 152.996 for phosphoglycerol anion). Final visualization used Cytoscape 3.8.2.
source_paper: M et al. (2023), PloS one, DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0294535, PMID: 38055702
