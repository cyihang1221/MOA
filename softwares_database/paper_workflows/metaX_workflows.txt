complete_workflow_description: The untargeted metabolomics workflow consisted of the following key steps: (1) Sample preparation: thawing at 4 °C, extraction, centrifugation, and supernatant collection; (2) Instrumental analysis: LC–MS/MS using a Thermo Fisher Q Exactive HF high-resolution mass spectrometer acquiring data in both positive and negative ion modes; (3) Primary data processing: Compound Discoverer 3.1 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used for peak extraction, retention time correction (within and between groups), combined ion merging, missing value filling, background peak labeling, and data quality control; (4) Metabolite identification and annotation: molecular weight, retention time, and peak area data were matched against KEGG, BGI Library, ChemSpider, LipidMaps, and HMDB databases; (5) Statistical and multivariate analysis: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) were performed to assess group separation and identify differentially expressed metabolites; VIP > 1 and p < 0.05 (Student’s t-test) were used as significance thresholds; (6) Secondary bioinformatic analysis: MetaboAnalyst 5.0 (online platform, https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/home.xhtml) was employed for volcano plot generation, hierarchical clustering heatmaps (top 25 differential metabolites), pathway enrichment analysis (using KEGG with impact value > 0.5 and p < 0.05), and ROC curve construction with AUC calculation; (7) Quality control: assessed via QC sample reproducibility—including base peak chromatogram (BPC) overlap in both ion modes, PCA of QC samples, and metabolite count statistics (e.g., % of features with RSD ≤ 30% across QCs, reported as 90.94% in positive mode and 97.6% in negative mode).

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: metaX
sample_description: Peripheral blood specimens from 22 sepsis patients and 10 healthy human volunteers were collected at the Emergency Intensive Care Unit (EICU) of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University between January and December 2019. Each sample was 5 mL, processed and stored at −80 °C in a biospecimen bank. All sepsis patients met the Sepsis-3.0 diagnostic criteria (SCCM/ESICM, 2016), were aged 16–75 years, and had no prior organ failure, immune disorders, hematologic disorders, or refusal to consent. Healthy controls were age-matched volunteers with no known acute or chronic disease.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow began with collection of peripheral blood samples from 22 sepsis patients and 10 healthy controls, followed by LC–MS/MS analysis on a Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer in both positive and negative ion modes. Raw data were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.1 for peak detection, alignment, and initial compound identification. Subsequent data preprocessing, multivariate statistical analysis (PCA and OPLS-DA), and differential metabolite selection were performed using the R package metaX and the web-based platform MetaboAnalyst 5.0. Differential metabolites were identified based on combined criteria: VIP > 1.0 from OPLS-DA (first two components), |fold change| > 1.2, and Student’s t-test p-value < 0.05. Volcano plots and heatmaps visualized differential expression. Identified metabolites were subjected to KEGG pathway enrichment analysis for functional interpretation. Finally, ROC curve analysis was conducted to evaluate diagnostic performance of top candidate metabolites for sepsis classification.
source_paper: Yang et al. (2023), Scientific reports, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-24878-z, PMID: 36631483

related_tool: metaX
sample_description: Murine cervical cancer U14 cells were cultured in DMEM high glucose medium with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, harvested during logarithmic growth phase (48–72 h post-seeding), and divided into two groups: melittin-treated (T) and vehicle control (C), with five biological replicates per group. Intracellular metabolites were extracted using an organic solvent precipitation method. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots from all experimental samples and injected before, after, and interspersed throughout the LC-MS run to monitor analytical stability.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomic workflow described in the paper begins with murine U14 cervical cancer cells cultured under standardized conditions and split into melittin-treated and vehicle control groups (n=5 per group). Cells are harvested in logarithmic phase, followed by metabolite extraction via organic solvent precipitation. QC samples are generated by pooling all samples and run interspersed in the LC-MS sequence. Untargeted LC-MS profiling is performed in both positive and negative ion modes. Raw MS data undergo peak picking, alignment, and signal correction using XCMS. Subsequent data preprocessing includes quality control, low-quality signal filtering, median normalization, and missing value imputation using metaX. Metabolite annotation is conducted by matching accurate m/z values and MS/MS spectra against HMDB, KEGG, and an in-house spectral library. Differential metabolite analysis employs Student’s t-test (P < 0.05) combined with VIP scores ≥ 1 from a PLS-DA model, with fold change thresholds set at ≥1.2 or ≤1/1.2. Final outputs include annotated metabolite features (m/z, RT, name, database IDs) and relative quantification data.

complete_workflow_description: The paper describes a comprehensive, integrated computational platform—MASSyPup64—for biological mass spectrometry data analysis, built around reproducible workflows orchestrated via the Taverna Workflow Management System (WMS). The end-to-end pipeline begins with vendor-specific raw MS data (e.g., from LC-MS or MALDI-TOF instruments), which are first converted to the standardized mzML format using ProteoWizard tools (e.g., msconvert), optionally including baseline subtraction, smoothing, normalization, and centroiding. For proteomics, centroided or profile mzML files are fed into search engines such as Comet or X!Tandem for peptide identification, followed by validation using Trans-Proteomic Pipeline (TPP) tools. For metabolomics, untargeted workflows use XCMS (via R/metaXCMS) for feature detection, retention time alignment, and peak integration; targeted workflows apply predefined transitions (e.g., MRM) and statistical modeling. Metabolite identification leverages SpiderMass for heuristic chemical formula filtering (Seven Golden Rules), custom database construction, and spectral matching against MassBank or METLIN. Statistical validation is performed in R using packages like XCMS, metaXCMS, and MetabR, while Data Mining—including association analysis, clustering, and predictive modeling—is enabled via Rattle and custom R scripts. Large-scale data manipulation is supported by teapot (a non-conventional spreadsheet) and command-line matrix transposition tools. Three concrete workflow examples are provided: (1) protein identification → peptide association analysis; (2) targeted metabolomics → clustering + Data Mining; and (3) untargeted metabolomics → raw data processing → feature-based quantification → metabolite ID → Data Mining. All workflows are modular, interoperable, and designed to mitigate 'workflow decay' through standardization, open-source tool integration, and Taverna-based orchestration.
source_paper: Robert et al. (2015), PeerJ, DOI: 10.7717/peerj.1401, PMID: 26618079

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: metaX
sample_description: Plasma samples from 210 patients diagnosed with preclinical atherosclerosis (PCA) and 50 healthy controls, all aged 40–75 years, recruited from Affiliated Zhongshan Hospital of Dalian University between July 2023 and February 2024. PCA diagnosis was based on carotid intima-media thickness (IMT) ≥0.9 mm or presence of atherosclerotic plaques >1.2 mm thick, assessed via Hitachi LOGIQ ultrasound. Blood was collected in K3EDTA tubes, centrifuged at 4°C and 3000 rpm for 10 min, plasma aliquoted (0.2 mL), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C. For metabolomics, 100 μL plasma was extracted with 400 μL pre-chilled 80% methanol, incubated on ice, centrifuged twice at 15,000 × g and 4°C for 20 min each, and diluted to 53% methanol prior to UHPLC-MS/MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow began with plasma sample collection (n=210 PCA patients, n=50 controls), followed by methanol-based protein precipitation extraction and sample preparation for UHPLC-MS/MS. Analysis was performed on a Thermo Fisher Vanquish UHPLC system coupled to an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF or HF-X mass spectrometer, using a Hypersil Gold column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm) with gradient elution over 12 min in both positive and negative ion modes; MS parameters included 70,000 MS1 and 17,500 MS2 resolution, HCD fragmentation at stepped collision energies (20%/40%/60%), and m/z 100–1000 scan range. Raw data were processed in Compound Discoverer 3.3 with peak alignment, picking, and quantitation using a mass tolerance of 5 ppm, signal intensity tolerance of 30%, QC1-based peak area correction, and normalization to total spectral intensity; metabolite identification leveraged mzCloud, mzVault, and MassList databases. Quality filtering excluded metabolites with CV > 30% in QC samples. Statistical analysis employed R (v3.4.3) and SPSS 27.0: univariate t-tests and χ² tests identified candidate features; binary logistic regression identified clinical risk factors; metaX performed PCA and PLS-DA; differential metabolites were defined as VIP > 1, P < 0.05, and |log2(FC)| ≥ 1; annotation used HMDB, LIPIDMAPS, and KEGG; pathway enrichment used KEGG with significance threshold P < 0.05 and enrichment ratio criterion x/n > y/N; visualization included volcano plots (ggplot2), z-score–normalized heatmaps (pheatmap), and correlation networks (corrplot).
source_paper: Li et al. (2025), Frontiers in physiology, DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2025.1677194, PMID: 41245272

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: metaX
sample_description: Seminal plasma samples from 11 asymptomatic, PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2-infected male patients and 7 uninfected healthy male controls were collected. All participants were aged 20–45 years, had no history of urological or reproductive disorders, and semen samples were SARS-CoV-2 RNA-negative by RT-PCR. Samples underwent protein extraction, digestion, and label-free quantitative LC-MS/MS analysis; raw intensity values were generated from the proteomic dataset for downstream statistical analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow began with collection and processing of seminal plasma from 11 asymptomatic COVID-19 patients and 7 uninfected controls, followed by protein extraction, trypsin digestion, and label-free quantitative LC-MS/MS analysis. Raw MS intensities were normalized using the 'medium' method. Differential expression analysis was performed using the metaX package in R (v4.0), with significance defined as P < 0.05 (t-test) and |fold change| > 1.5. Identified DEPs were visualized via volcano plot and heatmap using ggplot2 and pheatmap. Functional enrichment analyses (GO, KEGG, Reactome) were conducted using hypergeometric testing (P < 0.05 threshold). A PPI network was constructed using STRING database annotations and visualized in Cytoscape (v3.9.1); hub proteins were identified via MCODE and Cytohubba algorithms. Spearman’s correlation assessed associations between DEPs and semen parameters (sperm concentration, total count, motility). Validation was performed by Western blot for ITLN1, GSTM2, and PSAP in an independent cohort. Statistical comparisons used Shapiro-Wilk normality testing followed by unpaired t-test/Mann-Whitney U test (group comparisons) or paired t-test/Wilcoxon signed-rank test (longitudinal semen parameter changes), implemented in SAS v9.4.
source_paper: Jialyu et al. (2025), BMC genomics, DOI: 10.1186/s12864-025-11473-5, PMID: 40119256
