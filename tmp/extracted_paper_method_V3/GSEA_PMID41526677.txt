>Materials and methods   Mice and Genotyping   All the mice used in this study had a C57BL/6J genetic background.  Pcid2  has six transcripts. FLAG-tagged  Pcid2  KI mice were generated using the CRISPR/Cas9 system. A Linker-3×FLAG was knocked into the translation termination codon of the  Pcid2 −201 transcript (ENSMUST00000164416.8). The sequence of the Linker-3×FLAG was designed according to a study published in 2022 [ 27 ]. The sequence was GGAGGAGGAGGATCTGACTACAAAGACCATGACGGTGATTATAAAGATCATGACATCGATTACAAGGACGATGACGACAAG. The exogenous gene was expressed under the regulation of the endogenous  Pcid2 . The  Pcid2 -201 transcript contains 14 exons, with a length of 2860 bp. The translation initiation site is located in exon1, and the translation termination site TGA is located in exon14. The  Pcid2  gene was modified using the CRISPR/Cas9 system. The brief procedure was as follows, a vector was constructed in vitro, and the CRISPR/Cas9 system and the donor vector were microinjected into the oocyte of C57BL/6JGpt mice to obtain F0 generation mice. After PCR and sequencing verification, the positive F0 generation mice were mated with C57BL/6JGpt mice to obtain a stable positive F1 generation mouse model. The detailed sequences of the PCR primers are listed in Supplementary Table  S1 .   The  Stra8-GFP Cre  knock-in mouse line was kindly gifted by Dr Ming-Han Tong from the Shanghai Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences [ 28 ]. We generated floxed  Pcid2  ( Pcid2 Flox/Flox ) alleles using the CRISPR/Cas9 system to study the effects of  Pcid2  deletion in germ cells. Based on the Ensemble database, the  Pcid2  gene has six transcripts, and the exon2-exon5 of  Pcid2- 201 transcript is recommended as the knockout region. The region contains a 272 bp coding sequence, and knockout of this region results in disruption of protein function. Briefly, a pair of sgRNAs was transcribed in  vitro  and a donor vector was constructed. Cas9, sgRNA, and the donor were microinjected into the oocytes of C57BL/6J mice. Fertilised embryos were transplanted to obtain F0 mice which were confirmed using PCR and sequencing. A stable F1 generation mouse model was obtained by mating positive F0-generation mice with C57BL/6J mice.  Stra8-GFP Cre  knock-in mice were mated with  Pcid2 Flox/Flox  mice to obtain the  Pcid2 -SKO progeny. All mice were housed under a regulated lighting schedule comprising 12 h of light and 12 h of darkness at a temperature range of 21–22 °C, with free access to water and food. Genotyping was conducted by PCR using the genomic DNA obtained from the tails of 5 days postpartum (dpp) mice. The detailed sequences of the PCR primers are listed in Supplementary Table  S1 .   The Animal Care and Use Committee of Peking University Health Science Center (LA2021579) approved all experimental procedures and animal care protocols. Fertility test   Each male mouse (8–12 weeks,  n  = 3) of control and  Pcid2 -SKO was caged with two WT C57BL/6J females (6–8 weeks), and the female mice were checked for vaginal plugs every morning. Plugged female mice were separated into single cages. Pregnancy status and number of pups were recorded for each female. The fertility test lasted for six months. Histological analysis   After isoflurane inhalation in a closed room, mice were euthanised using the cervical dislocation method. The abdomens of the mice were sprayed with 75% ethanol and a V-shaped incision was made in the pelvic cavity using sterile scissors and forceps. Removing epididymal fat to locate the testicles, cut them with scissors, and place them in a culture dish containing phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Testicles were separated and fixed overnight with Blouin’s solution in a rotator. The tissue was embedded in paraffin, and a Leica vibratome machine was used to cut it into 5 μm thick slices. Slices were dewaxed and rehydrated using gradient concentrations of ethanol and then stained with haematoxylin and eosin. After fixing the slices with neutral resins, images were captured using NanoZoomer Digital Pathology (WISLEAP, WS-10, China). Immunofluorescence   Testicles extracted from male mice were isolated immediately after cervical dislocation and immobilised overnight in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA; Servicebio, G1101) at 4 °C for immunostaining. After sequential dehydration in an ethanol series, the tissues were embedded in paraffin wax and sliced using a Leica slicing machine (Leica, RM2235, Germany). After dewaxing and hydration, the slices were boiled in Tris-EDTA (pH 8.0) for 15 min, washed in 1× PBS containing 0.1% Tween-20 (PBST), blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 1 h, and then incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. After washing three times with PBST, the slices were incubated with secondary antibodies and 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, 1:1000, Abcam, ab285390) to stain the nuclei at room temperature for 1 h. The cells were then washed three times with PBST. Images were captured using a Zeiss LSM880 inverted confocal microscope. 10 to 15 regions were randomly selected to count the positively stained cells. The primary and secondary antibodies used for immunofluorescence are shown in Supplementary Table  S2 . Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining   Paraffin-embedded slides were fixed with 4% PFA and washed three times with PBS. The slices were boiled in sodium citrate buffer for 15 min for antigen retrieval. After blocking with 3% BSA, each slide was incubated overnight with primary antibodies at 4 °C and then washed three times with PBS. Slides were treated with 3% H 2 O 2  to eliminate internal peroxidase activity. After washing thrice with PBS, the slides were stained with a secondary antibody conjugated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP). Finally, the slides were stained with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) and the nuclei were stained with haematoxylin. Images were captured using the NanoZoomer Digital Pathology System (WISLEAP, WS-10, China). Western blotting   Male mouse tissues were separated, pulverised in liquid nitrogen, and collected in ice-cold radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (RIPA) containing protease inhibitors. The lysates were diluted with 2× Laemmli sample buffer (Bio-Rad, 1610737) and boiled in water for 10 min. Protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulphate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE, 10% acrylamide gel) and electrically transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. After transfer, 5% skimmed milk in Tris-buffered saline (10 mM Tris, 150 mM NaCl, pH 7.5) containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) was used to block the PVDF membrane for 1 h, followed by incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After washing with TBST, the membranes were incubated for 1 h with rabbit or mouse secondary antibodies. Finally, the protein bands were observed using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection system (Tanon-5200). Western blotting images were analysed using ImageJ software (Wayne Rasband, USA). Full and uncropped Western blotting are shown in the Supplementary Material. The antibodies used for Western blotting are listed in Supplementary Table  S2 . Immunoprocipitation-Mass Spectrometry (IP-MS)   The testes of male mice were separated and collected in ice-cold RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors. After tissue grinding and ultrasonication, the lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 ×g for 10 min and the supernatant was incubated with a volume of antibody at 4 °C for 3 h. Next, 40 µL Dynabeads-protein-A (Invitrogen, 10002D) was added and incubated at 4 °C overnight. The beads were washed four times with PBS, and the precipitated material was diluted with SDS-PAGE sample buffer and boiled for 10 min to separate the beads. The supernatant from each sample was loaded onto a 10% acrylamide gel and electrophoretically separated at 80 V. As a control for peptide visualisation, the gels were stained with liver stain for 30 min.   Proteins in the gel were hydrolysed using trypsin. In the LC-MS/MS analysis, the digested products were separated by 120 min gradient elution at a flow rate of 0.300 µL/min with a Thermo Ultimate 3000 nano-UPLC system directly interfaced with a Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer. The analytical column used was an Acclaim PepMap RSLC column (75 μm ID, 250 mm length, C18). Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid and mobile phase B consisted of 100% acetonitrile and 0.1% formic acid. The mass spectrometer was operated in data-dependent acquisition mode using the Xcalibur 4.1.50 software, and there was a single full-scan mass spectrum in the Orbitrap (375–1500 m/z, 60,000 resolution), followed by data-dependent MS/MS scans. Raw data were searched against the selected database downloaded from UniProt using the Proteome Discovery software (version 2.4).   The normalised abundance of each protein was transformed into log space and a differential test was performed using the R package limma (v3.50.3) [ 29 ]. Only proteins with an adjusted  p value  < 0.05, and fold change (IP/control) > 2 were identified as candidate interacting proteins of PCID2. Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)   Full-length sequences of mouse  Pcid2 ,  Snrpg ,  Sf3b1 , and  hnRNPH1  were obtained from testis cDNA and cloned into p3×FLAG-CMV-10 and pEGFP-C1 vectors using Phanta ®  Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase (Vazyme, P505). The constructed plasmids were transfected into HEK293T cells. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, cells were lysed in ice-cold ELB buffer (50 mM HEPES, 250 mM NaCl, 0.1% NP-40) supplemented with 1 mM PMSF (Sigma-Aldrich, P7626) and protease inhibitor cocktail (Roche, 04693132001). Following centrifugation at 12,000 ×g for 10 min, supernatants were incubated with 1 µg target antibody at 4 °C for 3 h. Subsequently, 15 µL Dynabeads-protein-A (Invitrogen, 10002D) was added, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C. After four washes with ELB buffer, immunoprecipitated complexes were denatured in SDS-PAGE sample buffer and analysed by Western blotting. RNA FISH   The probe  Pcid2 -C1 was designed for a target sequence between 1057 and 1999 bp. RNA FISH was performed using the RNAscope ®  Multiplex Fluorescent Reagent Kit v2 (ACD, 323100), according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. Briefly, the paraffin slices underwent the following steps: baked at 60 °C for 30 min, fresh xylene and anhydrous ethanol incubation, followed by air drying, incubated in hydrogen peroxide for 10 min, washed with distilled water and incubated in 1× codetection target repair reagent at 100 °C for 15 min, washed with distilled water and PBST. The slides were then incubated with the first antibody (MVH, see Supplementary Table  S2 ) overnight at 4 °C, washed twice with PBST, incubated with 10% neutral formalin solution for 30 min, washed with PBST, treated with protease Plus at 40 °C for 30 min, washed with distilled water, incubated with probe  Pcid2 -C1 (ACD, 1133331-C1) at 40 °C for 2 h, AMP1 and AMP2 at 40 °C for 30 min, AMP3 at 40 °C for 15 min, HRP-C1 at 40 °C for 15 min, Opal™ 570 (Akoya Biosciences, FP1488001KT) for  Pcid2  at 40 °C for 30 min, HRP-blocker at 40 °C for 15 min, washed twice with RNAscope ®  1× washing buffer, and incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat-anti-rabbit antibody at room temperature for 30 min. The slides were washed twice with PBST and incubated with DAPI solution at room temperature for 60 s. The slides were sealed with antifade mountant (Invitrogen,  P36930 ). Fluorescence images were obtained using a Zeiss LSM880 inverted confocal microscope. Magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS)   Cells were prepared from mouse testes. In the final wash of testicular cells, cells were resuspended in 4 mL of 1× MojoSort™ Buffer (BioLegend, 480017). The cells were then filtered with a 70 μm cell strainer, centrifuged at 300 ×g for 5 min, and resuspended in a volume of MojoSort™ Buffer. The cell concentration was adjusted to 1 × 10 8  cells/ml. A cell suspension (100 µL, 10 7  cells) was aliquoted into a new tube, and a volume of PE-conjugated antibody Anti-CD117 (c-KIT, eBioscience,12-1171-83) was added to the cell suspension, mixed well, and incubated on ice for 15 min. Cells were washed with 1× MojoSort™ buffer and centrifuged at 300 ×g for 5 min. The supernatant was discarded and resuspended in 100 µL of 1× MojoSort™ buffer. 10 μL of PE Nanobeads (BioLegend, 480080) were added, mixed well, and incubated on ice for 15 min. The cells were then washed with 1× MojoSort™ buffer (up to 4 mL). Next, the cells were centrifuged at 300 ×g for 5 min and the supernatant was discarded. Adding 2.5 mL of 1× MojoSort™ buffer was added, and the tube was placed in a MojoSort™ Magnet (BioLegend, 480019) for 5 min. The unlabelled fraction was discarded, and the labelled cells were resuspended in 2.5 mL MojoSort™ Buffer. Repeat the separation steps for three times to remove the unlabelled fractions and retain the labelled cells. RNA extraction and RT-PCR   Total RNA was extracted from whole testes or sorted cells using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Scientific, 15596018) and trichloromethane (TGREAG, 67-66-3) following the manufacturer’s instructions. For RT-PCR, isolated total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa, RR047A). The cDNA and specific primers were subjected to appropriate PCR cycles. The PCR products were separated by agarose gel electrophoresis and quantified using the ImageJ software. Splicing ratios are represented as percentage spliced in (PSI) values, PSI = (Splice-in)/(Splice-in + Splice-out), indicating the proportion of mRNA transcripts for a gene that contains a particular exon or splice site. The primers used for PCR are listed in Supplementary Table  S1 . LncRNA library construction and data analysis   After the RNA samples were evaluated and qualified, rRNA was removed from the total RNA samples (some IncRNAs had the same polyA tail structure as the mRNA and rRNA maximally retained IncRNAs containing polyA tails were removed). Library construction was performed according to the NEBNext Ultra Directional RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, E7420L) operating instructions and different index tags were selected for library construction.   Raw fastq was processed using the fastp software to remove sequencing adapters and low-quality reads. The clean reads were aligned to the mm10 genome (gene annotation was gencode-vM23) using STAR software. rMATS was used to analyse AS events between control and  Pcid2 -SKO testes. To detect effective AS events, events with a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.01 and |△PSI| >5% were classified as differential splicing events based on the original read counts. Single-cell transcriptome library construction and data analysis   Using the Chromium Single Cell 3’ GEM Kit v3.1, single-cell suspensions are processed on the Chromium Single Cell Chip for encapsulation with barcoded gel beads, targeting 6000 cells per sample. Cells are lysed, and RNA is reverse-transcribed and barcoded within gel beads, followed by cDNA amplification using a T100 PCR thermal cycler. cDNA quality and concentration are assessed with a Qubit fluorometer and Bioanalyzer 2100. Libraries are sequenced on the Illumina platform by Annoroad Gene Technology, with 40,000 reads per cell.   STARsolo (v2.7.9a) was used to align reads to mm10 (mm10-2020-A) mouse reference genome and generated the raw count matrix. Then raw count matrix was imported into Seurat (v4.3) for preprocessing. Only cells fulfill with the following criteria were kept for further analysis: (1) at least 500 detected genes; (2) percentage of mitochondria UMIs < 10%. The filtered cells were normalized to log (CPM/100 + 1).   Top 2000 highly variable genes were adopted for data integration. The data were integrated using mutual nearest neighborhood correction algorithm implemented by RunFastMNN function with default settings [ 30 ]. Then Louvain clustering was performed on the first 50 MNN embeddings with k = 100. Cells were annotated through projecting them onto the mouse testicular cell atlas (mTCA) using ProjectSVR [ 31 ]. Only cells with high-quality projection (adjust  p  value < 0.01) were kept. Then the annotations were checked manually according to the expression level of canonical markers. Clusters expressing markers from two different cell types were identified as doublets and were removed. To visualize the scRNA-seq data, the first 50 MNN embeddings of somatic cells were used to generate UMAP. The germ cells were visualized using the projected UMAP generated by ProjectSVR.   To calculate the pseudotime of germ cells along the spermatogenesis, the principle curve was fitted on projected UMAP embeddings using the princurve (v2.1.6) package in R. Pseudotime was calculated as the distance between the closest position on the curve and the start position.   The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of each stage of germ cells were identified through MAST algorithm [ 32 ]. DEGs were visualized using jjVolcano function implemented in R package scRNAtoolVis (v0.0.7).   The Single Cell Pathway Analysis (SCPA) was performed using the R package SCPA (v1.5.4) [ 33 ] according to the package tutorial. The affected pathway was visualized using the R package ComplexHeatmap (v2.10.0) [ 34 ]. The gene set enrichment analysis of type B spermatogonia was performed using the R package clusterProfiler (v4.2.2) [ 35 ]. Transferase dUTP nick-end labelling (TUNEL) assay   The terminal deoxynucleotidyl TUNEL assay was performed using the In situ Cell Death Detection Kit (Roche, 11684817910) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, the tissue slides were permeabilised with 0.3% Triton X-100. After the slides were equilibrated, TdT reaction mixture was added and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. The slides were then incubated with DAPI for 10 min for nuclear staining. After the final washing, the slides were dried and mounted with an antifade reagent (Invitrogen,  P36930 ) and stored in a black box at 4 °C until image scanning with Pannoramic MIDI. Statistical analysis   Each experiment was performed with three replicates. All statistical analyses were performed using Prism 8 (GraphPad software) and are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (mean ± SEM). Statistical significance was determined using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s  t -test unless otherwise stated in the figure legend. The significance threshold was * P  < 0.05, ** P  < 0.01, *** P  < 0.001, **** P  < 0.0001. Results   PCID2 is expressed at all stages of germ cells during spermatogenesis   According to sequence alignment and motif analyses, PCID2 is a highly conserved protein expressed in multiple vertebrate species (Figure  S1 A-C). We evaluated the expression levels of PCID2 in multiple organs. The results showed that the PCID2 protein was universally expressed but was enriched in the testes (Fig.  1 A). In our previous study, which aimed to reveal the molecular features of human iNOA, we discovered a substantial decrease in  PCID2  expression in both spermatogonia and spermatocytes compared to that in normal controls (Figure  S2 A) [ 26 ]. According to data from a previous single-cell transcriptome study [ 36 ],  Pcid2  is highly expressed at various stages of mouse spermatogenesis (Figure  S2 B). The results from the extraction and reanalysis of published proteomic data [ 37 ] were consistent with our Western blotting results, which showed PCID2 protein levels from 3 dpp to 56 dpp, indicating that PCID2 was highly expressed during spermatogenesis (Fig.  1 B and C). Because the commercial anti-PCID2 antibody cannot be used for immunofluorescence staining, to study the location and function of PCID2 during spermatogenesis, we engineered the  Pcid2  locus to produce FLAG-tagged knock-in (KI) mice using the CRISPR/Cas9 system. The design of the targeting vector for the KI allele and genotyping results are shown (Figure  S2 C and D). The localization and expression of PCID2 during spermatogenesis was further characterised by immunofluorescence and IHC staining of paraffin-embedded testicular sections. The results showed that FLAG-tagged PCID2 was highly expressed in spermatogonia, meiotic prophase I spermatocytes, and round spermatids (Fig.  1 D and E). We speculate that PCID2 plays an essential physiological role during spermatogenesis.   Fig. 1. Scale bar = 10 μm PCID2 is indispensable for mice spermatogenesis and male fertility   To study the physiological functions of PCID2 in the germline in vivo, we created  Pcid2 -floxed mice since global ablation of  Pcid2  resulted in embryonic lethality before the blastocyst stage [ 22 ], and used them to mate with  Stra8-GFP Cre  KI mice to obtain  Pcid2 fl/− ;  Stra8-GFP Cre  (referred to as  Pcid2 -SKO) male mice, in which  Pcid2  was specifically inactivated since the stage of type A1 spermatogonia (Fig.  2 A and Figure  S3 A). The expression of PCID2 in the 21-day-old testes lysate was detected by Western blotting, and the results showed that compared with wild-type (WT) mice, PCID2 levels in the testes of  Pcid2 -SKO mice were significantly decreased (Fig.  2 B). The results of RNA FISH showed that  Pcid2  mRNA level in germ cells was significantly lower in the testes of  Pcid2 -SKO mice than that in WT mice (Fig.  2 C and D), indicating that PCID2 was effectively ablated in germ cells of  Pcid2 -SKO mice.
