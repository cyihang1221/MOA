sample_description: The study included 420 women enrolled in the Environment and Reproductive Health (EARTH) Study who completed at least one IVF cycle between 2006 and 2017 (n=648 total cycles) at the Massachusetts General Hospital (MGH) Fertility Center. All participants had available urine biomarker data with at least one urine sample per IVF cycle for quantification of BPA, methyl-paraben, propyl-paraben, and eight phthalate metabolites (MEP, MBP, MiBP, MBzP, MEHP, MEHHP, MEOHP, MECPP). Urine samples (n=1,145 total) were collected as spot samples: 23% of cycles had one sample (collected between Day 3–9 of gonadotrophin phase), and 77% had two samples (first during Days 3–9, second on day of oocyte retrieval for fresh cycles or embryo transfer for cryo-thaw cycles). Samples were collected in sterile polypropylene cups, specific gravity (SG) was measured using a handheld refractometer calibrated with deionized water, aliquoted, frozen at −80°C, and shipped on dry ice to the CDC for storage at ≤−40°C until analysis. Chemical concentrations were SG-corrected using the formula Pc = P[(1.015 – 1)/(SG − 1)], where Pc is the SG-corrected concentration (µg/L), P is the measured concentration (µg/L), and 1.015 is the mean SG of the study population; values below LOD (0.1–1.2 ng/mL) were imputed as LOD/√2 prior to SG correction. Cycle-specific exposure was defined as the geometric mean of SG-adjusted concentrations from two samples per cycle, or the single-sample concentration when only one sample was available.

sample_description: The study used two types of synthetic gene expression datasets generated by GeneNetWeaver: one simulating Escherichia coli (E. coli) and another simulating yeast. For initial evaluation, 10 distinct GRNs were generated for each organism, each containing 30 genes and 41 time points. To further assess scalability and robustness, additional synthetic datasets were generated with larger network sizes: 10 networks per organism, each with 50 genes and 101 time points. All synthetic datasets were time-series microarray expression data designed to mimic realistic transcriptional dynamics, including regulatory time lags and noise. The purpose of generating multiple replicates (10 per condition) was to mitigate biases arising from idiosyncratic network topologies or stochastic variations in network generation.
software_configuration_description: The paper does not use Bayesian PCA; it explicitly distinguishes between standard Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) as two *separate*, non-Bayesian approaches for determining the number of hidden variables (m) in the State Space Model (SSM). PCA is applied to the gene expression matrix Y = [y₁, y₂, ..., y_T] ∈ ℝ^(l×T), where l is the number of genes and T is the number of time points. Each gene i corresponds to a vector g_i ∈ ℝ^T (the i-th row of Y), and the set {g_i} forms a linear space. PCA is performed on the covariance matrix of these g_i vectors. The number of hidden variables m is determined as the smallest integer satisfying ∑_{k=m+1}^{min(l,T)} λ_k / ∑_{k=1}^{min(l,T)} λ_k ≤ 20%, where λ_k are the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix sorted in descending order. No Bayesian priors, hyperparameters, iterative sampling, or probabilistic latent variable inference is described — only classical, deterministic, variance-maximizing PCA for dimensionality reduction.
