sample_description: The study used plasma samples from 348 unique patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including 159 with ulcerative colitis (UC), 169 with Crohn's disease (CD), and 20 with IBD-unclassified (IBD-U); 28 patients (8% of cohort, 11% of samples) had concomitant primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC). A total of 735 plasma samples were collected—each drawn prior to colonoscopy in 10 mL K2 EDTA vacutainer tubes (BD Vacutainer® SKU: 366643), centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C with maximum ramp-up and brake speeds, aliquoted into vials, and stored at −80°C for up to 9 years before analysis. For mass spectrometry, 20 µL of each plasma sample was extracted with 360 µL methanol after spiking with 140 µL of labelled amino acid standard sets A and B (Cambridge Isotope Laboratories); supernatants were centrifuged, dried, solubilised, and filtered. Each sample was analysed in three technical replicates, randomized in injection order.
complete_workflow_description: Data acquisition employed an in-house adapted direct infusion mass spectrometry (DI-MS) protocol using plasma (not blood spots), with samples injected via Advion Triverse Nanomate coupled to a Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive HF high-resolution mass spectrometer; positive-mode spectra were acquired for 3 min and negative-mode for 1.5 min, generating Thermo.raw files. Processing followed Wolthuis et al. (2022): raw files were converted to mzML using ProteoWizard; positive and negative mode scans were separated; alignment used internal standard m/z values per mode; individual scans were merged into one spectrum per mode; peak detection was performed using the MALDIquant R package; peaks were binned at 2 ppm resolution. Filtering excluded m/z features with >20% missingness across samples (per Bijlsma et al., 2006). Normalisation used MetaboAnalystR with quantile normalisation and autoscaling, followed by batch-effect correction for day-of-run using WaveICA (leveraging recorded batch and injection order metadata). Technical replicate signals were averaged in MetaboShiny to generate the final dataset. For machine learning, a nested cross-validation scheme was implemented: first, an 80/20 split stratified by subject (to prevent data leakage) assigned 80% of samples to ML training and 20% to enrichment analysis; within the 80%, StratifiedGroupKFold (SGKF) in R (adapted from Scikit-learn) performed 5-fold CV for signature determination; for PSC-IBD analysis, UDCA-negative patients were evenly distributed across folds—using GroupKFold for IBD-only patients and SGKF with UDCA treatment as grouping variable for PSC-positive patients (4 folds due to only 4 UDCA-negative individuals in ML subset). Models were built using caret’s ranger Random Forest (mtry = sqrt(total m/z features)), with ROC and PR curves summarised across folds using pROC; significance versus permuted-class null models was assessed via roc.test (p-value thresholds: * = 0.01–0.05; *** = 0.001–0.01; **** < 0.001). Bootstrapping involved 10,000 resamples matched to non-UDCA set size, with AUROC distributions compared via one-sample t-test. Feature selection included: (1) univariate t-tests and fold-change analyses in MetaboShiny yielding combined V-scores (V_PSC); (2) per-m/z logistic regression (glm) predicting PSC-IBD status, extracting absolute Wald z-values (|Z|) for ranking; (3) signature determination via iterative random forest modelling: starting from all m/z features, top-ranked features were progressively replaced (50 at a time) with permuted values (based on V_PSC or Z_PSC ranking), alongside two negative controls (50 randomly permuted m/z features; and full class-label permutation); the elbow point of the AUROC vs. number of permuted features curve—identified via gradient descent on geom_smooth fit—defined the metabolic signature size threshold. Enrichment analysis used the signature threshold derived from the ~80% feature selection partition.

Batch-wise variation was visually checked and adjusted if present. Complete-case analysis excluded observations with missing demographic values. Significance was defined as p ≤ 0.05 for the Hispanic term, with FDR correction at q < 0.05; only metabolites with FDR < 0.05 and annotation confidence score ≥10 were reported. Interaction between Hispanic ethnicity and fireground exposure status (baseline vs. post-fire) was tested by adding an interaction term to the main model, with FDR < 0.05 for significance.
