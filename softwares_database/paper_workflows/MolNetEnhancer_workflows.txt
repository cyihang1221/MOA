sample_description: The study used 10 sponge samples (3–5 mm³ each) of two marine sponge species: Aplysina caissara Pinheiro and Hajdu, 2001 and Aplysina fulva Pallas, 1766 (Verongiida, Aplysinidae), collected by scuba diving in July and October 2018 at two locations in São Sebastião, São Paulo, Brazil—Ponta Recife (23°49’30.36”S 45°24’47.879”W) and southern rock shores of Praia do Guaecá (23°49’22.8”S 45°28’19.2”W)—at depths of 3–8 m. Samples were placed in sterile plastic bags with natural seawater, kept in a cooler during transport, and processed immediately upon arrival at the laboratory. For bacterial isolation, 2.5 g of choanosome tissue from each sponge was macerated in 25 mL calcium/magnesium-free artificial seawater (ASW); supernatants were serially diluted (10⁻² to 10⁻⁶) and plated on multiple media: enriched (BHI), oligotrophic (MB, R2A), and selective (AIA for Actinobacteriota; SNAX for Cyanobacteria). Pure isolates were grown in corresponding broths (BHI, R2A, MB, or selective formulations) for 18 h at room temperature (20–25 °C, 200 rpm), then stored in 20% glycerol at −80 °C. Genomic DNA was extracted using the Wizard Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega), and the 16S rRNA gene was amplified using primers 27F-1492R and sequenced via Sanger sequencing on an ABI 3500. Additionally, metabarcoding data from 15 previously sequenced sponge samples (10 A. caissara and 5 A. fulva; Bioproject IDs PRJNA665805 and PRJNA673577) were re-analyzed for comparative ASV-based community profiling. For secondary metabolite analysis, bacterial strains positive for type I/II PKS or NRPS genes were cultured on solid BHI and MB media, harvested at stationary phase (~72 h), and extracted sequentially with ethyl acetate (30 min ultrasonication) and methanol; extracts were dried via rotary evaporation and vacuum concentration. Crude extracts (5.0 mg) were cleaned up using C18 solid-phase extraction cartridges (Chromabond, 45 μm, 500.0 mg, 6.0 mL), pre-activated with 3.0 mL MeOH and equilibrated with 3.0 mL MeOH/H₂O (8:2, v/v), then eluted with 1.5 mL of the same solvent mixture and reconstituted in MeOH to 2.0 mg/mL for LC-MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: LC-MS/MS raw data files were first converted to mzML format using MSConvert (ProteoWizard) with Peak Picking filter and vendor-checked algorithm. Data were uploaded to the Global Natural Products Social (GNPS) platform (https://gnps.ucsd.edu) and processed through the online molecular networking workflow. Key parameters included: removal of all MS/MS fragment ions within ±17 Da of the precursor m/z; window filtering selecting only the top six fragment ions within a ±50 Da window; precursor ion mass tolerance = 0.02 Da; MS/MS fragment ion tolerance = 0.05 Da; cosine score threshold ≥0.7; minimum matched peaks = 4; maximum molecular family size = 100 (with low-scoring edges pruned until family size <100). Spectral library matching against GNPS libraries applied identical filtering criteria (score >0.7, ≥4 matched peaks). For unannotated signals, manual annotation was performed using exact mass-derived chemical formulas and cross-referencing with Dictionary of Natural Products, The Natural Products Atlas, and literature. MS2LDA (http://ms2lda.org/) and NAP workflows were run in parallel. Dereplicator was executed with the following settings: precursor ion mass tolerance = 0.02 Da, MS/MS fragment ion tolerance = 0.05 Da, VarQuest algorithm, PNP database, max charge = 2, min AA = 5, max modification mass = 150 Da, min matched peaks = 5. ClassyFire (https://classyfire.wishartlab.com) was used for chemical ontology-based class annotations. All networks were visualized using Cytoscape v.3.8.2. Analytical blanks (MeOH only) were analyzed every 10 samples and their features removed from the final feature table.

(2) Preprocessing was performed in MZmine v2.41 using a batch.xml configuration (Online Resource 2): data cropping to RT 0.27–9.80 min; MS1 peak detection with noise threshold 10,000; ADAP-based chromatogram building (min 7 scans/peak, group intensity threshold 10,000, min highest intensity 150,000, m/z tolerance 0.001 Da or 5 ppm); Wavelets (ADAP) deconvolution (m/z center by median, MS2 pairing ranges: 0.01 Da and 0.4 min); isotope grouping (m/z tolerance 0.001 Da or 5 ppm, RT tolerance 0.1 min); peak alignment (m/z tolerance 0.001 Da or 5 ppm, RT tolerance 0.1 min, 75% m/z / 25% RT weighting); and peak filtering (min 15 consecutive peaks, same RT/peak duration constraints). (5) MASST and SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID were applied for additional structural elucidation when GNPS/Compound Discoverer yielded no hits: MASST queried single MS/MS spectra across all public GNPS datasets; SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID employed deep learning to predict molecular formula and structure from MS/MS spectra. (6) Contamination filtering used a Kendrick Mass Filter (da Silva et al. 2019) in Python/Jupyter (parameters: number of observed signals = 5, Kendrick mass defect = 0.01, fraction base = 1) to remove PEG and PPG-related features. (7) Statistical analysis used R v4.0.3 with packages ggplot2, ggfortify, ggsci, rlang, viridis, vegan (for PERMANOVA), and MetaboAnalystR3.0: missing values imputed as half the minimum positive value; glog transformation and Pareto scaling applied; fold change threshold = 2; t-tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with FDR correction; PCA including pooled samples; all scripts publicly available on GitHub.
