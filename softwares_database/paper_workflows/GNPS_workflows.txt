========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: GNPS
sample_description: A total of 102 Vanilla plant samples were analyzed, comprising 50 individuals of Vanilla pompona Schiede, 35 of Vanilla phaeantha Rchb.f., and 17 of Vanilla calyculata Schltr. All plants were collected from four major Brazilian biomes—Amazon (equatorial climate, 25–28 °C, high humidity, nutrient-poor leached soils), Cerrado (seasonal tropical savanna, 22–27 °C, acidic weathered soils), Caatinga (semi-arid, >25 °C, irregular rainfall, shallow stony soils), and Atlantic Forest (tropical humid, high rainfall/humidity, deep weathered soils)—and subsequently cultivated in a greenhouse at Embrapa Genetic Resources and Biotechnology in Brasília, DF, Brazil. Fresh leaves were harvested, cleaned, freeze-dried for 48 h using a Lyotech Freeze Dry System LS 3000, ground into fine powder with an electric grinder, and stored at −20 °C. The plant material is registered in the SisGen national genetic heritage system under code A301AFD.
complete_workflow_description: Raw LC-HRMS/MS data (.RAW files) were extracted using the RawFileReader library (github.com/thermofisherlsms/RawFileReader) implemented in a custom Python3 script. Signal intensities below 1% of the maximum intensity per scan were discarded; spectra were then summed and base peak normalized to yield relative intensities from 0 to 100%. Mass alignment across all 113 samples (102 biological + 11 QC) was performed, resulting in a final data matrix of 113 rows × 1078 columns (each column representing an m/z variable with its relative intensity). Statistical processing, normalization, and multivariate analyses—including Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA)—were carried out using custom Python routines. For PLS-DA, a one-vs-all strategy was employed with five latent components (determined optimal via performance plateauing in cross-validation), VIP scores >1.0 were used for multivariate variable importance, and Volcano plot analysis (−log₁₀(p-value) vs. log₂(fold-change)) with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (p < 0.05 and |log₂(FC)| > 1) was applied for univariate significance. Feature selection combined VIP >1.0 and Volcano significance via intersection. Model robustness was assessed using leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) for Q² calculation and permutation testing (n = 200) to confirm non-random discrimination. All chemometric workflows followed established protocols for untargeted plant metabolomics (Gowda & Djukovic, 2014; Smith et al., 2006; Bro & Smilde, 2014; Barker & Rayens, 2003; Brereton & Lloyd, 2014; Wold et al., 2001; Neto et al., 2021; Triba et al., 2015; Pan et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2024).

Ethyl acetate extraction (1:1 v/v) was performed using a separating funnel; crude extracts were concentrated via rotary evaporator at 50°C and dried in a water bath at 45°C with gentle rotation at 100 rpm. The resulting dried extracts served as the analytical samples for GC-MS, LC-HRMS, antimicrobial assays, and molecular networking.
complete_workflow_description: The computational and analytical workflow involved multiple software tools with specific configurations: (1) MS-DIAL version 6.0 was used for qualitative metabolite analysis, including peak discrimination, filtering (mass error ≤10 ppm), and alignment of LC-HRMS data; raw files (.abf and .raw formats) were imported using Abf Converter 4.0.0 and MS FileReader 2.2.62. (2) Compound Discoverer software (Thermo Scientific) and MS-FINDER were employed for fragmentation pattern (MS/MS) interpretation and compound annotation. (3) MetaboAnalyst 6.0 was used for multivariate statistical analysis: hierarchical clustering heatmap generation based on peak area data (M+H+ adducts) in CSV format; PCA and PLS-DA modeling with VIP >1 and adjusted FDR <0.05 as significance cutoffs; model validation via cross-validation and permutation testing; R²(Y) and Q²(Y) statistics assessed goodness-of-fit and predictive ability. (5) Statistical analysis of antimicrobial activity used Minitab 19.0: one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan’s multiple range test (p = 0.05); data expressed as mean ± SD of three replicates.

========== paper_workflow entry 8 ==========
related_tool: GNPS
sample_description: The research utilized raw metabolomics data sourced from multiple public repositories including GNPS/MassIVE, the National Metabolomics Data Repository (NMDR), and MetaboLights (MTBLS). The raw data consisted of mass spectrometry files in proprietary formats (.raw, .d, .wiff, .cdf) and open formats (mzML, mzXML), collectively referred to as 'raw data'. These files were indexed and accessed via MS Run Identifiers (MRIs), a subset of the Universal Spectrum Identifier (USI) standard that encodes scan information, dataset IDs, and file paths. Only raw files with at least one piece of sample-related metadata—such as analyzed organism, tissue type, cell type, biological fluid (e.g., sweat, T-cells), or solvent blank—were accepted into the harmonized Pan-ReDU metadata table. Sample-level metadata was extracted from diverse structured sources: ReDU used a single-table controlled-vocabulary format; NMDR employed the mwTAB key-value structure per study; and MTBLS followed the ISA-Tab framework with separate tables for sample, assay, and metabolite levels, incorporating both controlled ontologies and free-text fields. Metadata harmonization involved ontology mapping using translation sheets manually curated after term inspection and aligned with public ontologies for body parts (UBERON), plant organism parts (PO), cell types (CL), disease names (MONDO), and mass spectrometry instrumentation (MSO, PSI-MS). For ambiguous associations between raw files and metadata (e.g., multiple mwTAB files per study), polarity mode matching between mwTAB-reported polarity and scan header polarity in mzML/mzXML files was used to resolve mappings; studies without unambiguous spectral file–sample associations were discarded.
complete_workflow_description: The methodology comprises five integrated computational workflows: (1) MRI-based Raw Data Indexing & Retrieval: A three-step process—Step 1 (caching) checks local GNPS2 Dataset Cache for pre-downloaded, XML-valid mzML files; Step 2 (download) fetches data from GNPS/MassIVE, NMDR (via REST API), or MTBLS (via MetaboLights API) if uncached or corrupted; Step 3 (transparent reporting) generates per-MRI logs detailing download success/failure, cache hit rates, and error diagnostics. (2) Metadata Harmonization: A Python workflow automates integration of NMDR (mwTAB) and MTBLS (ISA-Tab) metadata into ReDU’s ontology. For NMDR, mwTAB files are parsed per study; substring matching and ontology-driven translation sheets map terms (e.g., SUBJECT_SPECIES → 'Organism') using UBERON, CL, PO, MONDO, and PSI-MS ontologies; raw file–sample linkage uses full paths, basename matches, delimiter-splitting (',', ';', ' '), and fallback substring matching. For MTBLS, column equivalence is established (e.g., 'NCBITaxonomy' → 'Organism', 'UBERONBodyPartName' → 'Organism part'), with non-standard columns (e.g., 'sex' or 'gender') searched for 'BiologicalSex'; only open-format files (mzML/mzXML) are retained when duplicates exist. (3) Pan-ReDU Metadata Curation Infrastructure: Includes a Dash-based Metadata Selection Dashboard (daily auto-updated, with linkouts to GNPS2 Molecular Networking, MassQL, and Dashboard); a Google Sheets-based Metadata Validation Sheet (with documentation, allowed terms, examples, and Apps Script validation logic); and a Dash-based Metadata Submission System requiring GNPS/MassIVE login, depositing submissions to GitHub (https://github.com/Wang-Bioinformatics-Lab/ReDU_metadata) for daily ingestion into Pan-ReDU. (4) Scan Type Analysis: Scan counts (by polarity and scan level) are extracted from downloaded mzML/mzXML files using pyteomics (v4.7.2); for MTBLS, scan-level metadata is obtained directly from the MTBLS team. (5) Research-to-Data Sharing Ratio Assessment: Web of Science was queried for 'metabolomics' AND 'mass spectrometry' in any field, filtering Research Articles published 2019–2024; Pan-ReDU dataset counts were restricted to those with YearOfAnalysis ≤ 2023. All workflows are publicly available via GitHub repositories including PublicDataset_ReDU_Metadata_Workflow, ReDU-MS2-GNPS2, GNPS_DatasetExplorer, Classical_Networking_Workflow, and downloadpublicdata.

========== paper_workflow entry 11 ==========
related_tool: GNPS
