related_tool: KEGG
sample_description: The study used human glioblastoma cell lines LN229 and T98G, cultured in high-glucose Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, under humidified 5% CO₂ at 37°C. For metabolomics, LN229 cells (1 × 10⁷ per sample) were treated with corilagin at 0 µg/mL (Control group) and 256.8 µg/mL (Corilagin group) for 6 h (metabolomics sample preparation section) — though later experiments (e.g., Fig. 2 and Fig. 3) state 24 h treatment; six biological replicates were used per group. Cells were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen, homogenized, and extracted using 400 µL of L-2-chlorophenylalanine as internal standard, followed by vortexing (30 s), ultrasonication (40 kHz, 30 min at 5°C), protein precipitation at −20°C for 30 min, and centrifugation (13,000 g, 4°C, 15 min); supernatants were collected for LC-MS analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling 20 µL from each sample supernatant.
complete_workflow_description: Metabolomic data processing and multivariate analysis were performed using Progenesis QI 2.3 (Nonlinear Dynamics, Waters, USA) for raw LC-MS data preprocessing, including peak detection, denoising, normalization, retention time alignment, and deconvolution, resulting in a peak list with mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) and retention time (RT). Multivariate statistical analyses were conducted using the ropls package (R package, version 1.6.2): principal component analysis (PCA) for unsupervised visualization of group distribution; partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) to assess model validity; and orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) to maximize separation between Control and Corilagin groups and identify metabolites driving group differences. Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) scores were calculated from the OPLS-DA model, and metabolites with VIP > 1.0 and p < 0.05 (two-tailed Student’s t-test) were selected as significantly altered. Fold change (Fc) was computed as the ratio of mean peak area in Corilagin group versus Control group. One-dimensional statistical analysis (Student’s t-test) was applied to assess significance of individual metabolite changes. Finally, metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis were performed using both KEGG and HMDB databases to annotate and functionally interpret the differential metabolites.

For in vivo experiments, male C57BL/6 mice (SPF grade, 6–8 weeks old, purchased from Sipeifu Company, Beijing) were used; 60 mice were randomly assigned to five groups: Control (n=11), E. coli model (n=13), low-dose FSHJ (L-FSHJ, 1.59 g/kg/day, n=12), medium-dose FSHJ (M-FSHJ, 3.18 g/kg/day, n=12), and high-dose FSHJ (H-FSHJ, 6.36 g/kg/day, n=12); sepsis-induced ARDS was established via intravenous tail vein injection of E. coli ATCC 25922 (2×10⁸ CFU per mouse); drug treatment began 3 h post-infection (oral gavage, 300 μL twice), and animals were euthanized at 36 h post-infection. Lung tissues, serum, and whole blood were collected for histopathology, ELISA, qRT-PCR, Western blot, and LC-MS analyses. Additionally, E. coli cultures were prepared by overnight growth in LB broth (180 rpm, 12–16 h), followed by centrifugation (5000 rpm, 10 min) and resuspension in sterile PBS to 2×10⁹ CFU/mL.
complete_workflow_description: The research employed a multi-step integrative bioinformatics and experimental workflow. First, active ingredients and their molecular targets of FSHJ were identified using the Batman-TCM 2.0 database with filter criteria: 'known targets only', adjusted P-value cutoff ≤ 0.05, and druggable score ≥ 0.9; duplicate targets were removed. Second, sepsis-induced ARDS disease targets were retrieved from GeneCards, OMIM, and TTD databases using keywords 'Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome' and 'Sepsis'; duplicates were removed and intersected with FSHJ targets via Venn diagram analysis. Third, a component–target–disease network was constructed and visualized in Cytoscape (v3.8.2), with topological parameters (degree, betweenness centrality, closeness centrality) calculated to identify core components. Fourth, a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was built using STRING database (species: Homo sapiens, default confidence score ≥ 0.4, no additional filters), and topological analysis (degree, betweenness, closeness, and Maximal Clique Centrality [MCC] algorithm) was performed in Cytoscape v3.8.2 to identify the top 10 core targets. Fifth, functional enrichment analysis (GO and KEGG) was conducted using R package clusterProfiler with adjusted P-value < 0.05 as significance threshold; top 10 GO terms and top 20 KEGG pathways were visualized. Sixth, molecular docking was performed using AutoDock Vina: top 10 compounds (by MCC ranking) and top 10 targets were selected; compound 3D structures (SDF format) were obtained from PubChem, and protein crystal structures were retrieved from RCSB PDB; docking affinity cutoff for stable binding was set to ≤ −5.0 kcal/mol. Seventh, molecular dynamics simulation (MDS) was carried out using GROMACS 2022: ligands were parameterized with General Amber Force Field (GAFF), proteins with AMBER ff14SB force field; solvation used TIP3P water molecules and periodic boundary conditions; simulations ran under NPT ensemble (298 K, 1 bar) with V-rescale thermostat and Berendsen barostat; integration timestep was 2 fs (bond lengths constrained via LINCS); non-bonded interactions cutoff was 1.0 nm, long-range electrostatics computed via Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) with 1.2 nm cutoff; neighbor lists updated every 10 steps; equilibration: 100 ps NVT + 100 ps NPT; production phase: 100 ns, with trajectory snapshots saved every 10 ps for RMSD, Rg, RMSF, and hydrogen bond analysis using VMD and PyMOL. Eighth, experimental validation included LC-MS (Thermo Fisher UHPLC-Q Exactive with Accucore™ C18 column at 40 °C, mobile phase: acetonitrile [A] and 0.1% formic acid [B], flow rate 0.40 mL/min, injection volume 5 μL, m/z 100–1500, positive/negative ion modes), histopathology (H&E staining of 4-μm paraffin sections), lung wet/dry ratio measurement, ELISA (Elabscience kits for IL-6 and TNF-α), qRT-PCR (using Animal Total RNA Isolation Kit, AccuRT DNA removal kit, EvaGreen Express 2× qPCR MasterMix-No Dye; cycling: 95 °C for 5 min, then 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s; melting curve: 60–95 °C, 0.3 °C increments), and Western blot (RIPA lysis with phosphatase inhibitors, BCA assay, SDS-PAGE, PVDF transfer, blocking with 5% skim milk, primary antibodies: anti-TLR4, anti-NF-κB p65, anti-GAPDH, incubated at 4 °C overnight; HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, chemiluminescent detection via Clinox ChemiScope 6100). Statistical analysis used GraphPad Prism v8: data expressed as mean ± SD; comparisons performed via independent-sample t-tests or one-way ANOVA with Fisher’s LSD post-hoc test; significance threshold P < 0.05.
