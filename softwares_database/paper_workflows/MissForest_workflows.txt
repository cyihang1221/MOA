complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end evaluation workflow for missing value imputation in metabolomics: (1) Four real or simulated metabolomics datasets were selected to represent diverse data structures and missingness mechanisms (MCAR, MAR, MNAR); (2) For each dataset, missing values were either naturally present or artificially induced to reflect each missingness type; (3) Eight imputation methods — zero, half minimum (HM), mean, median, kNN, RF (via missForest), SVD (via pcaMethods), and QRILC (via imputeLCMD) — were applied separately under appropriate missingness assumptions (kNN, RF, SVD, mean, median for MCAR/MAR; kNN, RF, SVD, QRILC, zero, HM for MNAR); (4) Imputation accuracy was evaluated using normalized root mean squared error (NRMSE) and NRMSE-based sum of ranks (SOR); (5) Downstream analytical robustness was assessed via PCA/PLS-Procrustes analysis to examine preservation of sample distribution, and via Student’s t-test followed by correlation analysis to evaluate impact on univariate statistical inference; (6) Results were synthesized to recommend RF for MCAR/MAR and QRILC for left-censored MNAR, culminating in the development of the MetImp web-tool.

related_tool: MissForest
sample_description: The study used proteomic datasets generated via label-free mass spectrometry, processed with two protein identification algorithms—FragPipe and MaxQuant. These datasets consisted of quantitative protein abundance values across biological samples (exact number, species, tissue type, or experimental conditions not specified in the provided text). Missing values were artificially introduced into subsets of these datasets to simulate varying missingness patterns: missing value (MV) rates of 10%, 25%, and 50%, and missing-not-at-random (MNAR) proportions of 0%, 20%, 40%, 80%, and 100%. Each MV/MNAR combination was replicated 10 times, yielding 150 synthetic test datasets. Additionally, real proteomic datasets containing natural missing values were used in the second evaluation stage, though specific sample metadata (e.g., organism, tissue, replicate count, or experimental design) is not detailed in the provided Methods section.
complete_workflow_description: The global analysis workflow consisted of two sequential stages. First, a simulation-based benchmarking stage: (i) starting from complete (no-MV) proteomic matrices derived from FragPipe or MaxQuant, artificial missing values were introduced using Jin et al.'s algorithm to generate 15 combinations of MV rate (10%, 25%, 50%) and MNAR proportion (0%, 20%, 40%, 80%, 100%), each replicated 10 times (150 total datasets); (ii) ten imputation methods—including MissForest (RF), kNN, LLS, LOD, ND, SampMin, LSA, BPCA, PPCA, and SVD—were applied to each dataset; (iii) imputation accuracy was quantified using NRMSE (from missForest) and classification performance was assessed by comparing imputed-data-based differential expression calls (adjusted p < 0.05) against ground-truth calls from the original complete data, enabling ROC curve construction and AUC calculation. Second, an empirical validation stage: (i) the same imputation methods were applied to full real proteomic datasets containing natural missing values; (ii) performance was evaluated based on true-positive (TP) protein identification counts, fold-change consistency, and PCA-based data structure preservation. All analyses were implemented in R v.4.4.0, leveraging pcaMethods (for LLS, SVD, BPCA, PPCA), VIM (for kNN), missForest (for RF and NRMSE), and custom in-house implementations (LOD, ND, SampMin, LSA). Logarithmization of data was tested as a preprocessing step across all methods.
source_paper: Grzegorz et al. (2024), International journal of molecular sciences, DOI: 10.3390/ijms252413491, PMID: 39769253

related_tool: MissForest
sample_description: Ten real-world medical datasets were used, each containing both numerical and categorical variables. These datasets represent clinical or biomedical data (though specific disease domains, sample sizes, or tissue/biological sources are not detailed in the provided text). Missing values were artificially introduced under the missing completely at random (MCAR) mechanism at five rates: 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50%. For each missing rate, the simulation was repeated ten times, resulting in multiple incomplete dataset instances per dataset and rate combination. Categorical variables were preprocessed using label encoding prior to imputation.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with ten complete medical datasets. For each dataset, missing values are simulated under the MCAR mechanism at five distinct missing rates (10%–50%), with each rate repeated ten times to generate stochastic variability. Five imputation methods—mean/mode, kNN (k=5), MICE (generating 5 imputed datasets, with multicollinearity threshold set to 1), MissForest (10 iterations, 100 forests), and the novel RFE-MF (using RF-RFE with enhanced resampling over 10 cross-validation folds, and 3 iterations for imputation)—are independently applied to each incomplete dataset instance. Categorical variables are label-encoded before imputation. Imputation quality is evaluated per method using NRMSE for numerical variables and PFC (proportion of falsely classified entries) for categorical variables. Statistical significance of performance differences is assessed via paired samples t-tests across the ten repetitions. All implementations are performed in R.
source_paper: Ya-Han et al. (2024), BMC medical research methodology, DOI: 10.1186/s12874-024-02392-2, PMID: 39516783
