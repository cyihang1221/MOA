========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: mixOmics
sample_description: The study utilized cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples from a total of 51 individuals: 11 normal (control) CSF samples purchased from commercial biorepositories (BioIVT, Discovery Life Sciences, and Lee Biosolutions), 30 samples from the Children's Brain Tumor Tissue Consortium (CBTTC), 5 from Johns Hopkins University (JHU), and 5 from Johns Hopkins All Children's Hospital (JHACH). Among these, 40 CSF samples were from medulloblastoma (MB) patients representing different molecular subtypes, and 11 were from non-MB (normal) individuals. Cell-free CSF samples were snap-frozen immediately after collection without further processing and stored at −80 °C until use. For omics analyses, specific volumes were used: 0.2 mL for total RNA isolation and RNA-seq library preparation; 0.1 mL for qRT-PCR validation; 50 μL for global metabolomics; and the remaining supernatant from methanol precipitation (500 μL) was used for global lipidomics. A subset of 30 patient CSF samples (6 normal and 24 MB) with complete transcriptomic, metabolomic, and lipidomic datasets was selected for integrative multi-omics analysis using mixOmics. One normal sample was excluded from downstream RNA analysis due to extremely low gene counts; sample CBTTC-3459 was removed from circRNA analysis due to outlier circRNA counts; and for metabolomic/lipidomic quality control, solvent blanks, extraction blanks (without internal standard), neat quality controls (amino acid internal standards mixtures), and pooled samples (normal pooled mixture of all normal replicates and cancer pooled mixture of all cancerous samples, each 5 μL) were prepared.
complete_workflow_description: The research employed multiple bioinformatic and computational tools for data processing and analysis. For RNA-seq preprocessing: fastp (v. not specified, cited as [19]) was used for quality control and filtering of FASTQ reads. STAR (v2.7, cited as [20]) aligned filtered reads to the Ensembl human genome v100. featureCounts (from Subread package, cited as [21]) quantified read counts from BAM files. Low-count genes (total expression across samples < 2) were removed; one normal sample was excluded due to extremely low gene counts. Normalization used trimmed mean of M-values (TMM) via edgeR (cited as [22]); only genes with counts per million (CPM) > 2 in at least three samples were retained. Differential expression analysis for linear RNAs used limma-voom (cited as [23]). For circular RNA (circRNA) analysis: bowtie2 (cited as [24]) mapped reads to human ribosomal DNA (GenBank: U13369.1) to remove rRNA; unmapped reads were extracted using samtools and bedtools; BWA-MEM aligned remaining reads to Ensembl v100 human reference genome; CIRI2 (cited as [25]) detected circRNAs from SAM files; circM (cited as [26]) aggregated circRNA counts into an RNA-vs-sample matrix; DESeq2 (cited as [27]) performed differential circRNA analysis (with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction). For metabolomics: MZmine 2.0 (cited as [28]) performed peak picking and metabolite identification by matching retention time and m/z to an in-house library of >1000 metabolites (level 1 identification per Metabolomics Standards Initiative); MetaboAnalyst 5.0 (cited as [30]) handled data processing with parameters including missing value imputation (half the minimum positive value), relative standard deviation (RSD)-based filtering, sum normalization, log transformation, and autoscaling. For lipidomics: LipidMatch Flow (integrated with MZmine 2) performed file conversion, peak picking, blank filtration, lipid annotation (cited as [29]), and combination of positive/negative mode datasets; lipid identifications leveraged data-dependent MS/MS analysis. Pathway analysis used KEGG (via MetaboAnalyst) with hypergeometric testing and relative betweenness centrality (p < 0.05). All plots (heatmaps, volcano plots, PCA, hierarchical clustering) were generated using R version 4.0.3 and Python packages as noted.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: mixOmics
sample_description: The study used age-matched serum samples from 9 healthy, 10 Barrett's esophagus (BE), and 10 esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) male patients. Serum glycoprotein enrichment was performed using 20 individual lectin-coated magnetic beads (AAL, BPL, DSA, EPHA, GNL, JAC, LPHA, MAA, NPL, SNA, STL, UEA, HPA, PSA, WFA, WGA, SBA, ConA, HAA, and ECA) targeting sub-glycoproteomes. Each sample contained 50 μg of serum, to which 10 picomoles of chicken ovalbumin (a non-homologous, lectin-binding internal standard) was spiked prior to denaturation. For biomarker qualification, an independent patient cohort was screened using LeMBA-MRM-MS for six lectins (AAL, EPHA, JAC, NPL, PSA, WGA); lectin-beads were prepared in a single batch to minimize experimental variation, and serum samples were randomized. Additional 19 population control (electoral roll) serum samples were included to assess confounders (reflux frequency, BMI, smoking, alcohol). All samples underwent standardized processing including on-bead trypsin digestion, LC-MS/MS or MRM-MS analysis, and rigorous quality control (e.g., 100 fmol/μl predigested BSA peptides pre/post plate; reference ion monitoring; iRT calibration; repeated runs for reproducibility).
complete_workflow_description: The method involved a multi-stage pipeline: (1) Lectin Magnetic Bead Array (LeMBA): 20 lectin-coated Dynabeads used for serum glycoprotein enrichment; on-bead trypsin digestion performed per manufacturer protocols with modifications in supplemental Methods. (2) Discovery LC-MS/MS: Agilent 6520 QTOF coupled with HPLC-Chip (160 nl C18 trap, 75 μm × 150 mm analytical column); gradient: 6–46% Buffer B (90% ACN/0.1% FA) over 45 min; MS1: 2 GHz extended dynamic range, 8 spectra/sec; MS/MS: 4 spectra/MS1; dynamic exclusion: 2 MS/MS within 0.25 min; precursor mass tolerance ±20 ppm; product mass tolerance ±50 ppm; carbamidomethylation (fixed), oxidized methionine (variable); database search against SwissProt human (20,242 entries, Jan 2012) using Spectrum Mill (Rev.B.04.00.127); filtering: protein score >15, peptide score >6, %SPI >60, FDR calculated via reversed database. (3) GlycoSelector pipeline: Custom R/PHP-based platform integrating mixOmics tools; steps included data normalization (per-lectin, ovalbumin-peptide intensity-based), outlier detection (graphical QC), sPLS-DA + stability analysis, and Group Binding Difference (on/off) analysis. (4) Qualification via LeMBA-MRM-MS: Agilent 6490 triple quadrupole with 1290 UHPLC (AdvanceBio Peptide Mapping column, 150 × 2.1 mm, 2.7 μm); gradient: 3–30% B (100% ACN/0.1% FA) over 20 min; MRM parameters: CE = 0.036 × m/z −4.8; Q1/Q3 resolution 0.7 Da; capillary voltage 4000 V; drying gas 15 L/min at 250 °C; sheath gas 11 L/min at 250 °C; nebulizer 30 psi; dwell time 20 ms; delta RT 2.5–4 min. (5) Skyline v2.1.0.4936 used for spectral library building (.mzXML/.pepXML), transition ranking, iRT calibration, peak integration, and normalization (median per lectin, then ovalbumin-SIS corrected, with separate handling of methionine/non-methionine peptides). (6) Statistical analysis: Kruskal-Wallis test (p<0.05) for univariate significance and confounder assessment; sPLS-DA stability >70% for multivariate selection; DAVID for GO enrichment with default settings and FDR correction.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: mixOmics
sample_description: The study enrolled women diagnosed with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), who received an 8-week probiotic intervention composed of Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains. Biological samples included stool for metagenomic sequencing, serum and urine for untargeted metabolomics (via LC-MS), and peripheral blood or relevant tissue (not explicitly specified but implied for qRT-PCR) for host gene expression analysis of insulin signaling (e.g., insulin receptor, AKT), inflammation (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-10, TGF-β), oxidative stress, and gut barrier markers (ZO-1, CLDN1). A paired longitudinal design was used — each participant served as her own control, with samples collected at baseline and after 8 weeks of probiotic supplementation. Sample size was determined a priori using G*Power (effect size = 0.8, α = 0.05, power = 80%), and the cohort met the minimum required N.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end multi-omics workflow began with sample collection (stool, serum, urine, and host tissue/blood) from GDM women at baseline and after 8 weeks of probiotic supplementation. Metagenomic sequencing data were pre-processed using QIIME2 with DADA2 for quality filtering, denoising, chimera removal, and ASV calling, followed by taxonomic classification against the SILVA database. Untargeted metabolomics data from LC-MS were analyzed using MetaboAnalyst 5.0 for preprocessing, normalization, and statistical testing. Host gene expression was assessed via qRT-PCR for selected targets related to insulin signaling, inflammation, oxidative stress, and gut barrier function. All omics datasets were integrated using R v4.2.0: PCA and PLS-DA were first applied for global pattern visualization; then mixOmics (sPLS-DA with 10-fold CV and 1,000 permutations) identified cross-omics discriminative features and VIP-ranked biomarkers. Spearman rank correlation (|r| ≥ 0.6, FDR-adjusted p < 0.05) was used to construct microbe-metabolite-host association networks, which were visualized and topologically analyzed in Cytoscape v3.9.1 using NetworkAnalyzer to identify keystone regulatory hubs. Multiple testing correction applied the Benjamini–Hochberg FDR method throughout. Pathway enrichment analysis leveraged KEGG and MetaboAnalyst. Statistical analyses were complemented by SPSS v26.0 for confirmatory tests.
source_paper: Xiaohua et al. (2026), Frontiers in cellular and infection microbiology, DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2026.1782744, PMID: 41918874
