>Materials and methods   Data collection and preparation, and experimental animals   Data collection and preparation   The SMILE number corresponding to the PCB2 component name or CAS number was found in the PubChem database. Then, the SMILE number was used to predict the target genes in the SwissTargetPrediction database. The target genes with a Probability value of 0 were removed, and 49 PCB2 drug target genes were obtained. These were then corrected to the official gene names using the UniProt database.   Data collection was carried out using the GeneCards database, OMIM database, and DisGeNET database respectively. 753 target genes were obtained from the GeneCards database, 165 target genes were found from the OMIM database, and 929 target genes were obtained from the DisGeNET database. The targets obtained from the three databases were combined, duplicate items were removed, and they were corrected to the standard gene names using the UniProt database. Eventually, 1812 MS disease target genes were derived.   The dataset  GSE131282  of MS was acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The samples were brain tissues from normal controls or MS patients. Experimental animals   A total of 40 male C57BL/6 mice (aged 7—8 weeks, weighing 20-22 g) of SPF grade were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (License number: SCXK (Beijing) 2016—0006). The mice were fed in the SPF-grade animal room of Shanxi University of Chinese Medicine. After one week of adaptive feeding, relevant experiments were conducted. All experimental operations were approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanxi University of Chinese Medicine. Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and Unsupervised Clustering   After the data was prepared, the “limma” package in R language was used to analyze the gene expression data between the MS group and the HC group to screen for DEGs. The specific parameter settings were a P value < 0.05 and |log2FC|> 1. A total of 857 DEGs were identified in this step, including 3 up-regulated genes and 854 down-regulated genes. Subsequently, a volcano plot representation was used to visually display the differential analysis results of each group.   The “ConsensusClusterPlus” package in R language was utilized to implement the Consensus Clustering algorithm to conduct an in-depth analysis of the transcriptome data of patients in the disease group. Through consensus clustering, 142 cases were divided into two optimal subtypes, named Cluster 1 and Cluster 2 respectively. Immune infiltration analysis   The appropriate R package "pheatmap" was used to draw a heatmap to visually display the expression differences of DEGs between different subtypes. Meanwhile, relevant R packages were used to conduct immune infiltration analysis, and CD274 gene expression levels were used to divide high and low groups. calculate the infiltration numbers of different immune cell types in the samples, and reveal the biological characteristics of the two disease subtypes. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)   To conduct an in-depth analysis of the gene expression data in  GSE131282 , the "WGCNA" package in R language was used to perform WGCNA on the gene expression data in  GSE131282 . Firstly, by calculating the network connectivity and scale independence under different soft thresholds, an appropriate soft threshold was determined. Then, the hierarchical clustering method was used to observe the clustering trend of the gene expression data, and a correlation heatmap was drawn to show the co-expression relationships among genes. Finally, by calculating the correlations between modules and phenotypes, the co-expression modules significantly related to the disease phenotype were determined, and a network heatmap was drawn to visually display the co-expression patterns and relationships of the selected genes in the weighted gene co-expression network. Gene ontology (GO) analysis, GSEA enrichment analysis, and single-gene GSEA enrichment analysis   We used the MS disease genes, DEGs genes, genes in the blue module of WGCNA, and the predicted target genes of PCB2 to draw a Venn diagram and take the intersection, obtaining 25 intersection genes. The “c5.go.v2024.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” data and the “clusterProfiler”, “ggplot2”, and “enrichplot” packages in R language were used to conduct GO analysis and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) on the screened key genes to reveal the biological processes significantly enriched by these genes. Similarly, single-gene GSEA was conducted on KCNH2, PTGS1, ESR1, and VEGFA using R language to verify the previous results. Screening of key genes by 8 machine learning algorithms   The machine learning packages in R language (such as “glmnet” for the LASSO algorithm, “glm” for the generalized linear model, etc.) were used to screen the predicted targets of PCB2. The inverse cumulative distribution residuals were calculated and box plots were drawn to evaluate the prediction performance of the models, and the feature importance was calculated to assess the contribution of different genes to the model prediction. We used the 33 genes screened by 8 machine learning algorithms and the 25 intersection genes to draw a Venn diagram and take the intersection again, obtaining 21 intersection genes. Then, according to the ranking of the frequency of appearance in the top ten of the feature importance ranking of the 8 machine learning algorithms, the top 8 genes were screened out. Construction of nomograms and diagnostic models and evaluation of their effects   To comprehensively understand the diagnostic effects and significance of these biomarker genes, relevant packages in R language (such as “rms” or “pROC”, etc.) were used to construct nomograms for the 8 screened key genes. We constructed nomograms and used the ROC curve to evaluate their diagnostic value. The results showed that these genes had relatively high prediction and discrimination abilities. In addition, individual ROC analysis and nomogram effect evaluation for each gene were also carried out, and a Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) network was constructed to reveal the interaction relationships among genes. Construction of the hub Genes—transcription factors (TFs)—microRNA (miRNA) regulatory network   The miRNAs of the 4 key genes were predicted through the miRWalk database ( https://mirwalk.umm.uniheidelberg.de/ ). The screening parameters were set as Score ≥ 0.95, the target site was limited to the 3UTR region, and the miRDB database was selected for verification (parameter setting was 1). Subsequently, the iRegulon plugin in the Cytoscape software was used to predict the transcription factors (TFs). The normalized enrichment score (NES) > 3 was set as the screening threshold to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results. Finally, the prediction results were integrated, and the TF-miRNA-target gene regulatory network was constructed through the Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1) to visually display the regulatory relationships and further analyze the potential regulatory mechanisms. Molecular docking   In this study, PCB2 was selected as the small molecule drug component. The 3D structure of the active ingredient (ligand) of the drug was downloaded from the PubChem database. The 3D structures of the receptor proteins KCNH2, PTGS1, ESR1, and VEGFA were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) database. Then, the molecular docking was carried out using AutoDock 1.5.6 software, and the optimal result docking diagrams were drawn using PyMOL 2.5.0 software. Establishment of the CPZ animal model   Forty mice were randomly and evenly divided into four groups according to body weight, namely the Normal group, the Normal + PCB2 group, the CPZ + NS group, and the CPZ + PCB2 group. The Normal group and the Normal + PCB2 group were fed with normal feed, while the CPZ + NS group and the CPZ + PCB2 group were fed with feed containing 0.2% CPZ (Sigma, USA, CAS number: 370-81-0) for 10 weeks to construct a chronic demyelinating mouse model. At the end of the 10th week, the CPZ feeding was stopped and replaced with normal feed. Starting from the 11th week, the Normal group and the CPZ + NS group were intraperitoneally injected with normal saline (NS), and the Normal + PCB2 group and the CPZ + PCB2 group were intraperitoneally injected with PCB2 (Chengdu Rufens Biological Technology Co., Ltd., purity ≥ 98%, at a dose of 60 mg/kg/day) for a total of 14 days. During the entire experiment, routine cage maintenance was carried out no less than three times a week, and the daily status of the mice was observed and recorded. Behavioral tests   The mice needed to adapt to the surrounding environment of the behavioral experiments in advance to avoid affecting the experimental results.     Open Field Test (OFT): The mice were placed in a quiet and clean open field facing the wall of the box. The behavioral software was used to record the activity of the mice in the open field for 10 min. Four mice could be detected simultaneously each time. After the test, the open field box was cleaned with 75% alcohol to exclude interference from other factors. After drying, the next group of experiments was continued.   Elevated Plus Maze (EPM) Test: EPM consisted of two open arms and two closed arms of equal size that crossed each other. The mice were placed in the central area facing the open arm and with their backs to the experimenter. The behavior of the mice was recorded, and the maze was cleaned with 75% alcohol before the next group of experiments was carried out.   T Maze Test: First, the mice were trained to be familiar with the left and right arms and to identify the position of the bait arm. Then, after fasting for 24 h, the mice were placed in the main arm of the T maze and allowed to move freely. The number of times the mice entered the bait arm and the distance were recorded to observe their working memory ability.   Mouse Climbing Pole Test (CPT): A metal pole with a diameter of 2 cm and a height of 70 cm was selected. A dome with a diameter of 4.5 cm was made at the upper end of the metal pole using cloth strips, and the top and body of the pole were wrapped with gauze. Meanwhile, equipment for recording the behavior of the mice, such as a video camera or a stopwatch, was prepared. First, adaptive training was carried out to make the mice familiar with the process of climbing from the top of the pole to the ground. In the testing stage, the time required for the mice to climb from the top of the pole to the ground and the number of mistakes (falling, slipping, etc.) were recorded. Three records were taken, and the median value was selected to obtain reliable results.   Sample collection and specimen preparation   After the behavioral experiments, 5 mice were randomly selected from each group and anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (50 mg/kg). The chest was opened to expose the heart, the right auricle was cut open, and the heart was perfused with normal saline until the liver turned white. Then, the heart was perfused with 4% paraformaldehyde. The brain tissues were dissected out, fixed with paraformaldehyde, and then placed in 10%, 20%, and 30% sucrose solutions for gradient dehydration. After that, they were embedded with OTC, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored in a -80 °C refrigerator for standby use. When in use, a cryostat was used to cut the brain tissue into 10 μm slices for pathological and immunofluorescence staining. Another 5 mice in each group were anesthetized in the same way as above and then only perfused with normal saline. The brains were taken out, the corpus callosum (CC) and the surrounding brain tissues were separated, weighed, and placed in sterile EP tubes. Part of the tissues was taken out, and lysis buffer was added according to the weight. The tissues were ground until a homogeneous state was achieved. The samples were lysed on ice for 30 min, and vortexed every 5 min. Then, they were transferred to a centrifuge at 4 °C, centrifuged at 12,000 rpm/min for 15 min. The supernatant was transferred to a new sterile EP tube using a pipette. After the protein concentration was measured, the samples were aliquoted, which was the brain tissue protein solution. To reduce protein degradation, the aliquots were placed in a -80 °C refrigerator together with the remaining brain tissues for subsequent detection. Luxol Fast Blue (LFB) and TrueGold staining detection   For Luxol Fast Blue staining (Solarbio, G3245, China), mouse brain sections were initially warmed and soaked in 70% ethanol for 15 min before being incubated in the staining solution at 56–60 °C overnight. The next day, the sections were thoroughly rinsed with 95% ethanol and deionized water to remove the excess stain, and then treated with a mixture of 75% ethanol, deionized water, and 0.05% lithium carbonate to clearly differentiate the gray and white matter. After a series of ethanol dehydration steps and xylene clearing, the sections were mounted with neutral resin for microscopic examination.   For TrueGold staining (Ousaisi Biotechnology Co., Ltd, BK-AC001, China), brain slices were warmed and dried at 37 °C for 30 min before diluting the stain and terminator in double-distilled water. The slices were then stained in the dark at 45 °C for 30 min, rinsed with (Phosphate Buffered Saline) PBS, and treated with the stain terminator at 45 °C for 2–3 min. After three washes with PBS, the slices were dried, mounted, and imaged using a microscope. Immunofluorescence staining   The prepared frozen brain tissue slices were washed with PBS three times, 5 min each time. The primary antibody was added to the PBS solution containing 0.3% Triton X-100 and 1% (bovine serum albumin) BSA, suspended, and then dropped onto the slices. The slices were incubated in a humid box at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the slices were rinsed with PBS three times, 5 min each time, and the secondary antibody corresponding to the species was added and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The slices were washed with PBS three times again, and then mounted with an anti-fluorescence quenching mounting medium containing DAPI. After 10 min, the results could be observed under a fluorescence microscope to detect the expression of Myelin basic protein (MBP, IF 1:200 dilution, Bio-Rad, aa82–87), Adenomatous Polyposis Coli Protein (APC, IF 1:200 dilution, Bioworld, MB62467, USA), and Neuron-Glial Antigen 2 Antibody (NG2, IF 1:500 dilution, Millipore, 05-710, USA) in the CC region. Finally, the fluorescence density was analyzed using Image J fiji 2.14.0. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and enzyme assay detection   Take the above-mentioned brain tissue protein solutions from each group and follow the instructions of ELISA and micro enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits. Read the optical density (OD) values using an ELISA reader to detect the content or activity of Interleukin-1 Beta (IL-1β, Invitrogen, 88-7013A-88, USA), Interleukin-6 (IL-6, Invitrogen, 88-7064-22, USA), Interleukin-10 (IL-10, Invitrogen, 88-7105-22, USA), Tumor Necrosis Factor-Alpha (TNF-α, Invitrogen, 88-7324-88, USA), Catalase (CAT, Pulilai Gene Technology Co., Ltd., E2030, China), Superoxide dismutase (SOD, Nanjing Jiancheng, A003-4-1, China), and Glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px, Nanjing Jiancheng, A005-1-2, China) in brain homogenate. Reverse transcription—polymerase chain reaction (RT—PCR) detection   An appropriate amount of brain tissue was placed in a mortar pre-cooled with liquid nitrogen. Liquid nitrogen was slowly added, and the tissue was continuously ground into powder with a pestle and then placed in an EP tube. Total RNA was extracted using the M5 Universal RNA Mini Kit. After measuring the concentration, cDNA was synthesized using the TaKaRa reverse transcription kit. The 2 × M5 HiPer SYBR Premix EsTaq kit was selected for real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR amplification. Finally, the relative expression levels of genes were calculated through the  2 - Δ Δ C t  formula to detect the gene expression levels of KCNH2, PTGS1, ESR1, and VEGFA in brain tissues. Statistical processing   All statistics were performed using R software (version 4.4.2) and GraphPad Prism 8.0.2. For all analyses,  P  < 0.05 was set as the level of statistical significance. The measurement data conforming to the normal distribution were expressed as  x ¯  ± SD. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was selected for comparison among multiple groups of data, and Tukey’s post-hoc test was used for pairwise comparisons between different groups.
