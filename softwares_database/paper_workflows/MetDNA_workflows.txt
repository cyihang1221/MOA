========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: MetDNA
sample_description: Urine samples were collected from 23 patients with pathologically confirmed stage II–III prostate adenocarcinoma (PCa) before and after carbon ion radiotherapy (CIRT): specifically, within 4 hours prior to the first CIRT fraction and within 4 hours after completion of the last fraction. Samples were immediately stored at 4°C, filtered through 0.22 μm membrane filters to remove bacterial contamination, and processed by adding 800 μL of chilled methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) to each 200 μL thawed urine sample. The mixture was centrifuged, supernatant transferred, evaporated to dryness, and reconstituted in 100 μL chilled acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes (50 μL each) from all individual samples. All samples were normalized to creatinine levels for metabolite quantification.
complete_workflow_description: Metabolomic data processing followed a standardized pipeline: (1) Raw data from AB SCIEX ExionLC ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to 500R QTOF MS/MS were imported into the R platform for peak detection and dataset alignment; (2) Metabolite identification was performed using MetDNA; (3) Processed peak tables were uploaded to MetaboAnalyst (https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/MetaboAnalyst/ModuleView.xhtml) for statistical analysis and pathway enrichment; (4) Statistical analyses included Wilcoxon signed-rank test (for pre- vs. post-CIRT comparisons), Mann–Whitney U test and Pearson χ² test (for SIB vs. non-SIB group comparisons), volcano plot analysis (FDR < 0.05 and |FC| > 2 for pre-post; p < 0.05 and |FC| > 2 for intergroup), PCA and PLS-DA (with cross-validation details in Supplementary Figure 1), and pathway analysis (FDR < 0.05, impact > 0.2); (5) Metabolite concentrations were expressed as peak area and normalized to urinary creatinine levels; (6) Heatmaps, box plots, and volcano plots were generated within MetaboAnalyst.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: MetDNA
sample_description: The study enrolled 15 patients with pathologically confirmed prostate adenocarcinoma (PCa) from July 2020 to December 2020. Urine samples were collected in two time points: (1) within 4 hours before receiving the first carbon ion radiotherapy (CIRT) fraction, and (2) within 4 hours after completing the last CIRT fraction. Each urine sample (200 μL) was processed by filtration through a 0.22-μm membrane to remove bacteria, followed by addition of 800 μL chilled methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v), centrifugation, supernatant extraction, evaporation to dryness, and reconstitution in 200 μL chilled acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes (50 μL each) from all individual samples. Three blanks and six QC replicates were injected at the start of the analytical batch for column conditioning, and QC samples were analyzed every ten injections; auto-calibrations were performed every five analyses.
complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow consisted of: (1) Sample preparation as described above; (2) High-throughput UPLC-MS/MS analysis using an ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide 1.7 μm column (2.1 × 100 mm) with mobile phase A (water with 10 mM NH4FA and 0.1% formic acid) and mobile phase B (acetonitrile:water = 95:5 v/v with 10 mM NH4FA and 0.1% formic acid) under a 17-min gradient program; (3) Data acquisition in electrospray ionization (ESI) mode; (4) Peak detection and dataset alignment using XCMS Online; (5) Metabolite identification using both MetDNA and MS-DIAL software; (6) Statistical and pathway analysis performed in MetaboAnalyst 5.0, including volcano plot analysis (fold change > 2, FDR < 0.05), unsupervised PCA, supervised PLS-DA and sparse PLS-DA (sPLS-DA), hierarchical cluster analysis (HCA), and pathway enrichment analysis (FDR < 0.05, impact > 2); (7) Metabolite concentrations were quantified as peak areas and normalized to urinary creatinine levels.

========== paper_workflow entry 3 ==========
related_tool: MetDNA
sample_description: The sample used in the research consists of metabolomic data acquired via liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS), including MS1 peak tables (.csv format) containing annotated m/z and retention times (RTs), and MS2 spectral data files in .mgf or .msp format. The MS1 peak table is generated from raw MS files using peak-picking software such as XCMS (version not specified, cited as ref 27) or MS-DIAL (cited as ref 36). MS2 data are converted from raw files using ProteoWizard (version 3.0.6150); support includes data from data-dependent acquisition (DDA), data-independent acquisition (DIA), and targeted MS2 acquisition. For DIA data processed with MS-DIAL, .msp-formatted MS2 files are directly supported. Theoretical RT prediction relies on experimental RTs from annotated seed metabolites matched to an in-house standard spectral library, and the chemical space is restricted to 6439 organic metabolites with strict chemical structures (excluding 1200 non-organic or structurally ambiguous formulas from the original KEGG-derived metabolic reaction network of 7639 nodes).
complete_workflow_description: The MetDNA workflow comprises six core sequential and recursive procedures: (1) Data import: MS1 peak table (.csv) and MS2 files (.mgf or .msp) are loaded; .mgf files are generated via ProteoWizard v3.0.6150; .msp files from MS-DIAL DIA processing bypass MS1–MS2 matching. (2) Initial seed metabolite annotation: MS1 peaks matched to MS2 spectra (±25 ppm, ±10 s); top-abundant MS2 selected per peak; matched pairs searched against in-house standard spectral library using modified DP scoring (Eq. 1); DP ≥0.8 in forward/reverse match retained; filtered by theoretical RT (±30%); resulting 50–200 high-confidence annotations become round-0 seed metabolites. (3) Isotope and adduct annotation: for each seed, theoretical isotope peaks ([M]–[M+4]) and adducts (from Supplementary Table 5) are calculated and matched to MS1 peaks using respective tolerances and scoring functions (Eqs. 2–6); isotope annotation of adducts follows identically. (4) MRN-based recursive neighbor annotation: seed metabolites mapped to the metabolic reaction network (MRN, 6439 nodes, 9603 edges, built from KEGG RPAIR/COMPOUND databases downloaded 7 March 2017 via KEGGREST); neighbor metabolites retrieved at reaction steps 1–3; surrogate MS2 spectra assigned; theoretical m/z (for adducts) and predicted RTs (via QSRR model) generated; experimental MS1 (±25 ppm), RT (±30%), and MS2 (dot-product ≥0.5) matched; Score_iden (Eq. 7) computed and used for ranking; isotope annotation repeated for newly annotated neighbors. (5) Recursive seed selection and iteration: newly annotated neighbor metabolites become seeds for next round; process repeats until no new seeds emerge. (6) Confidence assignment and redundancy removal: peaks grouped by annotation and RT (3 s window) into peak groups; grades assigned (1–4); recursive redundancy resolution prioritizes higher-grade annotations—first removing grade-4 annotations when higher-grade alternatives exist, then reassigning grades and repeating until redundancy stabilizes; final outputs include pathway enrichment (hypergeometric test on KEGG pathways), quantitative pathway expression (Pareto-scaled intensities averaged per pathway), and RT prediction (QSRR random forest trained on experimentally RT-annotated metabolites using optimized MD subsets for HILIC/RP).

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MetDNA
sample_description: Serum samples from 52 Chinese children (26 hypertensive and 26 age- and sex-matched normotensive controls, aged 6–11 years) recruited from the Huantai Childhood Cardiovascular Health Cohort Study baseline survey. Blood was collected after overnight fasting, centrifuged to obtain serum, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C. Metabolite extraction used precooled methanol:acetonitrile (1:1) solvent, followed by vortexing, ultrasound treatment, cold incubation, and centrifugation; supernatants were reconstituted in water/acetonitrile (1:1) prior to UHPLC-QTOF/MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow began with serum collection from 52 children (26 hypertensive, 26 normotensive), followed by standardized metabolite extraction using methanol:acetonitrile (1:1). Samples were analyzed via UHPLC-QTOF/MS in both positive and negative ion modes, with blank and pooled QC samples included. Raw .wiff files were converted to mzXML using ProteoWizard. Data preprocessing was performed using the R package XCMS (version 3.6), including noise filtering, baseline correction, peak alignment, deconvolution, retention time alignment, and missing value imputation. Peak annotation was conducted using the R package CAMERA. Low-quality features — those detected in fewer than 50% of QC samples or exhibiting relative standard deviation >30% — were removed. Metabolite identification employed an in-house MS2 database, supplemented by MetDNA for MRN-based annotation expansion. Multivariate statistical analysis included PCA for outlier detection and OPLS-DA (performed in SIMCA 14.0) to identify discriminating features, with VIP >1.0 and FDR-adjusted q <0.05 used for biomarker selection. Univariate logistic regression (in R 3.6 and SAS 9.4) assessed associations between log-transformed metabolites and hypertension status, adjusting sequentially for BMI (model 1), BMI + waist circumference (model 2), and BMI + waist circumference + lipid profile (model 3). Pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using KEGG and MetaboAnalyst. Classification performance was evaluated using ROC-AUC.
source_paper: Jiahong et al. (2021), International journal of hypertension, DOI: 10.1155/2021/6691734, PMID: 34484817

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MetDNA
sample_description: Blood samples from 100 elderly (≥65 years) critically ill COVID-19 patients admitted to the Second Hospital affiliated with Harbin Medical University between January 3 and March 30, 2023; all patients met criteria for critical-type COVID-19 (e.g., respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation, shock, or multiorgan failure), had ICU stays ≥7 days, and were partially immunized (as implied by the study’s emphasis on post-vaccination era context); samples were collected within 24 hours of ICU admission for untargeted metabolomics profiling using liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS).
complete_workflow_description: Raw LC-MS data were converted to MS2 format using MS Convert. Peak detection and alignment were performed using xcms (R package, version 1.46.0) with centWave method, ppm = 15, signal-to-noise threshold (snthr) = 10, peak width = c(5, 40), and minifrac = 0.5, generating an MS1 peak table containing m/z, retention time, and peak abundances. Metabolite annotation was carried out via MetDNA2 using the MS2 spectra and a 15 ppm mass tolerance. Annotated metabolite data were then processed in MetaboAnalyst R package: median normalization, log transformation, and auto-scaling were applied prior to multivariate analysis (e.g., PCA) and differential metabolite analysis. Clinically relevant metabolites (e.g., itaconic acid) and laboratory markers (LYM, IL-6, PCT, CRP) were selected based on univariate AUC analysis (performed in GraphPad) and statistical significance. These five variables were input into four machine learning models—KNN, Random Forest, Naive Bayes, and Decision Tree—implemented in Python 3.7; model training and internal validation used 50% split of the 100-patient derivation cohort, while external validation used the independent 50-patient cohort from another hospital. Model performance was evaluated using AUROC, sensitivity, and specificity at a 50% mortality risk threshold.
source_paper: Shanpeng et al. (2025), BMC infectious diseases, DOI: 10.1186/s12879-024-10402-3, PMID: 39748307

Metabolite extraction and sample preparation details are not explicitly described in the provided text, but the analysis focused on untargeted metabolomics of whole-organism or tissue-level metabolomes relevant to aging.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end MetDNA workflow consists of six sequential steps: (1) import of MS1 peak table (generated by XCMS) and MS2 spectra (mgf format); (2) initial annotation of seed metabolites using a standard spectral library (e.g., in-house, METLIN, NIST17), yielding 134 annotated metabolites, of which 132 with KEGG IDs served as seeds; (3) retrieval of reaction-paired neighbor metabolites from the preconstructed metabolic reaction network (MRN) derived from KEGG RPAIR data; (4) recursive annotation where seed metabolites’ experimental MS2 spectra serve as surrogate spectra to annotate neighbors based on m/z matching, RT prediction, and spectral similarity (dot product); newly annotated neighbors become seeds for the next round; (5) confidence scoring and redundancy removal using a self-check system that filters low-confidence or duplicate annotations; and (6) downstream dysregulated pathway analysis leveraging the expanded metabolite identifications. This pipeline starts from raw .wiff files, proceeds through ProteoWizard conversion → XCMS peak detection (centWave method, ppm=15, snthr=10, peakwidth=c(5,40), minfrac=0.5) → peak table formatting → MetDNA webserver submission → recursive MRN-based expansion → statistical testing → pathway interpretation.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MetDNA
sample_description: Eighteen human aortic tissue samples were analyzed: nine from patients diagnosed with aortic aneurysm (AA) and nine from patients with aortic dissection (AD), all obtained from Beijing Anzhen Hospital. Tissues were flash-frozen on dry ice, weighed (targeting <10% inter-sample weight error), homogenized in water under cooled N₂ gas flow (three cycles of 5500 rpm for 20 s each), followed by protein precipitation using methanol:acetonitrile (1:1 v/v), centrifugation at 13,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, supernatant evaporation to dryness, and reconstitution in 100 μL of 1:1 acetonitrile:water. Samples were stored at −80°C prior to LC-MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow began with collection of 18 human aortic tissue samples (9 AA, 9 AD) from Beijing Anzhen Hospital. Tissues were homogenized on dry ice, extracted using cold MeOH:ACN (1:1), precipitated proteins at −20°C, centrifuged (13,000 rpm, 15 min, 4°C), dried, and reconstituted in 1:1 ACN:water. LC-MS/MS analysis was performed on an Agilent 1290 UHPLC coupled to a SCIEX TripleTOF 6600+ mass spectrometer, acquiring data in both positive and negative ion modes using information-dependent acquisition (IDA) across m/z 60–1200, with collision energy set to 30 V. Quality control was implemented by injecting a pooled QC sample every six analytical runs; system stability was assessed via base peak chromatogram (BPC) overlay and RSD (<30% threshold) of features across QCs. Raw data were processed to generate a feature table, followed by statistical analysis using Student’s t-test (p < 0.05) and orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) with VIP > 1.0 to identify differentially abundant metabolites. Metabolite annotation was performed using the MetDNA database. Pathway and cluster analyses were then conducted on the resulting list of significantly altered metabolites (n = 138), with a focus on galactose metabolism. All steps — from tissue processing and extraction through LC-MS acquisition, QC validation, statistical filtering, database annotation (MetDNA), and biological interpretation — are described sequentially and coherently, forming a reproducible non-targeted metabolomics pipeline for human aortic tissue.
source_paper: Kefeng et al. (2022), Journal of clinical laboratory analysis, DOI: 10.1002/jcla.24623, PMID: 35881684
