========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: MS2DeepScore
sample_description: The research used multiple public mass spectral libraries for training and benchmarking: the GNPS library, the MassBank EU library, the MassBank of North America (MoNA) library, and MS2 spectra from MSnLib (created by Brungs et al.). After cleaning with the matchms library pipeline, the combined dataset comprised 36,638 unique molecules (519,580 spectra) in positive ionization mode and 18,480 unique molecules (145,594 spectra) in negative ionization mode; uniqueness was defined by the first 14 characters of the InChIKey (ignoring stereochemistry). For case studies, three experimental datasets were employed: (1) human urine fractions from the National Phenome Centre, acquired via reversed-phase UHPLC coupled to a Waters Xevo G2-S TOF mass spectrometer using electrospray ionization; (2) human blood plasma (NIST SRM1950), analyzed using UHPLC-RP-LC on a BEH C8 column coupled to a Waters Xevo G3 QTof; and (3) Rumex sanguineus plant tissue (25 mg homogenized and extracted with water/methanol/MTBE), analyzed on an Agilent 1290 UHPLC coupled to a Thermo Q-Exactive Focus Orbitrap in both positive and negative ionization modes with data-dependent acquisition (24 samples: 4 biological replicates × 2 technical duplicates across roots, stems, and leaves). All raw data were converted to .mzML format using ProteoWizard msconvert, peak-picked and converted to .mgf using MSDIAL (with parameters: min peak height = 1000 amplitude, mass slice width = 0.05 Da, MS2Dec sigma = 0.5, MS2 abundance cutoff = 200 amplitude, RT tolerance = 0.05 min, MS1 tolerance = 0.01 Da), and further processed with matchms (urine: retained only MS2 spectra with >4 fragments; Rumex and plasma: no minimum peak count applied).
complete_workflow_description: The workflow involved: (1) Data preprocessing: Raw files (.RAW) converted to .mzML using ProteoWizard msconvert; peak picking and .mgf generation via MSDIAL (version 4.9.221218) with specified parameters; further spectral cleaning using matchms; (2) Dataset splitting: Spectra separated by ionization mode; unique molecules (by first 14 InChIKey chars) partitioned into training (17/20), validation (1/20), and test (1/20) sets — with independent InChIKey selections for positive/negative modes to avoid cross-mode bias; dual-ionization training combined positive and negative training spectra; (3) Balanced pair sampling: A custom algorithm ensured near-uniform sampling frequency per molecule and equal numbers of molecule pairs per Tanimoto bin (10 equally spaced bins from 0–1); it prioritized least-sampled molecules and pairs, allowed controlled resampling, and capped per-molecule sampling disparity to <15%; (4) Model training: Using MSE loss between predicted and actual Tanimoto scores; validation/test evaluation computed average MSE per molecule pair (aggregating all spectrum pairs per molecule) then averaged across Tanimoto bins; (5) Benchmarking: Performance assessed per compound class (e.g., lipids, nucleosides, organic oxygen compounds) and adduct ([M+Na]+, [M+K]+); (6) Downstream applications: Molecular networking in Cytoscape using GraphML files generated by matchms; interactive UMAP visualization (HTML export); integration into MZmine 4.3.0 for GUI-based Feature-based Molecular Networking (FBMN) and Ion Identity Molecular Networking (IIMN); (7) Uncertainty estimation: Embedding Evaluator model trained to predict embedding quality (MSE) for out-of-distribution or low-quality spectra.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: MS2DeepScore
sample_description: The research utilized multiple biological samples across four case studies: (1) NIST 1950 Frozen Human Plasma standard reference material (SRM) for lipidomic profiling via reversed-phase chromatography (RPC); (2) a plasma Long-Term Reference (LTR) sample (10 L bulk plasma from Seralab, homogenized and aliquoted at –80 °C) analyzed using hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) for polar metabolites; (3) a pooled urine LTR sample (78 urine voids from healthy volunteers, combined into 20 L, homogenized, aliquoted, and stored at –80 °C) analyzed by RPC for small-molecule metabolites; and (4) extracts from three anammox bacterial strains — Kuenenia stuttgartiensis MBR1 (cultivated in a 12-L membrane bioreactor), Brocadia fulgida (6-L bioreactor), and Scalindua (5.5-L sequencing batch reactor) — grown under defined chemostat-like conditions with specific nitrogen sources (NaNO₂ and (NH₄)₂SO₄), trace elements, salts, and controlled temperature/stirring. All case study samples underwent standardized protein precipitation (e.g., with isopropanol or acetonitrile), centrifugation, supernatant collection, and LC-MS injection under strictly controlled pre-analytical conditions (e.g., 4 °C thawing, full-loop injection mode, 5× overfill).
complete_workflow_description: The MS2Query workflow was implemented using Python-based tools including matchms (v33) for spectral preprocessing, RDKit (v35) for chemical fingerprint generation and Tanimoto calculation, scikit-learn (v37) for the random forest model, and SQLite for metadata storage. Spectral preprocessing involved: (i) metadata cleaning and harmonization; (ii) separation into positive/negative ionization modes; (iii) intensity normalization; (iv) removal of peaks >1000 Da and peaks <0.1% of base peak intensity; (v) truncation to top 500 peaks for spectra with >500 peaks; and (vi) removal of spectra with <3 peaks. Missing InChIKeys were imputed using matchms-extras via PubChem lookup by compound name and molecular formula. Chemical similarity was quantified using RDKit-generated Daylight fingerprints (2048-bit) and Tanimoto scores. The random forest model (scikit-learn implementation) used 5 input features per spectrum pair: (1) Spec2Vec similarity, (2) query precursor m/z, (3) absolute precursor m/z difference, (4) average MS2Deepscore over 10 chemically most similar library structures (selected by highest Tanimoto score), and (5) average Tanimoto score among those 10 structures. The model was trained on spectrum pairs generated by selecting 1/40th of unique InChIKeys and 1/40th of remaining spectra, then pairing each selected spectrum with its top 100 MS2Deepscore-matching library spectra; training used mean squared error loss, 250 estimators, max depth = 5, and Gini impurity-based feature importance. Speed optimization included precomputation and caching of Spec2Vec and MS2Deepscore embeddings, precalculation and SQLite storage of top-10 Tanimoto-similar InChIKeys per library structure, and separate SQLite column indexing for precursor m/z. Automatic library generation and end-to-end model training workflows were integrated into MS2Query to support deployment on new spectral libraries.

========== paper_workflow entry 3 ==========
related_tool: MS2DeepScore
sample_description: The study used annotated MS/MS spectra from the Global Natural Products Social Molecular Networking (GNPS) platform, retrieved on 25/01/2021, totaling 210,407 spectra. After metadata cleaning using matchms v0.8.2—including compound name standardization, adduct extraction, metadata field harmonization, and InChI/SMILES/InChIKey conversions—184,698 spectra were annotated with InChIKey and/or SMILES/InChI. The final analytical subset comprised 109,734 positive-ion-mode spectra meeting strict criteria: presence of a valid 14-character InChIKey, at least one structural annotation (SMILES or InChI), and ≥5 peaks in the m/z range 10.0–1000.0 Da. Spectra were further preprocessed by intensity thresholding (<0.1% of max peak intensity removed), limiting to top 1000 highest-intensity peaks, square-root intensity transformation, and binning into 10,000 equal-width bins spanning m/z 10–1000; 9948 bins were retained after removing empty bins across the training set. Structural similarity labels were derived from Tanimoto scores computed on 2048-bit RDKit Daylight fingerprints generated from the most common InChI per unique 14-character InChIKey (n = 15,062). The dataset was split into training (n = 14,062 InChIKeys), validation (n = 500), and test (n = 500) sets, corresponding to 109,734 total spectra — with 3,597 validation spectra and 3,601 test spectra — and all possible spectrum pairs within each set were considered for evaluation (e.g., 6,485,401 unique pairs in the test set).
complete_workflow_description: The data processing and modeling pipeline involved multiple software tools and custom procedures. Spectrum preprocessing used matchms v0.8.2 for metadata cleaning, pubchempy v1.0.4 for automated PubChem lookups to fill missing InChI/SMILES, and custom Python scripts for peak filtering, intensity transformation, and binning. Structural similarity labels were computed using RDKit v2020.09.1 for fingerprint generation and Tanimoto scoring. Data generation employed two custom data generators from the MS2DeepScore library: DataGeneratorAllSpectrums (used for training and validation) and DataGeneratorAllInchikeys (for alternative sampling); training data cycles sampled spectrum pairs such that their structural similarity label fell into one of 10 equally spaced bins [0,1], with iterative bin widening (±0.1 increments) if no matching pair existed. Data augmentation was applied per training example: (1) low-intensity peak removal (0–20% of non-zero bins with pre-transformation intensity <0.4 set to zero), (2) peak intensity jitter (±40% variation applied to all non-zero post-transformation intensities), and (3) new peak addition (0–10 zero-intensity bins assigned random intensities between 0 and 0.01 post-transformation). Model training and evaluation used TensorFlow/Keras (backend unspecified but implied by architecture description) and scikit-learn v0.24.1 for t-SNE (metric='cosine', perplexity=100, learning_rate=200, n_iter=1000). Precision/recall analysis used custom thresholding over predicted spectral similarity scores versus ground-truth Tanimoto thresholds. All code, notebooks, and trained models are publicly available via GitHub and Zenodo.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MS2DeepScore
sample_description: The study used a curated collection of over 71,000 unique compounds with merged-energy positive-ion mode [M + H]+ MS/MS spectra sourced from four major repositories: NIST2023, Agilent-METLIN, GNPS2, and MSDIAL. Spectra were grouped by the first 14 characters of the InChIKey to resolve isomeric variants, and prioritized by data quality (NIST2023 > Agilent-METLIN > GNPS2 > MSDIAL). Spectra matching CASMI2022 target compounds were excluded to prevent data leakage. For each compound, multiple collision energy spectra were merged into a single composite spectrum after binning at 0.01 Da resolution, m/z alignment, and mean intensity combination; intensities were scaled to 0–100. The final benchmarking datasets included this main curated set (~71k compounds), CASMI2022 (processed from mzML files using R/Spectra v1.18.0 to extract top-5 highest-intensity MS/MS scans per compound within ±10 s RT and ±20 ppm precursor m/z tolerance), MassSpecGym, and Spectraverse v1.0.1 — all filtered for [M + H]+, normalized, binned at 0.01 Da, and clipped to 0–700 m/z.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with raw MS/MS spectra from NIST2023, Agilent-METLIN, GNPS2, and MSDIAL, filtered for [M + H]+ adducts and deduplicated by 14-character InChIKey prefix. Spectra from the same compound across collision energies are merged by binning at 0.01 Da resolution, m/z alignment, and mean intensity aggregation; intensities are scaled to 0–100. CASMI2022 data is processed separately from mzML files in R v4.5.0 using the Spectra package v1.18.0: MS/MS scans are selected based on retention time (±10 s) and precursor m/z (±20 ppm), top-5 highest-intensity scans per compound are merged, and intensities normalized to max=100. MassSpecGym and Spectraverse v1.0.1 are downloaded, parsed (Spectraverse using matchms v0.32.0), filtered for [M + H]+, binned at 0.01 Da, and normalized. All datasets exclude spectra matching CASMI2022 targets (by 14-char InChIKey) to prevent leakage. Spectra are restricted to 0–700 m/z range. Ten featurization strategies are applied—including fixed binning (70,000-dim binary vectors), adaptive/quantile binning (927 or 3621 bins), frequent-peaks (top-N peaks), spectrum hashing, and learned embeddings (Spec2Vec, MS2DeepScore *as reference only*, DreaMS, SpecEmbedding)—each producing a numerical vector input. These vectors feed into PyTorch v2.3.0a0-implemented feed-forward neural networks (architecture: N → 2048 → 1024 → 300, ReLU activations, 25% dropout), trained via Adam (lr=2e−4) to predict 300-dimensional Mol2Vec embeddings using MSE loss and early stopping (10 epochs, ΔMSE < 0.01). Model evaluation uses 5-fold cross-validation (scikit-learn v1.5.2) and retrieval performance (Top-1/Top-5 accuracy) against a 0.6-million-compound database at 0.1/3/10 ppm mass tolerances, with null ranking baselines. Validation is repeated across three independent benchmarks: the main curated set, CASMI2022, and open resources MassSpecGym and Spectraverse.
source_paper: Roger et al. (2026), Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry, DOI: 10.1021/jasms.5c00428, PMID: 42298320

complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data acquisition in both positive and negative ionization modes. Features are detected and MS/MS spectra are extracted using MZmine. Spectra are then processed by MS2DeepScore 2.0 (integrated in MZmine) to compute pairwise chemical similarity scores across and within ionization modes. Using matchms, GraphML files are generated with edges defined by MS2DeepScore scores ≥ 0.85, mutual top-20 ranking, and retention of up to the top 10 mutual edges per node. These GraphML files are imported into Cytoscape for visualization and exploration of unified dual-ionization-mode molecular networks. Additionally, UMAP is applied to MS2DeepScore embeddings for low-dimensional visualization of chemical space, enabling intuitive inspection of cross-mode spectral relationships. Expert annotation validates molecular links identified via this pipeline.
