sample_description: A total of 121 small-molecule exposome compounds (100–200 Da) were selected from the Blood Exposome Database based on three criteria: (i) availability of reference [M+H]+ CID MS/MS spectra in the NIST 2023 database (positive ionization mode), (ii) frequent occurrence in peer-reviewed metabolomics and exposomics literature, and (iii) molecular weight between 100 and 200 Da. Chemical identifiers (InChiKey, SMILES) and 3D structure coordinates were retrieved from PubChem. Compounds were categorized as cyclic (C; containing ≥1 ring) or acyclic (A; no ring) to inform QCxMS parameter selection. For each compound, protonated forms ([M+H]+) were generated using CREST v3.0.2 with GFN2-xTB semiempirical quantum method, retaining all topologically unique protomers within a 30 kcal/mol energy window relative to the global minimum; protomers were labeled sequentially (protomer_0 = lowest energy, ΔE = 0; protomer_1, protomer_2, etc., increasing in energy). A total of 18 compounds yielded only one protomer, 27 yielded two, and 76 yielded three or more (up to ten for sesamol). All protomers were used as independent inputs for QCxMS simulations.
software_configuration_description: Spectral similarity was quantified using entropy similarity scores computed via the IDSL.CSA R package (v not specified), where scores range from 0 to 1000 (1000 = perfect match). Spectral coverage was defined as an entropy similarity score ≥700 AND at least two correctly predicted m/z values (matched to two decimal places) regardless of intensity — a criterion designed to ensure chemically relevant fragmentation beyond precursor-only spectra. The entropy similarity metric was applied to compare each QCxMS-simulated spectrum against its corresponding experimental NIST23 reference spectrum. For final evaluation, the best-matching spectrum per compound was selected as the one with the highest entropy similarity score among all protomer-parameter grid combinations that met the coverage threshold. All in-house spectral analysis scripts (including entropy computation and peak matching logic) are publicly available on GitHub and Zenodo (as stated in Data Availability Statement).

software_configuration_description: Spectral entropy (SE) was computed for the alpha frequency band (8–15 Hz) using EEG data from two distinct temporal windows: (1) baseline (resting) SE was calculated from the [-400 to -100] ms pre-stimulus window between active N-back trials; and (2) active (ERD) SE was calculated from the [250–750] ms post-stimulus window aligned to letter presentation during the N-back task. SE was computed per EEG channel at each time point within the respective temporal windows using the formula: SE = (1 / ln(N)) × Σ[Pₙ(fᵢ) × ln(1 / Pₙ(fᵢ))], where N is the number of frequency components in the alpha band (f₁ = 8 Hz, f₂ = 15 Hz), and Pₙ(fᵢ) is the normalized power spectrum at frequency fᵢ. The analysis was applied to continuous EEG recordings segmented into epochs, following preprocessing including bandpass filtering (2–30 Hz), artifact removal via independent component analysis (ICA), and rejection of epochs with signal amplitude exceeding ±3 standard deviations from the sensor mean. SE values were derived separately for each sensor and condition (resting vs. active), and subsequently averaged across sensors within five predefined regional groupings: frontal (Fz, F3, F4), central (Cz, C3, C4), parietal (Pz, P3, P4), left lateral (F7, T3, T5), and right lateral (F8, T4, T6).

sample_description: The study included 56 obese subjects (21 women and 13 men in the OB group; 12 women and 10 men in the ObT2D group) aged 40–60 years, all with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m². The OB group had fasting glucose < 100 mg/dL, while the ObT2D group met diagnostic criteria for type 2 diabetes (fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL and 2-hour OGTT glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL). All subjects were physically inactive for ≥6 months, nonsmokers, free of peripheral neuropathy or overt heart disease (confirmed by physician-supervised maximal exercise stress test), and not taking beta-blockers or medications that alter HR or BP. Premenopausal women were tested during days 1–10 of their menstrual cycle; only 5 postmenopausal women were on hormone therapy. Subjects underwent testing before and after a 16-week supervised aerobic training program. Beat-to-beat R-R interval (RRI) data were acquired continuously at 1000 Hz using a modified CM5-lead ECG interfaced with a Biopac digital acquisition system (Santa Barbara, CA); only artifact-free, ectopic-free 5-minute epochs during paced breathing (12 breaths/min) in supine and upright tilt positions were analyzed. Inclusion required >98% qualified beats (i.e., <2% filtered R-R intervals) after automated and visual editing per Huikuri et al. methodology; for entropy analyses, >200 consecutive stable, ectopic-free RRI data points were used, with identical beat counts applied pre- and post-training within each subject.
software_configuration_description: The study did not use 'Spectral entropy' software; instead, it employed two distinct nonlinear entropy measures: Sample Entropy (SampEn) and Lempel-Ziv Entropy (LZEn), both calculated using WinCPRS software (Absolute Aliens Oy, Turku, Finland). For SampEn: embedding dimension m = 2; tolerance r = 20% of the standard deviation of the RRI time series; computed on >200 consecutive stable, ectopic-free RRI data points; output range 0–2, where higher values indicate greater irregularity. For LZEn: RRI values were binarized relative to the mean (values above mean → '1', below → '0'), forming a binary sequence; LZEn was computed based on the number of insertion-and-copying operations required to reconstruct the sequence using Kolmogorov complexity principles; higher LZEn values reflect greater signal randomness; same >200-beat dataset as SampEn was used. Neither SampEn nor LZEn is spectral entropy — spectral entropy would involve frequency-domain decomposition (e.g., FFT or autoregressive power spectra), whereas these are time-domain pattern-based entropies.
