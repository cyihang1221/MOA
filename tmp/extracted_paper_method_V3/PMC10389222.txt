</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="abstract1"><h2>Background:</h2>
<p>Multiple primary lung cancer (MPLC) is becoming increasingly common in clinical practice. Imaging examination is sometimes difficult to differentiate from intrapulmonary metastasis (IM) or single primary lung cancer (SPLC) before surgery. There is a lack of effective blood biomarkers as an auxiliary diagnostic method.</p>
<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Participants and Methods</h3>
<p>A total of 179 patients who were hospitalized and operated in our department from January to June 2019 were collected, and they were divided into SPLC with 136 patients, MPLC with 24 patients, and IM with 19 patients. In total, 96 healthy people without lung cancer were enrolled. Medical history, imaging, and pathology data were assembled from all participants. Plasma metabolomics analysis was performed by quadrupole time-of-flight tandem mass spectrometry, and data were analyzed using SPSS19.0/Simca 14.1/MetaboAnalyst5.0 software. Significant metabolites were selected by variable importance in projection, <em>P</em> value, and fold change. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was used to evaluate their diagnostic ability.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p>There were significant differences in plasma metabolite profiles between IM and MPLC. Seven metabolites were screened out. Two metabolites had higher levels in IM, and five metabolites had higher levels in MPLC. All had favorable discriminating capacity. Phosphatidyl ethanolamine (38:5) showed the highest sensitivity (0.95) and specificity (0.92). It was followed by <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-histidine with sensitivity 0.92 and specificity 0.84. <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine can be used to identify SPLC and MPLC. The panel composed of related metabolites exhibited higher diagnostic ability. Eight principal metabolites caused remarkable differences between healthy people and MPLC, and five of them had area under the curves greater than 0.85, showing good discriminating power.</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h3>
<p>Through the study of plasma metabolomics, it was found that there were obvious differences in the metabolite profiles of MPLC, IM, SPLC, and the healthy population. Some discovered metabolites possessed excellent diagnostic competence with high sensitivity and specificity. They had the potential to act as biomarkers for the screening and differential diagnosis of MPLCs.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> biomarker, differential diagnosis, intrapulmonary metastasis, metabolomics, multiple primary lung cancer</p></section></section><hr class="headless">
<section class="bt xbox font-sm" id="boxed-text1"><div class="caption p"><p>Highlights</p></div>
<ul class="list" style="list-style-type:disc">
<li><p>The principal metabolites of multiple primary lung cancer (MPLC) for differential diagnosis with other kinds of pulmonary nodules were discovered by plasma metabolomics.</p></li>
<li><p>There are seven metabolites that can be used as potential biomarkers to differentiate between intrapulmonary metastases and MPLCs.</p></li>
<li><p>A diagnostic panel consisting of <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine, 3-hydroxydecanoyl carnitine, and Avenestergenin had the capacity to differentiate between single and MPLCs.</p></li>
<li><p>Eight different low-molecular metabolites can be applied for the screening of MPLC in healthy people.</p></li>
</ul></section><p>The incidence of multiple pulmonary nodules has increased in recent years, which may be related to the wide application of multislice computed tomography (CT). According to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual<a href="#R1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R1">1</a>, multiple pulmonary nodules can be divided into four types according to their pathological types: (a) independent solid masses of different histological types (staged separately); (b) nodules containing ground glass components or partially solid (staged according to the main lesions); (c) pneumonic-type adenocarcinoma (often invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma); and (d) lung cancer with intrapulmonary metastasis (IM). Multiple primary lung cancer (MPLC) refers to lung cancer in which two or more primary lesions occur simultaneously or successively in different parts of the lung within the same patient. The first two types of multiple pulmonary nodules defined in the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging manual can be considered as MPLC. According to whether the diagnosis interval is more than 6 months, it is divided into simultaneous multiple primary lung cancer (sMPLC) and metachronous MPLC.</p>
<p>After multiple lung nodules are detected by imaging examination, there are always three possibilities for the nature of these nodules: all are benign (healthy people); only one lesion is malignant, and the others are benign [single primary lung cancer (SPLC)]; two or more lesions are malignant (MPLCs). Therefore, the diagnosis of MPLC often needs to be differentiated from that of single primary lung cancer (SPLC) and healthy people. Moreover, in clinical practice, MPLC sometimes needs to be distinguished from lung cancer with IM. The staging of the two diseases is always different. MPLC is generally at stage I/II, and IM is at stage III/IV<a href="#R2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R2">2</a>. This results in completely different treatment methods and prognosis. MPLC is often treated with surgery and has a favorable prognosis<a href="#R3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R3">3</a>, while IM often requires comprehensive treatment, including surgery, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy, with a poor prognosis<a href="#R4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R4">4</a>. It is crucial to accurately determine the nature of multiple lung nodules as early as possible. At present, the identification of the two mainly depends on the results of imaging, histopathology, and genetic testing. The combination of three methods can significantly improve the diagnostic accuracy<a href="#R5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R5">5</a>. But this requires surgery or puncture biopsy to obtain the diseased tissue; sometimes, the lesion is too small to be accurately punctured, or the sample is too small to perform gene detection. If the biomarkers can be found in the blood, it will contribute to accurate preoperative diagnosis of MPLC.</p>
<p>Metabolomics is an important complement to genomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics. Changes in metabolites are located downstream of changes in DNA, RNA, and proteins. Through the qualitative and quantitative study of metabolites, the functional status and pathophysiological changes of the body can be more directly and deeply understood. Metabolomics has been widely used to study various diseases, including lung cancer. Previous research on lung cancer metabolomics had often focused on the comparison of lung cancer with benign lung diseases, the comparison of different pathological types, and the prognosis of lung cancer<a href="#R6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R6">6</a>,<a href="#R7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R7">7</a>. However, few studies had involved MPLC. The purpose of this study was to screen out metabolites with diagnostic significance as potential biomarkers for MPLC, by comparing different plasma samples from MPLC, IM, SPLC, and healthy people. These metabolites may play a vital role in the differential diagnosis of MPLC in the future.</p>
<section id="sec4"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Participants and methods</h2>
<section id="sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Participants</h3>
<p>This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the National Cancer Center and the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, and its number is 19/223-2007. A total of 179 patients who underwent surgery in our department from January to June 2019 were collected and divided into three groups according to their postoperative pathological results. There were 136 patients in the SPLC group, 24 patients in the MPLC group, and 19 patients in the lung cancer with IM group. In total, 96 volunteers without lung cancer were enrolled as the healthy population group (HPG). MPLC was compared with the other groups separately. In this study, patients with MPLC had synchronous MPLC, and all lesions were clearly diagnosed by paraffin pathology after surgical resection.</p></section><section id="sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Sample collection and preparation</h3>
<p>To avoid the effects of food and time on metabolites, blood samples of all subjects were taken in the morning while they are fasting. The blood samples were immediately placed in an EDTA anticoagulation tube and then centrifuged at 4°C, 3000 g for 5 minutes. The plasma was taken and stored at −80°. After thawing the plasma samples at 4°C, we took 50 µl of plasma and added 950 µl of a methanol/acetonitrile (2/3, v/v) solution, and then vortexed for 1 minute. After standing at 4°C for 24 hours, the sample was centrifuged at 19,000<em>g</em> for 30 minutes. A volume of 50 µl of the supernatant was taken was added, 250 µl of dichloromethane, and 500 µl of ultrapure water was added and mixed. After vortexing for 30 seconds, the sample was centrifuged at 1250<em>g</em> for 6 minutes. A volume of 75 µl of the supernatant was transferred to a glass bottle and dried naturally at room temperature. The dried sample was redissolved by adding 150 µl of 50% methanol solution.</p></section><section id="sec7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Instrumental test</h3>
<p>The plasma metabolite profiling was performed on the platform of quadrupole time-of-flight tandem mass spectrometry. The temperature of the ion transfer tube was 220°, the spray voltage was 4.5 kV, and the flow rate of dry gas was 6 l/min. The signal was collected in positive ion mode, and the <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> acquisition range is 50–1000. All plasma samples from 275 participants were randomly divided into 14 analytical batches, and Quality Control samples were evenly inserted into each analytical batch to monitor instrument stability and correct for interbatch effects to ensure experimental quality. The Quality Control sample consisted of four different groups of plasma samples mixed in equal amounts. Moreover, four blank samples were tested, which could be used to detect the residue of the substance during the test.</p></section><section id="sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Biomarkers selection and annotation</h3>
<p>The original data of each sample were imported into the DataAnalysis 4.4 software, and the time period of 0–0.4 min when the main peak appeared in the detection process was taken to obtain about 900 characteristic peaks with different molecular weights between 50 and 1000 Da. The characteristic peaks with an absolute intensity less than 1000 were removed, and then the deconvolution process was performed to combine the isotopes of the same metabolite. Finally, representative features were selected for the identification of metabolites. A computer program was designed in Python to summarize the absolute intensity values of 155 metabolites. According to the 80% rule, the metabolite was considered detectable when it could be detected in at least 4/5 of all samples. The metabolites existing in at least 220 (275×0.8) samples were retained. As a result, a total of 81 metabolites were obtained. Although some metabolites were present in 80% or more of the samples, they still do not exist in a minority of them. The missing intensity value of the metabolite in such samples was set to half of the lowest absolute intensity value. The percentage of missing values for every feature is shown in Supplementary Table 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1, <a href="http://links.lww.com/JS9/A6" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://links.lww.com/JS9/A6</a>.</p>
<p>The difference in the comparison between the two groups was judged by indicators including variable importance in projection (VIP), <em>P</em> value, and fold change (FC). VIP greater than 1.5, <em>P</em> value less than 0.05, and |Log<sub>2</sub>FC| greater than 1 were set as the screening criteria for principal metabolites. The annotation of the corresponding metabolites was preliminarily determined by the accordance degree of isotope abundance, the difference in molecular weight, the types of inducers, the generation mode, and the existence scope of metabolites. The Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), PubChem, and MassBank databases were searched to assist with metabolite identification. Analyte mass accuracy (in the range of 0–10 ppm), retention time, isotope pattern, and tandem mass spectrometry fragmentation pattern were used for the final determination of the selected metabolites.</p></section><section id="sec9"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Statistical analysis</h3>
<p>Statistical analysis was mainly performed by SPSS19.0 software. Kolmogorov–Smirnov <em>Z</em> method was used to verify the data distribution, <em>t</em>-test was used for comparison between groups of data conforming to normal distribution, and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for comparison of data not conforming to normal distribution. <em>P</em> value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and the Bonferroni method was used to correct the <em>P</em> value if necessary. MetaboAnalyst 5.0 was used to normalize the data and perform a preliminary analysis. In MetaboAnalyst, a variety of centering, scaling, and transformation options can be applied to metabolomics data. In our study, sample normalization was carried out by sum. No data transformation was carried out. Data scaling was carried out by Pareto scaling. Normalized data were imported into Simca 14.1 software for multivariate statistical analysis, including principal component analysis (PCA), partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). The quality of the model was tested by cross-validation, and the validity of the model was judged by the <em>R</em>2 and <em>Q</em>2. The validity of the model is further tested by 20 permutation tests. <em>R</em>2 represents the percentage of the <em>X</em> and <em>Y</em> matrix information that the model can interpret, and <em>Q</em>2 represents the predictive ability of the model obtained through cross-validation calculation. A good model generally includes several conditions: <em>R</em>2 is always greater than <em>Q</em>2; the higher the values of <em>R</em>2 and <em>Q</em>2, the better; the difference between <em>R</em>2 and <em>Q</em>2 is not too big, preferably less than 0.3; <em>Q</em>2 is above 0.5. In PLS-DA, 10-fold cross-validation was performed for classification models. According to Supplementary Table 2, Supplemental Digital Content 1, <a href="http://links.lww.com/JS9/A6" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://links.lww.com/JS9/A6</a>, the PLS-DA model of each comparison among groups was a good model that met these conditions. The correlation analysis of the identified metabolites was carried out by cluster analysis. The sensitivity and specificity of the differential diagnostic ability of metabolites were evaluated by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.</p></section></section><section id="sec10"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h2>
<section id="sec11"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Comparison of general characteristics</h3>
<p>The study included four groups with different pathological types: MPLC (<em>n</em>=24), IM (<em>n</em>=19), SPLC (<em>n</em>=136), and HPG (<em>n</em>=96). There was a significant difference in gender ratio between MPLC and other groups (<em>P</em>=0.011), with a female patient ratio of 54.2%, higher than SPLC (27.9%) and IM (31.6%). There were no significant differences in age, smoking history, comorbidity, neoadjuvant therapy, tumor history, largest tumor size, pathological type, or gene mutation among the three lung cancer groups (<em>P</em>&gt;0.05); see Table <a href="#T1" class="usa-link">1</a> for details. In terms of pathological stage and tumor grade, there were statistical differences among the three groups, mainly manifested as later pathological stage and worse differentiation in patients with IM than SPLC and MPLC. Considering that pathological stage and tumor grade can only be obtained after surgery, these two covariates have no ability for preoperative differential diagnosis, and their clinical application potential is limited.</p>

<div class="tw-foot p">
<div class="fn" id="fn1"><p>Nonmucinous tumors were categorized into three subgroups based on the new histologic grading system of the IASLC: grade 1, lepidic predominant tumor with no or less than 20% of high-grade patterns; grade 2, acinar or papillary predominant tumor, both with no or less than 20% of high-grade patterns; and grade 3, any tumor with greater than equal to 20% high-grade patterns (solid, micropapillary, and complex glandular patterns).</p></div>
<div class="fn" id="fn2"><p>ACC, adenoid cystic carcinoma; Ad, adenocarcinoma; LCLC, large cell lung cancer; NA, not applicable (no gene test received); none, no mutated gene detected in test; SC, squamous carcinoma; SCLC, small-cell lung cancer.</p></div>
</div></section><p>This study included 24 MPLC patients, of whom four patients had three lung cancer lesions, and the other 20 patients had lung cancer lesions, for a total of 52 lung cancer lesions. In terms of lesion sites, 13 were located in the left upper lobe, six in the left lower lobe, 18 in the right upper lobe, seven in the right middle lobe, and eight in the right lower lobe. There were seven patients with lesions in the same lobe and 17 with lesions in different lobes. On the subject of lesion size, 25 tumors were less than 1 cm in maximum diameter; 15 were between 1 and 2 cm; seven were between 2 and 3 cm; three were between 3 and 4 cm; and three were between 4 and 5 cm. In the matter of lesion composition, there were 27 pure ground glass nodules (pGGN), 16 mixed ground glass nodules (mGGN), seven pure solid nodules, and two nodules with cystic airspace. All lesions can be divided into four pathological types, with 17 being adenocarcinomas <em>in situ</em>, 12 being microinvasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), 22 of invasive adenocarcinoma, and one of small-cell lung cancer (SCLC). According to the predominant histologic subtypes, the lesions can be divided into four types, including 28 lepidic, 15 acinar, six papillary, two micropapillary, and one SCLC without subtype. See Table <a href="#T2" class="usa-link">2</a> for details.</p>

<p>The two groups of data was normalized, and the comparison before and after normalization was shown in Figure <a href="#F1" class="usa-link">1</a>A. </div></section></section><section id="sec13"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Comparison of SPLC and MPLC</h3>
<p>A nonparametric test was performed after normalization of the two groups of data, and there were three metabolites with a <em>P</em> value less than 0.05, namely <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine, 3-hydroxydecanoyl carnitine, and avenestergenin. Performing statistical analysis of multivariate data, the score scatter plot of OPLS-DA showed that there were differences in sample distribution between the two groups. The VIP values of major metabolites leading to this difference were calculated, and there were 10 metabolites with VIP values greater than 1.5. After univariate and multivariate statistical analysis, it was found that the only metabolite satisfying VIP greater than 1.5 and a <em>P</em> value less than 0.05 was <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine, and its level in SPLC was higher than MPLC. The <em>P</em> value obtained by the nonparametric test was 0.025, indicating that the level of this metabolite was statistically different between the two groups. The ROC curve of <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine was plotted to obtain an AUC=0.642 (95% CI: 0.522–0.761; <em>P</em>=0.027). It showed that <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine had a certain ability in the differential diagnosis of SPLC and MPLC, with a sensitivity of 0.60 and a specificity of 0.71. To improve the differential diagnosis ability of metabolites, three metabolites (<span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine, 3-hydroxydecanoyl carnitine, and avenestergenin) meeting VIP less than 1.0 and <em>P</em> greater than 0.05 were combined into a diagnostic panel, its area under the ROC curve was 0.690 (95% CI: 0.592–0.788; <em>P</em>=0.003), with the sensitivity of 0.92 and the specificity of 0.50. A correlation heatmap could show the relationship between all metabolites, with the abscissa and ordinate representing the same metabolite. The deeper the color in the grid, the stronger the correlation between the two substances was. Red represents a positive correlation and blue represensts a negative correlation, as shown in Figure <a href="#F5" class="usa-link">5</a>D. Additional details regarding comparisons between the two groups are shown in Supplementary Figures 2 and 3, Supplemental Digital Content 2, <a href="http://links.lww.com/JS9/A7" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://links.lww.com/JS9/A7</a>.</p>

<p>The two groups of data were subjected to an independent sample <em>t</em>-test, and there were 61 metabolites with a <em>P</em> value less than 0.05. The FC comparison was performed, and there were 31 metabolites greater than the threshold of 2. The volcano plot showed that there were 29 metabolites satisfying both conditions, of which five were higher in HPG and 24 were higher in MPLC. After multivariate statistical analysis, the scores scatter plot of OPLS-DA showed that the sample distribution of the two groups was significantly different, as shown in Figure <a href="#F6" class="usa-link">6</a>. The S-plot showed that the further the point was from the origin, the greater the ability of the corresponding metabolite to distinguish between the two groups. These metabolites were ranked according to VIP value, and there were 12 metabolites greater than 1.5. All metabolites had higher intensities in MPLC. As can be seen intuitively from the heatmap, different metabolites increased or decreased in HPG and MPLC samples, and the metabolites that were elevated in MPLC samples were apparently more than in HPG. A total of eight metabolites were in line with the screening criteria, including 1-salicylate glucuronide, adrenoyl ethanolamide, anabasine, dihydrocaffeic acid, 3-<span class="font-variant-small-caps">o</span>-glucuronide, lysophosphatidyl glycerol (18 : 1), nicotinuric acid, <span class="font-variant-small-caps">o</span>-arachidonoyl ethanolamine, and S-nitrosoglutathione. All data were nonparametrically tested, and all <em>P</em> values were less than 0.001, indicating statistically significant differences in the levels of these eight metabolites between the two groups, as detailed in Table <a href="#T3" class="usa-link">3</a>. ROC curves for these metabolites were drawn, as shown in Figure <a href="#F7" class="usa-link">7</a>D. The ROC curve of anabasine had the highest AUC, greater than 0.95. The AUCs of 1-salicylate glucuronide, adrenoyl ethanolamide, dihydrocaffeic acid 3-<span class="font-variant-small-caps">o</span>-glucuronide, and nicotinuric acid were all greater than 0.85. The AUCs of lysophosphatidyl glycerol (18 : 1) and S-nitrosoglutathione’s ROC curves were greater than 0.78, while the AUC of <span class="font-variant-small-caps">o</span>-arachidonoyl ethanolamine was the smallest at 0.72. The sensitivity and specificity of each metabolite can be obtained at the cutoff point of the ROC curve. There were five metabolites with the specificity of 1, including 1-salicylate glucuronide, anabasine, dihydrocaffeic, acid 3-<span class="font-variant-small-caps">o</span>-glucuronide, nicotinuric acid, and S-nitrosoglutathione, but they varied in sensitivity. Anabasine had the highest sensitivity and specificity, with a sensitivity of 0.92 and specificity of 1. Other metabolites are listed in Table <a href="#T3" class="usa-link">3</a>. <p>The precision value represents the percentage of the numbers of the correct judgment of the metric in all the predictions, and the recall value is in the same sense as the sensitivity. The F1 metric is the harmonic mean of precision and recall value, and a higher F1 value represents a better combined performance of both. Cohen’s κ value is generally between 0 and 1, with a higher value indicating greater agreement between the two groups. We calculated the recall value and precision value (the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the PR curve, respectively) for the selected metabolites according to the obtained sensitivity and specificity values, and then calculated the F1 metric and Cohen’s κ value according to the corresponding formula. The detailed information is shown in Supplementary Table 3, Supplemental Digital Content 1, <a href="http://links.lww.com/JS9/A6" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://links.lww.com/JS9/A6</a>. As can be seen in the table, the results of these indicators are generally satisfactory.</p></section></section><section id="sec16"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Discussion</h2>
<p>The incidence of MPLC was about 15% of all lung cancer patients<a href="#R8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R8">8</a>, which was consistent with the conclusion of this study (15%, 24/160). Hu <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R9">9</a> found that the female gender was a risk factor for MPLC, which was consistent with our finding. As shown in Table <a href="#T1" class="usa-link">1</a>, the ratio of females in MPLC was 54.2% (13/23), which was significantly higher than 27.9% in SPLC, 31.6% in IM, and 26.1% in HGP, and statistical analysis showed that this difference was significant (<em>P</em>=0.01). The proportion of patients with previous smoking history in MPLC was 29.2% (7/24), lower than SPLC (43.4%), IM (36.8%), and HPG (38.5%), but the difference was not statistically significant (<em>P</em>=0.20). This is similar to other studies, in which the percentage of smokers in MPLC appears to be low, ranging from 32.3 to 59.0%<a href="#R10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R10">10</a>. Meanwhile, the proportion of smokers with multiple GGNs was lower than that of smokers with multiple solid nodules<a href="#R11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R11">11</a>. These findings suggested that smoking may have a low impact on the development of MPLC.</p>
<p>At present, CEA, ProGRP, NSE, CYFRA21-1, SCC, and CA12-5 are the most commonly used biomarkers in the preoperative differential diagnosis of lung cancer and benign pulmonary nodules. The sensitivity of one of these indicators applied alone is 0.30–0.50, and the specificity is 0.45–0.70. The sensitivity and specificity of the combined application of these indexes were ∼0.85 and 0.80<a href="#R12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R12">12</a>. In our literature search, they have not been used in MPLC and SPLC, lung cancer IM studies. Considering that these indicators are not satisfactory in the differential diagnosis of lung cancer, their ability to further differentiate MPLC from SPLC and IM of lung cancer is not optimistic, and further research is needed in the future. In our study, the sensitivity and specificity of major metabolites for distinguishing IM from MPLC were 0.70–0.95 and 0.68–1.00, respectively. The sensitivity and specificity of HPG and MPLC were 0.70–0.92 and 0.90–1.00, respectively. Considering that thin-slice chest CT has a strong ability to distinguish between SPLC and MPLC, the combined application of CT and metabolic markers can greatly improve the sensitivity and specificity of metabolic markers applied over chest CT alone. The sensitivity and specificity of the <span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-tyrosine and CT combination were 0.926 and 0.895, respectively. Meanwhile, the sensitivity and specificity of the metabolic panel and CT combination were 0.961 and 0.838, respectively. Compared with the original biomarkers, they had better sensitivity and specificity in their differential ability.</p>
<p>A large proportion of nodules in MPLC were GGN<a href="#R13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R13">13</a>. In our study, there were 43 (82.7%) GGNs in lung cancer lesions of MPLC, which is the same as this conclusion. In our study, 31 (59.6%) lesions were located in the upper lobes of both lungs in MPLC. This was consistent with previous studies<a href="#R5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R5">5</a>. In terms of tumor size, the majority of lung cancer lesions (90.4%) belonged to stage T1, which was also similar to the results of previous studies<a href="#R14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R14">14</a>. The MPLC in this study was divided into four types according to pathology, adenocarcinoma accounted for the vast majority (51, 98.1%), and early-stage adenocarcinoma (carcinoma <em>in situ</em>+MIA) accounted for the largest proportion (29, 55.8%). This was accordant with the result of previous studies<a href="#R14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R14">14</a>. Among the predominant histologic subtypes of MPLC, 43 (82.7%) belonged to highly differentiated type (acinar+lepidic), and the least (2, 3.8%) belonged to the poorly differentiated type (micropapillary). To sum up, the nodules in MPLC were mainly GGNs with a diameter of less than 3 cm. The pathology was generally carcinoma in situ or MIA, and the highly differentiated histological subtypes were common, such as lepidic and acinar types. These aspects were all important factors leading to the favorable prognosis of MPLC.</p>
<p>Clinically, it is often necessary to distinguish sMPLC from lung cancer with IM. Currently, there are three major methods: imaging examination, histological pathology, and molecular genetics. Imaging examinations, including CT and positron emission CT are the common methods to distinguish sMPLC and IM. If the imaging manifestations of two lung nodules meet the following characteristics: with pure ground glass or ground glass-dominant, with burr signs or vacuole signs, the standardized uptake value of different pulmonary lesion vary greatly<a href="#R15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R15">15</a>, and there is no lymph node metastasis. Then these two pulmonary lesions can initially be diagnosed as MPLC. Zhou <em>et al</em>
<a href="#R13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R13">.13</a>. found that male, solid nodule without ground glass opacity and lesions close to pleura were the risk factors for lung cancer with IM (<em>P</em>&lt;0.05). Histopathological result can be obtained from surgical or punctured specimens, and this is the principal method for clinical identification of IM and MPLC<a href="#R16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R16">16</a>. If the two tumor lesions have different histological types, or different predominant histologic subtypes, MPLC can be judged. If both are identical, further comparison of similarities in cytological and stromal characteristics is required<a href="#R17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R17">17</a>. Molecular genetics is mainly based on the type of gene mutation to make the diagnosis. DNA methylation and chromosomal rearrangement can also play an auxiliary role in diagnosis<a href="#R18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R18">18</a>. MPLC is diagnosed if the two lung cancer lesions have different genetic mutations. If they share two or more common mutations or one rare mutation in common, the diagnosis is IM<a href="#R19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R19">19</a>. The advantages and disadvantages of these methods are shown in Table <a href="#T4" class="usa-link">4</a>. The three methods are always comprehensively applied in clinical practice to improve the diagnostic accuracy. There is currently a lack of blood biomarkers that can distinguish the two. The principal metabolites discovered in this study may become important diagnostic methods in the future. Some people also seek the identification method from the perspective of proteomics. Ono <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R20">20</a> found that at the protein level, four cancer-related proteins (p53, p16, p27, and c-erbB2) were differentially expressed in MPLC. MPLC could be diagnosed if the sum of the differences in the ratios of four protein expressions between tumors was greater than 90.</p>

<div class="tw-foot p"><div class="fn" id="fn9"><p>IM, intrapulmonary metastasis; MPLC, multiple primary lung cancer; PET/CT, positron emission computed tomography; SUV, standardized uptake value.</p></div></div></section><p>Genetic testing was not performed in six MPLC patients in our study, five of which were because tumor lesions were too small to have sufficient tissue samples for genetic testing. Another tumor lesion was diagnosed as SCLC, so there was no further genetic testing. A total of 18 MPLC patients were tested for the main lesions, and the mutation rate was 88.9% (16/18). All mutation types were epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations, and no other mutation types were detected. Among them, EGFR exon 19 deletion had the highest incidence (50.0%, 9/18), followed by EGFR exon 21 mutation (27.8, 5/18). In addition, the EGFR exon 18 mutation and EGFR exon 20 mutation were also found, as shown in Table <a href="#T2" class="usa-link">2</a>. Liang <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R21">21</a> performed whole-exome sequencing on 115 lung cancer lesions from 32 MPLCs and found that the most common type of gene mutation in MPLC was EGFR mutation (31%), followed by BRAF (17%), KRAS (15%), and RBM10 (9%). No other types of gene mutations were found in our study, which may be associated with the small number of specimens.</p>
<p>Decanoylcarnitine exhibited good differential diagnosis ability between IM and MPLC with the specificity of 0.84 and sensitivity of 0.86. It is an organic compound containing fatty acids, belonging to endogenous lipids. Changes of decanoylcarnitine metabolic level occur in a variety of diseases, such as ulcerative colitis, Crohn’s disease, colorectal cancer<a href="#R22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R22">22</a>. In our study, its content in MPLC was four times higher than in IM. This may be due to the increased catabolism of decanoylcarnitine during lung cancer metastasis. Klupczynska <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R23">23</a> reported that carnitine levels were elevated in non–small-cell lung cancer patients, whereas amylcarnitine and propylcarnitine levels were decreased. Ni <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R24">24</a> found that the concentration of acylcarnitine in the plasma of lung cancer patients was significantly different from that of healthy people. Lim <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R25">25</a> discovered that acylcarnitine was one of the main metabolites that caused the difference in the metabolomics of lung tissues from various pathological types of lung cancer.</p>
<p>
<span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-Octanoylcarnitine is the physiologically active form of octanoylcarnitine. Octanoylcarnitine can be detected in midchain acyl-coA dehydrogenase deficiency. Octanoylcarnitine can distinguish lung cancer with IM and MPLC with sensitivity of 0.79 and specificity of 0.95. Cho <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R26">26</a> found that the metabolic level of octanoylcarnitine in the blood of patients with obesity and inflammatory bowel disease were significantly altered. Prostaglandin F2a ethanolamide is a class of lipid compounds naturally present in animal and plant membranes and is also a component of membrane-bound phospholipids. The plasma levels of these two metabolites in patients with MPLC were significantly higher than IM in our research.</p>
<p>The full names of PE(38:5) and PE(40:5) are PE[18:2(9Z,12Z)/20:3(8Z,11Z,14Z)] and PE[18:3(6Z,9Z, 12Z)/22:2(13Z,16Z)]. They are all phosphatidylethanolamines, and the phospholipids they contain are ubiquitous in nature, an important component of the lipid bilayer of cells, being involved in metabolism and signal transduction. In this study, the levels of these two metabolites in MPLC were significantly higher than IM, and their differential diagnosis ability were very high, with a sensitivity of 0.95 and specificity of 0.92 for PE(38:5). PE(40:5) had a sensitivity of 0.895 and specificity of 1.000. Qian <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R27">27</a> found that coordinated changes in the lipidome, including PE(38:5), and the gut microbiome can distinguish nonalcoholic steatohepatitis from isolated steatosis. Chen <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R28">28</a> reported that lipidomics including PE(38:5) can distinguish between early-stage non–small-cell lung cancer and healthy individuals. Demirkan <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R29">29</a> discovered eight kinds of circulating phospholipids were in relationship with blood pressure, including PE(40:5). Circulating phospholipid metabolites may contribute to the understanding of blood pressure regulation and had predictive value for the occurrence of hypertension.</p>
<p>
<span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-histidine, an essential amino acid, is a precursor for the biosynthesis of histamine and carnosine. Histidine is important for the maintenance of the myelin sheath that protects nerve cells and can be metabolized to the neurotransmitter histamine. Histamine plays important roles in immunity, gastric secretion, and sexual function. Disorders of histidine metabolism often cause increased levels of histidine in the blood, leading to symptoms such as developmental delay, tremors, ataxia, and psychosis. Our study found that the level of histidine in MPLC was significantly higher than IM, which could be used as a potential marker to identify the two. It had high diagnostic ability, with a sensitivity of 0.92 and a specificity of 0.84. Histidine has metabolic abnormalities in many cancers. Zhang <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R30">30</a> found that compared with pancreatitis and healthy subjects, the histidine content of pancreatic cancer patients was significantly lower. Brown <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R31">31</a> reported that the histidine content in colorectal cancer tumor tissue was significantly higher than that in adjacent normal tissue.</p>
<p>
<span class="font-variant-small-caps">l</span>-Tyrosine is a nonessential amino acid that can be converted from phenylalanine by phenylalanine hydroxylase. Many diseases have been associated with abnormal tyrosine metabolism, including Alzheimer’s disease<a href="#R32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R32">32</a>, colorectal cancer<a href="#R33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R33">33</a>, leukemia<a href="#R34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R34">34</a>, and other disease. In our study, it was found that its content in SPLC was higher than MPLC, and it had a certain differential diagnosis ability for both, with a sensitivity of 0.60 and specificity of 0.71. Gobeil <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R35">35</a> discovered that blood tyrosine levels in patients with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) was higher than those in patients without NAFLD. This study revealed a potential causal effect of NAFLD on blood tyrosine levels, suggesting that it may represent a new NAFLD biomarker.</p>
<p>Nicotinuric acid is an acylglycine, a minor metabolite of fatty acids. Studies have shown that the excretion of certain acylglycine is increased in some congenital metabolic diseases. Therefore, measurement of these metabolites in body fluids can be used to diagnose diseases associated with mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation. In our study, plasma concentrations of nicotinuric acid were significantly increased in MPLC compared with healthy subjects. The specificity and sensitivity of nicotinuric acid were 1.0 and 0.79, indicating good differential diagnosis ability. S-Nitrosoglutathione is a circulating endogenous nitric oxide (NO) reservoir and a potential NO donor with antiplatelet properties. This study showed that the content of this substance in MPLC is significantly higher than healthy people. However, Zhang <em>et al.</em>
<a href="#R36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R36">36</a> found that nitrosoglutathione, as an endogenous NO carrier, can induce apoptosis of lung cancer cells, and further research is needed to explore the relevant mechanism.</p>
<p>The limitations of this study are as follows: (a) Due to the limitation of sample size, no validation group was set up to further judge the diagnostic efficiency of the selected metabolites. (b) This study was nontargeted metabolomics, and it was necessary to conduct targeted metabolomics research on key metabolites in the future to lay the foundation for clinical translation. (c) Only plasma samples were taken as the research object. If tumor tissue samples were collected and studied together, it might have a more comprehensive understanding of the metabolic changes of MPLC.</p></section><section id="sec17"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusions</h2>
<p>There have been few studies on MPLC from the perspective of metabolomics. We conducted a research with the purpose of discovering new biomarkers to assist with differential diagnosis of MPLC. By comparing the plasma metabolic profiles of each group, some principal metabolites with diagnostic significance for MPLC were discovered. These metabolites exhibited favorable diagnostic ability with high sensitivity and specificity. They showed the potential to become the biomarkers for the differential diagnosis of MPLC. This study laid a foundation for further research and clinical translation.</p></section><section id="sec18"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Ethical approval</h2>
