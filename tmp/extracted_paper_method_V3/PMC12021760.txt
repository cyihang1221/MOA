</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="Abs1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Objective</h3>
<p id="Par1">To explore the key molecules and regulatory mechanisms of lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<p id="Par2">The differential genes and key genes of lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer were analyzed by utilizing multiple data sets. The key genes were analyzed by GSEA analysis, transcription factor analysis, nomogram prediction model construction, immune infiltration analysis, GSVA analysis, drug sensitive analysis and single cell data analysis.</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p id="Par3">Abnormal expression of key genes including CDRT15P1, DENND3, F2R, FNDC3B, IRAK3, MS4A2, PDK4, PKIA and activation of related signaling pathways might be the result of ultraviolet radiation-induced DNA damage, which was closely related to lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. The key genes were regulated by a variety of transcription factors, which were strongly connected with the invasion of immune cells and the sensitivity of a variety of drugs. The nomogram prediction model, which is based on the key genes associated with lymph node metastasis and the TNM of gastric cancer, demonstrated a high level of predictive efficiency.</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h3>
<p id="Par4">CDRT15P1, DENND3, F2R, FNDC3B, IRAK3, MS4A2, PDK4 and PKIA may be the key genes affecting lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, and F2R has higher biological importance.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, Bioinformatics analysis, Nomogram prediction model, Mendelian randomization</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p id="Par56">According to research, the probability of lymph node metastasis in patients diagnosed with early gastric cancer is 18.36% [<a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>], and the dissemination of cancer cells within lymph nodes is significantly associated with the development and metastasis of the primary cancer [<a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>, <a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a>], indicating that lymph node involvement elevates the likelihood of metastases. As a result, it is thoroughly significant to accurately identify the lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. The up regulation and down regulation of many genes are related to lymph node metastasis [<a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a>]. However, few molecular markers have been used to diagnose lymph node metastasis. Some large-scale sequencing failed to identify the driving mutation of tumor metastasis [<a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a>–<a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a>]. Recent studies have found some gene drivers that the early development of metastasis can be partially explained [<a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a>–<a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a>]. Consequently, it is requisite to discern the genome related to metastasis, thereby enabling a superior comprehension of the molecular mechanism of lymph node metastasis.</p>
<p id="Par57">The method of using bioinformatics as an auxiliary research method has been widely used in tumor research. The genes related to the development of tumor are analyzed and screened through bioinformatics database, laying the foundation for clinical disease diagnosis and new drug development [<a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">11</a>]. In traditional observational epidemiological studies, the relationship between exposure and conclusion is affected by many factors, such as confounding factors and reverse causality. Such studies have adverse factors such as time-consuming, laborious and long observation period. Mendelian Randomization (MR) primarily examines the association between exposures and outcomes by means of exposure factors and related instrumental variables (IVs) [<a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a>]. This research endeavors to delve into the crucial molecules and regulatory mechanisms of lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer by means of multiple datasets and MR, thereby furnishing a theoretical foundation for forecasting lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials and methods</h2>
<section id="Sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials</h3>
<section id="Sec4"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Download the original mRNA expression data of gastric cancer data based on TCGA database</h4>
<p id="Par58">A total of 393 samples associated with tumor lymph node metastasis were extracted for analysis. These samples were categorized into two groups: the lymph node non-metastasis group (n = 124) and the lymph node metastasis group (n = 269), which were utilized for subsequent analysis. The single-cell data file from <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE163558" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSE163558</a> was retrieved from the GEO public database. This dataset included five samples with intact single-cell expression profiles, comprising three samples without lymph node metastasis and two samples with lymph node metastasis.</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Exposed data and outcome data</h4>
<p id="Par59">Extract the required eQTL data through eQTLGen consortium database. The participants in the outcome-related GWAS included predominantly individuals of European ancestry. The outcome summary data were obtained from the EBI database (ebi-a-GCST90018849), the GWAS Catalog encompasses a range of top-level associations, publications, and comprehensive summary statistics. The data from the GWAS Catalog data have been mapped to the Genome Assembly and dbSNP Build. Within this dataset, there were 1,029 cases and 475,087 controls pertaining to gastric cancer.</p></section></section><section id="Sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<section id="Sec7"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Difference analysis</h4>
<p id="Par60">The limma package can analyze the differential genes between different groups. This study was categorized into two groups: one group consisted of patients with gastric cancer without lymph node metastasis, while the other group included patients with lymph node metastasis. Objective to analyze the molecular mechanism of lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. The molecular mechanisms underlying lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer were investigated. Gene expression differences were analyzed using the R package “Limma”, with the screening criterion of a p value less than 0.01. Differentially expressed genes between the two groups were identified, and a volcano plot illustrating these differentially expressed genes was generated.</p></section><section id="Sec8"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Metascape enrichment analysis</h4>
<p id="Par61">Metascape database (<a href="http://www.metascape.org" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.metascape.org</a>) was utilized to annotate the functions of differential genes. GO pathway analysis was conducted on the selected genes, with a minimum overlap of ≥ 3 and a p-value of ≤ 0.01 being regarded as statistically notable.</p></section><section id="Sec9"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Mendelian randomization analysis</h4>
<p id="Par62">The outcome identifier retrieved from the EBI database was derived from the summary data of Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) (<a href="https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/</a>). The causal relationships associated with eQTL were extracted from this database. For each gene, the screened SNPs that met the criteria for potential IVs were selected, applying a genome-wide significance threshold of P &lt; 1e-8. Calculate the LD among the SNPs, and among those with R2 &lt; 0.001 (clumping window size = 10,000 kb), only SNPs with P2 &lt; 5e-8 were retained. Four statistical methods—specifically, MR Egger, Weighted median, Inverse variance weighted, and Weighted mode—were employed sequentially. In instances where only one statistical approach was applicable to a SNP within the causal relationship, the Wald ratio was utilized exclusively. These methodologies were implemented to assess the reliability of causal relationships and to derive a thorough estimation of the influence of both cis and partially cross-regional gene expression in blood on gastric cancer.</p></section><section id="Sec10"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Sensitivity analysis</h4>
<p id="Par63">A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis utilizing MR was performed to evaluate the specific genetic variants on the risk of gastric cancer. This methodology employed a systematic approach to identify and exclude variants that have a disproportionate impact on the overall estimate. This was achieved by sequentially omitting each SNP and subsequently recalculating the pooled effect size of the remaining SNPs. The exclusion of each SNP resulted in the generation of a new point estimate accompanied by its respective 95% confidence interval. This process enabled the assessment of the unique contribution of each SNP and the overall robustness of the findings. A summary was provided that included the estimated value following the removal of each SNP, as well as the total estimated value when all SNPs were considered. By comparing the estimates of SNPs, it is possible to evaluate whether there is impact of excluding any individual SNP on the overall results, thus allowing for an assessment of the robustness of the analysis conducted.</p></section><section id="Sec11"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Go enrichment analysis of key genes</h4>
<p id="Par64">Following the identification of key genes, "ClusterProfiler" was employed to annotate the functions of these important genes, thereby facilitating a comprehensive exploration of their functional correlations. The enrichment analysis of GO pathways indicated that a q value and adjusted p value of less than 0.05 were established as the screening conditions for determining significant enrichment.</p></section><section id="Sec12"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">GSEA analysis of key genes</h4>
<p id="Par65">Patients were classified into high and low expression groups based upon the expression levels of key genes. GSEA was utilized to investigate the differences in signaling pathways between these groups. The gene set was sourced from the Molecular Signatures Database (MsigDB), and subsequent analysis involved annotating the gene set to elucidate the differences in signaling pathways between the categorized groups. The screening conditions for determining significant enrichment were set at a p-value of less than 0.05. Subsequently, the correlations were ranked based on their consistency scores.</p></section><section id="Sec13"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">GSVA analysis of key genes</h4>
<p id="Par66">Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) is a methodology employed for evaluating gene set enrichment in transcriptomes. GSVA enables the transformation of gene-level changes into modifications at the pathway level by systematically scoring relevant gene sets. This process allows for the identification of biological functions under investigation. In this study, gene sets were sourced from the MsigDB, also, the alterations in biological functions were assessed across various samples using GSVA comprehensive scoring.</p></section><section id="Sec14"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Transcription factor regulatory network of key genes</h4>
<p id="Par67">This research utilized the R package "RcisTarget" for the prediction of transcription factors. All analyses conducted by RcisTarget were grounded in motif analysis. The NES of a motif was contingent upon the total number of motifs present in the database. In addition to the motifs identified in the source data, supplementary annotation files were created based on the similarity between motifs and gene sequences. The initial step in evaluating the overexpression of specific motifs within a gene set involved calculating the Area Under the Curve (AUC) for each motif-motif pair. This was conducted based on the calculation of the recovery curve for gene sets associated with motif sequencing.</p></section><section id="Sec15"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Immune infiltration analysis of key genes</h4>
<p id="Par68">The CIBERSORT method was utilized to analyze the immune cells associated with key genes. Additionally, the relative proportions of 22 distinct immune cell types were evaluated, along with the expression levels of key genes within these immune cells.</p></section><section id="Sec16"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Drug sensitivity analysis of key genes</h4>
<p id="Par69">The R package “oncoPredict” was employed by utilizing the GDSC database to forecast the chemotherapy sensitivity of individual tumor samples. The IC50 estimates for each individual chemotherapeutic agent were derived through a regression analysis. The reliability of the regression and predictions was evaluated through tenfold cross-validation utilizing the GDSC database. Default values were applied for the parameters, including mitigating the impact of batch effect through "combat" and averaging the expression levels of identical genes.</p></section><section id="Sec17"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Nomogram model construction</h4>
<p id="Par70">Nomogram model was constructed utilizing regression analysis to visually depict the interactions among multiple variables within a predictive model. This representation involved plotting line segments with corresponding scales on a unified plane, in accordance with a predetermined ratio, thereby illustrating the relationship between gene expression and other variables. Through the development of a multifactor regression model, a score was allocated to each level of the influencing factors, reflecting their respective contributions to the outcome variable as demonstrated by the magnitude of the regression coefficients. The individual scores were then aggregated to yield a total score, which was employed to compute the predicted value. To validate the risk stratification capability of the nomogram, the linear predictor values for each sample were extracted from the established multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model using the predict() function, which served as risk scores. Subsequently, the samples were divided into low-risk and high-risk groups based on the median of the risk scores. Survival curves for both the low-risk and high-risk groups were plotted using the Kaplan–Meier method, and the survival differences between the two groups were compared using the log-rank test. Furthermore, separate Nomogram models were constructed using DSS and PFI as survival outcomes to evaluate patient prognosis from different perspectives.</p></section><section id="Sec18"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Single cell analysis</h4>
<p id="Par71">The expression profile was first examined utilizing the Seurat package, which involved filtering cells according to the total number of unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) per cell, the quantity of expressed genes, and the proportion of mitochondrial and ribosomal reads associated with each cell. The DoubletFinder tool (version 2.0.4) was employed to identify and filter double cells from each sample individually. Subsequently, the data underwent standardization and normalization processes, followed by PCA and sequential analysis. The optimal number of Pcs was identified utilizing an Elbow Plot, while the spatial relationships among the clusters were elucidated through UMAP analysis. The SingleR software was automated by querying the CellMarker and PanglaoDB databases, as well as relevant literature. An annotation-assisted methodology was employed to identify the cell types present in the respective tissue, along with their corresponding marker genes for cell annotation. Figure <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a> illustrates the workflow of this study.</p>

<p id="Par72">MR analysis is predicated on three presuppositions: (1) the correlation assumption; (2) the independence assumption; and (3) the exclusivity hypothesis. <section id="Sec21"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Differential gene analysis and differential gene pathway analysis</h3>
<p id="Par73">There were two groups: the metastasis group, which consisted of patients with lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer, and the non-metastasis group, which included patients without lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer. The Limma package was employed to identify differential gene expression (p-value &lt; 0.01) between these two groups. A total of 1,054 differentially expressed genes were identified, which included 317 related genes that were up-regulated and 737 related genes that were down-regulated (see Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>A-B). Go analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis were subsequently conducted on the screened differential genes. The Metascape analysis revealed that these genes were predominantly enriched in several biological processes, including acrosomal vesicle formation, regulation of neurotrophin TRK receptor signaling pathways, histone mRNA catabolic processes, protein catabolic processes, NAD biosynthetic processes, and monocarboxylic acid metabolic processes, among others (see Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>C). After excluding a broad array of pathways, the differentially expressed genes associated with lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer were primarily focused on the Wnt signaling pathway, pathways involved in cancer, the regulation of the neurotrophin TRK receptor signaling pathway, among other pathways. TRK signaling pathway regulates cell proliferation. The activation of the Wnt signaling pathway can influence the occurrence and progression of tumors. Serine/threonine protein kinase-related signaling pathways, including the MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways, are closely associated with the metastasis of gastric cancer. Consequently, these signaling pathways play a crucial role in the lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer.</p>

<p id="Par81">In all samples analyzed in this study, univariate Cox analysis (Fig. <a href="#Fig11" class="usa-link">11</a>A) and multivariate Cox analysis (Fig. <a href="#Fig11" class="usa-link">11</a>B) were performed on the variables. The univariate Cox analysis revealed that the P-values for variables such as Age, Stage, T, F2F, PDK4, and PKIA were all less than 0.05, indicating that these variables had a significant impact on survival time. The multivariate Cox analysis demonstrated that DENND3 (P-value = 0.026) and F2R (P-value = 0.034) yielded statistically significant results. Age (P-value &lt; 0.001) and Gender (P-value = 0.083) approached significance, but their effects may not be as pronounced as those of DENND3 and F2R. the expression distribution of key genes associated with lymph node metastatic gastric cancer and the values of various clinical indicators (including variables such as age, gender, TNM staging, etc.) exhibit varying degrees of contribution to the overall scoring process (see Fig. <a href="#Fig12" class="usa-link">12</a>A). Additionally, this study performed predictive analyses on overall survival (OS) at two intervals: three years and five years (see Fig. <a href="#Fig12" class="usa-link">12</a>B). The results indicated a high concordance between the predicted OS and the observed OS, further validating the accuracy of the Nomogram model. Additionally, we constructed the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve and the Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) curve, which demonstrated that the Nomogram model possesses a high degree of discriminative ability and clinical utility in predicting patient survival outcomes (Fig. <a href="#Fig12" class="usa-link">12</a>C-D). Survival analysis, stratified by the median risk score, revealed a clear separation between the survival curves of the high-risk and low-risk groups. The Log-rank test yielded a significant p-value, with the high-risk group exhibiting a markedly worse prognosis compared to the low-risk group (p &lt; 0.05), underscoring the robust risk stratification capability of the Nomogram (Fig. <a href="#Fig12" class="usa-link">12</a>E). In the model utilizing Disease-Specific Survival (DSS) as the survival outcome, various variables contribute to differing extents (Fig. <a href="#Fig13" class="usa-link">13</a>A). The model demonstrates relatively accurate predictions for 3-year and 5-year survival probabilities (Fig. <a href="#Fig13" class="usa-link">13</a>B). ROC curves and DCA curves are presented (Fig. <a href="#Fig13" class="usa-link">13</a>C-D). It is evident that the nomogram model based on DSS as the survival outcome exhibits superior predictive efficiency. The model utilizing Progression-Free Interval (PFI) as the survival outcome demonstrates varying degrees of contribution (Fig. <a href="#Fig14" class="usa-link">14</a>A) in the scoring process, depending on different variables. It predicts the 3-year and 5-year survival probabilities with greater accuracy, with the prediction accuracy for the 5-year survival being slightly superior to that of the 3-year survival (Fig. <a href="#Fig14" class="usa-link">14</a>B). Additionally, the ROC curve and DCA curve are presented (Fig. <a href="#Fig14" class="usa-link">14</a>C-D). Our findings indicate that the nomogram model employing PFI as the survival outcome exhibits enhanced predictive efficiency.</p>

<p id="Par83">The probability of cancer cells metastasizing to lymph nodes in individuals diagnosed with gastric cancer post-surgery is estimated to be 20% [<a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a>]. To date, the specific mechanisms that contribute to lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer are not fully elucidated. Consequently, this research seeks to explore the molecular mechanisms linked to lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer through the analysis of multiple datasets and MR.Additionally, the research seeks to identify novel therapeutic targets for lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, with the intention of contributing innovative perspectives to treatment strategies.</p>
<p id="Par84">In this study, the differential genes linked to lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer were primarily found to be concentrated in several key pathways, including the regulation of neurotrophin TRK receptor signaling pathway, pathways involved in cancer, and the Wnt signaling pathway, among others. The TRK, which is a NT receptor encoded by a proto-oncogene, serves as a crucial factor in the signaling pathways governing cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Currently, known NT receptors include TrkA, TrkB and TrkC, and their corresponding ligands mainly include NGF, BDNF and TrkC ligand NT3. Previous studies have found that BDNF has the highest activity on TrkB [<a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a>]. The activation of the TrkA-associated signaling pathway has been demonstrated to promote the proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer cells [<a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a>]. Furthermore, elevated levels of TrkA expression are significantly associated with lymph node metastasis. The incidence of distant metastasis or recurrence in patients with elevated expression levels of TrkC is markedly higher compared to that in patients with reduced expression levels of this receptor [<a href="#CR16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR16">16</a>]. The expression of TrkB is closely associated with the invasion of tumor cells, and the activation of the TrkB/BDNF signal transduction pathway confers anoikis inhibition and angiogenesis [<a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a>]. Studies have found that TrkB signal transduction pathway plays a prominent regulatory role in tumor progression. As a proto oncogene, TrkB can cause malignant transformation of fibroblasts [<a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a>], and can also activate downstream EMT related nuclear protein to promote tumor lymph node metastasis [<a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">19</a>]. In a particular study, a cohort of 67 patients diagnosed with gastric cancer was identified as the focus of the research. The findings demonstrated that the expression levels of TrkB in patients with lymph node metastases in gastric cancer (n = 42) were significantly higher than those observed in patients without lymph node metastases (n = 25) [<a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">20</a>]. Other studies have found that both BDNF and TrkB exhibit high expression levels in tissues affected by gastric cancer as well as in metastatic lymph nodes [<a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a>]. Subsequent research has demonstrated that TrkB is significantly associated with distant metastasis in gastric cancer, lymph node metastasis, and lymphatic invasion [<a href="#CR22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR22">22</a>].Through the studies mentioned above, we found that the high expression of TRK and the activation of related signaling pathways are closely associated with lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, as demonstrated in both clinical and in vitro studies. Therefore, the factors that are upstream and downstream of this pathway may be critical in influencing lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. Wnt is a secreted glycoprotein that activates specific receptor Frizzled (Fzd) family proteins on the cell membrane surface. This activation induces a conformational change that inhibits the degradation activity of the glycogen synthase complex. Consequently, β-catenin is released for intracellular accumulation, where it associates with the T cell factor/lymphocyte enhancer factor (Tcf/Lef) family to form a transcriptional complex. This complex subsequently activates the transcription of certain downstream proto-oncogenes. Clinical studies have found that among 74 specimens of gastric cancer, differential expression was observed in Wnt pathway-related proteins, including Wnt-5a, Fzd5, GSK3β, Axin, CK1, ubiquitin, CyclinD1, and c-myc [<a href="#CR23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR23">23</a>]. Wnt signaling pathway is crucial in the initiation and progression of multiple tumor types [<a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a>]. miRNAs have the capacity to either activate or inhibit the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby influencing tumor formation, cell proliferation, metastasis, and drug resistance [<a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">25</a>]. Recent investigations have indicated that the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway assumes a pivotal role in facilitating epithelial-mesenchymal transition and the metastatic behavior of gastric cancer cells [<a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a>]. From the studies mentioned above, we can conclude that the activation of the Wnt signaling pathway influences the proliferation, metastasis, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition of gastric cancer. Furthermore, the activation of Wnt is closely associated with cytokines in lymphocytes. Serine/threonine protein kinases mainly include: PKA, protein kinase C, CDK family, MAPK family, protein kinase G, integrin linked kinase, etc. Due to the large amount of protein kinases involved, the signaling pathways contained are extremely complicated. AKT is a serine/threonine kinase. AKT signaling pathway is a classic intracellular signaling pathway, which can participate in the development of various types of tumors [<a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a>]. Research has demonstrated that the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is critically involved in the onset and progression of gastric cancer [<a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">28</a>]. Modulation of this pathway has been shown to suppress the invasion, proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer MKN-45 cells [<a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a>]. MAPK signaling pathway also plays a crucial role in the occurrence and development of tumors. It is divided into four subgroups, ERK1/2, C-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), p38 mitogen activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) and extracellular regulated protein kinases ERK5, representing four classical MAPK pathways respectively. Studies have found that in gastric cancer, the ERK/MAPK pathway can regulate the activity of MMPs, facilitating the entry of cancer cells into the endothelium or lymphatic vessels. This regulation influences cell migration and invasion [<a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a>, <a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a>]. Additionally, JNK and p38 MAPK play crucial roles in stress responses, such as inflammation and apoptosis [<a href="#CR32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR32">32</a>], and are involved in the regulation of EMT in gastric cancer, thereby affecting its metastasis [<a href="#CR33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR33">33</a>]. Current studies have identified ERK5 as a crucial signaling molecule involved in nearly all biological characteristics of cancer cells and as a marker of poor prognosis in cancer patients [<a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a>]. Our research indicates that the serine/threonine protein kinase signaling pathway encompasses various tumor-related signaling pathways, which play a significant role in the metastasis of gastric cancer. Consequently, the factors both upstream and downstream of this pathway may also be critical in influencing lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. Based on the support of the above literature, it can be inferred that the signaling pathways linked to the differentially expressed genes identified in this study are implicated in the regulation of the initiation and progression of gastric cancer.</p>
<p id="Par85">Eight key genes related with lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, CDRT15P1, DENND3, F2R, FNDC3B, IRAK3, MS4A2, PDK4 and PKIA were finally screened by MR.CDRT15P1 (CMT1A Duplicated Region Transcript 15 Pseudogene 1) is the first pseudogene associated with CDRT15. CDRT15 was first identified in the study of the 1.4-Mb CMT1A Duplication/ HNPP Deletion Genomic Region [<a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>]. The CDRT15 gene is located on chromosome 17 and consists of 1,190 bases. It encodes the CDRT15 protein, which is composed of 188 amino acids. The predicted site of its expression is the plasma membrane [<a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a>]. Research has indicated that CDRT15P1 may play a role in immune invasion in Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), and its expression levels are elevated in CCA [<a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a>].DENND3 is the activator of small GTPase Rab12. So far, the activation mechanism of DENND3 is not clear [<a href="#CR37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR37">37</a>]. The mRNA expression of DENND3 was up-regulated in lung adenocarcinoma cells A549 and H2122 [<a href="#CR38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR38">38</a>]. By sequencing the genome of prostate basal cell carcinoma, it was found that DENND3 gene was also changed [<a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a>]. CircRNA is a class of single stranded RNA, which is effective in various diseases [<a href="#CR40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR40">40</a>]. CircFNDC3B is significantly up-regulated in oral squamous cell carcinoma and exhibits a positive association with lymph node metastasis [<a href="#CR41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR41">41</a>]. Furthermore, circFNDC3B has been found to enhance the invasion and migration of gastric cancer cells [<a href="#CR42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR42">42</a>]. Recombinant IRAK family proteins are associated with TLR and downstream signaling pathways of the interleukin receptor family [<a href="#CR43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR43">43</a>]. IRAK3 methylation is correlated with adverse prognosis and tumor stage in individuals diagnosed with liver cancer [<a href="#CR44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR44">44</a>], and IRAK3 expression is connected with colitis-related tumorigenesis in murine models [<a href="#CR45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR45">45</a>]. IRAK3 protein indirectly participates in the innate immune response of pathogenic factors by regulating TLR/NF-κB signal transduction [<a href="#CR46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR46">46</a>, <a href="#CR47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR47">47</a>]. Tumor associated macrophages mainly regulate tumor immune escape through M2 polarization, and the expression of MS4A2 is notably elevated in tumor associated macrophages. The expression of MS4A2 in mast cells is implicated in the initiation and advancement of colon cancer [<a href="#CR48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR48">48</a>]. Research has demonstrated that MS4A2 is correlated with the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma through single cell sequencing analysis [<a href="#CR49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR49">49</a>]. Studies have found that MS4A2 may be a novel biomarker for M2 macrophages and is linked to unfavorable prognostic outcomes in gastric cancer [<a href="#CR50" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR50">50</a>]. PDK molecular family is expressed in across various cancer types, and is significantly associated with tumor cytotoxic proliferation, migration and invasion [<a href="#CR51" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR51">51</a>, <a href="#CR52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR52">52</a>]. Notably, PDK4 has been identified as being correlated with the prognosis of gastric cancer [<a href="#CR53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR53">53</a>]. Inhibition of PDK4 expression can limit the metastasis of gastric cancer [<a href="#CR54" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR54">54</a>]. PKIA regulates intracellular signal transduction pathways mainly through interactions with other molecules. Studies have found that the methylation status of PKIA may serve as a promising prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma [<a href="#CR55" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR55">55</a>]. Furthermore, the expression levels of PKIA are linked to the prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma [<a href="#CR56" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR56">56</a>] and are also associated with serum levels of 25 hydroxyvitamin D in the context of colon cancer [<a href="#CR57" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR57">57</a>]. The pathway analysis of key genes showed that PKIA, F2R and MS4A2 were primarily associated with the enrichment of pathways related to immune response and signal transduction, and exhibited a stronger correlation with lymph node metastasis. Utilizing network analysis to explore key genes in the interaction network, it was found that F2R may have a substantial impact on the lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer. F2R, which is also referred to as PAR1, is classified within the G protein-coupled receptor family [<a href="#CR58" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR58">58</a>]. In most studies, F2R is considered an oncogene for its highly expression in tumor tissues. It is currently believed that F2R may take effect as a receptor for some substances that mediate metastasis of malignant tumors [<a href="#CR59" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR59">59</a>, <a href="#CR60" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR60">60</a>]. Furthermore, F2R is implicated in the initiation and advancement of tumors through its involvement in cellular signal transduction processes [<a href="#CR61" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR61">61</a>], activating inflammatory responses, breaking the endothelial barrier and participating in immune escape [<a href="#CR62" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR62">62</a>]. F2R is significantly overexpressed in various malignant tumors, and this heightened expression is closely associated with biological behaviors, including tumor metastasis and invasion [<a href="#CR63" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR63">63</a>]. The high expression of F2R can activate ERK1/2 and AKT, thus promoting tumor angiogenesis [<a href="#CR64" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR64">64</a>]. The key genes identified in this research are integral to the initiation and advancement of gastric cancer. In particular, the increased expression of F2R may have a substantial correlation with lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p>
<p id="Par86">Due to the important role of key genes in lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, this research conducted GSEA analysis and GSVA analysis on key genes. GSEA analysis showed that although the signal pathways involved in each key gene were not completely consistent, some key genes were concentrated on the same signal pathway. Mainly including NF-kappa B signaling pathway, B cell receptor signaling pathway, Hedgehog signaling pathway, Calcium signaling pathway, Relaxin signaling pathway. Studies have demonstrated that the expression levels of NF-κB in lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer are elevated compared to those observed in the corresponding primary tumors of gastric cancer. Therefore, the elevated expression of NF-κB and associated signaling pathways may be instrumental in the process of lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer [<a href="#CR65" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR65">65</a>]. The activation of NF-κB signaling pathway can promote epithelial mesenchymal metastasis, enhance the invasiveness and metastatic potential of gastric cancer, and is associated with an unfavorable prognosis in gastric cancer [<a href="#CR66" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR66">66</a>]. The suppression of the NF-κB signaling pathway has been shown to impede lymphangiogenesis and metastasis associated with gastric cancer [<a href="#CR65" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR65">65</a>]. B cell receptor signaling pathway involves a variety of B cell receptors. Studies have found that invasive B cells are more likely to exhibit tumor specificity than T cells in gastric cancer [<a href="#CR67" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR67">67</a>]. The Hedgehog signaling pathway has been shown to exhibit aberrant activation in a range of cancers. Research indicates that the inhibition of this pathway can effectively reduce the migration and proliferation of gastric cancer cells [<a href="#CR68" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR68">68</a>]. The latest research found that calcium related signaling pathways may facilitate the invasion and metastasis of gastric cancer [<a href="#CR69" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR69">69</a>]. Relaxin related signaling pathway can protect gastric cancer cells from oxidative damage [<a href="#CR70" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR70">70</a>]. It is thus clear that the signaling pathways involved in key genes significantly contribute to the onset and development of gastric cancer. Through the GSVA analysis, it was found that the five key genes CDRT15P1, DENND3, F2R, PDK4 and PKIA were significantly correlated to the genes down regulated in response to UV radiation, which may have more obvious DNA damage. Research has indicated that the UV_RESPONSE_DN signaling pathway mediated by LAMC1 may be significantly associated with the onset and development of gastric cancer [<a href="#CR71" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR71">71</a>]. Studies have found that ultraviolet radiation can cause extensive DNA damage, leading to the occurrence of gastric cancer [<a href="#CR72" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR72">72</a>]. DENND3, F2R, IRAK3, MS4A2 were closely related to IL6 mediated JAK-STAT3 signaling pathway. Studies have found that PD-L1 may stimulate the proliferation of MDSCs and regulate the immune escape of gastric cancer cells through the activation of the IL-6/STAT3 signaling pathway in vitro [<a href="#CR73" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR73">73</a>]. Cathepsin K is implicated in the enhancement of gastric cancer cells and participate in related EMT. Furthermore, gastric cancer patients exhibiting elevated levels of cathepsin K are likely to demonstrate enrichment of the IL-6-JAK-STAT3 signaling pathway [<a href="#CR74" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR74">74</a>]. In summary, through GSEA analysis and GSVA analysis, it was found that key genes may be based on changes in the expression of DNA damage under ultraviolet radiation or IL6 related factors, which may affect the activation of related signaling pathways and lead to lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p>
<p id="Par87">The transcription factor analysis of key genes showed that FNDC3B, MS4A2 and PDK4 genes were enriched in the basic sequence cisbp_M4665, and the highest score of NES was 5.88. DENND3 and FNDC3B genes were enriched in the basic sequence cisbp_M4347, and the NES score was 4.94. F2R, FNDC3B, IRAK3 and PKIA genes were enriched in the basic sequence cisbp_M1912, and the NES score was 4.16. It is thus obvious that key genes are regulated by a variety of transcription factors, and many key genes are enriched in the same basic sequence. It is widely accepted that lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer is significantly associated with immune responses.An analysis of immune infiltration reveals that key genes demonstrate a robust association with diverse immune cell types. This finding suggests that these key genes are implicated in the extent of immune cell infiltration and play a crucial role in shaping the immune microenvironment. We found that CDRT15P1 is negatively correlated with resting mast cells, while FNDC3B is positively correlated with activated mast cells. Additionally, MS4A2 plays a role in IgE signaling, all of which are associated with allergic diseases. Several genes, including DENND3, PKIA, MS4A2, and PDK4, showed a positive correlation with B cell subsets but a negative correlation with activated T cells, particularly T cells follicular helper and T cells CD4 memory activated. This suggests that these genes may play a role in regulating the balance between humoral and cellular immunity, potentially influencing the development of autoimmune diseases [<a href="#CR75" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR75">75</a>]. CDRT15P1, F2R, and FNDC3B are associated with different macrophage subtypes, specifically M0, M1, and M2 polarization. This suggests that these genes may be involved in regulating the functional states of macrophages, impacting the processes of inflammation and tissue repair, and potentially contributing to tumor immune escape [<a href="#CR76" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR76">76</a>–<a href="#CR78" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR78">78</a>]. PDK4 and IRAK3 are involved in metabolic regulation, specifically glucose metabolism, as well as in the regulation of negative signaling. They may influence immune cell function through metabolic reprogramming or by inhibiting overactivation [<a href="#CR79" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR79">79</a>, <a href="#CR80" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR80">80</a>]. The literature indicates that alterations in these genes can impact immune cell function, thereby modifying the response to external stimuli [<a href="#CR81" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR81">81</a>] and potentially contributing to lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p>
<p id="Par88">Furthermore, the analysis of drug sensitivity concerning these key genes uncovered a notable relationship between them and the responsiveness to a range of pharmacological agents. Notably, the primary drugs identified as sensitive include Bicalutamide, Afatinib, RSK inhibitors, and Lestaurtinib, among others. At present, there are many predictive models for lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer [<a href="#CR82" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR82">82</a>, <a href="#CR83" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR83">83</a>], but the difficulty of obtaining variables limits the clinical application of predictive models. It was found that the AUC value of external validation could reach 0.77 [<a href="#CR84" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR84">84</a>] when easily available variables such as tumor size, P53 positive results, CA19-9 results were incorporated into the predictive model for lymph node metastasis in early gastric cancer. Based on the data of key genes of lymph node metastasis and TNM staging of gastric cancer, this study established nomogram prediction model. The findings indicated that the nomogram prediction model, which used OS as the survival outcome, had a three-year validation AUC of 0.71, and the difference between the AUC of five-year validation and three-year validation was only 0.017, with small generalization error. Thus, the nomogram prediction model has good prediction effect. Through single cell data analysis, it was found that the key genes were different in the involved subpopulation cells. DENND3 exhibited the highest expression levels in neutrophils, while FNDC3B demonstrated elevated expression primarily in fibroblasts and macrophages. IRAK3 showed increased expression predominantly in macrophages and neutrophils. MS4A2 was primarily expressed at higher levels in mast cells, whereas PDK4 exhibited elevated expression mainly in endothelial cells. PKIA was predominantly expressed in proliferative cells, and F2R showed higher expression levels primarily in both endothelial cells and fibroblasts. GSEA analysis and GSVA analysis showed that the key genes were concentrated in NF-kappa B signaling pathway, B cell receptor signaling pathway, Hedgehog signaling pathway, and other signaling pathways. Through further gene analysis of immune metabolism related pathways in single cells by AUCell, the key genes are mainly concentrated in angiogenesis, tnfa_signaling_via_nfkb, epithelial_mesenchymal_transition, hedgehog_signaling, and other signaling pathways. We believe that GSEA analysis and GSVA analysis are similar to the gene analysis of immune metabolism related pathways in single cells, which further verifies that key genes affect immune cells and related signaling pathways in the immune microenvironment, thus causing lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p>
<p id="Par89">This research acknowledges certain limitations. Through the analysis of multiple datasets, we identified key genes that influence lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. Although our discussion highlights the significant role of these genes in the development and metastasis of gastric cancer, it is important to note that there is a paucity of research specifically addressing lymph node metastasis. Although this study did not experimentally verify the functions of the identified key genes, we conducted a theoretical analysis of eight key genes associated with lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer by examining multiple datasets. This analysis provides a foundational basis for future experimental studies. In clinical research, these key genes may serve as molecular markers for lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, facilitating further investigation and discussion. They hold potential to become significant biomarkers and therapeutic targets for lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer.</p></section><section id="Sec32"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h2>
<p id="Par90">This study showed that CDRT15P1, DENND3, F2R, FNDC3B, IRAK3, MS4A2, PDK4, PKIA might be the key genes affecting lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer, and F2R might have higher biological importance.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgements</h2>
