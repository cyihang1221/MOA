========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: RAMClust
sample_description: The study used standard reference material NIST SRM 1950 (human plasma) samples, with five replicate injections analyzed in both positive and negative ionization modes. Samples were prepared according to standard metabolomics protocols for plasma, and full-scan high-resolution mass spectrometry data were acquired. The raw data included 6715 features in positive mode and 4666 features in negative mode after initial xcms feature extraction.
complete_workflow_description: The paper describes a fully reproducible end-to-end untargeted metabolomics workflow called Paired-Mass Distance Dependent Analysis (PMDDA), implemented in R and packaged as a Docker image (xcmsrocker) and R package (rmwf). The workflow begins with raw LC-HRMS data (mzML format), applies retention time filtering (30–930 s), performs feature detection using xcms (parameters optimized via IPO for five NIST SRM 1950 replicates), corrects retention time, fills missing peaks, filters features by intensity fold-change (>3× over matrix blanks) and reproducibility (RSD <30% across SRM replicates), then applies the GlobalStd algorithm to remove redundant peaks (e.g., adducts, isotopes, in-source fragments) based on frequency of paired-mass distances rather than predefined lists. Remaining independent features are clustered to generate a minimal, non-redundant list of precursor ions ('one peak for one compound'). This list is formatted into an injection table for pseudo-targeted MS/MS acquisition — either as a fixed inclusion list for targeted MS/MS or as a preferred ion list for iterative DDA. Acquired MS/MS data (converted to mzML) are annotated via GNPS molecular networking (default settings: 2 Da precursor tolerance for isotopes, 0.5 Da fragment tolerance), and results are linked back to MS1 features using strict alignment criteria (<5 ppm m/z shift, <5 s RT shift). All scripts, data, and containerized environment (Docker xcmsrocker) are publicly available on GitHub and Figshare, enabling full reproduction.
source_paper: Miao et al. (2022), Journal of cheminformatics, DOI: 10.1186/s13321-022-00586-8, PMID: 35172886

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: RAMClust
sample_description: Urine samples from 100 female firefighters enrolled in the Fire Fighter Cancer Cohort Study (FFCCS), collected before and after live-fire training (total n = 200 samples: 100 pre-fire, 100 post-fire). Samples were collected between October 2022 and July 2024; pre-fire samples were obtained on the day of training prior to exposure, and post-fire samples were collected 1.8–11.0 hours after training (targeted 2–4 hours). All samples were processed using acetonitrile extraction with 13C-labeled internal standards, followed by HILIC(+) and HILIC(−) as well as C18(+) and C18(−) chromatographic modes, and analyzed via Vanquish Duo UPLC coupled to Exploris 120 HRMS.
complete_workflow_description: The study employed a high-resolution metabolomics (HRM) pipeline beginning with urine sample collection (pre- and post-fire), dilution correction using specific gravity, and QA/QC-integrated batch processing on an Opentrons OT2 liquid handler. Metabolite extraction used acetonitrile with 13C-labeled internal standards, followed by dual-mode chromatography (HILIC and C18) under both positive and negative ionization. Data acquisition was performed on Thermo Fisher Vanquish Duo UPLC coupled to Exploris 120 HRMS. Raw data were processed using a previously published pipeline (Liu et al., 2025a) involving log2 transformation, standardization, and filtering for level 1 annotated metabolites present in ≥75% of samples. Differential analysis used linear mixed-effects models in R (v4.4.1) with exposure status (pre-/post-fire) as the primary predictor and adjustment for age, education, Hispanic ethnicity, and fire type; FDR-corrected p-values (q < 0.05) were applied. Functional enrichment analysis (FEA) was conducted using metabolite-set enrichment analysis (MSEA) via MetaboAnalyst. Stratified analyses by fire type (flashover vs. burn room/tower) used multiple linear regression with identical covariate adjustment and FDR control. Volcano plots and circular bar charts of fold changes were generated for visualization.
source_paper: Tuo et al. (2026), International journal of hygiene and environmental health, DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheh.2026.114746, PMID: 41619359
