    algorithm: Multivariate Curve Resolution–Alternating Least Squares (MCR-ALS) with hierarchical clustering initialization
    parameters: max_iterations=100, convergence_threshold=1e-06, nonnegativity_constraints=True, closure_constraint=True, clustering_method=hierarchical, distance_metric=correlation, linkage_method=average
    input: raw .cdf or .raw GC-MS files (GC-TOF and GC-Orbitrap)
    output: pure mass spectra (deconvoluted ion chromatograms and spectral profiles)
    confidence: high
  Step 2: Compound identification via spectral matching
    tool: NIST MS Search (v2.3g)
    algorithm: dot-product similarity scoring
    parameters: mass_tolerance_ppm=5, minimum_match_factor=600, minimum_reverse_match_factor=600, top_n_matches=5
    input: deconvoluted mass spectra (mz/intensity lists)
    output: annotated compound list with match scores
    confidence: medium
  Step 3: Performance evaluation against reference standards
    tool: custom R/Python scripts
    algorithm: matching score calculation (NIST-style weighted dot product)
    parameters: score_weighting=intensity-weighted, reference_library=NIST17 + custom standard library
    input: deconvoluted spectra and ground-truth standard annotations
    output: average matching score, number of correctly matched compounds
    confidence: low
  Step 4: Benchmarking against alternative deconvolution tools
    tool: ['MS-DIAL', 'eRah', 'ADAP-GC 3.2'] (v['4.20', '1.0', '3.2'])
    algorithm: identical spectral matching pipeline (NIST MS Search)
    parameters: mass_tolerance_ppm=5, min_match_factor=600
    input: peak tables or deconvoluted spectra output from each tool
    output: comparative metrics: # matched compounds, average matching score
    confidence: medium
