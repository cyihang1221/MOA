related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The study used 58 daphnane standards (DA01 to DA58) isolated in a prior phytochemical investigation, and plant materials from 56 Thymelaeaceae species collected from China, Japan, and Turkey. Standards were dissolved in LC-MS–grade methanol at 1 ppm. Plant materials were air-dried, cut into small pieces, extracted with 95% ethanol at room temperature, and the resulting ethanol extracts (100 mg per sample) were suspended in distilled water and partitioned with ethyl acetate. The ethyl acetate fractions (5 mg per sample) were loaded onto Sep-Pak C18 Plus Long Cartridges and eluted sequentially with LC-MS–grade methanol:water (5:5 v/v and 10:0 v/v, 10 mL each). The 100% methanol eluate from each sample was collected, filtered through a 0.22 μm syringe filter, and subjected to LC-MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: Data processing was performed using FreeStyle 1.6 (for raw data confirmation) and MZmine 2.53 (for feature extraction and annotation). For standards: mass detection noise thresholds were set at 1.0 × 10^5 for MS1 and 5.0 × 10^2 for MS2; chromatogram building used the ADAP module with minimum group size = 5, group intensity threshold = 1.0 × 10^5, minimum height intensity = 1.0 × 10^5, and m/z tolerance = 5 ppm; deconvolution applied baseline cutoff with minimum peak height = 2.0 × 10^6, peak duration range = 0.00–5.00 min, and baseline level = 1.0 × 10^6; deisotoping used isotopic peak grouper with m/z tolerance = 5 ppm and RT tolerance = 0.15 min; feature alignment used join aligner with m/z tolerance = 5 ppm (weight = 75), RT tolerance = 0.1 min (weight = 25); standards' precursor ions (from table S1) were selected via feature list row filter using text-in-comment criteria; final outputs included quant table (.csv) and MS2 spectral summary (.mgf). For plant extracts: mass detection noise thresholds were 1.0 × 10^4 for MS1 and 1.0 × 10^3 for MS2; ADAP chromatogram building used minimum group size = 5, group intensity threshold = 1.0 × 10^3, minimum height intensity = 1.0 × 10^4, and m/z tolerance = 5 ppm (both ion modes); deconvolution used minimum peak height = 1.0 × 10^5, peak duration range = 0.01–3.00 min, baseline level = 5.0 × 10^4; deisotoping used same m/z/RT tolerances as standards; feature list row filter retained only peaks with MS2 scans and reset peak IDs; final outputs were .csv and .mgf files. Positive-ion mode .csv and .mgf files were input into a custom program (CNPs-MFSA, source code available on GitHub) for diterpenoid-like peak recognition and automatic annotation. CNPs-MFSA is explicitly referenced as the software used for recognizing diterpenoid-liked peaks and performing further automatic annotation.

complete_workflow_description: The computational metabolomic workflow involved four integrated software tools and multi-step procedures: (1) Peak detection and monoisotopic mass assignment using MS-DIAL (v3.90) with adduct ion dictionaries for positive mode ([M+H]+, [M+Na]+, [M+K]+, [M−H2O+H]+, [2M+H]+) and negative mode ([M−H]−, [M−H2O−H]−, [M+HCOOH−H]−, [M+CH3COOH−H]−, [2M−H]−); minimum two matched adducts required per peak; (2) Peak list alignment in MS-DIAL using ABF-converted MS/MS data, with MS tolerance = 0.01 Da, minimum peak height = 1×10⁷, and maximum charge = 2; (3) Multivariate statistical analysis using MetaboAnalyst: data normalized by sum and auto-scaled, followed by PLS-DA for group discrimination and hierarchical clustering heatmap generation; (4) Structural identification across four levels: Level 1 used MS-DIAL-linked MS/MS databases (ReSpect, BMDMS-NP, MetaboBASE, Fiehn/Vaniya, natural product libraries); Level 2 employed MS-FINDER as described above; Level 3 utilized GNPS (Global Natural Product Social Molecular Network): LC–MS/MS data converted to mzXML via MSConvert, processed by MZmine 2, uploaded to GNPS with parent mass tolerance = 2.0 Da, ion tolerance = 0.5 Da, max connected components = 19, minimum cluster size = 2, library match threshold = score > 0.7 and ≥6 matched peaks, visualized in Cytoscape v3.6.1; Level 4 involved physical isolation (silica gel, ODS, preparative HPLC) and structural elucidation via 1D/2D NMR (Bruker 500 MHz). Additionally, theoretical ECD calculations for compound N7 were performed using Gaussian 09: conformational search and geometry optimization at B3LYP/6-31G(d) level in MeOH, TDDFT-based ECD simulation at B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) level in MeOH, band width σ = 0.30 eV, and Boltzmann-weighted spectral averaging.

========== paper_workflow entry 5 ==========
related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The sample consisted of 22 mixes of authentic chemical standards, each containing between 10 and 94 compounds, measured using liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) under identical experimental conditions. The LC separation employed a reversed-phase Kinetex C18 EVO column (2.6 μm, 2.1 × 50 mm) with a 2.1 × 5 mm precolumn, using a gradient of water (A, 0.1% formic acid) and methanol (B, 0.1% formic acid): 95/5 at 0 min, 95/5 at 1 min, 0/100 at 13 min, 0/100 at 24 min, at a flow rate of 300 μL/min. Mass spectra were acquired on a Q Exactive Plus Orbitrap (Thermo Scientific) in data-dependent acquisition mode with electrospray ionization (ESI), using stepped HCD energies of 20/35/50, achieving <5 ppm mass accuracy and nominal MS/MS resolving power of 35,000 at m/z = 200. Positive-mode ([M+H]⁺) and negative-mode ([M−H]⁻) data were acquired separately. MS/MS peak lists were extracted using RMassBank with a 0.4-min retention time window around expected RT and reported as absolute ion intensities; subsequent data curation included recalibration to ≤5 ppm using known subformula annotations, removal of peaks lacking valid subformulas within 5 ppm, exclusion of compounds with double chromatographic peaks, identical spectra (detected via SPLASH), single-peak spectra, or maximum intensity <1×10⁵, and deduplication of multiply measured substances (retaining only the highest-intensity instance per ionization mode). Final spectra were saved in plain text and Mascot Generic Format (MGF) and deposited in MassBank.
complete_workflow_description: The method involved multiple software-based workflows across participating teams. Team Vaniya applied MS-FINDER v1.62 for Category 2: (1) input of experimental MS/MS peak lists (MGF format), precursor m/z, ion mode, and ±5 ppm mass accuracy; (2) disabling of default ESD and generation of challenge-specific ESDs containing only CASMI-provided candidates; (3) batch spectral matching against top 500 candidates per challenge; (4) structural scoring based on mass error, bond dissociation energy, linkage penalties, and HR rule compliance; (5) export of up to 500 top-scoring SMILES with scores for 199 challenges. Team Kind used MS-FINDER v1.60 + MD (MS/MS database search) for Category 3: (1) formula prediction prioritizing common formulas from large databases; (2) querying top 5 formulas against CASMI candidate lists and internal MS-FINDER database; (3) MS/MS confirmation via NIST MS Search GUI with precursor tolerance = 5 ppm and product ion tolerance = 200 ppm (or extended to 100 ppm for precursor when no MS/MS info existed), using NIST, MoNA, ReSpect, and MassBank databases; (4) cross-referencing low-scoring hits with STOFF-IDENT for environmentally relevant compounds; (5) integration of all evidence into a composite score. Team Brouard and Team Dührkop employed CSI:FingerID with SIRIUS-generated fragmentation trees: SIRIUS v3.1.4 computed trees using Q-TOF settings, discarding trees scoring <80% of optimal tree score (Team Dührkop), using fragment intensities to distinguish noise/signal but down-weighting intensities during learning; CSI:FingerID then predicted molecular fingerprints (CDK-based, ~6000 features) and matched them to candidate fingerprints; Team Brouard further applied Input Output Kernel Regression (IOKR) with uniform (CSI:IOKR_U) or Alignf-optimized (CSI:IOKR_A, CSI:IOKR_AR) multiple kernel learning combining spectral and tree kernels, trained on CASMI training data plus GNPS and MassBank spectra (e.g., 3868 positive-mode spectra from GNPS for CSI:IOKR_U/A; 7352 for CSI:IOKR_AR). Team Allen used CFM-ID: CFM_orig employed original CFM positive/negative models trained on METLIN, using Jaccard similarity and 10 ppm mass tolerance, repeating input spectrum across low/medium/high energies; CFM_retrain used a retrained model incorporating METLIN and NIST MS/MS library data, altered chemical features, and a neural network in the transition function, applying DotProduct similarity and combining energies pre-training; both entries were augmented with DB_SCORE (+10 per database hit in HMDB, ChEBI, FooDB, DrugBank, or plant-compound DB) for Category 3. All submissions adhered to CASMI formatting rules: tab-separated plain-text files with InChI/SMILES (col 1) and non-negative scores (col 2); post-submission stereoisomer deduplication used InChIKey first block; ranks were averaged for ties and rounded up for Top-X calculations.

related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The sample consisted of tandem mass spectral input data from the CASMI 2016 contest, comprising 312 training and 208 validation MS/MS spectra in *.MGF format. These spectra were acquired on a Q Exactive Plus Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher) with <5 ppm mass accuracy and MS/MS resolution of 35,000 using electrospray ionization (ESI). Spectra were collected in stepped collision energies of 20, 35, and 50 eV. Only [M+H]+ (positive mode) and [M−H]− (negative mode) ion species were available. Spectral metadata included ChemSpider ID, compound name, monoisotopic mass, molecular formula, SMILES, InChI, and InChIKey. Candidate structures were retrieved from ChemSpider using a ±5 ppm mass search window, yielding 432,511 candidates for the training set and 258,808 candidates for the validation set; candidate lists varied per spectrum from 1 (e.g., pentabromophenol) to up to 8,000 structures due to uneven mass distribution. Erroneous candidates—those mismatching the provided formula, SMILES, or InChIKey—were identified and corrected post-deadline using official CASMI-provided ground truth results.
complete_workflow_description: The research employed four in silico fragmentation tools—MS-FINDER (v1.70), CFM-ID (v2.2, rev26), MetFragCL (v2.2-CL), and MAGMa+ (Python wrapper of MAGMa)—each configured with tool-specific input/output formats and structural handling routines. A custom Java application was developed to harmonize structure conversions (addressing salt forms, isotopic elements, radicals, and format inconsistencies) and to process, standardize, and analyze outputs across tools. For CFM-ID, the cfm-id executable was run on a CentOS 7 server (48-core AMD Opteron 6344, 2.6 GHz); input spectra were repeated for low/medium/high energies emulating 10/20/40 eV CID, and spectral comparison used the dot product score (preferred over Jaccard) with ±5 ppm tolerance and original METLIN-trained positive/negative models. MetFragCL was run on macOS (2.7 GHz Intel Core i5, 16 GB RAM) using a parameter file (details in Additional file 1: Table S2); fragment matching accounted for proton addition (positive mode) or removal (negative mode, −1.007 Da) to align theoretical fragments with observed charged peaks. MAGMa+ was executed on a CentOS 7 cluster node (same hardware as CFM-ID); a modified Python script (process_hmdb.py) generated an SQLite database from CASMI-provided InChIs (replacing the default HMDB.sdf-based database), and another custom Python script converted CASMI peak lists and metadata into spectral-tree files required by MAGMa. Parameter optimization was performed on the 312-training-set spectra, followed by development of voting/consensus models that integrated ranked outputs from multiple tools, optionally augmented with database lookups (ChemSpider, MS/MS libraries) and metadata filtering; all consensus strategies and configurations are detailed in Table 1 and Additional file 1.

========== paper_workflow entry 7 ==========
related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The study utilized MS/MS records from MassBank, GNPS, and human plasma data sets. Specifically, 5063 MassBank records and 936 human plasma records (acquired via HILIC-ESI(+)-QTOF MS/MS) were used. The human plasma cohort data were obtained as part of the P20 Program NIH HL113452 Grant. Spectral data were recorded using a QTOFMS instrument supported by NIH Grant S10-RR031630. The curation details for these datasets—including filtering criteria, peak selection, and database integration—are described in the Supporting Information (SI Manuscript, Tables S1–S8).
complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow consisted of the following software-driven procedures: (1) Hydrogen rearrangement (HR) statistics construction: MS/MS records from MassBank were filtered by m/z accuracy (≤10 mDa from theoretical mass) and intensity (>10% of base peak); fragment ion assignment followed combinatorial bond cleavage rules—up to two bonds broken for tree-like structures, up to four for two-ring systems (ring fission = two bonds); hydrogen rearrangement considered ±2 H per cleavage; candidate fragments ranked by priority: even-electron rule (first cleavage), neutralized substructure variants (±2 H), second-cleavage even-electron fragments, second-cleavage fragments with assigned precursors at higher m/z, and minimum mass error fragments; (2) In silico MS/MS annotation: executed using HR rules instead of even-electron rule, with identical hierarchical ranking but substituting HR compliance for even-electron compliance; (3) Fragment stability verification: MOPAC2012 (PM7) manually applied to each ionic substructure in MOL format to compute heat of formation and assess thermodynamic stability; (4) Molecular formula generation: MS-FINDER generated formula candidates from precursor m/z and adduct type using user-defined mass tolerance and elemental constraints (C, H, N, O, P, S, F, Cl, Br, I, Si); valence and elemental ratio filters reduced candidate space; (5) Formula ranking: five diagnostic scores computed and summed into F (0–5): (i) mass error score via Gaussian function (Equation 1); (ii) isotopic ratio score averaging Gaussian penalties for M+1 and M+2 deviations (Equation 2); (iii) product ion formula assignment rate (excluding isotopic peaks post-deisotoping); (iv) neutral loss assignment rate against 135-entry internal database; (v) discrete database existence score (0.5 baseline + scaled multi-database count); (6) Structure candidate retrieval: queried internal database (14 metabolome sources); fallbacks to MINE or PubChem PUG REST; InChIKey-based deduplication and synonym-driven representative selection applied; (7) Structure ranking: in silico MS/MS annotation performed per HR rules; unresolved peaks penalized (Ph = 0.2); mass error penalty (M) and BDE penalty (B = 1 − Bf/Bm) computed per Equation (1) and (3); fragmentation linkage penalty (Pt = 0.5) applied; per-fragment score Sf = M × B × Pt × Ph; cumulative score normalized to 4 and augmented with database presence score (0–1) to yield structure score S (0–5); (8) Final molecular identification: overall score T = F + S determined final ranking.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The study used two primary sets of biological and chemical MS/MS spectral data: (1) 5063 manually curated MS/MS spectra from the MassBank database, representing 3127 authentic compound structures (disregarding stereochemistry), covering diverse metabolites across species and tissues; and (2) 936 manually identified MS/MS spectra from an untargeted HILIC-QTOF LC-MS/MS dataset of human plasma collected in a cohort study. All spectra were acquired in positive or negative electrospray ionization (ESI) mode using high-resolution tandem mass spectrometry (e.g., QTOF), with accurate mass measurement of precursor ions and fragment ions. Precursor m/z values, adduct types (e.g., [M+H]⁺, [M−H]⁻), isotope ratios, and full MS/MS spectra (including fragment m/z and relative intensities) were provided as input to MS-FINDER.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end MS-FINDER workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data (precursor m/z, retention time, adduct annotation, and MS/MS spectrum) as input. First, molecular formulas are computationally generated from the accurate precursor m/z within a defined mass tolerance, constrained by valence rules (OV/SV parity and SV ≥ 2×(TA−1)) and biologically plausible elemental ratios (e.g., H/C, O/C) derived from HMDB/KNApSAcK statistics. Second, all structural isomers matching the top formula candidates are retrieved from integrated metabolome databases (HMDB for human metabolites, KNApSAcK for plant metabolites, and RIKEN’s internal DB). Third, for each candidate structure, in silico MS/MS fragmentation is performed under low-energy CID assumptions, applying nine mechanistic hydrogen rearrangement (HR) rules (P1–P4 for ESI+, N1–N5 for ESI−) to predict fragment ions and their hydrogen-adjusted neutral losses (e.g., [M+H−H₂O]⁺, [M−H−CO₂]⁻). Fragment matches are classified as 'resolved' (exact HR rule match), 'semiresolved' (±2 H deviation), or 'formula-assigned' (no HR explanation). Finally, candidate structures are ranked using a composite score weighting fragment mass accuracy (ppm error), bond dissociation energy estimates, fragment linkage network coherence, and HR rule adherence (penalizing semiresolved and formula-assigned fragments). The top-3 ranked isomers are output with confidence metrics, enabling identification even for compounds absent from spectral libraries.
source_paper: Hiroshi et al. (2016), Analytical chemistry, DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b00770, PMID: 27419259
