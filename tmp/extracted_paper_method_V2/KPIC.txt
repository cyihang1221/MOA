sample_description: The study utilized a retrospective cohort of 8,200 adult patients (aged ≥18 years) diagnosed with sepsis upon ICU admission at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (BIDMC) in Boston, Massachusetts, between 2001 and 2012. Patients were drawn from the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care-III (MIMIC-III) v1.4 database—a large, de-identified, publicly available critical care database containing comprehensive bedside clinical data including vital signs, laboratory test results, medication prescriptions, procedural and diagnostic codes (ICD-9-CM), medical charts, and administrative records. The cohort was derived through rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria: (1) diagnosis of sepsis based on updated Angus abstraction criteria incorporating ICD-9-CM codes for infection and acute organ dysfunction (including newly added codes for viral meningitis, cholangitis, and orbital cellulitis); (2) exclusion of patients with zero Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score or age <18; (3) exclusion of patients with missing or undocumented laboratory tests or vital signs within the first 24 hours of ICU admission; (4) retention of only the first sepsis episode for patients with multiple admissions; and (5) use of averaged values for repeated vital sign or lab measurements within the first 24 hours. The final analytical sample comprised 8,200 sepsis patients across five ICU types: coronary care unit, cardiac surgery recovery unit, surgical intensive care unit, trauma/surgical intensive care unit, and medical intensive care unit. Covariates included demographic variables (age, sex, race, insurance type), clinical variables (vital signs: heart rate, systolic/diastolic blood pressure, respiratory rate, temperature; laboratory tests: hemoglobin, albumin, white blood cell count, potassium, sodium, bicarbonate, total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, C-reactive protein, lactate—excluding SOFA-related labs to avoid collinearity), BMI (calculated from height measured during hospital admission and average weight from first 24h of ICU stay), Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) (excluding severe sepsis/septic shock diagnostic codes), SOFA score (computed using MIMIC-III v1.4 data via an adapted SQL script from the official MIMIC GitHub repository), and ICU type (categorized as medical ICU, cardio-coronary ICU [coronary + cardiac surgery ICUs], or surgical ICU [trauma + surgical ICUs]). Immunomodulatory medications (e.g., corticosteroids) were also included as covariates.
