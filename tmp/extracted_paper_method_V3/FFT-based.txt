========== sample_description&software_configuration_description entry ==========
sample_description: The study used publicly available genome-wide histone modification data of H3K4me3 obtained from the Zenodo repository, originally published by Sadeh et al. This dataset was generated using cfChIP-Seq technology to profile histone modifications in cell-free plasma samples from 88 healthy donors and 128 colorectal cancer (CRC) patients. All human subject data were de-identified, and the study complied with the human ethics protocol (#ET000631) approved by the University of Western Australia’s Human Ethics Committee; no informed consent was required due to the use of anonymized public data. Genomic signal processing was performed at single-nucleotide resolution across multiple genomic region sizes: {300 kb, 600 kb, 1 Mb, 5 Mb, 10 Mb, 50 Mb}, with each region analyzed for H3K4me3 signal intensity defined as a vector x[n] of length L, where positions within experimentally identified peaks P = {(s,e,v)} — start s, end e, and intensity v — inherit v, and all other positions are assigned zero intensity.
software_configuration_description: A PyTorch-based Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) implementation was developed for compressing epigenomic signals. For a real-valued input signal vector x[n] of length L, the real FFT (rfft) was applied, yielding F(k) for k ∈ [0, L/2 + 1], producing complex outputs (real + imaginary components). The magnitude of each frequency component was computed as |F(k)| = √(Re(F(k))² + Im(F(k))²). Due to the memory overhead of complex numbers (2L float values vs. original L reals), the number m of highest-magnitude components retained for compression ratio r was set to m = L/(2r). Compression ratios tested were r ∈ {100, 300, 1000}, and corresponding m values were precomputed and tabulated (e.g., for 300 kb at 100X: m = 1,500; at 300X: m = 500; at 1000X: m = 150). Signal reconstruction used inverse FFT (irfft) on a frequency-domain array F′(k) of length L/2 + 1, populated only with the m highest-magnitude components (zero-padded elsewhere). The frequency axis was normalized per the Nyquist–Shannon theorem (sampling rate = 1 bp → max representable frequency = 0.5 cycles/bp). All FFT and irfft operations leveraged PyTorch 2.5.1’s native torch.fft.rfft and torch.fft.irfft functions, executed on NVIDIA Tesla V100-PCIe-32 GB GPUs. CPU timing benchmarks used Intel Xeon Gold 6254 @ 3.10 GHz (single core).
