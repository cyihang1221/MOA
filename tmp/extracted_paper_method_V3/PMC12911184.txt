</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="Abs1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Background</h3>
<p id="Par1">Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a complex thrombotic disorder with multiple environmental and genetic determinants. Nevertheless, the intricacies of DVT regulatory mechanisms have thus far precluded comprehensive research on its multi-omics characteristics.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<p id="Par2">In this study, non-targeted metabolomics (<em>n</em> = 62) and transcriptomics (<em>n</em> = 17) were utilised to comprehensively analyse the peripheral blood changes of DVT patients and healthy subjects. The hub differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs) and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) involved in the pathophysiological process of DVT were screened based on two-way orthogonal partial least squares (O2PLS) and machine learning (ML). The predictive performance of transcriptomic features was evaluated by constructing a nomogram and validated using calibration curves, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and validation sets (<em>n</em> = 18). Furthermore, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq, <em>n</em> = 6) facilitates the revelation of intrinsic regulatory relationships between DEMs and DEGs through correlation analysis and inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry (ICP-MS).</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p id="Par3">The metabolic and transcriptional profiles of DVT patients were found to be significantly different from those of healthy subjects, and a total of 85 DEMs and 193 DEGs were identified by difference analysis. Following an exploration of the relevant biological pathways via Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis, the top 25 DEMs and DEGs were identified by O2PLS. Following rigorous verification by the correlation network model and ML algorithm, it was determined that phosphatidylinositol (PI) and SLC39A11 exhibited significant characteristics. The nomogram demonstrated that SLC39A11 exhibited excellent prediction performance, with the area under the curve (AUC) value of 1.000. Moreover, SLC39A11 was found to be significantly up-regulated in the validation set (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.01). The SLC39A11 was localized to M1-like macrophages by scRNA-seq, and it was found that it was significantly related to PI metabolism in DVT by correlation analysis and ICP-MS (<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05).</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusions</h3>
<p id="Par4">This study demonstrated that the metabolic dysregulation of DVT was predominantly concentrated in lipid metabolism, as indicated by PI, and the gene dysregulation was represented by SLC39A11. SLC39A11 exerts an influence on the regulatory process of DVT through its interaction with zinc ion transport and PI metabolism.</p></section><section id="sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Supplementary Information</h3>
<p>The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12911-026-03342-2.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Deep vein thrombosis, Metabolomics, Transcriptomics, Multi-omics analysis, Machine learning, Single-cell RNA sequencing</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p id="Par5">Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a prevalent multifactorial disease with a high morbidity and mortality rate. The incidence of DVT is also increasing due to the increasing incidence of population ageing and related comorbidities, including obesity, heart failure and cancer [<a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>–<a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a>]. In clinical diagnosis, the diagnosis of DVT based solely on clinical presentation is unreliable due to the poor specificity of signs and symptoms. Venography is considered the “gold standard” for diagnosing DVT due to its invasiveness, high cost and technical features [<a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a>–<a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a>]. However, due to the potential harms of surgery, including radiation exposure and contrast agent risks and associated medical costs, imaging every patient with DVT is not advisable and is rarely used in daily practice [<a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">6</a>–<a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">9</a>]. In light of Virchow’s proposal of the three elements of DVT in the 19th century, the role of cytokines, chemokines and miRNAs in DVT has been extensively studied in order to gain further insights into the mechanisms of DVT [<a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>]. However, due to the susceptibility of the diagnostic process to interference from non-thrombotic diseases (including infection, surgery and tumours), there is a lack of non-invasive biomarkers with high specificity and sensitivity. Furthermore, there is also a lack of attention to the potential link between thrombosis-related metabolic changes and gene regulation.</p>
<p id="Par6">With advances in the biomedical field, the development of high-throughput technologies has led to the emergence of multi-omics techniques (including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics) as efficient tools for DVT diagnosis, management, monitoring, treatment and novel disease markers [<a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a>, <a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">11</a>]. As a disruptive perspective and tool in the biomedical field, multi-omics technology has the potential to optimise the systematic understanding of DVT as a whole, thereby enabling accurate analysis of the heterogeneity of DVT at the molecular level and dynamic tracking of the disease map. Genotyping arrays can be employed to elucidate differences in gene expression across different biological states, including those of health and disease. Proteomics and metabolomics are used to predict the effects of different biomolecules in living organisms. These omics techniques present a valuable opportunity to gain insight into the underlying causes of DVT [<a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a>].</p>
<p id="Par7">In recent years, machine learning (ML) algorithms have been increasingly applied to risk prediction of diseases and disease-related complications (including cardiovascular diseases and cancers) due to the powerful computational simulation capabilities of big data, including linear discriminant analysis (LDA), logistic regression (LR), decision tree (DT), random forests (RF), artificial neural network (ANN), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), support vector machines (SVM) and generalized linear model (GLM) [<a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a>]. At the same time, the use of ML methods to build models is also gaining more and more attention in medical research, and its advantages include automatic selection of information variables, capturing nonlinear relationships between variables, and improving diagnostic capabilities [<a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a>]. ML can provide a powerful alternative for exploring and validating the intrinsic pathogenesis of DVT, and accurately predicting the risk of DVT which performs important clinical value for early initiation of the prevention process in high-risk patients [<a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a>, <a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a>–<a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a>]. In the existing clinical environment, both ML and multi-omics lack in-depth application in the field of DVT, and the relevant omics characteristics need to be explored.</p>
<p id="Par8">The objective of this study is to analyse the multiomic characteristics of peripheral blood from DVT patients using ML and correlation network models, and to determine the cell localization through single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) methods. Concurrently, the regulatory network was subjected to detailed analysis to ascertain the significance of SLC39A11 in regulating phosphatidylinositol (PI) metabolism by means of zinc ion transport, thereby enhancing comprehension of the intricate mechanism of DVT. This will provide insights into the subsequent development of corresponding clinical drug targets.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials and methods</h2>
<section id="Sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Case collection</h3>
<p id="Par9">According to the 2016 ISTH SSC classification guidelines for DVT, the metabolomics study plans to recruit 32 DVT patients from the Department of Vascular Surgery, the Second Affiliated Hospital of University of South China, Hunan Province, China, between April 2022 and June 2023. And 30 healthy subjects were also from the physical examination center of the same hospital at the same time. To eliminate potential confounding variables, participants in both groups were matched by gender, age and body mass index. Additionally, 11 DVT patients and 6 healthy subjects for bulk RNA sequencing (bulk RNA-seq) were recruited study using the same methodology, which served as the test set. These samples were randomly selected from the participants of metabolomics study. The corresponding external validation set was collected during the same experimental period, comprising samples from 12 DVT patients and 6 healthy subjects. To facilitate further analysis of the pathological mechanism of DVT, different batches of samples from 3 DVT patients and 3 healthy subjects were selected for scRNA-seq independently. The inclusion criteria were as follows: i) at least one test result (including ultrasonography or D-dimer test) supports the diagnosis of DVT, ii) patients with first-episode acute DVT, iii) no anticoagulation, and iv) age ≥ 18 years. The exclusion criteria encompass the following 4 items: i) antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), ii) pregnancy or lactation, iii) infection or use of immunosuppressants within 2 weeks prior to enrollment, and iv) active or prior malignancy. The multi-omics data features utilised in this study are delineated in Supplementary Table <a href="#MOESM1" class="usa-link">1</a>. The study was conducted in accordance with the internationally recognized principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and was reviewed and approved by the Hospital Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of the University of South China (2022K091301). Prior to enrolment, written informed consent was obtained from all participants.</p></section><section id="Sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Total metabolim assay and quality testing</h3>
<p id="Par10">According to the standard protocol of LC-Bio Technology Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China), we used the methanol precipitated protein method to extract the non-targeted metabolites from the samples, and prepared quality control (QC) samples (mixed with the same amount of prepared experimental samples), and the samples to be tested were randomly sorted and then inserted into the QC samples for repeated evaluation of the experimental technique. All chromatographic separations were performed using an UltiMate 3000 HPLC System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States). And an ACQUITY UPLC T3 column (100 mm*2.1 mm, 1.8 µm, Waters, United States) was used for the reversed phase separation. And we used a tandem high-resolution mass spectrometry (THRMS) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States) to perform a mass positive and negative ion scan of the samples, and collected QC samples (<em>n</em> = 11) after every 10 samples to assess the stability of the acquisition process. The precise mass error of the precursor ion is confined to a margin of error of 10 ppm. The acquired data pretreatments including peak picking, peak grouping, retention time correction, second peak grouping, and annotation of isotopes and adducts was performed by using R package XCMS (v1.48.0).</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Total RNA assay and quality testing</h3>
<p id="Par11">According to the manufacturer’s protocol, total genes were isolated and purified using TRIzol (Aidlab, China) and also a Fragment Analyzer (Agilent, United States) was used to detect the total gene amount, concentration, volume, RNA integrity number (RIN) value and 28S/18S of the sample. And then use the MGIEasy rRNA removal kit (MGI, China) to remove ribosomal RNA (rRNA) from the total RNA according to the instructions. The construction of the library was undertaken in strict accordance with the established protocols. Subsequently, the DNBSEQ platform (MGI, China) was utilised for the execution of bulk RNA-seq. The data were analyzed on the Dr. Tom network platform (<a href="http://report.bgi.com" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://report.bgi.com</a>) of BGI Genomics Co., Ltd. (Shenzhen, China). The raw data obtained from sequencing were filtered using SOAPnuke (v1.5.6), so as to finally obtain clean data. The identification of variants was achieved through the alignment of clean data to a reference genome (GCF_000001405.39_GRCh38.p13) using HISTA2 (v2.1.0).</p></section><section id="Sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Identification of differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs)</h3>
<p id="Par12">The online Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) were used to annotate the metabolites by matching the exact molecular mass data (m/z) of samples with those from databases. At the same time, we used the functions princomp and prcomp to perform principal component analysis (PCA) on the identified metabolites to observe the separation trend between groups of samples and reflect the variability between and within groups from the original data. Then we used R package mixOmics (v6.3.2) and ropls (v1.34.0) to perform partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) on the samples, and also calculated the variable important for the projection (VIP) to measure the influence of each metabolite expression pattern on the classification discrimination of each group of samples and the explanatory ability. In the present study, log<sub>2</sub> [FC] was set to 1.5, and the R packages EdgeR (v3.32.1) and limma (v3.26.8) were utilised for the identification of DEMs (VIP was set to 1). The aforementioned results underwent filtration according to the false discovery rate (FDR) criterion, with a cut-off value of 0.05. In a similar fashion, in order to illustrate the macroscopic metabolic differences in DVT, the R packages clusterProfiler (v4.12.0) and org.Hs.eg.db (v3.18.0) were utilised in order to perform KEGG enrichment analysis for DEMs.</p></section><section id="Sec7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)</h3>
<p id="Par13">Analogous to non-targeted metabolite analysis, PCA was employed to comprehend the characteristics and similarities among the samples. Subsequently, log<sub>2</sub> [FC] was set to 0.8 and the R package limma was utilised to identify DEGs. In order to further explore the gene functions related to phenotypic changes, the R packages clusterProfiler and org.Hs.eg.db were utilised to perform Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis for DEGs. The candidate genes with FDR &lt; 0.05 were defined as the threshold. In order to gain deeper insights into the biological significance of the aforementioned DEGs, their interactions at the protein level were investigated using the STRING (<a href="https://string-db.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://string-db.org/</a>). The requisite confidence level (combined score) was designated as medium (0.400). Furthermore, the Cytoscape (v3.10.0) was utilised for the purpose of visualisation. Concurrently, multi-algorithm network analysis was performed using cytoHubba (v0.1) to identify the top 25 genes. 3 algorithms were selected for evaluation: degree, stress and radiality.</p></section><section id="Sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Identification of highly correlated genes and metabolites based on two-way orthogonal partial least squares (O2PLS)</h3>
<p id="Par14">In order to ascertain the potential regulatory relationship between the two omics components, the O2PLS analysis was performed on the DEGs and plasma-associated DEMs using the R package OmicsPLS (v2.0.2). The prevalent variation is conducive to the precise extraction of the gene-metabolite core network associated with the pathological state and function of DVT from multi-omics data. The number of elements in the O2PLS was determined through 10 cross-validations, and the model with the smallest prediction error was selected. The selected model, characterised by a particular number of shared components, optimally captures the stable covariance relationship between X and Y, thereby quantifying the model’s predictive capability [<a href="#CR18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR18">18</a>, <a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">19</a>]. Concurrently, the factor loading of the amalgamated elements was visualised, thus enabling the identification of the top 25 genes and metabolites that contributed most significantly to the covariance. Among these, the loading is defined as the strength and direction of the relationship between the original variable (i.e. a single gene or metabolite) and the “common component” extracted by the model [<a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">20</a>].</p></section><section id="Sec9"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Correlation analysis of the two omics</h3>
<p id="Par15">In accordance with the multi-omics regulation theory, the Spearman’s coefficient was set to 0.5, and the intra-group correlation analysis results of the aforementioned DEGs and DEMs were plotted, with the objective of screening and analysing the linkage effect within them. Concurrently, the Spearman’s coefficient was set to 0.5, and the R package psych (v2.4.3) was utilised to quantitatively calculate and screen the interaction between the two omics samples, and to plot the network diagram. The visualisation of the above network plots was conducted using the Cytoscape (v3.10.0) software. The top 15 genes and metabolites of the interactions were screened by cytoHubba (v0.1).</p></section><section id="Sec10"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Identification of hub DEMs and DEGs based on ML approaches</h3>
<p id="Par16">In order to ascertain the most efficacious models, the present study employed R packages caret (v6.0–94), DALEX (v2.4.3), kernlab (v0.9–32), randomForest (v4.7–1.1) and xgboost (v1.7.7.1) to perform four common ML algorithms (including SVM, GLM, RF and XGBoost). The study then utilised these algorithms to plot box plots and reverse cumulative distributions of the residuals, in order to identify statistically significant differences between model predictions. In order to ascertain the interpretability and clinical relevance of the model, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of the model was plotted using the R package pROC (v1.18.5). The discriminant ability of the model was evaluated by measuring the area under the curve (AUC), to identify potential targets associated with DVT. The delong method was used to calculate the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the AUC value to test the difference between AUC.</p></section><section id="Sec11"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Construction and evaluation for the clinical prediction model of DVT</h3>
<p id="Par17">The construction of a nomogram was facilitated by the R package rms (v6.7–1) to explore the potential clinical value of the expression of the hub genes in DVT patients and healthy subjects, with a view to predicting the risk of disease. Subsequently, the function lrm is employed to plot the calibration curve, thereby facilitating the assessment of the congruence between the predicted and actual values. As with the ML approach, the ROC curve is utilised to assess the precision of the prediction model in accordance with the same methodology. For each individual gene, the ROC curves were plotted separately to assess their accuracy and reliability. Verification of the expression trend of each gene in the aforementioned model is undertaken on the basis of the description provided in the external validation set.</p></section><section id="Sec12"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Analysis of scRNA-seq data</h3>
<p id="Par18">Fresh peripheral blood samples were separated according to standard procedures and prepared into a single-cell suspension. The prepared single-cell suspension (concentration range: 800 to 1200 cells/μl) was used to generate gel emulsion microspheres (GEMs) in the Chromium Controller (10X Genomics), followed by reverse transcription of messenger RNA (mRNA) to generate the complementary DNA (cDNA). The library has been constructed in accordance with the standard scheme formulated by BGI Genomics Co., Ltd., and all samples are subject to QC, filtration and analysis of the standard scheme. The cell count and subsequent filtration were conducted utilising the R package Seurat (v5.3.0), while the initial identification of 2000 highly variable feature genes was facilitated by DoubletDetection (v3.0). The PCA was employed to reduce data dimensionality, with the optimal inflection point determined using the elbow plot. Concurrently, the samples underwent nonlinear dimensionality reduction using the uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) algorithm. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used by default to identify significantly DEGs between cell clusters, with thresholds set at log<sub>2</sub> [FC] &gt; 1 or log<sub>2</sub> [FC] &lt; −1 and FDR &lt; 0.05. Manual cell type matching and annotation were performed using CellMarker (<a href="http://xteam.xbio.top" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://xteam.xbio.top</a>) and National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI, <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/</a>). The Spearman’s method was employed to analyse the correlation of DEGs with cell types in different samples, with R package ggpubr (v0.6.0) utilised for this purpose.</p></section><section id="Sec13"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">High-throughput determination of metal elements</h3>
<p id="Par19">In accordance with the aforementioned inclusion and exclusion criteria, peripheral blood samples were collected from 25 DVT patients and 25 healthy subjects. These samples were collected independently and within the same time period, in accordance with the same criteria. In accordance with prevailing protocols, freshly obtained peripheral blood samples are subjected to a dilution pretreatment, the purpose of which is to eliminate the presence of organic impurities. Following parameter optimisation, adjustments must be made to the oxide interference level (CeO<sup>+</sup>/Ce<sup>+</sup>) and the double-charge interference level (Ba<sup>2+</sup>/Ba<sup>+</sup>). Conduct inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) measurements in accordance with standard protocols, ensuring thorough flushing between samples with dilute nitric acid to prevent cross-contamination. The automatic calculation of sample concentrations was based on standard curves, with the internal standard method being used to correct for signal drift and matrix suppression effects in real time. The method was validated using QC samples.</p></section><section id="Sec14"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Analysis of statistical results</h3>
<p id="Par20">All the statistical analyses were performed with R (v4.3.3) and SPSS (v23.0). In the case of normally distributed data, a t-test is typically employed for analysis, with results typically expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Non-normally distributed data were subjected to the Mann-Whitney test and reported as the median of the quartile. The sample size for scRNA-seq is contingent upon the availability of clinical samples. The value of <em>p</em> &lt; 0.05 was considered statistically significant. *<em>p</em> &lt; 0.05; **<em>p</em> &lt; 0.01; ***<em>p</em> &lt; 0.001; ****<em>p</em> &lt; 0.0001; NS, no significant difference.</p></section></section><section id="Sec15"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h2>
<section id="Sec16"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Overview of metabolomic and transcriptome assay results</h3>
<p id="Par21">In accordance with the established protocol, we employ positive and negative ion modes to identify polar and non-polar molecules in cell samples. Following XCMS processing of the data, metaX was employed to match the m/z values of the original data with HMDB, thereby facilitating primary metabolite identification. A total of 62 samples (including 11 QC samples) and 71,786 positive and negative ions were detected. Concurrently, the QC samples, the DVT patient samples and the healthy subject samples were all in similar geometric positions, indicating that the samples were naturally clustered, with minor discrepancies between groups and an overall satisfactory distribution. Concurrently, the DNBSEQ platform identified 35 samples and 120,455 genes through sample screening, sample extraction, and high-throughput sequencing, with an average data set of 11.33 gigabytes and an average genome alignment rate of 97.39%. The Q-Sanger value was greater than 30 for more than 90% of the filtered clean data, and the proportion of GRCh38.p13 genome alignment in 35 samples was greater than 92%.</p></section><section id="Sec17"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Identification and integrative analysis of DEMs</h3>
<p id="Par22">According to the HMDB classification annotation, the majority of the positive and negative ions are classified as lipids and lipid-like molecules, and the overall distribution of positive and negative ions is basically the same (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>a). Furthermore, the KEGG enrichment pathway demonstrates that the detected ions are concentrated in various metabolic pathways, including those associated with carbohydrate, amino acid and lipid metabolism (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>b). In the absence of supervision, PCA revealed that there was, in fact, partial overlap in the sample points of the two groups (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>c). This is a true reflection of the high heterogeneity inherent in biological samples. In order to reveal the specific variants associated with DVT in greater detail, the PLS-DA was employed again. As demonstrated in Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>d, the two sets of samples exhibited a distinct separation tendency in the PLS-DA model. The model underwent rigorous permutation testing (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>e) and proved to be significantly predictive and unfitted (Q<sup>2</sup> &lt; 0). In order to identify the DEMs, the log<sub>2</sub> [FC] was set to 1.5 and a total of 909 differential positive ions (up-regulated positive ions: 495, down-regulated positive ions: 414) and 678 differential negative ions (up-regulated negative ions: 460, down-regulated negative ions: 218) were screened (Figs. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>f and <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>g). The heatmap is a visualisation tool that utilises colour changes (red: up-regulated ions, blue: down-regulated ions) to depict the trend of metabolite ion expression in the sample. Furthermore, the investigation revealed that the trend of differences in each metabolic fraction was essentially the same between the two groups (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>h). Following the screening of metabolite ions, the KEGG pathway annotation revealed that the differential ions were still concentrated in the human metabolic pathways, especially in 2-oxocarboxylic acid metabolism (KEGG: hsa01210), biosynthesis of amino acids (KEGG: hsa01230) and glycerophospholipid metabolism (KEGG: hsa00564) (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a>i).</p>

<p id="Par23">Concurrently, analogous to the outcomes of metabolomic analysis, PCA results demonstrate that the samples exhibit intragroup disparities and that these disparities are more pronounced between the groups (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>a). In order to further elucidate the intricate interactions among DEGs and clarify their connections to signalling pathways and metabolic processes, the aforementioned genes were sought to be identified in the STRING and subsequently a comprehensive protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using Cytoscape (Fig. <a href="#Fig2" class="usa-link">2</a>f). <p id="Par30">In this study, the plasma of 32 DVT patients and 30 healthy subjects were subjected to high-throughput analysis by non-targeted metabolomics, which was combined with bulk RNA-seq (<em>n</em> = 17). The O2PLS model and correlation network analysis identified 25 hub DEGs and DEMs. To validate these findings, the performance of 4 ML methods was compared, including SVM, GLM, RF and XGBoost. In order to provide a comprehensive explanation of the model’s accuracy in clinical practice, a nomogram was developed to assess its predictive capacity for disease. This was accompanied by the evaluation of calibration curves and ROC curves, which served to illustrate the model’s potential in the validation set <em>(n</em> = 18). Furthermore, to facilitate a more profound investigation into the regulatory relationship of multiomics for DVT, the scRNA-seq (<em>n</em> = 6) was performed to ascertain the potential internal connection between SLC39A11 and PI on M1-like macrophages. In addition, the subsequent treatment and prediction of DVT were provided with corresponding references.</p>
<p id="Par31">According to HMDB annotation results, lipids and lipid-like molecules are the most abundant metabolites in the human body, and coincidentally, the causal relationship between blood lipids and DVT is established. Additionally, phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-triphosphate (PIP3) is a typical lipid second messenger and one of the key metabolites of the PI3K-AKT pathway [<a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">21</a>]. Also, PIP3 plays a pivotal role in platelet signalling by recruiting and activating PH-containing proteins [<a href="#CR22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR22">22</a>–<a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a>]. PI is a premetabolite of PIP3 [<a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">25</a>]. Phosphorylation of PI on the cell membrane results in the formation of PIP3, thus activating the PI3K-AKT pathway [<a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a>, <a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a>]. This process has been shown to induce inflammation, regulate cell metabolism, and promote cell viability. The present study also made a comparison of the expressed levels of PI in DVT patients and healthy subjects, which revealed significant differences. Moreover, the findings of the metabolomics with ML algorithm are in accordance with the identification of PI, which is a recurring pattern across multiple models. In the standard physiological environment, PTEN is able to act as an antagonist to PI3K, thus exerting a negative regulatory influence on the PI3K-AKT pathway [<a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">28</a>, <a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a>]. In this study, the up-regulation of PTEN in the DVT environment is hypothesised to be a compensatory immune response to ensure normal PI metabolism. This suggests that PI may serve as an indicator [<a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a>]. A recent two-sample mendelian randomization (MR) analysis also demonstrated that distinct types of PI promote or inhibit DVT, thereby contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of the risk factors associated with DVT [<a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a>–<a href="#CR33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR33">33</a>].</p>
<p id="Par32">SLC39A11 functions as a zinc transporter, localised in the Golgi apparatus, and its primary function is to transport zinc to the cytoplasm to maintain zinc homeostasis [<a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a>, <a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>]. In colorectal cancer (CRC) and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [<a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a>–<a href="#CR38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR38">38</a>], SLC39A11 is typically highly expressed, with the result that it promotes cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by activating mTOR signalling or providing zinc ions. In the study of DVT, it was found that SLC39A11 is enriched in M1-like macrophages, and the level of free zinc ions in the peripheral blood of clinical patients also increases to varying degrees. This strongly suggests that it may play an important role in the immune and inflammatory microenvironment of DVT by regulating zinc levels, affecting macrophage polarization and inflammatory factor secretion. At present, there is no conclusive evidence that SLC39A11 directly causes DVT. However, in view of the biological functions of SLC39A11, it is more likely to be reflected in its indirect regulatory effect, which can determine the strength of individual immune-inflammatory responses and the amplification effect on classic hypercoagulable genes. The relevant potential physiological mechanism is that intracellular zinc ions inhibit the activity of PTEN [<a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a>, <a href="#CR40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR40">40</a>], which indirectly leads to the enhancement of PI3K signalling and affects PI metabolism, which may promote platelet activation and inflammatory response. Consequently, SLC39A11, as a pivotal molecule connecting zinc metabolism and growth signalling pathways, has the potential to represent a new breakthrough and a prospective therapeutic target for elucidating the pathological process of DVT.</p>
<p id="Par33">Furthermore, it is imperative to acknowledge that DVT is a multisystem disease influenced by metabolic and environmental interactions, with numerous risk factors (including smoking and genetic factors) potentially contributing directly or indirectly to its pathogenesis [<a href="#CR41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR41">41</a>–<a href="#CR45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR45">45</a>]. The study of non-induced DVT cases in China found that the prevalence of hereditary antithrombin (AT) deficiency was approximately 11.2%, and the prevalence of F2 gain-of-function mutations was about 3.5%. The findings of the aforementioned studies indicate the potential for certain genetic defects to exert a significant influence on both the occurrence and the management of DVT [<a href="#CR46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR46">46</a>]. Furthermore, a large-scale genome-wide association study (GWAS) from Bangladesh demonstrated that smoking has a potential mechanism related to solute carrier transporter genes, suggesting that genetic markers may have potential translational significance for the incidence of DVT in smokers [<a href="#CR47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR47">47</a>]. In a similar manner to smoking as a trigger, neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) have been extensively studied in the context of DVT stents, enhanced coagulation activity, and the promotion of DVT. SLC39A11 has been hypothesised to regulate zinc ions, thereby affecting neutrophil activation and NETs release. Recent studies have indicated that a specific gene, designated as CD39, plays a pivotal role in the inhibition of NETs formation, thereby contributing to the attenuation of DVT [<a href="#CR48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR48">48</a>]. It is evident that the aforementioned alterations in genes and metabolites associated with DVT will provide a novel foundation and strategy for comprehending the pathological mechanism [<a href="#CR49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR49">49</a>–<a href="#CR51" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR51">51</a>]. Subsequent large-scale clinical population cohorts and functional genetic variation experiments will explore the multiple associations between SLC39A11 expression levels and smoking status, DVT risk index, plasma zinc ion levels, inflammatory markers and genetic factors. Concurrently, given the high degree of similarity between the pathogenesis of pulmonary embolism (PE) and DVT, the aforementioned conclusions will be progressively applied to a broader range of fields related to DVT.</p>
<p id="Par34">In recent years, ML has been employed extensively to predict new disease targets and provide novel insights into disease pathogenesis, due to its superior performance in clinical diagnosis [<a href="#CR52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR52">52</a>, <a href="#CR53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR53">53</a>]. While there are currently scoring scales available in clinical practice for predicting the risk of DVT, Mooney et al. have demonstrated that ML is superior to traditional scoring methods in terms of predictive accuracy [<a href="#CR54" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR54">54</a>]. In this study, we employed the ML approach to analyse blood samples from the DVT patients and healthy subjects. This enabled us to respectively identify 10 biomarker-significant genes and metabolites from the 193 DEGs and 85 DEMs. To the best of our knowledge, there is no similar study to identify potential biological targets associated with DVT by comparing multiple ML algorithms, including XGBoost, SVM, GLM and RF, and applying the nomogram to predict DVT risk [<a href="#CR55" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR55">55</a>–<a href="#CR57" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR57">57</a>]. The development trend of ML in the DVT field is undergoing a transition, shifting from a position as an auxiliary tool to a role as a primary driving force [<a href="#CR58" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR58">58</a>]. Its applications include multimodal data fusion, interpretable artificial intelligence (AI) upgrades and dynamic disease prediction and intervention [<a href="#CR59" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR59">59</a>, <a href="#CR60" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR60">60</a>]. Furthermore, the integration of multi-omics and ML is propelling the field of DVT research from a cognition-driven model, predicated on a single marker and phenotype, to a novel era that emphasises system networks and large-scale data screening. These advances not only improve the accuracy of clinical prediction and diagnosis, but also contribute greatly to the promotion of in-depth understanding of DVT [<a href="#CR61" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR61">61</a>–<a href="#CR63" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR63">63</a>]. Nevertheless, it is also important to recognise that the effect of predictors on the occurrence of DVT is evaluated solely on the basis of statistical differences, which differs from the clinical situation and the pathophysiology of DVT.</p>
<p id="Par35">Currently, scRNA-seq is a popular tool for the unbiased assessment of cellular heterogeneity [<a href="#CR64" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR64">64</a>–<a href="#CR67" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR67">67</a>]. It is also used to identify new cell states and populations, and to determine the presence of rare cell types [<a href="#CR68" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR68">68</a>]. As a widely used technique in the life sciences, scRNA-seq facilitates the elucidation of complex cellular phenotypes in DVT, including synthetic differentiation of smooth muscle cells (SMCs) and identity confusion in endothelial cells (ECs) [<a href="#CR69" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR69">69</a>, <a href="#CR70" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR70">70</a>]. In this study, scRNA-seq technology was employed to analyse the localisation of immune cells in DVT, highlighting the depletion and infiltration states of SLC39A11 across distinct cell populations and revealing an imbalance in the distribution of macrophage subpopulations. Furthermore, by analysing the dynamic changes of relevant core genes during proportional processes, this approach provides “cell-molecular” resolution for in-depth DVT research, driving translational studies from basic science to clinical applications. Recent advancements in platelet-related scRNA-seq technology, coupled with the development of human vascular organoids, have paved the way for the creation of more sophisticated DVT models that better resemble the complexity of human tissue. Furthermore, the integration of multi-omics and ML into scRNA-seq facilitates the quantification of cell communication, the prioritisation of targets, and the identification of biosignalling axes. Consequently, this development signifies a paradigm shift in the domain of DVT research, ushering in an era characterised by high resolution, high throughput and data-driven methodologies [<a href="#CR64" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR64">64</a>, <a href="#CR71" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR71">71</a>, <a href="#CR72" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR72">72</a>]. This development has the potential to significantly enhance the understanding of DVT diagnosis, treatment and prognosis, thereby informing clinical decision-making and advancing precision medicine [<a href="#CR64" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR64">64</a>, <a href="#CR69" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR69">69</a>].</p>
<p id="Par36">The principal advantage of this study is the joint analysis of omics data, which permits a comprehensive investigation of the interaction between genes and metabolites at the system level [<a href="#CR73" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR73">73</a>]. The application of multi-omics studies represents a promising approach. Further research is required to apply the integrated ML algorithm to efficiently identify diagnostic biomarker panels that exhibit both high validation and significant classification effects [<a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">15</a>, <a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a>, <a href="#CR74" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR74">74</a>, <a href="#CR75" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR75">75</a>]. This will enable the establishment of multi-index joint detection, evaluation of the process of disease occurrence and prognosis, assistance with clinical diagnosis, identification of time windows and guidance of clinical decision-making. But it is inevitable that several potential flaws in our research should be notified. Firstly, the multi-omics data generated by this study were not validated in the follow-up of the participants. With regard to the clinical prediction model, due to the small sample size, there was instability and uncertainty in the judgement of clinical decision-making. Due to practical technical limitations, clinical peripheral blood samples are highly susceptible to hemolysis during collection and transportation, which can compromise the integrity of the samples and affect the QC process. Concurrently, due to clinical ethical restrictions, it is not feasible to collect human blood samples on a large scale, and the insights provided by the corresponding sequencing data need to be further verified in the future. Furthermore, the complexity inherent in mass spectrometry signals can result in uncertain metabolite identification in some instances, and the intricate pathogenesis of DVT necessitates further research to fully elucidate peripheral blood changes in patients [<a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">14</a>, <a href="#CR76" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR76">76</a>, <a href="#CR77" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR77">77</a>].</p></section><section id="Sec74"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Electronic supplementary material</h2>
