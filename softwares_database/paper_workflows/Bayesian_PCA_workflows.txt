complete_workflow_description: The research employed a two-stage statistical methodology combining Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Latent Class Analysis (LCA) to assess cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factors and improve CVD diagnosis. First, preprocessing was performed using XLSTAT [20], wherein missing-value samples were removed and the cleaned dataset was split by sex into female-only and male-only subsets. Second, PCA was applied as a dimensionality reduction, visualization, and feature extraction tool: it operated on the n × p data matrix X (where n = number of observations and p = 13 variables), seeking linear combinations Xa = Σⱼ₌₁ᵖ (aⱼ·xⱼ) that maximize variance; this enabled (i) quick visualization and correlation analysis among the N = 13 variables, (ii) low-dimensional mapping (e.g., 2D/3D plots) of M observations under an optimal variability criterion, and (iii) construction of P uncorrelated principal components (factors). Third, Latent Class Analysis (LCA) was applied to identify latent subgroups (i.e., underlying CVD patient patterns) from multivariate categorical and numerical responses; the LCA model used the probability formulation P(Y = y) = Σ꜀₌₁ᶜ γ꜀ Πⱼ₌₁ʲ Πᵣⱼ₌₁ᴿʲ Pⱼ.ᵣⱼ|꜀^I(yⱼ = rⱼ), where γ꜀ denotes latent class membership probabilities (summing to 1) and Pⱼ.ᵣⱼ|꜀ denotes item-response probabilities conditional on latent class c; the analysis treated CVD patient patterns as the unobserved latent classes. Finally, Pearson correlation matrices (Tables 2 and 3) were computed separately for female and male subsets to quantify pairwise correlations among the 13 CVD risk factors at significance level α = 0.05.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: Bayesian PCA
sample_description: The study included 420 women enrolled in the Environment and Reproductive Health (EARTH) Study who completed at least one IVF cycle between 2006 and 2017 (n=648 total cycles) at the Massachusetts General Hospital (MGH) Fertility Center. All participants had available urine biomarker data with at least one urine sample per IVF cycle for quantification of BPA, methyl-paraben, propyl-paraben, and eight phthalate metabolites (MEP, MBP, MiBP, MBzP, MEHP, MEHHP, MEOHP, MECPP). Urine samples (n=1,145 total) were collected as spot samples: 23% of cycles had one sample (collected between Day 3–9 of gonadotrophin phase), and 77% had two samples (first during Days 3–9, second on day of oocyte retrieval for fresh cycles or embryo transfer for cryo-thaw cycles). Samples were collected in sterile polypropylene cups, specific gravity (SG) was measured using a handheld refractometer calibrated with deionized water, aliquoted, frozen at −80°C, and shipped on dry ice to the CDC for storage at ≤−40°C until analysis. Chemical concentrations were SG-corrected using the formula Pc = P[(1.015 – 1)/(SG − 1)], where Pc is the SG-corrected concentration (µg/L), P is the measured concentration (µg/L), and 1.015 is the mean SG of the study population; values below LOD (0.1–1.2 ng/mL) were imputed as LOD/√2 prior to SG correction. Cycle-specific exposure was defined as the geometric mean of SG-adjusted concentrations from two samples per cycle, or the single-sample concentration when only one sample was available.
complete_workflow_description: Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) for all models except BKMR, which was performed using the R package 'bkmr'. For PCA: exposures were loge-transformed due to right skewness, standardized to z-scores, and submitted to PCA with varimax rotation; components with eigenvalue >1 were retained (three factors explaining 43%, 16%, and 16% of total variance); factor scores were quartiled with the lowest quartile as reference. For multivariable modeling: generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs) with random intercepts (to account for repeated IVF cycles per woman) were fitted using Poisson distribution/log link for oocyte counts, normal distribution/identity link for endometrial thickness, and binomial distribution/logit link for binary outcomes (fertilization, implantation, clinical pregnancy, live birth); population marginal means were reported after adjustment for confounders (age, BMI, year of IVF cycle, SART infertility diagnosis, prior IUI at MGH, and scores of the other two PCA-derived factors). BKMR models included hierarchical variable selection, kernel-based smooth functional representation of exposures, and adjustment for the same confounders (excluding the other PCA factor scores). Sensitivity analyses included restricting to 2006–2012 cycles, cycles with two urine samples, first IVF cycle per woman, and excluding cryo-thaw cycles; stratified analyses assessed effect modification by age (<37 vs. ≥37), BMI (<25 vs. ≥25 kg/m²), and protocol type (agonist vs. other), with p<0.10 considered evidence of interaction. All tests were two-tailed; p<0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

========== paper_workflow entry 3 ==========
related_tool: Bayesian PCA
sample_description: The study used two types of synthetic gene expression datasets generated by GeneNetWeaver: one simulating Escherichia coli (E. coli) and another simulating yeast. For initial evaluation, 10 distinct GRNs were generated for each organism, each containing 30 genes and 41 time points. To further assess scalability and robustness, additional synthetic datasets were generated with larger network sizes: 10 networks per organism, each with 50 genes and 101 time points. All synthetic datasets were time-series microarray expression data designed to mimic realistic transcriptional dynamics, including regulatory time lags and noise. The purpose of generating multiple replicates (10 per condition) was to mitigate biases arising from idiosyncratic network topologies or stochastic variations in network generation.
complete_workflow_description: The method employs a State Space Model (SSM) for gene regulatory network (GRN) inference, implemented via the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm. The SSM consists of two equations: (1) state transition equation x_t = F x_{t−1} + w_t and (2) observation equation y_t = H x_t + v_t, where x_t ∈ ℝ^m are hidden variables (m = number of latent factors), y_t ∈ ℝ^l are observed gene expression levels at time t, F ∈ ℝ^(m×m) is the state transition matrix, H ∈ ℝ^(l×m) is the observation matrix, and w_t, v_t are Gaussian noise terms. GRN structure is derived from the inferred matrices via C = H F (H′H)⁻¹H′, yielding a directed regulatory matrix C ∈ ℝ^(l×l), where C_ij represents regulation of gene i by gene j. The number of hidden variables m is determined using either: (a) BIC, computed as BIC = ln P(x_t, y_t | θ) − ½ N_θ ln N, where θ includes all SSM parameters, N_θ is the total number of free parameters, and N is the number of data points; the optimal m maximizes BIC; or (b) PCA, as described above. For evaluation, inferred GRNs are thresholded: an edge from gene j to gene i is included if |C_ij| ≥ th, where th is varied to generate ROC curves. Precision is calculated as true positive edges / total inferred edges, and average precision across 10 replicate networks is reported. Computational efficiency is benchmarked against time-delayed Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN), with DBN configured to allow up to 3 maximum parents per gene.

sample_description: The study used Dataset A, which consists of 80 quality control (QC) samples from mass spectrometry-based proteomics experiments. Peptides were quantified as intensity values (log₂-transformed), and missing values (MVs) were defined as absent or undetected peptide intensities across samples. The dataset was stratified into nine sectors based on peptide-level mean log₂ intensity (quantiles: low, medium, high) and peptide-level missing rate (quantiles: low, medium, high), resulting in a 3×3 grid (e.g., LIHM = low intensity, high missingness; HILM = high intensity, low missingness). Biological origin of samples is not explicitly stated, but the context implies technical replicates or pooled QC samples typical in proteomics instrument calibration workflows.
complete_workflow_description: The paper describes a complete, reproducible end-to-end imputation workflow for MS-based proteomics data: (1) Input: a peptide abundance matrix (intensity values, log₂-transformed) with missing values across n = 80 QC samples (Dataset A); (2) Stratification: peptides are binned into nine sectors using quantiles of mean log₂ intensity (25th/75th percentiles) and missing rate (25th/75th percentiles); (3) Sector-wise evaluation: nine imputation methods — kNN, RF, local least squares (LLS), maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), BPCA, SVD, collaborative filtering (CF), variational autoencoder (VAE), and denoising autoencoder (DAE) — are applied independently within each sector; (4) Performance assessment: normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) is computed per sector against held-out ground truth (likely via masking-and-recovery evaluation on non-missing entries or cross-validation); (5) Method selection: the method with the numerically lowest NRMSE in each sector is designated 'optimal'; (6) Mixed imputation: missing values in each sector are replaced using its sector-specific optimal method, producing the 'Mix' imputed dataset; (7) Validation: performance is confirmed on both training (n = 48) and independent testing (n = 32) subsets of Dataset A using NRMSE and Spearman correlation (rho) between imputed and original values. All steps are grounded in empirical observation of the intensity–missingness relationship (R² = 0.73) and validated across sectors and datasets.
