sample_description: The study used 10 sponge samples (3–5 mm³ each) of two marine sponge species: Aplysina caissara Pinheiro and Hajdu, 2001 and Aplysina fulva Pallas, 1766 (Verongiida, Aplysinidae), collected by scuba diving in July and October 2018 at two locations in São Sebastião, São Paulo, Brazil—Ponta Recife (23°49’30.36”S 45°24’47.879”W) and southern rock shores of Praia do Guaecá (23°49’22.8”S 45°28’19.2”W)—at depths of 3–8 m. Samples were placed in sterile plastic bags with natural seawater, kept in a cooler during transport, and processed immediately upon arrival at the laboratory. For bacterial isolation, 2.5 g of choanosome tissue from each sponge was macerated in 25 mL calcium/magnesium-free artificial seawater (ASW); supernatants were serially diluted (10⁻² to 10⁻⁶) and plated on multiple media: enriched (BHI), oligotrophic (MB, R2A), and selective (AIA for Actinobacteriota; SNAX for Cyanobacteria). Pure isolates were grown in corresponding broths (BHI, R2A, MB, or selective formulations) for 18 h at room temperature (20–25 °C, 200 rpm), then stored in 20% glycerol at −80 °C. Genomic DNA was extracted using the Wizard Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega), and the 16S rRNA gene was amplified using primers 27F-1492R and sequenced via Sanger sequencing on an ABI 3500. Additionally, metabarcoding data from 15 previously sequenced sponge samples (10 A. caissara and 5 A. fulva; Bioproject IDs PRJNA665805 and PRJNA673577) were re-analyzed for comparative ASV-based community profiling. For secondary metabolite analysis, bacterial strains positive for type I/II PKS or NRPS genes were cultured on solid BHI and MB media, harvested at stationary phase (~72 h), and extracted sequentially with ethyl acetate (30 min ultrasonication) and methanol; extracts were dried via rotary evaporation and vacuum concentration. Crude extracts (5.0 mg) were cleaned up using C18 solid-phase extraction cartridges (Chromabond, 45 μm, 500.0 mg, 6.0 mL), pre-activated with 3.0 mL MeOH and equilibrated with 3.0 mL MeOH/H₂O (8:2, v/v), then eluted with 1.5 mL of the same solvent mixture and reconstituted in MeOH to 2.0 mg/mL for LC-MS analysis.
software_configuration_description: The GNPS MolNetEnhancer workflow (https://ccms-ucsd.github.io/GNPSDocumentation/molnetenhancer/) was employed to integrate complementary annotation data into the molecular network. It combined results from four distinct annotation sources: (1) GNPS Library Search (with cosine score >0.7, minimum 4 matched peaks, precursor ion mass tolerance 0.02 Da, MS/MS fragment ion tolerance 0.05 Da), (2) MS2LDA (via http://ms2lda.org/), (3) Network Annotation Propagation (NAP) workflows, and (4) Dereplicator (Insilico Peptidic Natural Products tool) configured with precursor ion mass tolerance = 0.02 Da, MS/MS fragment ion tolerance = 0.05 Da, VarQuest algorithm, PNP database selected, maximum allowed charge = 2, minimum amino acid count = 5, maximum allowed modification mass = 150 Da, and minimum matched peaks = 5. The integrated outputs were used to enhance structural annotations across the molecular network, enabling propagation of chemical class information (via ClassyFire ontology) and visualization in Cytoscape v.3.8.2. Solvent blank (MeOH) and media blank features were removed from the final feature table prior to MolNetEnhancer processing.

sample_description: The study used four bacterial isolates belonging to the Bacillus genus: B. licheniformis M017, B. licheniformis 1001, B. amyloliquefaciens, and B. laterosporus; and a commercial microbial consortium named Bacstim® 100, containing equal volumes of the above four Bacillus strains. Bacterial cultures were grown in Lysogeny broth (LB) medium at 28°C and 140 rpm for 36 h under sterile conditions. Growth was monitored via OD600 measurements every 3 h, and cells were harvested at four defined time points corresponding to lag phase (3 h), exponential phase (7.5 h), and two stationary phase time points (24 h and 31.5 h). Harvesting involved centrifugation at 5,000 rpm for 15 min at 28°C; supernatants were decanted and pellets (wet weight measured) were stored at −80°C. Intracellular metabolites were extracted from pellets using 100% LC-MS grade methanol (15 mg pellet per 320 μL solvent), followed by vortexing, probe sonication at 55% amplitude for 1 min, and centrifugation at 3,700 rpm for 30 min at 28°C. Supernatants were filtered through 0.22 μm nylon filters into pre-labeled HPLC glass vials with 500 μL inserts.
software_configuration_description: MolNetEnhancer was applied within the GNPS ecosystem to enhance feature-based molecular networks (FBMN) by integrating multiple in silico annotation tools. The configuration included substructure recognition via MS2LDA (MS2 Latent Dirichlet Allocation) with default advanced parameters: overlap score threshold = 0.3, probability score = 0.1, TopX = 5; MotifDB-annotated MotifSets incorporated included MassBank, GNPS, Euphorbia, Streptomyces, Salinisporus, Photorhabdus, and Xenorhabdus. DEREPLICATOR was used for dereplication against GNPS, HMDB, SUPNAT, CHEBI, and DRUGBANK databases. Network Annotation Propagation (NAP) was configured with two scoring methods: (i) fusion scoring—combining MetFrag in silico fragmentation predictions with spectral library matches within molecular families, and (ii) consensus scoring—leveraging structural similarity across spectral nodes in a molecular family. NAP searched structure databases including GNPS, DNP (Dictionary of Natural Products), ChEBI, and SUPER NATURAL II. All MolNetEnhancer jobs were executed via GNPS web platform using uploaded GNPS MGF files and GNPS Sample Tables (feature quantification tables) generated from MS-DIAL processing.

software_configuration_description: MolNetEnhancer is a Python/R software package that integrates outputs from GNPS (for molecular networking), MS2LDA (for unsupervised substructure discovery), and in silico annotation tools including Network Annotation Propagation (NAP), DEREPLICATOR, or SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID. It maps Mass2Motifs from MS2LDA onto molecular network nodes as pie charts weighted by overlap scores, adds multiple edges between nodes sharing motifs, and submits putative structures (from GNPS library matches and in silico annotations) to ClassyFire for hierarchical chemical classification (kingdom → direct parent). User-defined parameters include the number x of top-shared motifs to display per molecular family. Outputs are generated in GraphML format for Cytoscape visualization. The workflow does not perform raw data processing itself but consumes precomputed outputs from external tools.
source_paper: Madeleine et al. (2019), Metabolites, DOI: 10.3390/metabo9070144, PMID: 31315242
