sample_description: The study recruited 270 participants aged 25–65 years from patients visiting the Dental Clinic of Instituto Superior de Ciências da Saúde Norte (CESPU), Gandra, Paredes, Portugal, between January 2016 and July 2017. Inclusion required ≥18 natural teeth and absence of removable dental prostheses, orthodontic devices, antibiotic treatment or routine oral antiseptic use within the prior 3 months, and no systemic diseases, medications, or radiotherapy known to affect salivary production/composition. Ethical approval was obtained from CESPU’s Ethics Committee (registration number 35/CE-IUCS/2019), and all participants provided written informed consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2000 revision). From the initial 270, a convenience subsample of 81 participants was selected using a newly developed oral health scale (OG), stratified as follows: OG 0 (n=17), OG 1 (n=25), OG 2 (n=28), OG 3 (n=11); additionally, 47 participants had periodontal grade (PG) 0 with varying dental grades (DG), and 46 had DG 0 with varying PG. Unstimulated whole saliva (2–3 mL) was collected via spitting after ≥1 hour fasting and abstention from toothbrushing; samples were immediately stored at −80 °C until processing.

sample_description: The study used sixteen mature, healthy Harrier Hounds (Canis familiaris) sourced from the Centre for Canine Nutrition at Massey University, Palmerston North; one dog was excluded for non-study-related reasons, resulting in fifteen dogs completing the trial. Dogs had a mean age of 5.81 years (SE: 0.72) and body weight ranging from 21.8 to 31.9 kg. They were housed in groups of four in large outdoor pens (10 m × 10 m) during daytime and in pairs in indoor pens (2 m × 2 m) with attached outdoor access (2 m × 2 m) at night. The dogs were randomly assigned to one of two dietary treatments: a commercially available kibbled diet (n = 8: 3 males, 5 females) or a raw red meat diet (n = 7: 2 males, 5 females), formulated to meet AAFCO (2016) nutrient requirements for adult maintenance. The meat diet consisted of 73% beef muscle, 10% beef liver, 5% bone chip, 5% beef tripe, 3.5% beef heart, 3.5% beef kidney, and 0.2% mineral pre-mix. Daily energy intake was calculated using ME (kJ) = 552 × BW^0.75 and adjusted weekly to maintain stable body weight; water was provided ad libitum. Digestibility assessments were conducted over four-day collection periods in weeks 3, 6, and 9, during which individual food intake, refusals, and total faecal output were recorded. For each digestibility period, subsamples of diets and faeces were frozen at −20 °C, freeze-dried, and ground for macronutrient analysis. Fresh faecal samples were collected within 10 minutes of excretion and immediately stored at −85 °C for volatile fatty acid (VFA) quantification and microbial composition analysis. A total of 24 faecal samples—matched by dog and time point (weeks 3, 6, and 9)—were retained for integrated multi-omics analyses after excluding samples missing physical measurement, metabolomic, or 16S amplicon data.
software_configuration_description: The mixOmics R package (González et al., 2012) was used for all data integration analyses. Prior to integration, the 16S rDNA amplicon sequencing data underwent preprocessing using the 'nearZeroVar' function to remove uninformative and non-redundant features: specifically, columns with only one non-zero value and columns exhibiting very few unique values relative to the number of samples (i.e., low-variance features that failed to generate substantial variation). This filtering ensured inclusion only of biologically meaningful, variable taxa. Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) was applied to assess multivariate associations between two high-dimensional datasets: (1) the relative abundances of microbial taxa (post-filtering and taxonomic assignment) and (2) the combined phenotypic dataset comprising metabolomic variables (faecal VFA concentrations: acetate, propionate, butyrate, isobutyrate, isovalerate, valerate), dietary composition data (macronutrient profiles including dry matter, crude protein, crude fat, crude fibre, ash, NFE), and faecal physical characteristics (faecal health score, faecal weight). To address high co-correlation within the metabolomic and physical measurement dataset, Shrunk CCA—a regularized variant of CCA that penalizes model complexity—was specifically employed for integrating faecal 16S rDNA amplicon data with the combined metabolomic and physical measurement data. Principal Component Analyses (PCAs) were performed separately on each high-dimensional dataset using standard R commands (R Core Team, 2015) to detect intrinsic patterns prior to integration.
