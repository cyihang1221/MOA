>MATERIALS AND METHODS   Patient Enrollment and Sample Collection   TA samples were collected, at birth or within 6 h of birth before surfactant administration, from infants born prematurely at ≤28 wk gestational age (GA) and admitted to the University of Alabama at Birmingham regional neonatal intensive care unit ( 17 ). All infants were followed until 36 wk postmenstrual age, at which time the physiological definition of BPD ( 27 ) was used to classify them as BPD predisposed (developed severe BPD) or BPD resistant (did not develop BPD). The Institutional Review Board of University of Alabama at Birmingham approved the study with waiver of consent as remnant samples were obtained from routine clinical care. Microbiome Metagenome Prediction   The 16S rRNA sequencing technique used to analyze the microbiome of the TA samples has been described in detail previously ( 17 ). We used the PICRUSt (Phylogenetic Investigation of Communities by Reconstruction of Unobserved States) software to predict the microbial metagenome using this 16S rRNA sequencing data and the KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathway to identify metagenomic contents ( 14 ,  18 ). Abundance profiles of functional annotations were obtained by measuring differences in the functional genomic content after normalizing abundances of each genome category to total number of proteins predicted for each sample. Metabolomics Analysis   Extraction of lung condensates.   TA supernatant fluid (50 µl) was added to 200 µl of supercooled (−43°C) methanol to precipitate proteins and other macromolecules and to extract metabolites for 1 h. This extract was centrifuged at 10,000  g  for 10 min at 4°C. The supernatant was evaporated to dryness and resuspended in 100 µl of 0.1% formic acid before mass spectrometry. LC-MS-MS analysis.   An aliquot (5 µl) of each sample was loaded onto a Nano cHiPLC 200 μm ID × 6 mm ChromXP C18-CL 3 μm, 120 Å reverse-phase trap cartridge (Eksigent, Dublin, CA). After a washing of the cartridge for 5 min with 0.1% formic acid, the bound metabolites were flushed onto a Nano cHiPLC column (200 μm ID × 15 cm ChromXP C18-CL) with a 20-min, linear gradient of 5–95% acetonitrile in 0.1% formic acid at 1,000 nl/min, using an Eksigent 415 NanoLC system. The column was washed with 95% acetonitrile-0.1% formic acid for 5 min and then reequilibrated with 5% acetonitrile-0.1% formic acid for 5 min. The SCIEX 5600 Triple-Tof mass spectrometer (SCIEX, Toronto, ON, Canada) was used to analyze the metabolite profile. The IonSpray voltages for positive and negative modes were ± 2300 V and declustering potential was ± 80 V. Ionspray and curtain gases were set at 10 and 25 psi, respectively. The interface heater temperature was 120°C. Eluted compounds were subjected to a time-of-flight survey scan from  m / z  50–1,000 to determine the top 20 most intense ions for MS-MS analysis. Product ion time-of-flight scans to obtain the tandem mass spectra of the selected parent ions over the range from  m / z  50–1,000 were collected over 50-ms intervals using a collision energy spread of 15 eV with a set collision point of 35 eV. Spectra were centroided and deisotoped by Analyst software, version 1.6 TF (Sciex, Toronto, ON, Canada). Data analysis and metabolite identification.   LC-MS data were processed using XCMS-Online (Scripps, La Jolla, CA) to identify peaks occurring across all samples, the peak areas and their retention times. Individual Excel .csv files for each sample were created containing the  m / z , peak area and retention time of each metabolite ion. The files were placed in folders corresponding to the two groups and the folders converted to single .zip file. The data were uploaded to MetaboAnalyst 3.0 ( www.metaboanalyst.ca ). Since the aspirate fluids were washings with saline, it was necessary to normalize to the total ion current intensities for each sample. The data were mean-centered and subjected to Pareto scaling before univariate (Volcano plot) and multivariate [principal components analysis and partial least square discriminant analysis (PLSDA)] statistical analyses. By use of the normalized and mean centered data set generated by MetaboAnalyst, an Excel .txt file was created containing in individual columns the  m / z  values of each metabolite ions, their retention times, the  P  values and the  t -scores for differences between the two groups. This set of data was analyzed by mummichog 1.0.5, a statistical program that searches for subnetwork chemical connections (based on allowed mass differences between metabolites) and for evidence of pathway enrichment in known pathways ( 4 ). Because of their correlation with retention time, metabolite adduct ions were identified to reduce data set size. Metabolite ions with  P  values ≤ 0.05 were considered significantly different. To establish a nonsignificant association with pathways, an equal number of metabolites with  P  values > 0.05 were randomly selected from the data set and mapped to metabolic pathways. This was repeated 100 times. Significance of pathway enrichment by the metabolites with significant  P  values was calculated using the Fisher exact test. Statistical analyses were performed with Graphpad software (version 7; Graphpad Software, La Jolla, CA). RESULTS   Demographics of BPD-Predisposed and BPD-Resistant Infants   Fifteen infants were BPD predisposed (severe BPD only) and 15 infants were BPD resistant. These infants included the same infants from both discovery and validation cohorts used in our previous report ( 17 ). Clinical characteristics of the groups are provided in  Table 1 . No significant differences were noted in GA or sex between the two groups. Sepsis incidence was similar between the groups, whereas number of mechanical ventilation days and oxygen use were higher for BPD-predisposed infants, as expected, given the known risk factors for BPD. In addition, surfactant requirement and RDS incidence were similar between the two groups.   Table 1. Metagenome Abundance Varied Between BPD-Predisposed and BPD-Resistant Infants   The PICRUSt program predicted the metagenome of the TA microbiome ( Tables 2  and  3 ). The acetyl-CoA/propionyl-CoA carboxylase ortholog, which is a gene group that plays a central role in fatty acid metabolism (and energy metabolism in general) was one of the most reduced metagenomes in BPD-predisposed compared with BPD-resistant infants. Other metagenomes including O-antigen biosynthesis protein ortholog (role in lipopolysaccharide biosynthesis), spore germination protein KB (role in glucose metabolism), and transcription attenuation protein (role in translation) were also less abundant in BPD-predisposed vs. BPD-resistant infants. At KEGG level 1 ( 14 ) pathways representing human diseases, cardiovascular diseases and cellular processes, and cell growth and death were less abundant in the TA microbiome of BPD-predisposed infants. In addition, genomic pathways that represent caffeine metabolism were also less abundant in this group than in BPD-resistant infants.   Table 2.
