Serum samples were stored at -80 °C since collection and underwent metabolic profiling in 2019–2022 using NMR (Bruker IVDr B.I.LISA™) and targeted high-resolution mass spectrometry (Biocrates Absolute IDQ p400 HR kit on Orbitrap Q Exactive Plus with UHPLC). complete_workflow_description: The global workflow began with serum sample collection from 2283 HUNT2 female participants (1995–1997), stored at -80 °C. Metabolic profiling was performed in two modalities: (1) NMR spectroscopy using Bruker IVDr Lipoprotein Subclass Analysis (B.I.LISA™) to quantify 89 lipoprotein subfractions (after excluding contaminated LDL-2/LDL-4 subclasses and calculated parameters/particle numbers) and small-molecule metabolites via peak integration in CMPG spectra, corrected for T2 relaxation; (2) targeted MS using Biocrates Absolute IDQ p400 HR kit on an Orbitrap Q Exactive Plus coupled to Agilent 1290 UHPLC, with data processed via Xcalibur 4.1 and MetIDQ DB110-2976 software to yield μM concentrations. For MS data, preprocessing included: exclusion of metabolites with ≥10% missing completely at random (MCAR) values or ≥50% missing not at random (MNAR, i.e., below detection limit); logarithmic transformation; batch correction using an empirical Bayes method (batches defined per 96-well plate); imputation of remaining MNAR values with truncated normal random draws (0 to median LOD); and imputation of remaining MCAR values using k-nearest neighbors (k=3) within sample groups. Quality control assessed coefficients of variation (CVs): <15% for most metabolites and lipoprotein subfractions; final analytical CVs were <15% for 280/284 MS metabolites and <15% for 65/89 lipoprotein measurements. Downstream statistical analysis involved variance partitioning (e.g., R² decomposition) to quantify contributions of age, BMI, hormonal factors, education, and physical activity to metabolite variance, followed by hierarchical clustering of participants based on lifestyle variables and multivariate association modeling.
