sample_description: The study enrolled 66 participants with prolonged disorders of consciousness (DOC) admitted to the Critical Care Rehabilitation Department of Jiangning Hospital, Nanjing Medical University from March 2022 to March 2023; 58 participants completed all follow-up assessments and were included in the final analysis. Inclusion criteria: (1) aged 18–75 years; (2) diagnosed with traumatic brain injury, intracerebral haemorrhage, or hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy; (3) clinical diagnosis of vegetative state (VS) or minimally conscious state (MCS) confirmed by a neuropsychologist using five consecutive Chinese Coma Recovery Scale-Revised (CRS-R) assessments per week; (4) 1–3 months post-injury; and (5) stable clinical condition. Exclusion criteria: (1) pre-existing neurological diseases; (2) unstable clinical conditions (e.g., severe cardiac stress or respiratory failure); (3) use of sedatives, anti-epileptic drugs, or neurological stimulants; (4) incomplete clinical data; (5) declined participation; and (6) refusal to be followed up. All participants’ legal caregivers provided written informed consent. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Jiangning Hospital, Nanjing Medical University (2022-03-047-k01) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki II and good clinical practice guidelines.
complete_workflow_description: Neurophysiological examination of the blink reflex (BR) was performed using electromyography (EMG). Active electrodes were bilaterally placed on the orbicularis oculi muscle, with reference electrodes positioned laterally to the lateral canthus. Right and left supraorbital nerves were stimulated over the supraorbital foramen. Electrical stimuli parameters were set at 20–40 mA intensity (approximately three times the subjective sensory threshold), 0.1 ms duration, and interstimulus intervals varying arrhythmically between 5 and 10 s. Responses were elicited eight times consecutively on both sides. Early component (R1), ipsilateral late component (iR2), and contralateral late component (cR2) responses were recorded. Latency and amplitude were measured, and averages were calculated. Signal processing included offline analysis with a band-pass filter of 10–5000 Hz, sensitivity of 200 µV/Div, and sweep speed of 10 ms/Div. Two blinded authors independently performed electrophysiological evaluations; discrepancies were resolved through discussion. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics V.22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables with normal distributions were expressed as means ± SD; non-normally distributed variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (25th–75th percentiles). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. BR latencies/amplitudes were compared using independent samples t-tests (for normally distributed data) or Mann–Whitney U-tests (for non-normal data). Multivariate logistic regression was performed to assess the independent predictive value of BR parameters, adjusting for age, sex, and disease duration. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to determine prognostic validity of BR parameters for differentiating improved versus non-improved patients. Correlation analyses included Pearson correlation coefficients (r) for unadjusted linear relationships between BR parameters and baseline CRS-R scores, followed by partial correlation analysis (adj.r) adjusting for age, sex, disease duration, and aetiology. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05 for two-sided tests.

complete_workflow_description: Data processing was performed using a standardized Terra pipeline and multiple bioinformatics tools. Raw reads were aligned to hg38 (K562) or mm10 (mESC) reference genomes using BWA-MEM (parameters: -5SP -T0). Ligation events were parsed with pairtools parse v.0.3.0 (parameters: --min-mapq 40 --walks-policy 5unique --max-inter-align-gap 30 --add-columns pos5,pos3,dist_to_5,dist_to_3,read_len) to retain fragment 5' and 3' end positions essential for fragment start/end determination. Parsed files were sorted (pairtools sort) and PCR duplicates removed (pairtools dedup). Final BAM and pairs files were generated via pairtools split (nproc-in=8, nproc-out=8) followed by samtools view (-bS -@16) and samtools sort (-@16), then indexed. Replicates were merged using pairtools merge; for RCMC data, interactions were further filtered to require both fragments within the same captured locus using pairtools select with custom boolean expressions (e.g., for Fbn2: '(chrom1 == "chr18") and (chrom2 == "chr18") and (57899000 <= pos1 <= 58901000) and (57899000 <= pos2 <= 58901000)'). Loop calling was performed using FitHiChIP Peak-to-Peak (v28) with 2.5 kb loop anchor bin size on merged replicate pairs files. Balanced contact maps were generated using cooler cload pairix (after bgzip compression and pairix indexing) and ICE-balanced .mcool files created via cooler zoomify --balance. Fragment starts and ends were computed as min(pos5, pos3) and max(pos5, pos3) respectively, enabled by --add-columns pos5,pos3 in pairtools parse. Short-fragment analysis (<120 bp) used filtering on fragment length derived from these coordinates. Contact maps were visualized in R using HiContacts::plotMatrix and HiCExperiment::import; public ChIP-seq/ATAC-seq bigWig files were imported via rtracklayer::import and cross-mapped (mm9/mm39 → mm10) when necessary. CTCF motifs were obtained from the CTCF R package (AH104729 for hg38, AH104755 for mm10) and annotated using FIMO. CAMEL p-value estimation involved simulating 100 million multinomial samples under null (equal quadrant probability) across total read counts N = 5–500, computing empirical CDF of log2(α̂) on a 0.01-step grid (α̂ ∈ [1,32]), and interpolating observed α̂ to lookup p-values. Precision-recall curves used FitHiChIP 2.5 kb loop anchors, defining true positives as CTCF motifs unique within anchor and within 30 bp of CTCF ChIP-seq peak, and true negatives as anchor regions ≥200 bp from the motif. Cohesin footprint estimation employed 2D kernel density plots (via ggplot2::stat_density_2d leveraging MASS::kde2d) of short fragment starts/ends aligned to the 35 bp CTCF motif (MA1930.1) start coordinate; bandwidth was tuned to balance smoothing and resolution. Fully extruded state estimation involved counting upstream fragments overlapping CBS(+) that interact with downstream convergent CBS(-), correcting for ~50% CTCF occupancy, and validating against trans contacts. Chromatin-state-dependent extrusion analysis used ChromHMM 15-state annotations (grouped into quiescent, polycomb/bivalent, active) on 1 Mb windows downstream of CBS(+), clustered via Ward’s hierarchical clustering; extruded loop size was defined as average log10 interaction length of long-range TF-protected fragments (length <120 bp, fragment end ≥48 bp from motif start, interaction length >10 kb). H3K27ac/RNAPII stratification used top/bottom 10% of basepair coverage or peak count in those 1 Mb windows. All reproducible code is available at https://github.com/aryeelab/cohesin_extrusion_reproducibility.

complete_workflow_description: Electrical stimulation was delivered to the right supraorbital nerve (SON) using square-wave pulses (0.5 ms duration) via two disposable Ag/AgCl gel electrodes (10 mm diameter; Biorode SDC-H, Vyaire Medical, Tokyo), placed near the supraorbital foramen and ~3 cm above it. The PPI paradigm used prepulse–test intervals (PTIs) of 5 ms and 10–150 ms in 10-ms steps, organized into four randomized blocks: Block 1 (5, 10, 20, 30 ms), Block 2 (40, 50, 60, 70 ms), Block 3 (80, 90, 100, 110 ms), and Block 4 (120, 130, 140, 150 ms); each block included five or six conditions (test alone, prepulse alone, and prepulse + test at four PTIs). Each condition had 15 epochs per run, with either 10 or 12 runs per block (totaling 30 epochs per condition per block), separated by 30-s inter-run intervals and 2-min inter-block intervals. Run order within each block followed an ascending–descending (U-shaped) sequence, with test-alone runs positioned first and last. For analysis, blink responses were fully rectified and averaged per block; baseline (pre-stimulus) was subtracted, and area under the curve (AUC) between 15–40 ms was calculated as R1 response magnitude. Inhibition was quantified as %Amplitude = (R1 amplitude for prepulse + test / R1 amplitude for test alone) × 100. Statistical analyses included one-way repeated-measures ANOVA across 16 PTIs, 95% confidence interval assessment for significance (non-overlap with 100% indicating significant inhibition), two-way ANOVA (sex × PTI), simple and partial correlation (age vs. PPI, controlling for sex), and one-way repeated-measures ANOVA for block-order effects on test-alone amplitude. Significance threshold was p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.

complete_workflow_description: The study employed three experimental paradigms: (1) Simple Reaction Time (SRT) Test: Subjects contracted forearm muscles upon perceiving visual (LEDs), tactile (peripheral nerve stimulation via FINE/CFINE or vibrotactile motor), or combined visual-tactile stimuli. Stimuli were randomized across 50 trials per modality, grouped into three blocks with breaks; reaction time was measured as the inflection point from baseline to first major peak in filtered, rectified, and smoothed EMG (recorded via surface EMG for Subject 1 and implanted EMG for Subjects 2 and 3). (2) Intensity-Based Reaction Time Test: Stimulation intensity was modulated by varying pulse width (from threshold to 'max comfortable') while holding pulse amplitude and frequency constant; intensity was quantified using Activation Charge Rate (ACR = PF × PA × (PW − PW_thresh)), and reaction times were fit to Piéron’s law (SRT = β(PW − PW_thresh)^−α) using least-squares fitting in Python. Ten equally spaced pulse widths were tested across thumb and index fingertip percepts, with five blocks of 100 randomized trials (five per intensity level). (3) Backmasking Experiment: To assess pre-perceptual processing, three trial types were used: (i) weak 50-ms stimulus (100% perceptible alone), (ii) strong 50-ms 'comfortable' stimulus delayed by 150 ms, and (iii) backmasked trial with weak stimulus followed by strong stimulus after 100-ms interburst interval. Subjects reported number of perceived bursts and reacted as fast as possible; reaction time for masked trials was measured from the strong stimulus onset if only one burst was reported. Catch trials (two strong stimuli) confirmed masking efficacy. Statistical analysis included ANOVA with Tukey correction (SRT), least-squares curve fitting in Python (intensity-based), and t-tests (Subjects 1 & 3) or Mann–Whitney U tests (Subject 2) for backmasking comparisons.
