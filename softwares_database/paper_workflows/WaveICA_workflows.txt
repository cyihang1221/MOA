========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: WaveICA
sample_description: Urine samples from 100 female municipal firefighters enrolled in the Fire Fighter Cancer Cohort Study, collected before and after live-fire training (total n = 200 samples: 100 pre-fire, 100 post-fire). Samples were collected between October 2022 and July 2024; pre-fire samples were obtained on the day of training prior to exposure, and post-fire samples were collected 1.8–11.0 hours after training (targeted 2–4 hours). All samples were processed with acetonitrile extraction, internal standards (13C-labeled), and analyzed via high-resolution metabolomics across four analytical modes: HILIC(+), HILIC(−), C18(+), and C18(−).
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with urine collection (pre- and post-fire) from 100 female firefighters, followed by sample processing including thawing, mixing, specific gravity measurement, aliquoting, and QA/QC integration using an Opentrons OT2 liquid handler. Metabolite extraction used 40 μL urine + 120 μL acetonitrile with 13C-labeled internal standards, vortexing, cold equilibration, and centrifugation. Supernatants were split for HILIC (in 1:1 acetonitrile/water) and C18 (in water) analysis. Chromatography was performed on Vanquish Duo UPLC systems with Higgins TARGA C18 or HILIC columns at controlled temperatures (30–40 °C), coupled to Exploris 120 HRMS in positive/negative ionization modes. Raw data underwent log2 transformation and standardization; only level 1 annotated metabolites (structure confirmed via reference standards) present in ≥75% of samples were retained. Differential analysis used linear mixed-effects models in R (v4.4.1) with exposure status (pre/post-fire) as fixed effect and random intercepts for participants, adjusting for age, education, Hispanic ethnicity, and fire type; FDR correction (q < 0.05) was applied. Functional enrichment analysis (FEA) was conducted using MetaboAnalyst’s MSEA module. Stratified analysis by fire type (flashover vs. burn room/tower) employed multiple linear regression with identical covariates and FDR adjustment. Volcano plots and circular bar charts of fold changes were generated for visualization.
source_paper: Tuo et al. (2026), International journal of hygiene and environmental health, DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheh.2026.114746, PMID: 41619359

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: WaveICA
sample_description: Blood samples were collected from 197 individual broiler chickens across 22 farms in three countries (Brazil: 98 samples from 10 farms; Italy: 30 samples from 5 farms; Spain: 69 samples from 7 farms). Blood was spotted onto Whatman 903 Protein Saver Cards, dried at room temperature, stored with desiccant at 4 °C, then shipped to UMC Utrecht and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Samples were classified by local experts and farmers as 'high health' or 'low health' based on observable phenotypes including reduced size, rough feathers, reduced activity, and dirty vent feathers.
complete_workflow_description: The study employed a multi-step end-to-end metabolomics workflow: (1) Sample collection via blood spots on Whatman 903 cards from broilers in Brazil, Italy, and Spain, with binary health labels assigned per chicken by local experts; (2) Sample preparation involving 3-mm disk punching, ultrasonic extraction in acetonitrile/formic acid with internal standards; (3) Direct infusion mass spectrometry (DI-MS) using chip-based nano-electrospray on a Thermo Q-Exactive HF in both positive and negative ion modes (3 min and 1.5 min acquisition, respectively), with three technical replicates per sample saved as .raw files; (4) Data preprocessing and statistical analysis using the in-house software MetaboShiny and its companion database suite MetaDBparse (Wolthuis et al., 2020); (5) Feature ranking via a novel 'V-score' combining univariate t-test p-values and fold-change magnitudes, visualized in volcano plots; (6) Forward and reverse feature selection to optimize m/z count for Random Forest (RF) classification; (7) Model evaluation using Leave-One-Country-Out (LOCO) cross-validation — iteratively holding out all samples from one country as test set while training on the other two; (8) Biological interpretation of the top-ranking 3784-m/z signature using mummichog enrichment analysis to identify perturbed pathways (e.g., amino acid metabolism, branched-chain amino acids, polyamines). The pipeline explicitly links raw DI-MS data to interpretable biological insights while controlling for country-level confounding.
source_paper: C et al. (2023), Metabolomics : Official journal of the Metabolomic Society, DOI: 10.1007/s11306-023-01973-4, PMID: 36732451
