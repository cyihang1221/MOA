>METHODS   Ethics statement   The study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of the Uniformed Services University, Emory University, and the University of Rochester. Samples and ICD-9 records were assigned code numbers, and the key linking the samples and ICD-9 codes was retained at the Armed Forces Health Surveillance Center and not shared with the research groups conducting this study. Sample Characteristics   Pre- and post-deployment serum samples for 200 active-duty service personnel who were deployed to Iraq or Afghanistan (the Case group) were retrieved from the DoD Serum Repository as previously described ( 17 ) Go  et al ; 2019  JOEM  Current Issue). A group of 200, non-deployed Service Members (Controls) without similar deployment exposures but with specimens in the DoDSR were selected as controls for this study. Additional selection criteria for Case and Controls included time in service between collections, and were not matched based on gender or age. We also obtained a database of medical records using ICD-9 codes and linked to the serum samples with code numbers. All data on DoD Service members contained in the Defense Medical Surveillance System (DMSS) are validated against DoD personnel data. Data elements (i.e., gender, age, grade, race/ethnic, and marital status) for a medical event are derived from personnel data considered to be current on the date of a medical event. Overall population statistics (i.e., person year denominators) are calculated based on longitudinal personnel data. Unknown (or missing) data are excluded from the calculations. We included persons where the ICD9 code was recorded at only one visit. Records of healthcare encounters from military treatment facilities and purchased care hospitalizations as well as ambulatory medical care were included in this study. First time encounters with an ICD-9 code of interest, within the surveillance period were captured. An individual censored for one encounter remained in the study to identify other encounters of interest.   Based on these criteria, we identified a subset of 49 ICD-9 codes representing cardiopulmonary symptoms and diagnoses that we hypothesized could be reasonably related to deployment-related inhalational exposures (See  Table I ). Prior to analysis, subjects were filtered based on missing data and manual curation of clinical data to check for mismatch between deployment dates and medical appointment dates. We then classified subjects by the timing of medical encounters which referenced specified ICD-9 codes as either 1) Case-Onset, which includes personnel who developed new cardiopulmonary ICD-9 codes after the start of deployment (N=41); 2) Case-Never group that included service personnel who never exhibited cardiopulmonary symptoms before, during or after deployment (N=25) or 3) Case-Pre, which included the individuals that exhibited cardiopulmonary symptoms prior to deployment (N=125). We performed the same analysis for the non-deployed Control group. We identified 18 subjects with onset of at least one cardiopulmonary ICD-9 code during their military service (Control-Onset), 33 subjects who never exhibited any of the listed symptoms or diagnoses (Control-Never), and 133 subjects who exhibited cardiopulmonary symptoms before blood collection period (Control-Pre). Measurement of microRNAs, plasma proteins, and cytokines   The serum samples were processed for several classes of biomarkers ( 20 ), miRNAs ( 19 ,  20 ), cytokines ( 14 ), cotinine ( 21 ) environmental chemicals like PAHs, dioxins and furans ( 16 ), and the metabolome (Go  et al;  2019  JOEM  Current Issue). The methods and initial results for each of these measurements are described in the respective articles. Statistical analysis between deployment and serum biomarkers   To identify possible associations between symptom onset and serum biomarkers, we performed non-parametric Wilcoxon sign rank test to compare biomarkers levels between Case-Onset x Case-Never and Control-Onset x Control-Never, using the absolute values of the biomarkers from the post-deployment serum sample. A false discovery correction was not applied for this exploratory study due to small sample sizes and exploratory nature of the analyses. Control-Onset x Control-Never was used as a control and only markers significant in Case-Onset comparisons were investigated. Multiple Correspondence Analysis (MCA) for ICD-9 data   Multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) is a dimensionality reduction technique for categorical data that allows detection and exploration of structural relationships between variables and samples ( 24 ). The MCA() function in R package FactoMineR was used to perform MCA to select the top three dimensions corresponding to 10% or more variation (Total Variance 33.2%). The explain_code() function in R package icd was used for mapping ICD-9 codes with their corresponding description. The contributions of ICD-9 codes to each dimension were visualized using the corrplot() function in R package corrplot. Integrative network analysis: identifying associations between clinical data, biomolecules, and environmental chemicals   High-resolution metabolomics, microRNA, plasma protein markers and cytokines, benzo(a)pyrene diol epoxide (BPDE) and dioxin/furan measurements, and cotinine levels from a subset of 66 Case-Onset (N=41) and Case-Never (N=25) groups were integrated using xMWAS ( 25 ). The input data was categorized into: 1) the molecular layer, which included 9,092 metabolic features ( m/z  and time), 32 plasma proteins and immune markers, 144 microRNAs; 2) the environmental exposures layer, which included cotinine, benzo(a)pyrene diol epoxide (BPDE), and 3 Principal Components (PC) for the dioxins as previously described in (Khan et al 2019 JOEM Current Issue) (PC1, PC2, and PC3); and 3) the clinical layer, which included the first three principal dimensions from the MCA analysis corresponding to cardiopulmonary disease sub-category. Integrative network analysis was performed using sparse partial least squares regression analysis, a multivariate approach for data integration, to only include associations with |r|>0.3 and p<0.05. The multilevel community detection method ( 26 ) in xMWAS was used for identifying communities of tightly connected clinical outcomes, biomolecules, and environmental exposures. Pathway analysis was performed using Mummichog pathway analysis tool in MetaboAnalyst v4.0.
