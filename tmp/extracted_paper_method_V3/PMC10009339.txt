</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="ab0010"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Tripterygium glycosides tablets (TGT) are the commonly used preparation for rheumatoid arthritis (RA). However, the changes in TGT on RA are still unclear at the metabolic level. This study aimed to reveal the biological processes of TGT in collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) rats through integrated metabolomics and network analysis. First, the CIA model in rats was established, and the CIA rats were given three doses of TGT. Then, the endogenous metabolites in the serum from normal rats, CIA rats, and CIA rats treated with varying doses of TGT were detected by UHPLC-QTOF-MS/MS. Next, univariate and multivariate statistical analyses were performed to find the differential metabolites. Finally, differential metabolites, metabolic pathways, and hub genes were analyzed integrally to reveal the biological processes of TGT in CIA rats. The paw diameter, arthritis score, immunoglobulin G (IgG) concentration, CT image, and histological assay showed that TGT had evident therapeutic effects on CIA rats. Untargeted metabolomics revealed that TGT could ameliorate the down-regulation of lipid levels in CIA rats. Four key differential metabolites were found including LysoP(18:0), LysoPA(20:4), LysoPA(18:2), and PS(O-20:0/17:1). The glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway was perturbed in treating CIA with TGT. A total of 24 genes, including <em>PLD1</em>, <em>LPCAT4</em>, <em>AGPAT1</em>, and <em>PLA2G4A</em>, were found to be the hub genes of TGT in CIA rats. In conclusion, the integrated analysis provided a novel and holistic perspective on the biological processes of TGT in CIA rats, which could give helpful guidance for further TGT on RA. Future studies based on human samples are necessary.</p>

<p id="p0005">Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a common autoimmune disease characterized by chronic synovitis, pannus formation, cartilage erosion, and bone destruction <a href="#bib1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib1">[1]</a>, <a href="#bib2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib2">[2]</a>, <a href="#bib3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib3">[3]</a>. The global prevalence of RA is about 1 % <a href="#bib4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib4">[4]</a>. If the treatment is not timely, patients with RA will suffer not only from irreversible damage, such as joint destruction and deformity, but also from coronary heart disease, myocardial infarction, and other diseases <a href="#bib5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib5">[5]</a>. Currently, the primary treatment of RA is to relieve pain and control the disease's progress through drugs <a href="#bib6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib6">[6]</a>.</p>
<p id="p0010">Tripterygium glycosides tablets (TGT), the principal clinical preparation of <em>Tripterygium wilfordii</em> Hook. F., are frequently prescribed medicines for RA <a href="#bib7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib7">[7]</a>. TGT were recognized by World Health Organization as "a new botanical preparation pioneered in China" for RA and enrolled in the 2019 edition of the Medical Insurance Medicine List <a href="#bib8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib8">[8]</a>, <a href="#bib9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib9">[9]</a>. Clinical research results showed that TGT had anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects <a href="#bib10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib10">[10]</a>, <a href="#bib11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib11">[11]</a>. Pharmacological studies demonstrated that TGT could suppress pannus formation and bone destruction in rat models of RA <a href="#bib12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib12">[12]</a>. Clinical evidence suggested that TGT had better efficacy than conventional synthetic disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs) <a href="#bib13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib13">[13]</a>.</p>
<p id="p0015">Several scholars have conducted numerous meaningful explorations of TGT on RA. Lu et al. demonstrated that TGT could effectively reduce clinical symptoms and inflammatory responses in RA patients, down-regulate platelet activating factors, and improve immune function <a href="#bib14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib14">[14]</a>. In another report, Liu et al. observed that TGT can reduce the proportion of Th17 cells and increase the proportion of Treg cells by down-regulating the expression level of miR-146a to alleviate the condition of RA patients <a href="#bib15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib15">[15]</a>. Moreover, Wang et al. proved that TGT could inhibit the angiogenesis of synovial tissue in CIA rats and the tube formation of human umbilical vein endothelial cells by regulating the HIF1α/Ang1 signal axis <a href="#bib16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib16">[16]</a>. Furthermore, <em>PLA2G10</em>, <em>PTGES</em>, <em>PLA2G1B</em>, <em>MX1</em>, <em>OASL</em>, <em>SPINK1</em>, <em>CRK</em>, <em>GRAPL</em>, and <em>RNF2</em> were found as targets of TGT for RA therapeutic intervention <a href="#bib17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib17">[17]</a>, <a href="#bib18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib18">[18]</a>. As products of cellular regulatory processes, metabolites are considered terminal indicators of interfering responses to drugs, toxins, or diseases <a href="#bib19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib19">[19]</a>. However, it is still unclear which metabolites are associated with the efficacy of TGT on RA and how TGT modulate these metabolites to exert therapeutic effects.</p>
<p id="p0020">Metabolomics is a discipline developed after genomics and proteomics, which is mainly used to study the changes in the type and quantity of endogenous small molecules in organisms <a href="#bib20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib20">[20]</a>, <a href="#bib21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib21">[21]</a>. In recent years, metabolomics has played an important role in capturing the pathways and potential biomarkers associated with the disease process <a href="#bib22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib22">[22]</a>, <a href="#bib23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib23">[23]</a>. Moreover, metabolomics can also be used to reveal the mechanism of drugs by discovering drug biomarkers and metabolic pathways <a href="#bib24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib24">[24]</a>, <a href="#bib25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib25">[25]</a>. Network analysis can help us to find the relationship between endogenous metabolites and targets from a large amount of omics data <a href="#bib26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib26">[26]</a>. The application of integrated network analysis and metabolomics can provide a novel perspective on the mechanism of drug treatment of diseases.</p>
<p id="p0025">In this study, for the first time, changes in endogenous metabolites in the serum of CIA rats were investigated after different doses of TGT administration. An integrated approach was used to illustrate the key differential metabolites, critical metabolic pathways, and hub genes of TGT in CIA rats. The flowchart of the integrated strategy was showed in <a href="#fig0005" class="usa-link">Fig. 1</a>. The study not only contributes to the efficacy and mechanism of TGT in CIA rats but also provides a holistic view of the biological processes of TGT in CIA rats. Further research utilizing human samples is required to investigate the metabolic changes in human RA following TGT administration.</p>

<section id="sec0015"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1. Reagents</h3>
<p id="p0030">HPLC-grade acetonitrile was purchased from Tedia Company (Fairfield, OH, USA). Formic acid of HPLC-MS grade was obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific Ltd. (New York, USA). Purified water was obtained from Wahaha Group Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China). Tripterygium Glycosides Tablets (Batch NO.2005109B) were purchased from Zhejiang DND PHARMACEUTICAL Co., Ltd. (Zhejiang, China), which has good batch-to-batch consistency <a href="#bib27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib27">[27]</a>. Bovine type II collagen was purchased from Chondrex (Washington, USA). Complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA) was obtained from Sigma (St. Louis, Missouri, USA). Sodium carboxymethylcellulose was provided by Tianjin Yongda Reagent Co., Ltd. (Tianjin, China). Immunoglobulin G (IgG) ELISA kits were purchased from MultiSciences (Hangzhou, China).</p></section><section id="sec0020"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2. Animal and experimental design</h3>
<p id="p0035">Male Sprague-Dawley rats (5–6 weeks old) were supplied by Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). All rats had housed in ventilated cages individually with a dark/light cycle for 12 h at 22 ± 1.5 °C and 50 ± 10 % humidity, giving them fresh food and water. The rats were acclimatized for one week and fasted for 12 h but were supplied with water before the experiments. Rats were first intradermally injected with bovine type II collagen emulsion (mixed with the CFA in equal ratios) at the back (0.05 mL), the base of the tail (0.05 mL), and right plantar (0.1 mL) on day 0. Rats were challenged with the same dose of the emulsion (bovine type II collagen and CFA were emulsified in a 1:2 ratio) at the back, base of the tail, and left plantar on the 14th day, and the control group was injected with sterile saline in the same manner. On day 21, the CIA rats were divided into four groups: CIA group, TGT-L group, TGT-M group, and TGT-H group. TGT were crushed into powder, and the appropriate amount of TGT was weighed and suspended in 0.3 % CMC-Na before administration. TGT-L group, TGT-M group, and TGT-H group were treated with low (9.45 mg/kg), medium (94.5 mg/kg), and high doses (189.0 mg/kg) of TGT by intragastric gavage from day 21 to day 42, respectively. In contrast, the control and CIA groups were given an equal volume of 0.3 % CMC-Na. Paw diameter and arthritis score were evaluated twice a week from day 14 until the rats were sacrificed. The paw diameters were measured by an electronic vernier caliper to evaluate the swelling of the paw in rats. The arthritis score of each rat was the sum of the scores of four limbs. The clinical score of each limb was evaluated as follows: 1 point was defined for each inflamed toe or knuckle; 5 points were given for an affected ankle (in total 15 points per limb, 60 points per rat). Statistical significance analysis was performed by one-way ANOVA using GraphPad Prism 8.0. All the animal experiments were conducted following the Guidelines for the Use of Laboratory Animals (National Research Council), and the experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee, Hebei Medical University, China.</p></section><section id="sec0025"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.3. Serum samples collection and IgG analysis</h3>
<p id="p0040">Blood samples were obtained from the orbital venous plexus before the first administration and the abdominal aorta 24 h after the last administration, respectively. Blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min to get the serum. The levels of IgG in serum were measured by an ELISA Kit. The rest of the serum samples were stored at - 80 °C until analysis.</p></section><section id="sec0030"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4. Histological and micro-CT analyses</h3>
<p id="p0045">The ankle joints were fixed in 4 % neutral paraformaldehyde, and then the joints were incubated in EDTA solution at room temperature for decalcification. Microtome slices of 5 µm were prepared for histological examination after paraffinization. The ankle joint sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin, respectively, and then the images were captured by microscope. Micro-computed tomography (Micro-CT) of the hindfoot was performed on an IVIS spectrum CT system (PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA).</p></section><section id="sec0035"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.5. Serum samples preparation for metabolic profiling</h3>
<p id="p0050">Serum samples, used for metabolomic analysis, were obtained 24 h after the last administration. 150 μL of serum was precipitated by 450 μL of methanol. The mixture was centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 10 min after being vortexed for 3 min. The supernatant was separated and evaporated to dryness under a stream of nitrogen. Before analysis, the residue was reconstituted with 100 μL of mobile phase A-B (8:2, V/V) and then centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 10 min. Finally, the supernatant was injected into the UHPLC-QTOF-MS system for analysis. Quality control (QC) samples were obtained by pooling 30 μL of each serum and then prepared as serum samples.</p></section><section id="sec0040"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.6. UHPLC-MS/MS conditions</h3>
<p id="p0055">The UHPLC-QTOF-MS consisted of a Prominence™ UHPLC System (Shimadzu, Japan) and an AB SCIEX Triple TOF™ 5600 + system equipped with Duo-Spray™ ion sources in the electrospray ionization (ESI) technology. A Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.8 µm, Waters Corporation, Milford, UK) was applied for chromatographic separation. Mobile phase A was water with 0.1 % formic acid. Mobile phase B consisted of 90 % acetonitrile and 10 % isopropanol with 0.1 % formic acid. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–1 min, 15 % B; 1-15min, 15-100 % B; 15−20 min, 100 % B; 21 min, stop. 10 % isopropanol was added to the organic phase to increase the elution of weakly polar compounds. The method is suitable for the analysis of polar and weakly polar compounds. A post time of 5 min was then used to give the column time to equilibrate in between injections. The injection volume was 5 μL.</p>
<p id="p0060">The mass data were collected separately in positive and negative ion modes. Information-dependent acquisition (IDA) function and dynamic background subtraction were used during data acquisition. The most intensive 8 precursors per cycle were selected to generate high resolution and high mass accuracy MS/MS spectra. The mass ranges were set at 100–1000 Da for TOF MS scans and 50–1000 Da for TOF MS/MS scans. The declustering potential (DP), ion spray voltage, and collision energy (CE) were set at 50 V, 5500 V, and, 35 eV, respectively, while at −50 V, −4500 V, and −35 eV in negative ion mode, respectively. Turbo spray temperature, collision energy spread (CES), curtain gas (CUR), nebulizer gas (GS1), and heater gas (GS2) were set at 600 °C, 15 eV, 30 psi, 50 psi, and 50 psi, respectively. Online automatic mass calibration was performed every 6 consecutive samples using the Calibrant Delivery System (CDS) with APCI-TOF Calibration Solution (AB SCIEX, USA).</p></section><section id="sec0045"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.7. Serum metabolomics assay</h3>
<p id="p0065">UHPLC-QTOF-MS methods were used for serum untargeted metabolomics research. System suitability was determined by 6–7 replicate injections of the same QC sample. A random sequence was used for sample analysis, and a QC sample was injected every 5–6 consecutive samples. The raw data obtained from the UHPLC-QTOF-MS/MS system were imported to Progenesis QI 2.2 (Waters, Milford, Massachusetts, USA) for peak detection, deconvolution, alignment, and normalization. Before statistical analysis, the coefficient of variance (CV) of QC (CV ≤ 30 %) and the "80 % rule" were performed for data cleaning. The processed and cleaned data were imported into SIMCA 14.1 software (Umetrics, Sweden) for multivariate data analysis (PCA, OPLS-DA). Combined with the univariate statistical analyses, including student t-test and fold change (FC), the compounds with the variable importance in projection (VIP)&gt;1.0, FC ≥1.2 or ≤0.8, and P &lt; 0.05 were selected to be differential metabolites. The isotope similarity, adducts, and MS/MS fragments information of the compounds in the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB) (<a href="http://www.hmdb.ca/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://www.hmdb.<em>ca</em>/</a>) and LIPID MAPS (<a href="https://lipidmaps.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://lipidmaps.org/</a>) were compared with the differential metabolites, and the compound with the highest matching rate was chosen as the identification result. The allowable range of matched mass deviations was 10 ppm. The relative abundance and Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis of key differential metabolites were performed by the GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. The online software MetaboAnalyst 5.0 (<a href="http://www.metaboanalyst.ca/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://www.metaboanalyst.<em>ca</em>/</a>) was used to analyze the pathways for differential metabolites. The online database STRING (<a href="https://cn.string-db.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://cn.string-db.org/</a>) was used to construct the gene-gene interaction network. Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1) was used for screening hub genes and visualization of the metabolite-enzyme-reaction-gene network.</p></section></section><section id="sec0050"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">3. Results</h2>
<section id="sec0055"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.1. The therapeutic efficacy of TGT</h3>
<p id="p0070">Paw diameter, arthritis score, serum IgG concentration, micro-CT, and histological analysis of ankle joints were conducted during the studies to investigate whether the TGT can relieve symptoms of CIA rats. Symptoms of CIA are erythema and swelling in the toes, paws, and ankle joints, as well as bone erosion caused by synovial cavity inflammation. It can be seen in <a href="#fig0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a> that paw swelling and arthritis score in the CIA group and TGT groups increased noticeably after 14 days of primary immunization, indicating arthritis appeared. After TGT treatment, the paw swelling and arthritis score in the medium and high dose groups were alleviated gradually. At the end of the experiment, there was a significant decrease (P &lt; 0.001, P &lt; 0.05, P &lt; 0.01) in paw diameter, arthritis scores, and serum IgG concentration in the TGT-H group compared with the CIA group (<a href="#fig0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a>). As shown in <a href="#fig0015" class="usa-link">Fig. 3</a>, the photograph and micro-CT of the inflamed hind foot on day 42 showed serious symptoms in the CIA group. Swelling, erythema, and bone erosion of the ankle, plantar, hind paw, and toes appeared in CIA rats. After 21 days of treatment, the TGT-L group had shown a slight improvement in swelling; the TGT-M group demonstrated apparent inhibition of bone erosion in toes and a significant reduction of swelling and erythema in plantar, hind paws, and toes. In contrast, the TGT-H group was closer to the control group, demonstrating remarkable improvement in swelling, erythema, and bone erosion in the whole hind foot. In addition, histological analysis of ankle joints (<a href="#fig0020" class="usa-link">Fig. 4</a>) indicated severe bone damage, and synovia hyperplasia accompanied by pannus in CIA rats. In the TGT-L group, no obvious improvement was observed. In the TGT-M and TGT-H groups, bone damage and synovia hyperplasia reduction could be seen compared with the CIA group.</p>

<p id="p0075">The system stability was evaluated by Pearson correlation analysis between the same QC samples. The results in <a href="#fig0025" class="usa-link">Fig. 5</a> showed that the LC-MS system was stable in positive and negative ion modes. After data processing and cleaning, the PCA model was used to evaluate the quality of data. As shown in <a href="#fig0030" class="usa-link">Fig. 6</a>A, the QC samples showed good aggregation in the PCA score graph, indicating that the pre-processing and experimental conditions of the samples were stable, and the obtained data were accurate and reliable. It can also be seen from the PCA score graph that the metabolic profiles of TGT groups were closer to the control group compared CIA group, which suggested the metabolic profiles of TGT groups had an altering the rend to the control group. The red line in the DModX plot (<a href="#fig0030" class="usa-link">Fig. 6</a>B) represents the mean standard deviation (SD) of the samples. The fact that none of the samples (including QC samples) in the figure exceeded 2 SD indicates that our current metabolomics data were stable and reproducible.</p>

Red denotes a rise in compound concentration, whereas blue denotes a decrease in compound concentration.</p>

Key differential metabolites study</h3>
<p id="p0090">Differential metabolites that were significantly altered in the CIA group (<em>versus</em> the control group) and recovered with increasing TGT dose after administration of TGT were identified as key differential metabolites of TGT in CIA rats. Therefore, four key differential metabolites (LysoPC(18:0), LysoPA(20:4), PS(O-20:0/17:1), and LysoPA(18:2)) were screened out. Their relative abundance was down-regulated in the CIA group (<em>versus</em> the control group) and was up-regulated after administration of TGT in a dose-dependent manner. FC values of LysoPC(18:0) in TGT-L, TGT-M, and TGT-H groups (<em>versus</em> CIA group) were 1.39, 1.46, and 1.53, respectively. FC values of LysoPA(20:4) in TGT-L, TGT-M, and TGT-H groups (<em>versus</em> CIA group) were 2.14, 2.59, and 3.11, respectively. FC values of PS(O-20:0/17:1) in TGT-L, TGT-M, and TGT-H groups (<em>versus</em> CIA group) were 1.49, 1.71, and 1.86, respectively. FC values of LysoPA(18:2) in TGT-L, TGT-M, and TGT-H groups (<em>versus</em> CIA group) were 2.35, 3.93, and 4.65, respectively. The relative abundance of four key differential metabolites in the five experimental groups was shown in <a href="#fig0045" class="usa-link">Fig. 9</a>B.</p>
<p id="p0095">The area under the ROC curve (AUC) of the key differential metabolites can represent the classification ability between the two groups. To further evaluate the between-group differences of key differential metabolites, ROC curve analyses were performed on four key differential metabolites (<a href="#fig0050" class="usa-link">Fig. 10</a>). The AUC of LysoPC(18:0), LysoPA(20:4), and LysoPA(18:2) increased with the dose, while the AUC of PS(O-20:0/17:1) were both 0.8438 between CIA group and TGT-M group, CIA group, and TGT-H group. The area under the ROC curve for the four biomarkers had a maximum of 1 and a minimum of 0.7968, which proved that the metabolites differed greatly between the CIA group and TGT groups.</p>

<p id="p0100">The 23 differential metabolites were imported into the MetaboAnalyst online database for pathway analysis, and it was discovered that seven metabolic pathways were linked to the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis by TGT, with the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway (raw P = 2.39E-12, impact = 0.4504) having the greatest influence (<a href="#fig0055" class="usa-link">Fig. 11</a>A). <a href="#fig0055" class="usa-link">Fig. 11</a>B depicted the link between the various metabolites in the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway. The metabolites marked in red were the differential metabolites identified in this study. The glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway was implicated by the metabolites LysoPC(22:6), LysoPE(16:0), LysoPA(20:4), PS(O-20:0/17:1), PE(P-18:0/0/:0), PC(17:2/2:0), and LysoPA(18:2), which were all down-regulated in the CIA group and up-regulated after taking TGT.</p>

<p id="p0105">Metascape is a plugin within the Cytoscape software that enables annotation analysis of metabolite-related genes. The 23 differential metabolites were imported into Cytoscape software and analyzed by Metascape. A total of 73 genes (<a href="#tbl0015" class="usa-link">Table 3</a>) of TGT for the treatment of CIA were obtained. These genes were imported into the STRING online database for the construction of the gene-gene interaction network. The degree value as the screening criterion, the genes with a degree value greater than 40 were regarded as hub genes, and a total of 24 hub genes were obtained (<a href="#fig0055" class="usa-link">Fig. 11</a>C).</p>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="table/tbl0015/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div></section></section><section id="sec0085"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.7. Network analysis</h3>
<p id="p0110">To further demonstrate the regulatory effect of TGT in CIA rats, the key differential metabolites and hub genes involved in the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway were imported into Metascape to construct a metabolite-enzyme-reaction-gene network (<a href="#fig0060" class="usa-link">Fig. 12</a>). It can be seen from the network how the key differential metabolites were regulated by hub genes in the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway. The chief regulators of 1-acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (LysoPC(18:0) were <em>PLA2G6</em> and <em>PISD</em>. The hub genes involved in controlling phosphotidate (LysoPA(18:2)) were <em>PLD1</em>, <em>PLD2</em>, <em>AGPAT3</em>, and <em>CDS2</em>. The main regulators of phosphatidylserine (PS(O-20:0/17:1)) were <em>PLA2G6</em>, <em>PLD1,</em> and <em>PLD2</em>. <em>AGPAT1</em>, <em>AGPAT2</em>, <em>AGPAT4</em>, <em>AGPAT5</em>, and <em>AGPAT3</em> were primarily responsible for controlling 1-acyl-sn-glycerol 3-phosphate (LysoPA(20:4)).</p>

<p id="p0115">In this study, CIA rats were used to explore the mechanism of TGT in CIA rats, and three different doses of TGT were used. Compared with normal rats given TGT, the study of the TGT mechanism based on CIA rats is closer to the clinical application of TGT. The efficacy indicators (paw diameter, arthritis score, IgG concentration, CT image, and histological assay) showed that the therapeutic impact of TGT on CIA was improving with increasing TGT dosage. In the search for the differential metabolites of TGT on CIA, we selected the differential metabolites existing in the TGT-L, TGT-M, and TGT-H groups (<em>versus</em> the CIA group). Compared with a single dose, the set of three different TGT doses can increase the accuracy of differential metabolites.</p>
<p id="p0120">Four key differential metabolites of TGT on CIA were found: LysoPC(18:0), LysoPA(20:4), LysoPA(18:2), and PS(O-20:0/17:1). The levels of four metabolites increased in a dose-dependent manner following TGT administration. In the CIA group, the levels of the four metabolites were significantly reduced, which is consistent with the previous results of Liu et al. <a href="#bib30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib30">[30]</a>. Among the 4 key differential metabolites, the AUC of LysoPC(18:0) between the CIA group and TGT-H group was 1, indicating that LysoPC(18:0) had a good ability to evaluate the efficacy of TGT on CIA, and it was expected to be a potential biomarker of TGT on CIA. Moreover, LysoPC (18:0) was recognized as a biomarker for the diagnosis of RA in the study by Yu et al. <a href="#bib31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib31">[31]</a>. In that study, the relative abundance of LysoPC(18:0) in the feces of RA patients and healthy controls were determined, and the relative abundance of LysoPC(18:0) was up-regulated in the RA group. Therefore, we speculated that the excretion of LysoPC(18:0) would be accelerated after patients suffered from RA, resulting in a decrease in the level of LysoPC(18:0) in patients with RA. However, further experiments are needed to confirm whether the increase of LysoPC(18:0) in the serum of rats after taking TGT is because TGT inhibits the excretion of LysoPC(18:0) or increases the synthesis of LysoPC(18:0) in serum.</p>
<p id="p0125">In addition, it is notable that in TGT-M and TGT-H groups, the levels of key differential metabolites in some samples were higher than those in the normal group, indicating that TGT may produce toxic side effects with increasing doses in the treatment of CIA. Studies have shown that TGT had the risk of ineffective treatment and the risk of side effects due to individual differences in the treatment of RA <a href="#bib8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib8">[8]</a>. Therefore, in addition to characterizing the efficacy of TGT on RA, these key differential metabolites can also be monitored for their levels to determine whether TGT produce toxicity in treating RA. Lipid accumulation plays a role in the initiation of inflammatory responses, and lipid metabolism can regulate immune cell function <a href="#bib32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib32">[32]</a>, <a href="#bib33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib33">[33]</a>. As a result, we infer that the efficacy and toxicity of TGT on RA are both related to the anti-inflammatory and anti-immune effects of TGT, which is consistent with the findings of Lin et al.<a href="#bib34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib34">[34]</a>. In conclusion, it suggests that we should pay attention to monitoring the blood concentration of TGT and the levels of related differential metabolites when treating RA with TGT.</p>
<p id="p0130">The glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway was found to be the main metabolic pathway involved in the therapeutic effect of TGT in CIA rats. In addition to forming cell membranes, glycerophospholipid also participates in signal recognition and transduction. The glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway is a key metabolic pathway in systemic immune and inflammatory <a href="#bib35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib35">[35]</a>. Previous studies on the mechanism of action of Huang-Lian-Jie-Du-Tang, Qiwei Tongbi oral liquid, <em>Saposhnikovia divaricata</em> decoction, and geniposide in arthritis rats found that the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway also showed a significant correlation <a href="#bib36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib36">[36]</a>, <a href="#bib37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib37">[37]</a>, <a href="#bib38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib38">[38]</a>, <a href="#bib39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib39">[39]</a>. Metabolites involved in the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway in this study, such as LysoPC(18:0), LysoPC(22:6), LysoPE(18:0), and PS(O-20:0/17:1), were down-regulated in CIA group and up-regulated after taking TGT, which suggested that the excretion of lipids would be accelerated after suffering from CIA <a href="#bib36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib36">[36]</a>, and TGT can reverse this process. In addition, Su et al. demonstrated that the regulation of RA by glycerophospholipid metabolism was related to the IL-6/JAK signaling pathway <a href="#bib40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib40">[40]</a>. Therefore, the therapeutic effect of TGT on RA may be through down-regulated IL-6 secretion and p-JAK2 expression.</p>
<p id="p0135">In this work, 73 genes, including 24 hub genes, were obtained as related to TGT treatment for CIA. <em>PLD1</em> and <em>PLD2</em>, two isoforms of phospholipase D, have been identified in mammalian cells <a href="#bib41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib41">[41]</a>. Studies had shown that proinflammatory factor secretion would decrease when both <em>PLD</em> isoforms were inhibited together <a href="#bib42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib42">[42]</a>. However, according to Kang et al., <em>PLD1</em>, but not <em>PLD2</em>, was selectively upregulated in IL-1β-stimulated synoviocytes, as well as synovium, from RA patients <a href="#bib43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib43">[43]</a>. In addition, studies have shown that <em>PLD1</em> knockdown can inhibit osteoclast formation and bone resorption in CIA rats and reduce the metastasis and inflammation of fibroblast synovial cells in RA <a href="#bib44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib44">[44]</a>, <a href="#bib45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib45">[45]</a>. Hypomethylation of the promoter region of the <em>AGPAT1</em> is implicated in inflammation and autoimmunity, whereas DMARD treatment induced hypermethylation of the same region <a href="#bib46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib46">[46]</a>. Singh et al. found that <em>PLA2G4A</em> was the significant protein of RA through drug-target interaction <a href="#bib47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib47">[47]</a>, <a href="#bib48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib48">[48]</a>. Similarly, in our study, <em>PLA2G4A</em> was found to be the hub gene of TGT on RA. Sommerfelt et al. demonstrated that decreased <em>PLA2G4A</em> gene expression could significantly reduce the cellular release of arachidonic acid (AA), prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), interleukin 8 (IL-8), and stromelysin-1 (MMP3) <a href="#bib49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib49">[49]</a>. <em>PLA2G5</em> was required for phospholipid hydrolysis and release of IL-1β and IL-18 <a href="#bib50" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib50">[50]</a>. <em>PLA2G10</em> and <em>PLA2G1B</em> are the two isoforms of phospholipase A(2). In our previous studies, we found that 14-oxo-19 - (4 → 3) abeo-abieta-3,8,12-tetraen-19,18-olide, 7-oxo-18 (4 → 3) abeo-abieta-3,8,11,13-tetraen-18-oic acid, hypoglaucide, triptotriterpenic acid A, wilforol F, wilforlide B, triptoquinone B and wilforlide A in TGT exerted therapeutic effects on RA through these two genes <a href="#bib17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib17">[17]</a>. Furthermore, genes such as <em>LPCAT4</em>, <em>CHPT1, AGTAT3, PLA2G15</em>, <em>PEMT</em>, <em>PLA2G6</em>, <em>PISD</em>, <em>PLA2G2F</em>, <em>CHPT1</em>, and <em>PLA2G2E</em> were also found to be associated with TGT on RA in our study. However, the role of these genes in RA has not yet been reported in the literature.</p>
<p id="p0140">The instructions for TGT recommend a daily dose of 1–1.5 mg/kg. According to the human-rat dose conversion formula in <em>Experimental Methodology of Pharmacology</em>
<a href="#bib51" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib51">[51]</a>, the equivalent dose for rats is 6.3–9.45 mg/kg daily. Our pre-experiment found that the dose of 4.73 mg/kg per day (half of 9.45 mg/kg) for 21 days did not show significant efficacy. So 9.45 mg/kg per day was used as the low dose in our study.</p>
<p id="p0145">Untargeted metabolomics research can be carried out using both internal standard and non-internal standard methods. Multiple isotopic internal standards are often needed in HPLC-MS/MS untargeted metabolomics due to the large amount of endogenous material in organisms. But isotopic internal standards are not readily available. Therefore, the non-internal standards method was used in the present study and the results of QC samples demonstrated that the analysis was stable and reproducible.</p>
<p id="p0150">This study provided a holistic perspective of the biological process of TGT in CIA rats, which could give helpful guidance for further TGT on RA. However, the research conducted in the CIA rat model did not fully represent human RA. Partial differences have been found in the immune response and pathological mechanisms between rat CIA and human RA <a href="#bib52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib52">[52]</a>, <a href="#bib53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib53">[53]</a>. Therefore, the results based on CIA rats in the present study cannot fully reflect the metabolic changes in human RA-taking TGT. Future studies on human samples are warranted. In addition, the potential use of other new "omics" approaches should be considered <a href="#bib54" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib54">[54]</a>.</p></section><section id="sec0095"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">5. Conclusion</h2>
<p id="p0155">Overall, our data suggested that TGT had a favorable therapeutic effect on CIA rats. This study demonstrated the alteration of metabolites after TGT administration in CIA rats for the first time. Untargeted metabolomics showed that the key differential metabolites of TGT on RA were LysoPC(18:0), LysoPA(20:4), PS(O-20:0/ 17:1), and LysoPA(18:2). Moreover, TGT had the impact of ameliorating the RA-related down-regulation of lipid levels in the body. Through pathway analysis and network analysis, it was discovered that the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway and hub genes such as <em>PLD1</em>, <em>LPCAT4</em>, <em>AGPAT1,</em> and <em>PLA2G4A</em> were connected to the mechanism of TGT on RA. Our work offered a fresh perspective and understanding of the use of TGT on RA.</p></section><section id="sec0100"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">CRediT authorship contribution statement</h2>
