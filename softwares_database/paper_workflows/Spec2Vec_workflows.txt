related_tool: Spec2Vec
sample_description: The study utilized two primary spectral datasets: (1) a million-scale in-silico electron ionization mass spectrometry (EI-MS) library, generated as described by Yang et al. and used for training and fine-tuning; from this, a subset of 50,000 spectra was randomly selected for the Tanimoto similarity-guided fine-tuning stage due to computational constraints in pairwise Tanimoto score calculation; and (2) the NIST 2023 Tandem Mass Spectral Library (mainlib), from which query spectra were selected exclusively for the held-out test set — ensuring complete separation from the in-silico training data. All spectra underwent peak filtering to retain only the top 30 most intense peaks per spectrum, based on intensity-based selection to enrich structurally informative signals while removing low-intensity background noise. The textualization pipeline converted each filtered spectrum into a structured text format with explicit semantic labels: 'base_peak' (peak with maximum intensity), 'max_peak' (highest m/z among top 30), 'key_peaks' (top 3 most intense peaks excluding base_peak), and 'extra_peaks' (remaining peaks among the top 30). This textual representation served as input to the LLM-based models.
complete_workflow_description: The research employed a multi-stage software-driven pipeline to develop and evaluate the LLM4MS model: (1) Mass spectra preprocessing and textualization: Raw spectra from the in-silico EI-MS library were filtered to retain only the top k=30 most intense peaks; each spectrum was then converted into a structured textual format with labeled peak types ('base_peak', 'max_peak', 'key_peaks', 'extra_peaks') to encode domain-specific spectral semantics. (2) Model development: Starting from the publicly available Llama 3.1-8B (8-billion-parameter) base LLM, the pipeline applied unsupervised pre-adaptation using the LLM2Vec framework — specifically Masked Next Token Prediction (MNTP) and Simple Contrastive Learning of Sentence Embeddings (SimCSE) — on general-domain corpora (e.g., English Wikipedia) to produce the intermediate LLM2Vec model. This was followed by domain-specific contrastive fine-tuning on the 50,000 textualized spectra, guided by pairwise Tanimoto similarity scores computed from the original spectral data: for each anchor spectrum, the highest-Tanimoto-scoring spectrum was treated as the positive pair, and others as negative pairs, optimizing embeddings via a contrastive learning objective. (3) Similarity computation: After embedding generation using LLM4MS, cosine similarity was computed between n=4096-dimensional embedding vectors A and B as S_C(A,B) = (A ⋅ B) / (∥A∥ ∥B∥), where ∥·∥ denotes the Euclidean (L2) norm; this metric was chosen for its invariance to vector magnitude and established efficacy in semantic similarity tasks. (4) Baseline comparison: GPT-4o embeddings were obtained via its official API using identical textualized spectra; performance was evaluated using Recall@1 on the NIST23 test set. (5) Hardware and environment: All training and fine-tuning procedures were conducted on a Linux machine equipped with an AMD 128-core CPU (2 GHz/core), 1 TB RAM, and one RTX A6000 GPU (48 GB VRAM); the final LLM4MS model and GUI software are publicly available on Zenodo under a noncommercial use license.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: Spec2Vec
sample_description: The study used a curated collection of over 71,000 unique compounds with merged-energy positive-ion mode [M+H]+ MS/MS spectra sourced from four major repositories: NIST2023, Agilent-METLIN, GNPS2, and MSDIAL. Compounds were grouped by the first 14 characters of their InChIKey to resolve isomeric variants, and spectra from multiple sources per compound were prioritized by curation quality (NIST2023 > Agilent-METLIN > GNPS2 > MSDIAL). Spectra were merged across collision energies after binning at 0.01 Da resolution, m/z alignment, and intensity averaging; intensities were scaled to 0–100. The final benchmarking data included three independent test sets: (i) the main curated database (~71,561 compounds), (ii) CASMI2022 (real experimental mzML files processed in R using Spectra v1.18.0 to extract top-5 highest-intensity MS/MS scans per compound within ±10 s RT and ±20 ppm precursor m/z windows, then normalized and exported to MGF), and (iii) two fully open benchmarks — MassSpecGym (downloaded from Polaris Hub) and Spectraverse v1.0.1 (from Zenodo), both filtered to [M+H]+ mode, binned at 0.01 Da, intensity-normalized to 0–100, and stripped of any spectra matching CASMI2022 InChIKey prefixes.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with acquisition and curation of ~71,561 [M+H]+ MS/MS spectra from NIST2023, Agilent-METLIN, GNPS2, and MSDIAL, followed by InChIKey-based deduplication (14-character prefix) and hierarchical source prioritization. Spectra acquired at multiple collision energies per compound are merged by binning at 0.01 Da resolution, m/z alignment, and mean intensity aggregation; intensities are scaled to 0–100. CASMI2022 raw mzML files are processed in R v4.5.0 using Spectra v1.18.0 to extract up to five highest-intensity MS/MS scans per compound within ±10 s retention time and ±20 ppm precursor m/z windows, then merged and normalized. MassSpecGym and Spectraverse v1.0.1 are downloaded, filtered to [M+H]+ mode, binned at 0.01 Da, intensity-normalized, and CASMI2022-contaminant spectra removed. All datasets are restricted to 0–700 m/z range. Multiple featurization strategies—including fixed binning (70,000-D binary vectors), adaptive/quantile binning (927 or 3621 bins), frequent-peaks selection, spectrum hashing, and learned embeddings (Spec2Vec, MS2DeepScore, DreaMS, SpecEmbedding)—are applied to generate input vectors. Each representation is fed into a PyTorch v2.3.0a0 feed-forward neural network (architecture: N → 2048 → 1024 → 300, ReLU activations, 25% dropout) trained to predict 300-D Mol2Vec embeddings using MSE loss and Adam optimizer (lr = 2e−4), with early stopping (10 epochs, ΔMSE < 0.01). Model evaluation uses 5-fold cross-validation (scikit-learn v1.5.2) and Top-N retrieval accuracy against a 0.6-million-compound structure database at 0.1/3/10 ppm mass tolerances, benchmarked against a null random-ranking baseline.
source_paper: Roger et al. (2026), Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry, DOI: 10.1021/jasms.5c00428, PMID: 42298320

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: Spec2Vec
sample_description: Four experimental case studies were analyzed: (1) a human urine sample, (2) two human blood plasma samples (one from the LTR cohort and one from the NIST reference material), and (3) an anammox bacterial sample set. The urine and plasma samples were collected from human subjects and processed using LC-MS/MS assays optimized for metabolite or lipid profiling; the anammox bacterial sample was derived from cultured anammox bacteria. Spectra were generated in positive ionization mode, and for case studies 1–3, consensus spectra were generated via MSCluster on GNPS; for case study 4 (anammox bacteria), raw data were converted to mzML using ProteoWizard, followed by LC-MS feature detection using XCMS (findChromPeaks function) to generate an MS2 spectral MGF file. A total of 139 spectra from the NIST plasma sample were analyzed with MS2Query, and 50 features were tentatively validated in the anammox sample.
complete_workflow_description: The global workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data acquisition across four case studies (urine, two plasma samples, anammox bacteria). For case studies 1–3, raw spectra were uploaded to GNPS and processed using MSCluster to generate consensus spectra; for case study 4, raw files were first converted to mzML using ProteoWizard, then subjected to feature detection using XCMS (findChromPeaks function) to extract LC-MS features and generate an MS2 spectral MGF file. All resulting spectral files (consensus spectra or feature-derived MGF) were input to MS2Query, which leverages Spec2Vec and MS2Deepscore for spectral embedding and similarity computation, combines these with precursor mass difference information, and applies a trained random forest model to produce ranked analogue and exact match predictions. Predictions with a random forest score ≥ 0.633 were retained. Manual validation was performed using retention time, MS1 mass accuracy, MS2 spectral matching against online libraries (e.g., GNPS, MassBank), and comparison to in-house reference standards where available. Parallel analogue searches were conducted using GNPS (for case studies 1–3) and FBMN (for case study 4) for contextual comparison, though MS2Query was the primary tool under evaluation.
source_paper: F et al. (2023), Nature communications, DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-37446-4, PMID: 36990978

complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end computational workflow for cross-ionization mode molecular networking: (1) Tandem mass spectra (MS2) from untargeted metabolomics experiments—acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes—are preprocessed (e.g., peak filtering, intensity transformation); (2) Spectra are embedded using the MS2DeepScore 2.0 model, which takes as input binned fragment m/z values, square-root-transformed intensities, precursor m/z, and ionization mode; (3) Pairwise chemical similarity scores are computed via the trained Siamese neural network; (4) Molecular networks are constructed using matchms to generate GraphML files, applying a minimum MS2DeepScore threshold of 0.85, 'top-n' = 20, and mutual linking logic (i.e., edges retained only if each spectrum ranks the other within its top 20 similarities); (5) Networks are visualized in Cytoscape; (6) Embeddings are projected using UMAP for interactive exploration; (7) Annotations are performed manually by experts, supported by reference libraries and confidence assessment. The workflow is integrated into MZmine for seamless feature detection → MS2DeepScore networking → statistical analysis.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: Spec2Vec
sample_description: Bacterial supernatants were collected from pure bacterial isolates cultured in Mueller Hinton Broth for 24 h; cells were harvested by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, and supernatants were filtered through 0.22-μm nylon filters. Three biological replicates were prepared, each analyzed in three technical replicates, yielding methanolic extracts analyzed via UHPLC-qTOF-MS.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with culturing bacterial isolates in Mueller Hinton Broth (24 h), harvesting supernatants via centrifugation (5000 rpm, 15 min, 4°C), filtering through 0.22-μm nylon filters, and preparing methanolic extracts. Samples (n = 3 biological × 3 technical replicates) are analyzed by Shimadzu LC-q-TOF-MS (9030) using a Shim-pack Velox C18 column with a 30-min gradient (water/methanol + 0.1% formic acid), positive ESI mode, MS1/MS2 acquisition in DDA mode (m/z 100–2000, intensity threshold >1000), collision energy 30 eV ±5, argon CID, and NaI calibration. Raw data is converted to mzML format, then uploaded to GNPS (gnps.ucsd.edu) for molecular networking: precursor ions within ±17 Da are filtered out; MS-CLUSTER is applied with precursor mass tolerance 0.02 Da and fragment mass tolerance 0.02 Da; edges require ≥6 matching peaks and cosine score ≥0.7; only TopK=10 reciprocal similarities retained, max 100 nodes per family; annotations follow MSI Level 2 using accurate mass, MS2 fragmentation, literature, and dereplication databases (e.g., KNApSAcK). Network metrics are evaluated using both classical molecular networking and Spec2vec. Statistical optimization of DDA parameters is performed using central composite design (CCD) in Design-Expert, with 10 experimental runs (including duplicates), model significance assessed at p < 0.005, and response surface analysis to maximize metabolite coverage. Visualization includes Cytoscape 3.10.1 for networks and online Bioinformatics.com.cn for Upset plots and Venn diagrams.
source_paper: Adivhaho et al. (2025), International journal of microbiology, DOI: 10.1155/ijm/4388417, PMID: 40727305
