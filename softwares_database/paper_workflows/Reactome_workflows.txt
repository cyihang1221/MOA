related_tool: Reactome
sample_description: A total of 49 colorectal cancer (CRC) tissue samples were collected, including 28 RAS-mutant CRC samples and 21 wild-type CRC samples. All samples were human intestinal tissue obtained from patients with pathologically confirmed primary CRC, excluding those who received preoperative radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy; those with comorbid malignant tumors; severe hepatic or renal insufficiency; familial adenomatous polyposis or hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer; or insufficient sample size (<20 mg) or unqualified quality (e.g., >30% necrotic tissue). Each sample was flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen within 30 minutes post-collection and stored at −80°C without formalin fixation to prevent metabolite degradation. For analysis, exactly 20 mg of each tissue sample was weighed into a 1.5 mL EP tube, homogenized with two steel beads and 300 µL methanol-water (4:1 v/v) containing internal standards at 4 µg/mL, pre-cooled at −40°C for 2 min, homogenized at 45 Hz for 2 min, sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min, incubated overnight at −40°C, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 20 min; 150 µL of supernatant was transferred to LC-MS vials with footed inserts. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by combining equal volumes of all sample extracts.
complete_workflow_description: Data processing and statistical analysis were performed using multiple software tools in R and standalone applications. Raw LC-MS data were processed using XCMS v4.5.1 for baseline filtering, peak detection, integration, and retention time correction. Compound identification leveraged HMDB, Lipidmaps (v2.3), METLIN, and the LuMet-Animal3.0 local database (>10,000 metabolites), with a scoring system requiring ≥36/80 points for confident identification (Level 1–2 metabolites retained). Data preprocessing included RSD filtering (RSD > 0.3 removed), missing value imputation (metabolites with >50% missing values per group excluded; remaining zeros replaced with half the minimum ion intensity), log2 transformation, and Probabilistic Quotient Normalization (PQN). Multivariate statistical analyses—PCA and OPLS-DA—were conducted using the ropls package (v1.26.0) in R (v4.2.1), with rigorous model validation via 7-fold cross-validation and 200-fold response permutation testing. Differential metabolite screening used P < 0.05 (two-sample t-test, verified normality via Shapiro–Wilk test), |fold change| ≥ 1.2, and VIP > 1. Visualization employed ggplot2 (v3.4.0) for volcano plots, heatmaps, foldchange_bars, boxplots, lollipop plots, and correlation matrices (Pearson). Pathway enrichment used KEGG (Level 2–3) and Reactome databases; KEGG analysis was performed via standard overrepresentation analysis (ORA) with FDR < 0.05; Reactome analysis followed analogous statistical practices. Multiple testing correction across all statistical tests used the Benjamini–Hochberg method (FDR < 0.05).

related_tool: Reactome
sample_description: The study utilized two independent case-control datasets: (1) The Ausimmune Study (2003–2007), a multicenter incident matched case–control study across four regions of eastern Australia, including 206 first clinical demyelination (FCD) cases who did not receive a non-MS diagnosis by 10-year follow-up (93% [n=192] met clinically definite MS [CDMS] criteria per 2010 McDonald criteria; 7% [n=14] lost to follow-up) and 348 matched controls with DNA methylation (DNAm) data; cases and controls were matched on age (±2 years), sex, and study region; participants provided written informed consent and the study was approved by 9 regional human research ethics committees; biospecimens (whole blood and serum) were collected via phlebotomy at study clinics; DNAm was measured using the Illumina Infinium Human Methylation EPIC BeadChip Kit, yielding 742,961 CpGs after preprocessing; (2) The Epidemiological Investigation of Multiple Sclerosis (EIMS) (2005–2011), a Swedish case–control study including 140 MS cases and 139 matched controls (all Swedish, matched on age, sex, ethnicity, and smoking status); cases were diagnosed per 2010 McDonald criteria; biospecimens were collected via phlebotomy; DNAm was measured using the Illumina 450K array, yielding 478,687 CpGs for analysis; the EIMS DNAm dataset is publicly available in GEO (GSE43976 and GSE106648); both studies collected comprehensive epidemiological data including self-reported infectious mononucleosis (IM) history, sun exposure (dichotomized at ≥2 hours/day in Ausimmune; composite score >6 in EIMS), serum anti-EBNA IgG titers (dichotomized at ≥640 in Ausimmune and at median=6716 in EIMS), seasonally adjusted serum 25(OH)D concentrations (measured by LC-MS/MS in Ausimmune and chemiluminescent immunoassay in EIMS), HLA-DRB1*1501 genotype (imputed or directly genotyped), and covariates including sociodemographics, diet, smoking, supplement use, and disease-modifying therapy (DMT) use; all analyses were complete-case; inverse probability weighting (IPW) was applied in Ausimmune to account for selection bias related to DNAm data availability, adjusting for age, sex, region, education, and ancestry; sensitivity analyses restricted to first demyelinating events (FDEs) without prior suspected demyelination and to DMT-naïve participants (Ausimmune n=163, EIMS n=52) were conducted.
complete_workflow_description: All statistical and bioinformatic analyses were conducted in R software (version 4.3.2); for DNAm module construction in Ausimmune: (i) epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) was performed using a Bioconductor workflow, adjusting for cell-type proportions (via EpiDish R package), ancestry (first 2 principal components), and matching covariates; (ii) top 2,432 CpGs (Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p < 5×10⁻⁶) were input into Weighted Gene Correlation Network Analysis (WGCNA) R package with settings: unsigned network, minModuleSize = 30, reassignment threshold = 0.05, mergeCutHeight = 0.25, and soft power threshold = 7 (determined via scale independence and mean connectivity assessment); (iii) resulting modules (A1–A5) were represented by their first principal component; for DNAm module construction in EIMS: sparse Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (sPLS-DA) was implemented using the mixOmics R package with a 75%/25% train/test split; parameter tuning selected 3 components containing 1,000, 400, and 200 CpGs respectively; for mediation analysis: counterfactual mediation analysis was performed using the medflex R package to estimate total, direct, and indirect effects of exposures (e.g., anti-EBNA IgG, HLA-DRB1*1501) on MS risk via DNAm modules, reporting proportion mediated (indirect/total effect × 100%); for functional annotation: (i) gene enrichment and active sub-network analysis used pathfindR R package with Reactome and Gene Ontology databases and Benjamini-Hochberg correction; (ii) upstream transcription factor (TF) inference used the Lisa platform (in silico, selecting top 20 TFs per module); (iii) TF–disease associations were cross-referenced using Open Targets platform; all analyses accounted for matching factors in logistic regression models (Ausimmune: age, sex, region; EIMS: age, sex, ethnicity, smoking status); interaction analyses used relative excess risk due to interaction (RERI) and attributable proportion (AP) statistics; IPW was implemented using the survey and ipw R packages; STROBE guidelines were followed for reporting.

For secretome collection, 0.75 × 10⁶ hPFTSECs at passage 3 were seeded in T-75 flasks, treated with 5 µM or 10 µM clomiphene citrate (CC) for 24 h, starved in serum-free DMEM/F12 for 4 h, then incubated in serum-free DMEM/F12 supplemented with EGF and ITS for 24 h; spent media were collected, centrifuged to remove debris, and pooled across five independent biological replicates (one per patient).

(5) Signal peptide prediction was performed using SignalP-5.0 (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/SignalP-5.0) with default neural network parameters and organism type set to 'eukaryote'. (6) Venn diagrams were created using Venny 2.1.0 (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/) with default overlap logic and no manual parameter adjustment. (7) Statistical power analysis and inferential statistics (one-way ANOVA) were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (RRID:SCR_002798) with significance defined as p < 0.05 and data presented as mean ± SE.

sample_description: Baseline urine samples were collected from n=33 advanced-stage NSCLC patients before or within one week after the first cycle of immunotherapy; follow-up urine samples were collected from n=17 patients 120 (±7 days) after the first cycle. Urinary extracellular vesicles (EVs) were isolated from 900 µL of each urine sample using the EVtrap method—a magnetic bead-based affinity approach leveraging amphiphilic beads that bind EVs via lipid bilayer interactions—performed by Tymora Analytical Operations. EV isolation followed MISEV2023 recommendations and included verification of EV-specific markers (CD9, CD63, CD81) by Western blot. Isolated EVs were lysed using phase-transfer surfactant (PTS)-aided procedure; proteins were reduced with 10 mM TCEP and alkylated with 40 mM chloroacetamide at 95°C for 10 min, digested sequentially with Lys-C (1:100 enzyme-to-protein ratio, 3 h at 37°C) and trypsin (1:50 enzyme-to-protein ratio, overnight at 37°C), followed by PTS removal via acidification with 1% TFA and ethyl acetate extraction (two rounds), vacuum centrifugation drying, and desalting using Top-Tip C18 tips. Peptide concentration was quantified using Pierce Quantitative Colorimetric Peptide Assay, and samples were stored at -80°C prior to LC-MS/MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: Pathway analysis was conducted using WebGestalt software (version not specified) with Reactome and Gene Ontology (GO) biological process databases. Input consisted of human urine EV protein lists satisfying either: (i) significant Spearman correlation with PFS (r(s) > |0.3|, p < 0.05) or (ii) significant differential abundance (p < 0.05) in Wilcoxon rank-sum tests comparing short- vs. long-PFS groups. Proteins with fewer than 20 measurement data points were excluded. Over-representation analysis (ORA) was performed to calculate enrichment ratios and FDR-adjusted p-values. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied for multiple hypothesis testing. Affinity Propagation clustering was used to remove redundant pathways, and only pathways with FDR < 0.1 were considered significant. Visualization of enrichment results (enrichment ratio on x-axis, FDR indicated by color tone and labels) was generated in horizontal bar charts. All analyses were executed as part of an integrated bioinformatics pipeline including LC-MS/MS data processing (Proteome Discoverer 2.3 with Byonic and Sequest search engines), label-free quantitation (Precursor Ions Quantifier node), and statistical testing (Spearman correlation, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests) in Python/R environments.
