related_tool: CAMERA
sample_description: The paper describes two case studies: (i) a synthetic benchmark dataset consisting of a mixture of 1100 known compounds (mass range 100–1300 Da), used to evaluate raw spectral processing performance; and (ii) a clinical dataset of 48 fecal samples from 24 pediatric Crohn’s Disease (CD) patients and 24 pediatric healthy controls. The benchmark dataset was generated using LC-MS and served as ground truth with 820 manually verified true features. The clinical IBD dataset was used to demonstrate the full end-to-end workflow, including preprocessing, statistical analysis, and functional interpretation.
complete_workflow_description: MetaboAnalystR 2.0 implements an end-to-end LC-MS metabolomics workflow entirely within R: (1) raw data import (e.g., .mzML files); (2) peak picking using XCMS (with unspecified but consistent parameters matching prior literature); (3) retention time correction and peak alignment; (4) peak annotation using CAMERA for adduct, isotope, and in-source fragment assignment; (5) feature table generation and normalization (e.g., QC-RLSC or probabilistic quotient normalization); (6) univariate and multivariate statistical analysis (e.g., t-tests, ANOVA, PCA, PLS-DA); (7) functional interpretation via two complementary pathway-level methods — mummichog (over-representation analysis on ranked significant peaks) and GSEA (rank-based enrichment without significance cutoffs) — both implemented natively in R; and (8) integration with KEGG and HMDB databases for pathway mapping. The workflow is demonstrated reproducibly on both a synthetic benchmark dataset (1100-compound mixture, 35,215 detected peaks, 820 true features) and a clinical pediatric IBD dataset (48 fecal samples), with full R scripts and a step-by-step vignette ('The MetaboAnalystR 2.0 Workflow') provided in the GitHub repository. The tutorial processes a subset of 12 IBD samples on a 6-core Ubuntu system in ~14 minutes total.
source_paper: Jasmine et al. (2019), Metabolites, DOI: 10.3390/metabo9030057, PMID: 30909447

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: CAMERA
sample_description: Articular cartilage samples were collected from 10 patients diagnosed with Kashin-Beck disease (KBD) and 10 patients diagnosed with osteoarthritis (OA), all undergoing primary total knee arthroplasty. Patients were Chinese Han residents of Shaanxi province, matched for sex, and excluded if they had other osteoarticular or systemic chronic diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, diabetes, hypertension). Cartilage tissue was processed for untargeted metabolomics using liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS); raw data were acquired in positive and negative ionization modes and converted to mzXML format prior to analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow began with collection of articular cartilage from 10 KBD and 10 OA patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty. Samples were subjected to untargeted LC-MS analysis; raw data files were converted to mzXML format. Preprocessing included peak picking, retention time correction, peak grouping, and annotation of isotopes and adducts using XCMS. CAMERA was then employed to refine compound spectra and annotate isotopic/adduct features. Subsequently, metaX was used for data filtering (removing features detected in <50% of quality control samples or <80% of biological samples), missing value imputation via k-nearest neighbor algorithm, quality control-based LOESS signal correction to compensate for instrumental drift, and removal of features with relative standard deviation >30% across quality controls. Statistical analysis included PCA for outlier and batch effect assessment, independent-samples t-tests for group comparisons, and supervised PLS-DA using metaX to identify discriminative features. Differential metabolites were defined by VIP ≥ 1.0, |log2(fold change)| ≥ 0.263 (i.e., FC ≥ 1.2 or ≤ 0.83), and p < 0.05. Metabolite identification relied on matching exact molecular mass (m/z) against the KEGG database with ≤10 ppm mass error tolerance, followed by isotopic distribution validation and cross-referencing with an in-house fragment spectrum library.
source_paper: Hong et al. (2024), Bone & joint research, DOI: 10.1302/2046-3758.137.BJR-2023-0403.R1, PMID: 39013544

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: CAMERA
sample_description: Standard Reference Material NIST 1950 (human plasma sample), analyzed in both positive and negative ionization modes; five SRM samples were used for optimization and validation; samples underwent full-scan high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) with retention time filtering (30–930 s) and peak intensity filtering (fold change >3 vs. matrix samples, RSD <30% in SRM samples).
complete_workflow_description: The paper describes a fully reproducible end-to-end untargeted metabolomics workflow called Paired-Mass Distance Dependent Analysis (PMDDA), implemented in R and packaged as the 'rmwf' data processing template. The workflow begins with raw LC-HRMS data (mzML format), applies retention time filtering (30–930 s), performs peak picking using xcms (parameters optimized by IPO for five NIST 1950 SRM samples), corrects retention time, fills missing peaks, filters features by intensity fold change (>3× vs. matrix samples) and reproducibility (RSD <30% across SRM replicates), then applies the GlobalStd algorithm to remove redundant peaks based on frequency of paired-mass distances. Remaining independent peaks are clustered to generate a minimal, non-redundant precursor ion list. This list is formatted into an injection table for pseudo-targeted MS/MS acquisition (either as inclusion list for iterative DDA or for scheduled targeted MS/MS). Acquired MS/MS data (converted to mzML) are annotated via GNPS molecular networking (default settings: 2 Da precursor shift, 0.5 Da fragment shift), then linked back to MS1 features using strict tolerances (<5 ppm m/z, <5 s RT). Overlap between positive/negative mode peaks is also assessed. The entire workflow—including data, scripts, and environment—is containerized in the publicly available Docker image 'xcmsrocker' and archived on GitHub (https://github.com/yufree/pmdda) and Figshare.
source_paper: Miao et al. (2022), Journal of cheminformatics, DOI: 10.1186/s13321-022-00586-8, PMID: 35172886
