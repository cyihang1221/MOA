>Materials and methods   Data collection for bioinformatic analyses   Five independent datasets were harnessed to evaluate COL22A1 expression and clinical impact.     Primary TCGA Bioinformatic Cohort: Transcriptomic (mRNA) data and clinical covariates (sex, age, histology, grade, overall survival, vital status) for 690 gliomas were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas ( https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/ ). Cases originally annotated under legacy criteria were re‑classified per the 2021 WHO guidelines [ 5 ] into IDH‑wild‑type, IDH‑mutant astrocytoma, and oligodendroglioma clusters.   Proteomic Data Commons (TCGA PDC): Protein-level corroboration utilized 110 mass-spectrometry profiles (100 GBM, 10 normal brain) plus clinical metadata (retrieved December 2024;  https://proteomic.datacommons.cancer.gov/pdc/ ).   TCGA and CGGA Immune/Heterogeneity Cohorts: To probe tumour heterogeneity and immune infiltration, bulk RNA‑seq from TCGA and the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (http://  www.cgga.org.cn/ , accessed December 2024) were analysed.   Ivy Glioblastoma Atlas Project: COL22A1 regional expression was examined in 270 samples across five anatomical niches (leading edge, infiltrating border, cellular tumor, microvascular proliferation, and pseudopalisading necrosis, defined either by histology or gene expression) from 10 patients ( https://glioblastoma.alleninstitute.org/ ).   Single-Cell RNA Sequencing: Two GEO datasets were utilized: IDH-wildtype glioma ( GSE131928 ) and IDH-mutant glioma ( GSE89567 ), both accessed in December 2024.   Transcriptome, proteomic, and single‑cell processing   All analyses were carried out in R 4.1.0 ( www.r-project.org ). Firstly, we converted FPKM (Reads Per Kilobase per Million) values to TPM (Transcripts Per Million) for precise sample comparison. Differential expression was determined via ggpubr and limma with FDR-adjusted p < 0.05. Survival associations were assessed using Kaplan–Meier curves and multivariate Cox proportional-hazards models. For the single‑cell analysis, we analysed author‑curated, analysis‑ready objects in BioTuring BBrowser [ 17 ] for IDH‑wildtype glioma ( GSE131928 ) and IDH‑mutant glioma ( GSE89567 ). Batch correction and normalisation follow the original studies’ pipelines embedded in these objects; we retained those settings unmodified and document dataset identifiers, version/build dates, and provenance in Supplementary Methods. Reproducibility steps: Download and open  GSE131928  and  GSE89567  through the BBrowser list → Analysis view → Gene expression overlay: “COL22A1” (ENSG00000173785) → Group by default setting: cell type (Tumour, myeloid, T, and reactive glia) → using default log‑normalised COL22A1 expression for Statistics calculation by group. Gene‑set enrichment analysis (GSEA)   GSEA ( https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp , accessed December 2024) compared high-versus low‑COL22A1 tumours (median split) using hallmark gene sets (h.all.v2024.1.Hs. symbols.gmt). Other parameters remained at their default values [ 18 ]. Twelve‑cell‑state and CIBERSORT deconvolution   Two orthogonal strategies inferred cell-type composition. The twelve‑cell‑state framework of Verhaak et al. [ 19 ] identifies 12 principal cell states in bulk tumours, whereas CIBERSORT ( https://cibersort.stanford.edu/ ) estimates the relative abundance of 22 immune subsets [ 20 ]. Transcriptomic profiles from the TCGA, CGGA, and Ivy cohorts were input, retaining samples with a deconvolution p-value < 0.05. Spearman correlations were calculated between COL22A1 expression and each cell population. Tissue microarray and immunohistochemistry   A human glioma microarray (GL1001a; Biomax,  https://www.biomax.us/ ) containing 68 glioma specimens (7 Grade 1, 43 Grade 2, 9 Grade 3, 9 Grade 4) plus 7 normal‑brain controls (core diameter 1.5 mm; thickness 5 µm) was examined. All patient material was obtained with informed Consent. Sections were processed on a Ventana Benchmark® XT automated stainer. Following de-paraffinization, antigen retrieval was performed in 0.01 M sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.2) under pressure (125°C, 30 min), followed by three 5-min PBS washes. Slides were loaded onto the autostainer according to the vendor's protocol. Primary antibodies: COL22A1 (Abcam #ab121846), Ki‑67 (Abcam #ab15580), and p53 (Cell Signaling #2524). Detection utilised the Roche OptiView DAB IHC kit. Specificity was verified with matched positive and negative controls. Immunohistochemical scoring   FFPE TMA cores were stained on a Ventana Benchmark XT with DAB chromogen and digitised at 10 × . Using Fiji/ImageJ ( https://imagej.net/software/fiji/downloads ), custom macros were used to quantify the total tissue area and cells exhibiting a specific chromogen signal [ 21 – 24 ]. Firstly, images (TIFF) were processed in Fiji/ImageJ using Colour Deconvolution (vectors = H DAB) to isolate the DAB channel (Colour_2; Supplementary_3). The DAB image was converted to 8‑bit (white background = 255; black foreground = 0). Positivity was defined on the grayscale histogram as 0–120, then partitioned into fixed intensity bins applied uniformly across all slides: 0–40 (3 + , strong), 41–80 (2 + , moderate), 81–120 (1 + , weak). For each bin, binary masks were generated (Analyze Particles, size ≥ 5 px) to quantify the stained area. A whole‑tissue mask (0–240) provided the denominator. Batch processing and mask export used the macro “3 level DAB” (Supplementary_4), which outputs a per‑core Summary.csv containing areas for 3 + , 2 + , 1 + , and whole tissue. We calculated an Immunoscore equivalent Immunoscore as: Immunoscore = (1 × % of weak area) + (2 × % of moderate area) + (3 × % of strong area), yielding a 0–300 range. The resulting Immunoscores were compared with clinical variables via one‑way ANOVA. Spatial transcriptomics   A publicly available 10 × Genomics Visium section from an adult GBM ( https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/human-glioblastoma-whole-transcriptome-analysis-1-standard-1-2-0 ) comprising 3468 capture spots was interrogated. Log2‑scaled COL22A1 expression was visualised across the necrotic core, peri‑necrotic rim, cellular tumour, infiltrative border, and leading edge. Statistical analysis—survival modelling, multiplicity, and cut‑off policy   Overall survival was analysed using Cox proportional‑hazards models in R (v4.1.0; survival package). The pre‑specified multivariable model included COL22A1 as a continuous, z‑scored predictor together with age, sex, WHO grade, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS), radiotherapy (RT), and temozolomide (TMZ): Surv(time, status) ~ COL22A1_z + age + sex + WHO_grade + KPS + RT + TMZ. Continuous covariates were centred and scaled. Potential collinearity was examined using variance‑inflation diagnostics and correlation matrices; diagnostics did not indicate concerning collinearity, so no variables were removed. The proportional‑hazards assumption was evaluated with Schoenfeld residuals (with visual inspection of log–log plots); no material violations were detected. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals and two‑sided P values are reported. For discovery-stage analyses (collagen-gene family screens and MSigDB Hallmark gene-set enrichment), P values were adjusted using Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (q < 0.05). The COL22A1 term in the multivariable Cox model constituted a primary, pre‑specified hypothesis and was not subjected to additional multiplicity correction. To avoid threshold‑selection bias, inferential models treated COL22A1 continuously; Kaplan–Meier plots using a median split are provided solely for visualisation, with alternative quantile displays yielding concordant patterns. Results   Result 1: COL22A1 up‑regulation identified in glioblastoma and lower‑grade glioma   Analysis of pan‑cancer RNA‑seq data revealed a consistent up‑regulation of COL22A1 transcripts in malignant tissues relative to their non‑neoplastic counterparts. Across 9,162 primary tumors and 731 normal from The Cancer Genome Atlas (Fig.  1 A), COL22A1 abundance was significantly elevated in 15 of 34 evaluable cancer types (Wilcoxon rank‑sum test, p < 0.05), including glioma (GBM and LGG), while only one cancer type, Uterine Corpus Endometrial Carcinoma (UCEC) showed significant reverse correlation (Fig.  1 B). Furthermore, in this dataset, the more advanced cancer (metastatic melanoma, annotated as SKCM-Metastasis) exhibited higher COL22A1 expression than the primary tumor (annotated as SKCM-Primary, Fig.  1 B). These data indicate that COL22A1 upregulation is a pervasive event in oncogenesis across various tissue contexts, as well as at higher stages of the disease, suggesting a conserved pro-tumorigenic function and underscoring its potential value as a biomarker or therapeutic target in human cancers, including glioma. Colored boxes denote grades, black dots mark outliers, and divergent grade trends are highlighted accordingly Result 6: COL22A1 protein expression increased with grade and proliferation in glioma   Protein‑level interrogation confirmed the transcriptomic prominence of COL22A1 in glioma. Analysis of the TCGA Proteomic Data Commons demonstrated significantly higher COL22A1 abundance in tumour lysates than in normal brain (Fig.  6 A,  P  = 0.048). Stratification by optimal cut‑off further revealed an adverse clinical implication: patients harbouring high‑protein tumours experienced markedly shorter overall survival than their low‑protein counterparts (median 540 days vs 676 days; log‑rank P = 0.036; Fig.  6 B,  C ). Independent validation by quantitative immunohistochemistry on a tissue micro‑array comprising 75 surgical specimens reproduced and refined these findings (Fig.  6 D,  E ) . Mean immunoscore escalated stepwise from normal parenchyma (mean ± SD = 5.164 ± 8.592) to low‑grade lesions (IDH‑wildtype 55.726 ± 46.879; IDH‑mutant 53.958 ± 40.540) and further to high‑grade tumors (IDH‑wildtype 67.821 ± 46.830; IDH‑mutant 119.038 ± 50.480). Pair-wise comparisons confirmed significant overexpression in every tumor subgroup relative to normal tissue (Fig.  6 F–G,  P  ≤ 0.001) and demonstrated a grade-associated escalation within both IDH molecular classes. We next evaluated the relationship between COL22A1 protein abundance and proliferative activity (Ki‑67 index). Sub‑stratification showed a borderline‑significant trend in IDH‑wildtype lesions (Fig.  6 H,  R  = 0.280, P = 0.073) and a moderate, statistically significant association in IDH‑mutant tumors (F i g.  6 I,  R  = 0.469, P = 0.016). Together, these data verify that the COL22A1 protein is robustly up-regulated in glioma, increases with histological malignancy, and aligns with proliferative potential, thereby strengthening its candidacy as a clinically relevant biomarker. (Scale bar indicated 50 μm) Result 7: COL22A1 drives EMT‑inflammatory signatures in glioma from GSEA analyses   Gene-set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed on the 50 cancer hallmark collections after median stratification of COL22A1 expression within each TCGA glioma subtype. In IDH‑wild‑type astrocytoma, high COL22A1 was associated with the enrichment of 14 gene-sets encompassing cell‑cycle progression (E2F_TARGETS, G2M_CHECKPOINT, MITOTIC_SPINDLE), metabolic stress (HYPOXIA, OXIDATIVE_PHOSPHORYLATION, BILE_ACID_METABOLISM, PEROXISOME), and aggressive stromal signaling (notably EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION, IL6_JAK_STAT3_SIGNALING, and TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB), as demonstrated in the (Fig.  7 A–C). IDH-mutant astrocytoma showed 11 significant pathways, including EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION, G2M_CHECKPOINT, IL6_JAK_STAT3_SIGNALING, INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE, INTERFERON_GAMMA_RESPONSE, and TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB, which overlapped with those of IDH-wildtype astrocytoma. In contrast, immune-reactive signatures (ALLOGRAFT_REJECTION, COMPLEMENT, INTERFERON_ALPHA_RESPONSE) were uniquely enriched. Venn analysis confirmed these six recurrent circuits, underscoring a conserved link between COL22A1 upregulation and the mesenchymal, proliferative, and inflammatory axes, irrespective of IDH status (Fig.  7 D). In contrast, Oligodendroglioma exhibited a markedly restricted landscape, limited to proliferative modules (E2F_TARGETS, G2M_CHECKPOINT, MYC_TARGETS_V1) and lacking EMT or cytokine‑driven responses, mirroring its comparatively indolent phenotype. Collectively, the data indicate that heightened COL22A1 expression reconfigures glioma transcriptional wiring toward EMT, checkpoint activation, and inflammatory signaling; these alterations are most extensive in IDH-wildtype tumors, partially conserved in IDH-mutant astrocytoma, and largely absent in Oligodendroglioma, aligning with the gene’s subtype-specific prognostic impact.
