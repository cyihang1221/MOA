sample_description: The study used 58 daphnane standards (DA01 to DA58) isolated in a prior phytochemical investigation, and plant materials from 56 Thymelaeaceae species collected from China, Japan, and Turkey. Standards were dissolved in LC-MS–grade methanol at 1 ppm. Plant materials were air-dried, cut into small pieces, extracted with 95% ethanol at room temperature, and the resulting ethanol extracts (100 mg per sample) were suspended in distilled water and partitioned with ethyl acetate. The ethyl acetate fractions (5 mg per sample) were loaded onto Sep-Pak C18 Plus Long Cartridges and eluted sequentially with LC-MS–grade methanol:water (5:5 v/v and 10:0 v/v, 10 mL each). Positive-mode ([M+H]⁺) and negative-mode ([M−H]⁻) data were acquired separately. MS/MS peak lists were extracted using RMassBank with a 0.4-min retention time window around expected RT and reported as absolute ion intensities; subsequent data curation included recalibration to ≤5 ppm using known subformula annotations, removal of peaks lacking valid subformulas within 5 ppm, exclusion of compounds with double chromatographic peaks, identical spectra (detected via SPLASH), single-peak spectra, or maximum intensity <1×10⁵, and deduplication of multiply measured substances (retaining only the highest-intensity instance per ionization mode). Final spectra were saved in plain text and Mascot Generic Format (MGF) and deposited in MassBank.

complete_workflow_description: The research employed four in silico fragmentation tools—MS-FINDER (v1.70), CFM-ID (v2.2, rev26), MetFragCL (v2.2-CL), and MAGMa+ (Python wrapper of MAGMa)—each configured with tool-specific input/output formats and structural handling routines. For CFM-ID, the cfm-id executable was run on a CentOS 7 server (48-core AMD Opteron 6344, 2.6 GHz); input spectra were repeated for low/medium/high energies emulating 10/20/40 eV CID, and spectral comparison used the dot product score (preferred over Jaccard) with ±5 ppm tolerance and original METLIN-trained positive/negative models. MetFragCL was run on macOS (2.7 GHz Intel Core i5, 16 GB RAM) using a parameter file (details in Additional file 1: Table S2); fragment matching accounted for proton addition (positive mode) or removal (negative mode, −1.007 Da) to align theoretical fragments with observed charged peaks. Parameter optimization was performed on the 312-training-set spectra, followed by development of voting/consensus models that integrated ranked outputs from multiple tools, optionally augmented with database lookups (ChemSpider, MS/MS libraries) and metadata filtering; all consensus strategies and configurations are detailed in Table 1 and Additional file 1.

related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The study utilized MS/MS records from MassBank, GNPS, and human plasma data sets. Specifically, 5063 MassBank records and 936 human plasma records (acquired via HILIC-ESI(+)-QTOF MS/MS) were used. complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow consisted of the following software-driven procedures: (1) Hydrogen rearrangement (HR) statistics construction: MS/MS records from MassBank were filtered by m/z accuracy (≤10 mDa from theoretical mass) and intensity (>10% of base peak); fragment ion assignment followed combinatorial bond cleavage rules—up to two bonds broken for tree-like structures, up to four for two-ring systems (ring fission = two bonds); hydrogen rearrangement considered ±2 H per cleavage; candidate fragments ranked by priority: even-electron rule (first cleavage), neutralized substructure variants (±2 H), second-cleavage even-electron fragments, second-cleavage fragments with assigned precursors at higher m/z, and minimum mass error fragments; (2) In silico MS/MS annotation: executed using HR rules instead of even-electron rule, with identical hierarchical ranking but substituting HR compliance for even-electron compliance; (3) Fragment stability verification: MOPAC2012 (PM7) manually applied to each ionic substructure in MOL format to compute heat of formation and assess thermodynamic stability; (4) Molecular formula generation: MS-FINDER generated formula candidates from precursor m/z and adduct type using user-defined mass tolerance and elemental constraints (C, H, N, O, P, S, F, Cl, Br, I, Si); valence and elemental ratio filters reduced candidate space; (5) Formula ranking: five diagnostic scores computed and summed into F (0–5): (i) mass error score via Gaussian function (Equation 1); (ii) isotopic ratio score averaging Gaussian penalties for M+1 and M+2 deviations (Equation 2); (iii) product ion formula assignment rate (excluding isotopic peaks post-deisotoping); (iv) neutral loss assignment rate against 135-entry internal database; (v) discrete database existence score (0.5 baseline + scaled multi-database count); (6) Structure candidate retrieval: queried internal database (14 metabolome sources); fallbacks to MINE or PubChem PUG REST; InChIKey-based deduplication and synonym-driven representative selection applied; (7) Structure ranking: in silico MS/MS annotation performed per HR rules; unresolved peaks penalized (Ph = 0.2); mass error penalty (M) and BDE penalty (B = 1 − Bf/Bm) computed per Equation (1) and (3); fragmentation linkage penalty (Pt = 0.5) applied; per-fragment score Sf = M × B × Pt × Ph; cumulative score normalized to 4 and augmented with database presence score (0–1) to yield structure score S (0–5); (8) Final molecular identification: overall score T = F + S determined final ranking.

related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: The study used two primary sets of biological and chemical MS/MS spectral data: (1) 5063 manually curated MS/MS spectra from the MassBank database, representing 3127 authentic compound structures (disregarding stereochemistry), covering diverse metabolites across species and tissues; and (2) 936 manually identified MS/MS spectra from an untargeted HILIC-QTOF LC-MS/MS dataset of human plasma collected in a cohort study. All spectra were acquired in positive or negative electrospray ionization (ESI) mode using high-resolution tandem mass spectrometry (e.g., QTOF), with accurate mass measurement of precursor ions and fragment ions. Precursor m/z values, adduct types (e.g., [M+H]⁺, [M−H]⁻), isotope ratios, and full MS/MS spectra (including fragment m/z and relative intensities) were provided as input to MS-FINDER.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end MS-FINDER workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data (precursor m/z, retention time, adduct annotation, and MS/MS spectrum) as input. First, molecular formulas are computationally generated from the accurate precursor m/z within a defined mass tolerance, constrained by valence rules (OV/SV parity and SV ≥ 2×(TA−1)) and biologically plausible elemental ratios (e.g., H/C, O/C) derived from HMDB/KNApSAcK statistics. Second, all structural isomers matching the top formula candidates are retrieved from integrated metabolome databases (HMDB for human metabolites, KNApSAcK for plant metabolites, and RIKEN’s internal DB). Third, for each candidate structure, in silico MS/MS fragmentation is performed under low-energy CID assumptions, applying nine mechanistic hydrogen rearrangement (HR) rules (P1–P4 for ESI+, N1–N5 for ESI−) to predict fragment ions and their hydrogen-adjusted neutral losses (e.g., [M+H−H₂O]⁺, [M−H−CO₂]⁻). Fragment matches are classified as 'resolved' (exact HR rule match), 'semiresolved' (±2 H deviation), or 'formula-assigned' (no HR explanation). Finally, candidate structures are ranked using a composite score weighting fragment mass accuracy (ppm error), bond dissociation energy estimates, fragment linkage network coherence, and HR rule adherence (penalizing semiresolved and formula-assigned fragments). The top-3 ranked isomers are output with confidence metrics, enabling identification even for compounds absent from spectral libraries.
source_paper: Hiroshi et al. (2016), Analytical chemistry, DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b00770, PMID: 27419259

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MS-Finder
sample_description: TOOL_NOT_USED_IN_THIS_STUDY
complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end computational workflow for metabolite identification from tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) data, integrated into the SIRIUS 4.0 software suite. The workflow begins with LC-MS data, followed by molecular formula annotation using SIRIUS (based on isotope pattern and mass accuracy), then fragmentation tree computation to model plausible fragmentation pathways of the candidate molecule. Next, CSI:FingerID predicts a molecular fingerprint (a binary vector encoding 881 molecular properties, e.g., PubChem CACTVS fingerprint) from the MS/MS spectrum and fragmentation tree using an ensemble of Support Vector Machines trained with Platt-calibrated posterior probabilities. The novel contribution of this paper is the replacement of the standard independent-probability scoring (Platt or modified Platt score) with a Bayesian tree network-based scoring: (1) marginal probabilities for each fingerprint bit are taken from SVM outputs; (2) a tree-structured Bayesian network is estimated on-the-fly using mutual information between molecular properties across candidate structures in the database (e.g., PubChem); (3) covariances along tree edges are estimated to reflect dependencies; (4) joint probabilities satisfying both marginals and covariances are computed via constrained optimization; and (5) candidates are ranked by the resulting joint posterior probability of the observed fingerprint bits.
