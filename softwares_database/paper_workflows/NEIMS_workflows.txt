========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: NEIMS
sample_description: The NEIMS model was applied to predict electron ionization mass spectra (EI-MS) for 2,146,690 molecular structures derived from f-NIST and f-ChEMBL — filtered and deduplicated subsets of the NIST 2017 library (0.27 million compounds with experimental EI-MS) and ChEMBL 28 (containing ~2 million bioactive molecules). The f-NIST and f-ChEMBL datasets collectively represent diverse chemical space, including metabolites, drugs, and synthetic organic compounds. For validation, a test set comprising >10,000 experimentally measured EI-MS spectra from NIST 2017 was used; an additional extra test set included 10 synthetic organic compounds with measured spectra not present in NIST 2017. Molecular structures were represented as extended circular fingerprints (ECFPs), calculated using RDKit, and served as input features to NEIMS.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with two curated structure databases: f-NIST (filtered/deduplicated NIST 2017) and f-ChEMBL (filtered/deduplicated ChEMBL 28), totaling 2,146,690 unique molecular structures. For each molecule, RDKit is used to compute 1024-bit extended circular fingerprints (ECFPs). These ECFPs are fed into the pre-trained NEIMS model (downloaded from its official repository) to generate predicted electron ionization mass spectra, forming a million-scale in-silico spectral library. Query spectra (experimental EI-MS) are then matched against this library using FastEI, which first applies Word2vec-based spectral embedding to convert spectra into dense vector representations, followed by approximate nearest neighbor search using a Hierarchical Navigable Small-World graph (HNSW) index. Matching performance is evaluated using recall@k metrics (e.g., recall@1, recall@10) on two test sets: (1) >10,000 NIST 2017 experimental spectra (in-library validation), and (2) 10 synthetic compounds with experimental spectra absent from NIST 2017 (out-of-library validation). A 5 Da mass filter is optionally applied as a post-hoc filtering step to improve accuracy. The entire pipeline — from structure input → ECFP calculation → NEIMS prediction → FastEI embedding + HNSW search → ranked candidate output — is implemented as a standalone software package.
source_paper: Qiong et al. (2023), Nature communications, DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-39279-7, PMID: 37349295

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: NEIMS
sample_description: The NEIMS model was trained on a dataset of 6790 merged electrospray ionization tandem mass spectra (ESI-MS/MS) of underived (i.e., non-chemically derivatized) small molecules, sourced from the MassBank of North America (MoNA). These spectra were filtered to include only positive-mode, ESI-ionized spectra with MoNA rank >4, and fragments acquired at multiple collision energies were merged per molecule to generate single representative spectra. The dataset comprises diverse chemical structures but no explicit information on biological origin, tissue, or organism — it is a general-purpose, publicly available reference spectral library of synthetic and natural small molecules.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end workflow begins with chemical derivatization of standards (e.g., dansylation of amines/hydroxyls/carboxyls or benzoylation of amines/hydroxyls), followed by LC-MS/MS analysis on a HESI Q-Exactive Plus Orbitrap in positive mode, acquiring merged MS/MS spectra across collision energies (10–50 eV). Experimental spectra are curated into training (90%), validation (10%), and hold-out test sets (167–168 spectra), with random splitting independent of structural similarity. A pre-trained NEIMS MLP model—originally trained on 6790 MoNA ESI-MS/MS spectra of underived molecules—is fine-tuned via transfer learning by freezing all but the final layer and updating weights using the derivatized-molecule spectra. The resulting specialized models (Dns-MS or Bz-MS) predict collision-energy-merged ESI-MS/MS spectra at 1 Da resolution for in silico libraries (e.g., DnsBank). These predicted libraries are then benchmarked against experimental test spectra using weighted cosine similarity (WCS), and applied to annotate unknowns in real-world samples (e.g., dansylated metabolites in ozonated leather industrial wastewater) via spectral matching tools including CFM-ID 4.0 (using DotProduct scoring, 10 ppm mass tolerance, [M+H]+ adduct) and SIRIUS 4 (with PubChem database and Orbitrap instrument parameters).
source_paper: B et al. (2024), Nature communications, DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-52805-5, PMID: 39333165
