complete_workflow_description: The process procedure involved a multi-step proteomic workflow: (1) Whole-cell protein extraction from B. thuringiensis cells harvested at 10, 20, and 32 h — cells were washed with chilled PBS (10 mM, pH 7.8), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, ground to powder, lysed in buffer (8 M urea, 4% CHAPS, 40 mM Tris, 65 mM DTT, protease inhibitor cocktail) at 4°C for 1 h, centrifuged (4°C, 13,200 rpm, 30 min), and precipitated overnight with ice-cold acetone containing 10% trichloroacetic acid at −20°C; pellets were lyophilized and stored at −40°C. (2) Protein digestion — pellets were dissolved in reducing solution (6 M guanidine hydrochloride, 100 mM Tris pH 8.3); protein concentration was determined using the 2-D Quant kit (Amersham Biosciences); ~300 μg protein was adjusted to 3 μg/μl, reduced with 1 μl of 1 M DTT at 37°C for 2.5 h, alkylated with 5 μl of 1 M iodoacetamide in the dark at RT for 40 min, precipitated with ice-cold ACN-acetone-acetic acid (50:50:0.1 vol/vol/vol) for >12 h, washed thrice with ice-cold ethanol, resuspended in 100 μl of 50 mM NH₄HCO₃, and digested with trypsin at 1:100 (wt/wt) ratio for 4 h at 37°C with shaking, followed by additional trypsin at 1:50 (wt/wt) ratio overnight at 37°C; digestion was quenched with formic acid and samples were ultrafiltered using 10-kDa Microcon filtration devices (Sartorius, Germany) and lyophilized. (3) 2D nano-LC-MS/MS analysis — performed on an LTQ XL mass spectrometer (ThermoFisher) coupled with two Finnigan quaternary pumps and an autosampler (LC Packings), equipped with a homemade nano-ionization source; first-dimension separation used a BioBasic SCX column (0.32 mm × 100 mm, 5 μm) with 12-step ammonium chloride elution (25–1000 mM, 4% ACN, 0.1% FA); peptides were trapped alternately on two Zorbax enrichment columns (0.3 mm × 5 mm, 5 μm) and subjected to second-dimension reversed-phase separation on a BioBasic-C18 column (0.1 mm × 150 mm, 5 μm) with gradient elution (1 min 100% buffer A [5% ACN, 0.1% FA], 70-min gradient to 65% buffer B [0.1% FA in ACN], 20-min to 80% buffer B, 5 min at 80% buffer B, 1-min back to 100% buffer A, 12-min reequilibration); flow rate was 200 nl/min; electrospray voltage was 2.0 kV; heated capillary temperature was 180°C; capillary voltage was 4.5 kV; MS/MS data were acquired in data-dependent mode scanning m/z 300–2000 for full MS and selecting top 10 most intense ions for CID fragmentation at normalized collision energy 35%; dynamic exclusion was set to repeat count 2, repeat duration 30 s, exclusion duration 90 s; automatic gain control regulated ion trap fill. (4) In-house database search using SEQUEST (integrated in Proteome Discoverer 1.1 software package) — searched against a custom FASTA database built from UniProt Knowledgebase (Swiss-Prot + TrEMBL) containing all B. thuringiensis subspecies (116,723 entries) plus reversed decoy sequences; search parameters: precursor ion tolerance = 1.5 Da, fragment ion tolerance = 1.0 Da; up to two missed cleavages allowed; only b/y ions considered; static modification: carbamidomethyl (C); variable modifications: oxidation (M); target-decoy FDR threshold ≤0.01; results validated manually; protein redundancy resolved by assigning shared peptides to the highest-scoring protein entry, except for isoforms from the same subspecies which were all retained. (5) Bioinformatic analysis — functional classification using UniProt annotations, BioCyc, and KEGG databases; BLASTP queries against UniProt for unknown-function proteins; ClustalW alignment for signature identification; theoretical pI and molecular mass calculated using Expasy’s PI tool. (6) Validation by Western blot — CotJc and glutamine synthetase proteins were selected; genes cotJc and glnA were cloned into pET30a vector, expressed in E. coli BL21, purified via HisTrap FF column on ÄKTA purifier 100 system (GE Healthcare), and used to generate rabbit antisera; whole-cell proteins from T₁, T₂, T₃ were separated on NuPAGE 12% Bis-Tris gels, transferred to PVDF membranes, blocked, probed with primary antisera (1:1000 dilution), detected with DyLight 680-conjugated anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:10,000), and visualized using the Odyssey infrared imaging system (Li-COR Biosciences).

complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end computational workflow for metabolite identification from tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) data, integrated into the SIRIUS 4.0 software platform. The workflow begins with LC-MS data acquisition from biological samples (though no specific sample type, species, or number is detailed in the Methods section), followed by molecular formula annotation using SIRIUS (based on high-resolution MS1 data). For each candidate molecular formula, a fragmentation tree is computed from the MS/MS spectrum using the SIRIUS fragmentation tree algorithm. From this tree and the spectrum, CSI:FingerID predicts a probabilistic molecular fingerprint — a vector of Platt-calibrated posterior probabilities (one per molecular property) — using an ensemble of support vector machines trained on reference spectra and structures. The novel contribution of this paper is the replacement of the standard independent-probability scoring (Platt or modified Platt score) with a Bayesian tree network-based scoring: (i) marginal probabilities are taken from the SVM outputs; (ii) a tree-structured Bayesian network topology is estimated on-the-fly using mutual information between molecular properties across top-ranked candidate fingerprints; (iii) edge-wise covariances are estimated to reflect dependencies; (iv) joint probabilities satisfying both marginals and covariances are computed via constrained optimization; and (v) candidates are ranked by the resulting joint probability of observed fingerprint bits. This scoring is implemented as 'Bayesian (fixed tree)' and fully integrated into CSI:FingerID within SIRIUS 4.0. The workflow concludes with structure database search against PubChem using the scored fingerprints, returning ranked candidate structures (constitutional isomers only, excluding stereochemistry). No external tools like BioCyc are invoked or configured in this pipeline.
source_paper: Marcus et al. (2018), Bioinformatics (Oxford, England), DOI: 10.1093/bioinformatics/bty245, PMID: 29949965
