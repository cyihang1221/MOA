complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end workflow for evaluating and applying Spec2Vec: (1) Data acquisition and preprocessing — starting from GNPS MS/MS libraries, spectra were filtered to retain only those with ≥10 fragment peaks, yielding the AllPositive dataset (95,320 positive-mode spectra, 77,092 with InChIKey annotations); a subset, UniqueInchikey (12,797 spectra with unique planar InChIKeys), was used for quantitative evaluation. (2) Structural similarity computation — Tanimoto scores were calculated using RDKit from Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 2048 bits) derived from InChIKeys. (3) Spectral similarity computation — three scores were computed for all pairwise combinations in UniqueInchikey: standard cosine, modified cosine (incorporating precursor m/z shift), and Spec2Vec (trained either on the same 12,797-spectrum subset or on the full 95,320-spectrum AllPositive set); Spec2Vec embeddings used Word2Vec-inspired training with default hyperparameters implied by the implementation (though exact parameters like vector dimension, window size, epochs, and intensity weighting are not explicitly detailed in the main text). (4) Correlation analysis — Spearman rank correlation between spectral similarity scores and structural Tanimoto scores was assessed, and top-scoring pairs were analyzed for enrichment in high structural similarity. (5) Molecular networking application — a simplified network construction was implemented: for each spectrum, up to 10 highest-scoring edges above a user-defined similarity threshold were retained; Louvain community detection was applied for clustering; clusters were labeled as well- or poorly-clustered based on mean intra-cluster Tanimoto score ≥0.5 or <0.5, respectively. All computations were performed using custom Python code built on top of open-source libraries (e.g., NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, RDKit, gensim for Word2Vec backend), with runtime performance reported on an Intel i7-8550U CPU.

complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end deep learning workflow for predicting structural similarity (Tanimoto scores) directly from tandem mass spectra. The workflow begins with retrieval of 109,734 MS/MS spectra from GNPS, followed by curation and cleaning using matchms to ensure spectral quality and consistency. Chemical structure annotations (SMILES and/or InChI) are used to compute RDKit 2048-bit molecular fingerprints and corresponding Tanimoto similarity scores for spectrum pairs. A Siamese neural network is trained on binned spectra (m/z range 10.0–1000.0 Da, up to 10,000 bins), where each spectrum is encoded through two identical base networks consisting of three dense layers (500, 500, and 200 nodes), with L1 and L2 regularization (10⁻⁶), dropout (rate = 0.2), batch normalization, and data augmentation (random intensity perturbations and low-intensity peak removal/addition). Spectral pairs are sampled with probability weighted by their structural similarity to mitigate class imbalance. Model uncertainty is estimated via Monte-Carlo Dropout. The final model is evaluated on a held-out test set of 3601 spectra from 500 unseen compounds, reporting root mean squared error (RMSE) for predicted Tanimoto scores, and is demonstrated for spectral library matching and embedding-based clustering.
source_paper: Florian et al. (2021), Journal of cheminformatics, DOI: 10.1186/s13321-021-00558-4, PMID: 34715914
