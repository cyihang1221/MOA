</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="ab0005"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Wampee is a fruit with a long history of consumption and medicinal value. In this study, four groups of wampee sample from the same nursery were analyzed for systematic analysis of tastes and metabolites using electronic tongue, UHPLC-QTOF-MS/MS and GC-TOF-MS. Through principal component analysis, correlation analysis, OPLS-DA, analysis of variance, and clustered heat map analysis, we performed a comprehensive discrimination of flavors and metabolites of wampees, and performed metabolic pathway KEGG enrichment analysis for differential metabolites. The results showed that there were significant differences in tastes, metabolites, and metabolic pathways among different groups of wampees, and 20 biomarkers could distinguish the groups. A6, A9, and A10 are potentially refreshing and purely sweet varieties. These findings provide metabolic profiling support for a full understanding of the wampee, and provide basic support for wampee species tracing, species selection, and specialty food development.</p>
<section id="ks0005" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Wampee, Metabolomics, Biomarker, Metabolic pathway, Taste analysis</p></section></section><section class="abstract" id="ab0010"><h2>Highlights</h2>
<ul id="l0005" class="list" style="list-style-type:none">
<li id="li0005">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0005">Tastes analysis for different group of wampee were performed by Electronic tongue.</p>
</li>
<li id="li0010">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0010">Different metabolic pathways were found in four varieties of wampees.</p>
</li>
<li id="li0015">
<span class="label">•</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0015">20 biomarkers were screened through metabolomics by UHPLC-QTOF-MS/MS and GC-TOF-MS.</p>
</li>
</ul></section><section id="s0005"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">1. Introduction</h2>
<p id="p0025">Wampee (<em>Clausena lansium</em>) is a deciduous tree widely grown in southern China, is a member of Rutaceae genus of the Clausena family. The fruits, leaves, and rhizomes of wampee have a long history of consumption and medicinal value in China (<a href="#bb0040" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0040">Liu, Guo, Liu, et al., 2019</a>; <a href="#bb0045" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0045">Liu, Guo, Wang, et al., 2019</a>), and are often used to diuretically reduce swelling, quench thirst, promote circulation of Qi and relieve pain (<a href="#bb0145" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0145">Zhang et al., 2024</a>; Liu et al., 2019; <a href="#bb0075" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0075">Shu et al., 2024</a>). Wampee fruits are popular among consumers for their unique flavor and nutritional value, and is classified as sweet, sweet-sour, and sour (<a href="#bb0130" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0130">Yin, Ji, et al., 2022</a>; <a href="#bb0010" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0010">Chang et al., 2022</a>), with significant differences in flavor and texture (<a href="#bb0020" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0020">Chen et al., 2024</a>; <a href="#bb0135" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0135">Yin, Zhang, et al., 2022</a>). Wampee fruit is not only rich in vitamins, minerals, amino acids and other essential nutrients (<a href="#bb0005" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0005">Chai et al., 2017</a>; <a href="#bb0070" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0070">Shang et al., 2025</a>; <a href="#bb0150" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0150">Zhao et al., 2023</a>) but also rich in organic acids, phenolic acids, flavonoids, alkaloids and other small molecules of bioactive compounds (<a href="#bb0005" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0005">Chai et al., 2017</a>; <a href="#bb0125" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0125">Ye et al., 2019</a>). Wampee's main aromatic compounds (<a href="#bb0055" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0055">Mo et al., 2025</a>) are α-phellandrene, α-pinene, linalool, (<em>E</em>)-β-farnesene, β-caryophyllene, α-farnesene, and p-cymene with typically citrus, spice, and turpentine aromas (<a href="#bb0055" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0055">Mo et al., 2025</a>; <a href="#bb0150" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0150">Zhao et al., 2023</a>). The rich material composition indicates that wampee can not only be directly consumed as fresh fruit, but also processed into jam, juice, fruit wine (<a href="#bb0085" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0085">Wang et al., 2024</a>) and other specialty foods (<a href="#bb0140" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0140">Zeng et al., 2024</a>), what's more wampee is an excellent resource for functional foods.</p>
<p id="p0030">Wampee is a potentially functional natural food. In daily life, consumers tend to buy sweet wampees in order to consume them directly. Wampees with sweet -sour taste and strong acidity are usually purchased as raw materials and auxiliary materials to make hand-processed products, and taste plays a decisive role for consuming wampees. Analysis of metabolites in sweet wampee YF1 and sour-sweet wampee YF2 showed that D-galactose, D-mannose, and <span class="font-variant-small-caps">d</span>-fructose 6-phosphate were the main sweetness sources, and L-citramalic acid, 2-hydroxyglutaric acid, and 3-methylmalic acid were the main sourness sources (Yin et al., 2022a; Yin et al., 2022b). <a href="#bb0150" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0150">Zhao et al. (2023)</a> quantified a total of 29 flavor compounds of wampee by HPLC and calculated their Taste activity value (TAV), and the results showed that fructose, sucrose and glucose play an important role in the sweetness, organic acids play an important role in the acidity, and alanine, aspartic acid, and glutamic acid are the important sources of the sweetness and some of the umami of the wampees, whereas most of the remaining free amino acids contribute little to the flavor of the wampees. There are fewer studies on taste analysis of wampee. Therefore, the main flavor presenting substances of wampee need to be further analyzed and explored comprehensively.</p>
<p id="p0035">Metabolomics is widely used in the study of the differences between different parts, varieties and extracts of wampee. <a href="#bb0025" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0025">Fan et al. (2020)</a> performed chemical analysis of wampee using UPLC-Q-Orbitrap-MS identified 364 metabolites, and screened 62 potential biomarkers from leaves, barks, flowers, peels, pulps, and seeds. <a href="#bb0075" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0075">Shu et al. (2024)</a> conducted a compositional analysis of wampee powder extract using HPLC-MS and identified 311 metabolites, providing a basic study to understand the potential cardioprotective and gastrointestinal microbiota-modulating bioactivities of wampee. <a href="#bb0140" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0140">Zeng et al. (2024)</a> identified 86 phenolic compounds in crude phenols of wampee peels, crude phenols of wampee seeds and crude phenols of wampee pulps of wampee using UPLC-MS/MS for basic substance characterization for in vitro anti-inflammatory activity. Yin et al. (2022a) and <a href="#bb0010" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0010">Chang et al. (2022)</a> conducted extensive targeted metabolomic analysis of YF1 sweet flavored wampee and YF2 sour flavored wampee using UPLC-MS/MS, and a total of 1012 metabolites were identified, and 449 differential metabolites were identified and classified. <a href="#bb0145" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0145">Zhang et al. (2024)</a> used LC-MS/MS to analyze the metabolomics of the peel, pulp and seeds of 17 wampees. 1286 metabolites were detected and differentially analyzed, and 59 potential biomarkers were identified, which can provide reference value for the isolation and identification of the functional components as well as for the selection and breeding of varieties. GC-TOF-MS is often used for the detection of volatile substances and thermally stable compounds, such as volatiles, aromatic hydrocarbons and short-chain fatty acids. While it was not reported in the metabolomics study of wampee.</p>
<p id="p0040">Metabolomics is not only a reliable analytical tool for understanding of wampee through biomarker screening and metabolic pathway characterization but also play an important role in variety identification (<a href="#bb0115" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0115">Xiao et al., 2025</a>) and origin tracing (<a href="#bb0035" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0035">Li et al., 2024</a>; <a href="#bb0060" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0060">Peng et al., 2022</a>). However, at this stage, little metabolomics-based study has been reported on the classification and identification of wampee varieties.</p>
<p id="p0045">In this study, UHPLC-QTOF-MS/MS and GC-TOF-MS were combined to comprehensively analyze and annotate the metabolites of four groups of wampee sample from different varieties originating from the same nursery, and taste discrimination of the wampees was carried out using an electronic tongue. Principal component analysis, correlation analysis, OPLS-DA, analysis of variance, and clustered heatmap analysis were used for differential metabolite and metabolic pathway analysis of wampee. This study aims to establish a method for distinguishing wampee varieties, screening biomarkers and differential metabolism analysis, so as to provide metabolic profiling support for a comprehensive understanding of wampee, and basic support for wampee variety traceability, variety selection and breeding, and special food development.</p></section><section id="s0010"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. Materials and methods</h2>
<section id="s0015"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1. Samples and chemicals</h3>
<p id="p0050">Wampee samples were collected in July 2023 from Germplasm Resource Nursery of Wampee (E108°14′43.904″N22°51′21.053″) in China from July 2023. A total of 2520 wampee samples came from four groups (<a href="#f0005" class="usa-link">Fig. 1</a>): 600 from sweet wampee (TH Group), 840 from chicken heart wampee (JX Group), 480 from round pear wampee (YL Group), and 600 from seedless wampee (WH Group). All samples sent to the laboratory immediately after harvest and were stored in a freezer at −80 °C until extraction and preparation. The resources of wampee sample were preserved in Chinese Academy of Tropical Agricultural Sciences, No.4 Academy Road, Longhua District, Haikou City, Hainan Province, China. Detailed information of each sample was listed in Table S1.</p>

<p id="p0060">6 to 7 wampee fruits selected in ripening period were peeled to remove the seeds and the pulp was crushed and homogenized for the further analysis. 50 ± 1 mg sample was transferred into a 2 mL tube, and 1000 μL pre-cold extraction mixture (methanol/H<sub>2</sub>O/ (v: v) =3:1, including internal adonitol, 0.5 mg/mL stock) was added, vortex mixing for 30 s. Add the steel ball, process with 35 Hz grinding instrument for 4 min, and ultrasonic treatment with ice water bath for 5 min (repeat three times). After centrifugation at 4 °C for 15 min at 12000 rpm (RCF = 13,800(×g), <em>R</em> = 8.6 cm), 100 μL supernatant was transferred to a fresh tube. Dry extract in vacuum concentrator. To prepare the QC (Quality control) sample, 50 μL of each sample was taken out and combined together. After evaporation in a vacuum concentrator, 60 μL of Methoxyamination hydrochloride (20 mg/mL in pyridine) was added and then incubated at 80 °C for 30 min, then derivatized by 80 μL of BSTFA regent (1 % TMCS, <em>v</em>/v) at 70 °C for 1.5 h. Gradually cooling samples to room temperature, 5 μL of FAMEs (in chloroform) was added to QC sample.</p></section><section id="s0025"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.3. Sample preparation for UHPLC-QE-MS</h3>
<p id="p0065">6 to 7 wampee fruits selected in ripening period were peeled to remove the seeds and the pulp was crushed and homogenized for the further analysis. 50 mg of sample was weighted to an EP tube, and 1000 μL extract solution (methanol: water = 3: 1, with isotopically-labelled internal standard mixture) was added. Then the samples were homogenized at 35 Hz for 4 min and sonicated for 5 min in ice-water bath. The homogenization and sonication cycle was repeated for 3 times. Then the samples were incubated for 1 h at −40 °C and centrifuged at 12000 rpm (RCF = 13,800(×g), <em>R</em> = 8.6 cm) for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was transferred to a liquid phase vial for analysis.</p></section><section id="s0030"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4. Soluble solid and acidity analysis</h3>
<p id="p0070">The pulp of 10 wampee fruits was placed in the gauze to squeeze the juice. The juice was subjected to measurement of total soluble solids (%) and acidity grade (%) by a PAL-BXI ACID 6 (ATAGO, Tokyo, Japan) at room temperature.</p></section><section id="s0035"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.5. Electronic tongue based taste analysis</h3>
<p id="p0075">Electronic tongue analysis was performed using an Alpha MOS Astree II equipped with a sixth sensor system. A total of 7 sensors including AHS, PKS, CTS, NMS, CPS, ANS and SCS were used and Ag/AgCl was used as the reference electrode. 7 to 25 wampee fruits were peeled to remove the seeds and the pulp was crushed and homogenized. The prepared sample was filtered through neutral filter paper or gauze to obtain the sample to be measured. Then the filtered sample was placed in a beaker for electronic tongue testing: sample volume 25 mL, sample collection time 120 s, collection frequency 1.0 s.</p></section><section id="s0040"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.6. GC-TOF-MS based metabolomics analysis</h3>
<p id="p0080">GC-TOF-MS analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890 gas chromatograph coupled with a Leco Pegasus HT time-of-flight mass spectrometer. The system utilized a DB-5MS capillary column. 1 μL aliquot of sample was injected in splitless mode. Helium was used as the carrier gas, the front inlet purge flow was 3 mL min<sup>−1</sup>, and the gas flow rate through the column was 1 mL min<sup>−1</sup>. The initial temperature was kept at 50 °C for 1 min, then raised to 310 °C at a rate of 10 °C min<sup>−1</sup>, then kept for 8 min at 310 °C. The injection, transfer line, and ion source temperatures were 280 °C, 280 °C and 250 °C, respectively. The energy was −70 eV in electron impact mode. The mass spectrometry data were acquired in full-scan mode with the <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> range of 50–500 at a rate of 12.5 spectra per second after a solvent delay of 6.4 min.</p>
<p id="p0085">GC-TOF-MS raw data analysis, including peak extraction, baseline adjustment, deconvolution, alignment and integration, was finished with Chroma TOF (V 4.3×, LECO) software and LECO-Fiehn Rtx5 database was used for metabolite identification by matching the mass spectrum and retention index. Finally, the peaks detected in less than half of QC samples or RSD&gt;30 % in QC samples was removed.</p></section><section id="s0045"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.7. UHPLC-QE-MS based metabolomics analysis</h3>
<p id="p0090">LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using an UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a UPLC HSS T3 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.8 μm) coupled to Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo). The mobile phase consisted of 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 5 mmol/L acetic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The auto-sampler temperature was 4 °C, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur, Thermo). In this mode, the acquisition software continuously evaluates the full scan MS spectrum. The ESI source conditions were set as following: sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary temperature 320 °C, full MS resolution as 60,000, MS/MS resolution as 15,000 collision energy as 10/30/60 in NCE mode, spray Voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or − 3.4 kV (negative), respectively.</p>
<p id="p0095">UHPLC-QE-MS raw data were converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard and processed with an in-house program, which was developed using R and based on XCMS, for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. Then an in-house MS2 database (Biotree DB) was applied in metabolite annotation. The cutoff for annotation was set at 0.3.</p></section><section id="s0050"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.8. Statistical analysis</h3>
<p id="p0100">Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Clustered Heatmap Analysis, Venn diagrams, and Correlation Analysis were analyzed and plotted using SIMCA (version 16.0.2), Origin (version 2023), and R Studio (version 3.3.5).</p></section></section><section id="s0055"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">3. Results and discussion</h2>
<section id="s0060"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.1. Weight, acidity and soluble solids analysis and electronic tongue analysis of wampee fruit</h3>
<p id="p0105">Weight, acidity, soluble solid content, soluble solids to acidity ratio of 4 groups of wampees were presented in Table S2. The WH group had the heaviest mean weight (72.77 ± 20.37), the YL group had the highest soluble solid content (17.39 ± 1.16) and acidity (0.21 ± 0.02), and JX group had the highest soluble solids to acidity ratio (94.91 ± 32.50).In summary, the soluble solid content and acidity of YL group was relatively high and stable, while soluble solids to acidity ratio was low and stable, which indicated that YL group was the group of potentially rich flavor and fuller. The acidity of TH-A6, TH-A9 and TH-A10 in TH group was 0 indicating they were potential pure sweet wampees. Seven sensors were used to analyze the electronic tongue taste of four groups of wampee sample and the relative intensity of AHS (Sourness), CTS (Saltiness), NMS (umami), ANS (Sweetness), SCS (Bitterness), PKS (fair) and CPS (fair) is shown in Table S3. Among the four groups of wampees, the TH group had the lowest mean of AHS(3.34 ± 2.83) and NMS(3.22 ± 2.63), while had the highest mean of CTS (8.62 ± 2.86), ANS (8.80 ± 2.57) and SCS (8.66 ± 2.79). The JX group had the highest mean of NMS (6.64 ± 0.75). The YL group had the lowest mean of CTS (5.05 ± 0.70) and ANS (4.53 ± 0.53). The WH group had the highest mean of CTS (7.24 ± 0.34), while had the lowest mean of SCS (4.72 ± 0.33).</p>
<p id="p0110">Combining the basal data of wampees, the YL group had the highest soluble solids, acidity, but lower sweetness and umami, indicating that YL is not suitable for fresh consumption, but is suitable as a raw material for processed products and extracts. The TH group had the greatest sweetness and the lowest acidity, which makes it suitable for fresh eating, especially A6, A9 and A10. The WH group has the highest weight and least bitterness, and is seedless which could be easily favored by consumers. The JX group exhibits the highest umami and solid to acid ratio, representing the typical wampee variety with a rich mouthfeel for daily consumption.</p></section><section id="s0065"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.2. Correlation analysis and principal component analysis of electronic tongue with taste indicators</h3>
<p id="p0115">Correlation analysis (<a href="#f0010" class="usa-link">Fig. 2</a>A) was carried out for 11 indicators: AHS, CTS, NMS, ANS, SCS, PKS, CPS, weight, soluble solid content, acidity, soluble solids to acidity ratio. Weight was not correlated with any of the other indicators. AHS and NMS showed significant positive correlation with acidity and soluble solids to acidity ratio, while CTS, ANS, PKS, CPS and SCS showed significant negative correlation with acidity and soluble solids to acidity ratio. This indicates that wampee samples with higher sourness intensity correlate with greater perceived umami, while exhibiting lower intensities of saltiness, sweetness, and bitterness. Conversely, samples with stronger sweetness show concurrently higher intensities of saltiness and bitterness. From the correlation results, soluble solid content of wampee was not correlated with taste indexes, due to the fact that the electronic tongue was tested with clear juice as the sample. Weight and soluble solids indexes are also very important because consumers tend to choose heavier fruits when purchasing, and soluble solids do not affect the taste but directly affect the mouthfeel. Acidity was strongly correlated with taste, samples with high acidity also have high AHS and NMS, and low CTS, ANS and SCS. Acidity is easier to compare with electronic tongue flavor, and can be used as a reference before judging the flavor of wampee.</p>

Correlation analysis of metabolites with taste indicators</h3>
<p id="p0130">Correlation analyses of (<a href="#f0015" class="usa-link">Fig. 3</a>B) 11 taste indicators including AHS, CTS, NMS, ANS, SCS, PKS, CPS, weight, soluble solid content, acidity, soluble solids to acidity ratio and 924 metabolites were performed on four groups of wampee sample. According to the metabolite characterization correlation cluster analysis, the 11 indicators of taste were clustered into five clusters, Cluster I for Acidity, AHS and NMS, Cluster II for Soluble solid content, Soluble solids to acidity ratio, Cluster III for Weight, Cluster IV for ANS, CTS and SCS, Cluster V is PKS and CKS. The 924 metabolites were categorized into 4 major clusters based on the data characteristics. Cluster 1 was dominated by glycosides, nucleosides, fatty acids, and alkaloids. Cluster 2 was dominated by organic acids, alkaloids, and terpenes. Cluster 3 was dominated by hydroxyl fatty acids (FAHFAs), lactones, fatty acids, and their derivatives. Cluster 4 was dominated by lipids.</p>
<p id="p0135">Acidity, AHS and NMS were significantly negatively correlated with Cluster 1, significantly positively correlated with Cluster 2. CTS, ANS, SCS had positive correlation with Cluster 1, indicating that abundant glycosides, nucleosides and derivatives were the basis of sweetness and saltiness of wampee. AHS, NMS had positive correlation with Cluster 2, suggesting that organic acids and terpenoids have the potential influences on the sourness and umami of wampee. Soluble solid content had a positive correlation with cluster 3, indicating that esters are the basis of Soluble solid content of wampee. Cluster 4 did not correlate with the flavor indicator and correlated with the weight, indicating that abundant lipids are the basis for the growth and metabolism of wampee fruits, and are non-flavor presenting substances in wampee.</p>
<p id="p0140">In wampee, the substances presenting sourness mainly derived from organic acid compounds, the substances presenting umami mainly include amino acids and nucleosides, the substances presenting sweetness mainly include sugar, glycosides, glycoproteins, glycopeptides, the substances presenting bitterness mainly include polyphenols, alkaloids, terpenoids, bitter peptides. The distinct taste profile of wampee is determined by the specific types and concentrations of the metabolites.</p></section><section id="s0080"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.5. PCA and OPLS-DA analysis of wampee metabolites</h3>
<p id="p0145">Principal component analysis (<a href="#f0020" class="usa-link">Fig. 4</a>A) was performed on the metabolites, and PC1 and PC2 in the PCA analysis could only explain 15.7 % of the features of the original data. PCA separated A13 and A33 independently, and the rest of the samples overlapped, indicating that PCA could not distinguish the four groups of wampees from each other, so OPLS-DA was used for discriminant analysis of four groups of wampees. OPLS-DA is a multivariate statistical method for supervised pattern recognition, which can effectively screen for differential metabolites by excluding effects that are not relevant to the study. The OPLS-DA scoring plot (<a href="#f0020" class="usa-link">Fig. 4</a>B) showed different varietal clusters, and no overlap was observed in the four groups, suggesting that OPLS-DA can distinguish different varieties of wampees.</p>

Analysis of differences in metabolites between groups</h3>
<p id="p0155">Differential metabolites were identified and analyzed (<a href="#f0025" class="usa-link">Fig. 5</a>A) in different groups using multivariate analysis VIP &gt; 1 and univariate statistical significance <em>p</em> &lt; 0.05 as indicators. There were a total of 120 differential metabolites in TH vs JX, 196 in TH vs YL, 172 in TH vs WH, 122 in JX vs YL, 139 in JX vs WH and 117 in WH vs YL, it showed that metabolite differences were greatest in the TH vs YL groups and least in the WH vs YL groups. KEGG enrichment analysis of wampee metabolites</h3>
<p id="p0160">In order to explore the biological differences between different groups of wampees, clustering heatmap analysis (<a href="#f0030" class="usa-link">Fig. 6</a>A) and KEGG enrichment (<a href="#f0030" class="usa-link">Fig. 6</a>B) of wampees metabolites was performed using metabolites as clues to connect possible metabolic pathways and mapping all pathways with metabolite differences.</p>

(B) KEGG enrichment map of wampee metabolites.</p></figcaption></figure><p id="p0165">Four metabolic pathways were enriched extremely significantly in TH vs JX: beta-Alanine metabolism, ABC transporters, Arginine and proline metabolism, Tropane, piperidine and pyridine alkaloid biosynthesis. TH vs YL enriched five extremely significant metabolic pathways: ABC transporters, Arginine and proline metabolism, beta-Alanine metabolism, Biosynthesis of amino acids, Glutathione metabolism. TH vs WH enriched 11 extremely significant metabolic pathways: ABC transporters, Biosynthesis of amino acids, Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, Alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, D-Amino acid metabolism, 2-Oxocarboxylic acid metabolism, Valine, leucine and isoleucine biosynthesis, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, Carbon metabolism, Citrate cycle (TCA cycle), Glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism. WH vs YL enriched 1 extremely significant metabolic pathway for Glycerophospholipid metabolism.</p>
<p id="p0170">Among the enriched differential metabolic pathways, the potential pathways associated with sweet and sour tastes were uncovered. Citrate cycle (TCA cycle), ascorbate and aldarate metabolism are key metabolic pathways influencing organic acid accumulation. Citric acid, malic acid, and L-malic acid were least abundant in TH, while Citric acid and Malic acid were most abundant in YL and WH. Citric acid and Malic acid are the primary sources of sourness in wampee, accounting for the low acidity in TH and the high acidity in YL and WH. Starch and sucrose metabolism, ascorbate and aldarate metabolism are key metabolic pathways influencing sugar accumulation. <span class="font-variant-small-caps">d</span>-Glucose, Sucrose, Trehalose, D-Maltose and D-Tagatose were most abundant in TH, while <span class="font-variant-small-caps">d</span>-Glucose was least abundant in JX, which is the primary reason for its high sweetness in TH and low sweetness in JX. Overall, sweetness and sourness are the most prominent tastes in wampee. The three metabolic pathways associated with acid and sugar accumulation collectively contribute to the taste characteristics of different wampee varieties.</p>
<p id="p0175">The enrichment of metabolites into different metabolic pathways between groups suggests variability and diversity of metabolites and metabolic pathways in different wampees. The biosynthesis of Tropane, piperidine and pyridine alkaloid biosynthesis, Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, Stilbenoid, diarylheptanoid and gingerol biosynthesis reflect the unique metabolic profile of the WH group. Differences in metabolic pathways are reflected by metabolites, which depend on the different wampee varieties. The genome analysis of <a href="#bb0100" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0100">Wei et al. (2024)</a> showed that seedless wampee (WH) is triploid and was found to have high homology with chicken heart wampee (JX), suggesting that seedless wampee (WH) and chicken heart wampee (JX) diverged from the same source. In this paper, the analysis of metabolites and KEGG-enriched metabolic pathways in the WH and JX groups did not reveal a high degree of similarity between the two, which suggests that genetic similarities can account for origins and evolutionary history, but that acquired environments and climates can affect evolution.</p></section><section id="s0095"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.8. Potential biomarker of wampee</h3>
<p id="p0180">Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves was established and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated (Fig. S1) to evaluate the predictive ability of the discriminant markers. 20 potential biomarkers (<a href="#t0005" class="usa-link">Table 1</a>) with AUC values approaching 1 were finally identified. The screened differential metabolites included 4 lipids, 2 alkaloids, 2 lactones, 2 vitamins, 1 fatty acid, and 1 amino acid, 1 coumarin, 1 anthraquinone, 1 flavonoid and 5 others. Among them, Cuscohygrine is a pyrrolizidine alkaloid with central sedative and peripheral anticholinergic effects. Nantenine is widely used as an antimicrobial and antiviral agent with depressant effects on the central nervous system, and is used in the treatment of hypertension, heart disease, and neurasthenia (Ribeiro et al., 2003). (<em>R</em>)-lipoic acid is an antioxidant and an important cofactor in the mitochondrial enzyme complex (<a href="#bb0095" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0095">Watson et al., 2023</a>). Inositol 1,3,4-trisphosphate has important signal transduction functions in organisms (<a href="#bb0120" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0120">Yang et al., 1999</a>). Rhein is an anthraquinone with anti-inflammatory, antitumor, antioxidant, antifibrotic, hepatoprotective and nephroprotective activities (<a href="#bb0110" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0110">Wu et al., 2025</a>). Sissotrin is a member of the carotenoid family, has antimicrobial and antioxidant activity and protects cells from damage caused by oxidative stress (<a href="#bb0080" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0080">Vitor et al., 2004</a>). Skimmin, a coumarin analog present in cone hydrangea, inhibits the deposition of immune complexes and has anti-inflammatory activity (<a href="#bb0090" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bb0090">Wang et al., 2022</a>). Hierarchical clustering and heat map analysis (<a href="#f0035" class="usa-link">Fig. 7</a>A) of the potential biomarkers showed a clear grouping pattern of metabolites. The 20 potential biomarkers were metabolites with characteristic differences, which could be used to distinguish wampee from different groups. Based on the relative quantification to establish the differential metabolite boxplot (<a href="#f0035" class="usa-link">Fig. 7</a>B), the results showed the presence of highly different compounds in the metabolites of different group of wampees.</p>

<p id="p0185">In this study, four groups of wampee sample from the same nursery were analyzed for solid to acid ratio, taste analysis, and non-targeted metabolomics. Based on the electronic tongue, taste discriminations were made for sourness, saltiness, umami, sweetness, and bitterness of four groups of wampee sample. Correlation analysis and principal component analysis by taste indicators showed that A6, A9 and A10 are potentially refreshing and purely sweet varieties. A total of 924 metabolites were identified based on UHPLC-QTOF-MS/MS and GC-TOF-MS untargeted metabolomics. Multivariate analysis revealed significant differences in the metabolomics of samples from different groups of wampees. The metabolic pathways of different wampees were mapped by two-by-two comparative KEGG enrichment analysis of metabolites. Twenty potential biomarkers can be used to distinguish wampee from different groups.</p>
<p id="p0190">Sweet wampee (TH Group) is the variety with the typical characteristics of wampee, and with the least acidity, easily favored by consumers and suitable for fresh eating. Round pear wampee (YL Group) is suitable for processed products and extracted raw materials. Seedless wampee (WH Group) is seedless with a high edible portion. Chicken heart wampee (JX Group) is the typical wampee for daily consumption. These findings provide metabolic profiling support for a full understanding of the wampee, and provide basic support for wampee species tracing, species selection, and specialty food development.</p></section><section id="s0105"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">CRediT authorship contribution statement</h2>
