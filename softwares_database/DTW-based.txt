sample_description: The biological samples consist of pre-processed 32-channel EEG signals recorded from 32 healthy human subjects (15 females, 17 males; mean age 27.19 ± 4.45 years) while watching 40 one-minute emotionally evocative music videos. Each subject contributed 40 trials, with valence and arousal ratings collected via the Self-Assessment Manikin (SAM) on a 1–9 scale. Emotional labels were binarized: High Arousal (HA) vs. Low Arousal (LA) and High Valence (HV) vs. Low Valence (LV), based on a threshold of 5. EEG data were sampled at 128 Hz and already pre-processed (artifact-minimized) in the DEAP dataset, requiring no additional denoising. For analysis, single-channel EEG segments (e.g., P7 for arousal, O2 for valence) were used, extracted in non-overlapping 5-second windows — identified as optimal in experiments — and transformed into Brain Rhythm Entropy Matrices (BREM) via Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)-based rhythm decomposition (delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma) followed by six entropy feature extractions (PSDE, SSE, SE, FE, AE, PE).
software_configuration_description: Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) was applied to compute similarity between a test BREM and template BREMs (one positive/HA or HV, one negative/LA or LV) per subject using Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV). DTW was configured as a nonlinear time-series alignment algorithm minimizing cumulative distance C(i,j) = d(A_i,B_j) + min{C(i−1,j), C(i,j−1), C(i−1,j−1)}, where d is local Euclidean distance, and final DTW distance equals C(n,m), the bottom-right element of the cumulative distance matrix. Smaller DTW values indicate higher similarity. The method operated on BREM matrices (not raw time series), with each BREM representing entropy dynamics across five brain rhythms over a 5 s window. No explicit implementation details (e.g., warping window constraint, step pattern, or open-source library/version) are provided; parameters such as distance metric and boundary conditions are implied but not specified.
