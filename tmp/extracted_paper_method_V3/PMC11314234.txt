</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="abstract1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Chardonnay is one of the most popular white grape wine varieties in the world, but this wine lacks typical aroma, considered a sensory defect. Our research group identified a Chardonnay bud sport with typical muscat characteristics. The goal of this work was to discover the key candidate genes related to muscat characteristics in this Chardonnay bud sport to reveal the mechanism of muscat formation and guide molecular design breeding. To this end, HS−SPME−GC−MS and RNA−Seq were used to analyze volatile organic compounds and the differentially expressed genes in Chardonnay and its aromatic bud sport. Forty-nine volatiles were identified as potential biomarkers, which included mainly aldehydes and terpenes. Geraniol, linalool, and phenylacetaldehyde were identified as the main aroma components of the mutant. The GO, KEGG, GSEA, and correlation analysis revealed <em>HMGR</em>, <em>TPS1</em>, <em>TPS2</em>, <em>TPS5</em>, <em>novel.939</em>, and <em>CYP450</em> as key genes for terpene synthesis. <em>MAO1</em> and <em>MAO2</em> were significantly downregulated, but there was an increased content of phenylacetaldehyde. These key candidate genes provide a reference for the development of functional markers for muscat varieties and also provide insight into the formation mechanism of muscat aroma.</p>
<section id="kwd-group1" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> ‘Chardonnay’ bud sport, SPME−GC−MS, muscat aroma, transcriptome</p></section></section><section id="sec1-molecules-29-03671"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">1. Introduction</h2>
<p>Chardonnay is one of the most popular white grape wine varieties and is widely cultivated around the world [<a href="#B1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B1-molecules-29-03671">1</a>]. However, the lack of a typical aroma of its berry results in a wine lacking in aroma. Bud sports are common variations that can occur in the natural growth of grapes, and these can be used to obtain improved varieties by modifying individual traits without changing the desirable qualities of the parent plant [<a href="#B2-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B2-molecules-29-03671">2</a>]. Bud sport selection is an important grape variety selection technique that has been used to cultivate varieties with desired traits, such as shorter ripening cycles, larger berries, and different berry colors [<a href="#B3-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B3-molecules-29-03671">3</a>]. However, there have been no reports of bud sports producing berries with altered aromas. Further, the underlying regulatory mechanisms of aroma remain poorly understood. Our research group found a bud sport of Chardonnay that produces fruit with a strong aroma but is not significantly different in other biological traits. Aroma is an important intrinsic quality of grape and wine and a key contributor to the overall sensory experience of wine. According to the aroma characteristics, we divided the grapes into muscat varieties like <em>V. vinifera</em> cv. Muscat blanc and cv. Muscat of Alexandria; non-muscat aromatic varieties, e.g., <em>V. vinifera</em> cv. Riesling; and neutral varieties such as <em>V. vinifera</em> cv. Chardonnay [<a href="#B4-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B4-molecules-29-03671">4</a>]. Chardonnay is considered a ‘neutral’ grape variety, so Chardonnay wine is typically fermented in oak barrels to obtain a pleasant aroma, though this greatly increases production costs. Thus, it is necessary to breed new varieties or strains of grapes with better aromas to promote the development of grape and wine industrialization in China.</p>
<p>Recent studies of grape aroma have focused on detecting differences in volatile compounds (VOCs) and the evolution of these VOCs during grape berry ripening [<a href="#B5-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B5-molecules-29-03671">5</a>,<a href="#B6-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B6-molecules-29-03671">6</a>]. There are many kinds of aroma substances in grapes, including terpenes, aldehydes, alcohols, ketones, C13-norisoprenoids, and esters [<a href="#B7-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B7-molecules-29-03671">7</a>], according to their biosynthetic origins as fatty acid derivatives, amino acid derivatives, terpenoids, and phenylpropanoids/benzenoids [<a href="#B8-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B8-molecules-29-03671">8</a>]. Terpenes, such as geraniol, linalool, nerol, α-terpineol, and citronellol, have been identified as the main contributors to the characteristic aroma of muscat varieties [<a href="#B5-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B5-molecules-29-03671">5</a>]. The main aroma components of muscat grapes and wine are produced by the mevalonate (MVA) and methyl pentose (MEP) pathways. The MVA pathway mainly synthesizes sesquiterpene and triterpene in the cytoplasm, and the MEP pathway mainly synthesizes monoterpene, diterpene, and tetraterpene in the plastid. Although there is spatial separation of plastids and cytoplasm in plants, the MVA pathway can also synthesize monoterpenes [<a href="#B9-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B9-molecules-29-03671">9</a>,<a href="#B10-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B10-molecules-29-03671">10</a>]. The odor of phenylacetaldehyde is often described as a rich rose fragrance, and its threshold is low, so this compound plays an important role in the formation of grape characteristic aroma [<a href="#B11-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B11-molecules-29-03671">11</a>]. Phenylacetaldehyde is produced by the deamination and decarboxylation of L-phenylalanine, and subsequent reduction leads to the formation of phenylethanol [<a href="#B12-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B12-molecules-29-03671">12</a>]. Decarboxylase, monoamine oxidase (MAO), and aryl alcohol dehydrogenase (AALDH) are the three key enzymes involved in converting phenylalanine to phenylacetaldehyde and phenylethanol [<a href="#B13-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B13-molecules-29-03671">13</a>]. Although some studies have investigated the types, sources, and influencing factors of grape aroma substances, there has been little in-depth research on the grape aroma formation mechanism.</p>
<p>The main purpose of this study was to explore the possible causes of muscat formation in grape berries by comparing Chardonnay with a muscat-scented bud sport, CH09. We compared the volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in CH and CH09 and identified the main odor-contributing volatile components. Next, we conducted global expression analysis using RNA-Seq to identify the key candidate genes controlling grape aroma and used qPCR to validate our findings. The results will be helpful for understanding the formation of aroma in the bud mutant fruit and the pathways of grape aroma formation and can guide the generation of improved resources for the breeding of new varieties.</p></section><section id="sec2-molecules-29-03671"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. Results</h2>
<section id="sec2dot1-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1. Differences in the Genetic Background and Physiological and Biochemical Indicators between Chardonnay and a Chardonnay Bud Sport</h3>
<p>The genetic identification of CH09 as a bud sport of CH was carried out using the ISSR technique, and the genetic relationship between CH and CH09 was assessed by gel analysis (<a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Figure S1A</a>). NTSYS2.10e software was used for cluster analysis (<a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Figure S1B</a>). The results showed a similar genetic background for CH and CH09, with a similarity coefficient of 0.85. This further confirms that CH09 is a bud mutant of CH.</p>
<p>The physiological properties of CH and CH09 were compared. There was no significant difference between CH and CH09 in the buds, leaves, and branches (<a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Figure S2</a>). The cluster of CH had secondary shoots, but CH09 had no secondary shoots (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f001" class="usa-link">Figure 1</a>).</p>

Volatile Metabolite Analysis of CH and CH09</h3>
<section id="sec2dot2dot1-molecules-29-03671"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.1. Overview of VOC Profiles in CH and CH09</h4>
<p>The composition and relative content of aroma compounds in CH and CH09 were analyzed and identified by HS−SPME and GC−MS. As shown in <a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Table S3</a>, a total of 57 compounds were detected in CH09 and CH, including 18 alcohols, 14 alkanes, 12 aldehydes, 7 terpenes, 4 ketones, and 2 other compounds. Compared with CH, the total concentrations of volatile compounds in CH09 were much lower than those of CH, and the total contents of alcohols, alkanes, ketones, and other classes were also lower than those in CH, but the contents of terpenes (52.59 μg/L) and aldehydes (170.84 μg/L) were higher than those of CH (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f002" class="usa-link">Figure 2</a>A). A total of 42 VOCs were detected in CH, and 36 VOCs were detected in CH09. CH had 20 unique aroma compounds, accounting for 35.08% of the total number of compounds. Fourteen compounds were unique to CH09, accounting for 25% of the total compounds. Twenty-two VOCs were found in both samples, accounting for 38.60% (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f002" class="usa-link">Figure 2</a>B).</p>

Transcription Sequencing Analysis</h3>
<section id="sec2dot3dot1-molecules-29-03671"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.3.1. Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs)</h4>
<p>To study the molecular mechanisms underlying the differences in aromatic volatile metabolites, we next performed transcriptome analysis of mature grape berries from Chardonnay and its mutant. A total of 37.25 G clean data were obtained, with at least 6.04 G for each sample, Q20 ≥ 97.32%, Q30 ≥ 93%, GC content of 46.64%~45.16%, and an overall sequencing error rate less than 0.03%, indicating good quality sequencing data. The mapping ratio was obtained by sequence alignment, with 2.96%~3.27% multiply mapped reads and 85.36%~87.01% uniquely mapped reads. The six samples were consistent with the reference genome, and the repeatability was high, indicating the good accuracy of the experiment (<a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Table S5</a>).</p>
<p>The standards of |log2FC ≥ 0| and adjusted <em>p</em> &lt; 0.05 were used as criteria for the identification of the DEGs. We identified a total of 1251 DEGs from the comparison of CH09 vs. CH, with 743 genes upregulated and 509 genes downregulated (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f004" class="usa-link">Figure 4</a>A). Thus, there was a significant difference at the transcriptional level between CH and CH09 fruits at the mature stage.</p>

Compared with CH, VIT_03s0038g04100 (<em>HMGR</em>), VIT_14s0171g00110 (<em>LIP2</em>), VIT_03s0088g01150 (<em>CYP450</em>), VIT_15s0048g01430, VIT_11s0037g00570 (<em>SHT</em>), VIT_00s0391g00070 (<em>SHKB</em>), VIT_04s0008g06570 (<em>CM2</em>), and VIT_08s0058g01000 (<em>ASP1</em>) exhibited high expression in CH09. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)</h4>
<p>The traditional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes, such as KEGG and GO analysis, does not reveal the comprehensive regulation of pathways. Determining the upstream inputs and downstream effects is essential to fully understand the complexity of pathway regulation, as small changes in the levels or activities of upstream genes may lead to significant changes in the levels or activities of downstream genes. Additionally, if a fixed threshold is used for screening, some genes may not be identified, limiting the accuracy of enrichment analysis [<a href="#B16-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B16-molecules-29-03671">16</a>,<a href="#B17-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B17-molecules-29-03671">17</a>]. To gain more insight than that provided by traditional enrichment methods, we next performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to look at the expression of different genes in a pathway and the overall expression trend of the pathway [<a href="#B18-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B18-molecules-29-03671">18</a>]. </p>
<p>The GSEA results based on the KEGG pathway of the mutant showed the significant downregulation of the monoterpene biosynthesis pathway and phenylalanine-related genes (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f004" class="usa-link">Figure 4</a>C). These results suggest that the monoterpenes of CH09 are not synthesized by the monoterpene biosynthesis pathway but may be regulated by other terpenoid biosynthesis-related genes. This analysis also supports the finding that <em>HMGR</em> is a key gene for monoterpene formation in CH09.</p></section></section><section id="sec2dot4-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4. Correlation Analysis between Related Genes in the Terpene Metabolic Pathway and Terpene Substances</h3>
<p>To explore the relationship between genes in metabolic pathways and terpenoids based on the key genes obtained from GO, KEGG, and GSEA results, the correlation between genes enriched in terpenoid biosynthesis and terpenoid volatiles was analyzed for CH09 and CH (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f006" class="usa-link">Figure 6</a>A). Linalool and geraniol, as the main fragrances of CH09, were significantly negatively correlated with genes <em>TPS1</em>, <em>TPS2</em>, <em>TPS5</em>, and <em>novel.939</em>, but significantly positively correlated with <em>HMGR</em> and <em>CYP450</em>. This indicates that these genes play a key role in the synthesis of linalool and geraniol. We analyzed the correlation between <em>MAO1</em>, <em>MAO2</em>, and aldehydes and found that these two genes were negatively correlated with phenylacetaldehyde (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f006" class="usa-link">Figure 6</a>B).</p>

Discussion</h2>
<section id="sec3dot1-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.1. Differences in Characteristics of Bud Mutation Variety</h3>
<p>Compared with conventional hybridization breeding, bud sport is a consequence of genetic variation in somatic cells leading to the occurrence of qualitative and quantitative phenotypic alteration in plants, which can be observed in many vegetatively propagating plants, including grapes [<a href="#B19-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B19-molecules-29-03671">19</a>]. We studied the biological traits, basic physical and chemical indexes, and nutritional indexes of Chardonnay and bud sport. Considering the above point of view, the results were different from those of previous studies [<a href="#B19-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B19-molecules-29-03671">19</a>,<a href="#B20-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B20-molecules-29-03671">20</a>,<a href="#B21-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B21-molecules-29-03671">21</a>]; that is, it was difficult to find the difference between CH09 and CH only from the external phenotype.</p>
<p>The volatile compounds of CH and CH09 were qualitatively and quantitatively studied by SPME-GC-MS, and OPLS-DA and PCA results showed significant differences in the types and concentrations of VOCs between CH and CH09. Terpenes, especially monoterpenes such as geraniol and linalool, were almost exclusively clustered in CH09 (<a href="#molecules-29-03671-f003" class="usa-link">Figure 3</a>D), and these compounds are the main contributors to muscat aroma [<a href="#B22-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B22-molecules-29-03671">22</a>,<a href="#B23-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B23-molecules-29-03671">23</a>]. In addition, phenylacetaldehyde, which has a desirable rose aroma [<a href="#B24-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B24-molecules-29-03671">24</a>], was detected in CH09 but not in CH. Consistent with previous findings, the analysis showed that Chardonnay is mainly composed of aldehydes and alcohols, compounds that typically lack unique aroma [<a href="#B25-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B25-molecules-29-03671">25</a>]. These results indicated linalool, geraniol, and phenylacetaldehyde were identified as the main VOCs and important biomarkers. Compared with Chardonnay, the aroma characteristics of CH09 were similar to those of muscat varieties, consistent with this bud mutant having a muscat aroma. There were no significant differences in other indicators, indicating that the mutant largely retained the characteristics of the parent Chardonnay. A similar analysis of ‘Kyoho’ and its early ripening bud mutant ‘Fengzao’ showed differences in the maturity stage and berry diameter but no significant differences in other biological traits [<a href="#B26-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B26-molecules-29-03671">26</a>]. Similarly, ‘Jinzao Wuhe’ maintained the early maturity and seedless traits of the parent ‘Himord Seedless’ but had larger fruits than the parent [<a href="#B20-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B20-molecules-29-03671">20</a>]. The phenomenon of grape bud mutation is quite common, with the maintenance of the original genetic structure. These results indicate that bud mutations of different varieties are diverse.</p></section><section id="sec3dot2-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.2. The Key Genes of Muscat Aroma in Bud Mutant</h3>
<p>In order to clarify the molecular mechanism of aroma differences between Chardonnay and bud mutants, the main metabolic pathways and potential key genes of aroma differences between CH and CH09 fruits were explored by transcriptome sequencing. GO analysis revealed enrichment in glycosyltransferase and other pathways. Monoterpene compounds in grapes are present in free and bound glycoside forms, which are interconvertible [<a href="#B27-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B27-molecules-29-03671">27</a>]. The glycosylation reaction is mediated by glycosyltransferases (GTs), which catalyze the transfer of activated nucleotide sugars (such as UDP glucose) to the receptor glycoside ligand to form O-, S- and N-glycosides [<a href="#B28-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B28-molecules-29-03671">28</a>]. GTs can catalyze the glycosylation modification reaction and change the solubility and storage of terpene metabolites [<a href="#B29-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B29-molecules-29-03671">29</a>]. VvGT14 can catalyze the glycosylation of linalool, geraniol, and nerol to form the corresponding glycoside compounds [<a href="#B30-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B30-molecules-29-03671">30</a>]. Glycoside-bound monoterpenes can also be hydrolyzed into free compounds in developing berries, thus becoming a potentially important source of berry aroma [<a href="#B31-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B31-molecules-29-03671">31</a>]. Thus, the enrichment of GTs suggests a mechanistic basis for the formation and loss of grape rose aroma. To clarify the molecular mechanism of aroma differences between Chardonnay and the bud mutant, the main metabolic pathways and the potential key genes of aroma differences between CH and CH09 fruits were explored by transcriptome sequencing. <em>HMGR</em> is a key enzyme in the MVA pathway, which is mainly involved in the synthesis of sesquiterpenes and triterpenes. <em>HMGR</em> can have different effects on the aroma formation of grape varieties with different aroma types [<a href="#B32-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B32-molecules-29-03671">32</a>]. <em>VvHMGR3</em> improved the types of aroma components in strawberry-flavor grape, with significant increases in linalool, α-terpineol, and β-pinene content [<a href="#B33-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B33-molecules-29-03671">33</a>]. <em>VvHMGR3</em> is linked with the genes in the carotenoid biosynthesis and MEP pathways, which can interact with MVA kinases [<a href="#B34-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B34-molecules-29-03671">34</a>]. The expression of <em>HMGR</em> was positively correlated with the aroma intensity of muscat grape [<a href="#B23-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B23-molecules-29-03671">23</a>]. In this study, the KEGG enrichment and expression pattern analysis showed that <em>HMGR</em> was upregulated in CH09. However, no other sesquiterpenes and triterpenes were found in CH09, and instead, monoterpenes were prominent. This is similar to the above findings that there is an exchange between the MVA and MEP pathways. MVA is the main pathway for the synthesis of CH09 monoterpenes, with <em>HMGR</em> playing an important role. In addition, we found that two monoamine oxidase genes <em>MAO1</em> and <em>MAO2</em> were downregulated in CH09, which would seem inconsistent with the high content of phenylacetaldehyde in CH09. Phenylalanine can be directly catalyzed by phenylacetaldehyde synthases (PAASs) to form phenylacetaldehyde in the main pathway of phenylacetaldehyde biosynthesis. Phenylalanine can also form phenylethylamine under the catalysis of aromatic amino acid decarboxylases (AAADs), and phenylethylamine can form phenylacetaldehyde under the catalysis of MAO, but the formation of phenylacetaldehyde through this pathway is very limited [<a href="#B35-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B35-molecules-29-03671">35</a>]. The low expression of <em>MAO1</em> and <em>MAO2</em> could change the biosynthesis pathway of phenylacetaldehyde, with the direct conversion of phenylalanine into more phenylacetaldehyde. Similarly, Libin Sun et al. (2021) found that a low expression of <em>MAO</em> inhibited the formation of phenylacetaldehyde from phenylethylamine but promoted the degradation of phenylalanine to directly form phenylacetaldehyde, resulting in an increase in the phenylacetaldehyde content in Lentinula edodes [<a href="#B13-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B13-molecules-29-03671">13</a>]. GO and KEGG were screened by fixed thresholds, so some genes may not have been identified, limiting the accuracy of enrichment analysis. Therefore, GSEA and correlation analysis were carried out. The overall downregulation of the monoterpene biosynthesis pathway and the positive correlation between linalool, geraniol, and <em>HMGR</em> showed that <em>HMGR</em> acts as a key gene regulating the synthesis of monoterpenes in CH09. Studies have shown that the transient expression of <em>HMGR</em> in tomato increased the content of nerol by 5.7 times and linalool by 1.8 times [<a href="#B36-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B36-molecules-29-03671">36</a>]. The genes directly involved in monoterpene synthesis were found to be downregulated in the GSEA; however, we measured high levels of monoterpenes in CH09. These results indicate that there is an exchange between the MVA and MEP pathways, such that MVA is the main pathway for the synthesis of a large number of monoterpenes in the bud mutant, and <em>HMGR</em> is the key to the synthesis of monoterpenes in this pathway. Further analysis will be required to carefully determine the function of the identified genes and pathways and clarify their direct effects on monoterpene content.</p></section></section><section id="sec4-molecules-29-03671"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">4. Materials and Methods</h2>
<section id="sec4dot1-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.1. Materials </h3>
<p>The experiments were performed at the Saishang Jiangnan winery in Ningxia (38°06′ N, 105°56′ E). Three-year-old CH and CH09 plants were used as experimental materials. The bud sport emerged from one branch of the Chardonnay plants, and berries were enriched with muscat flavor. The plant spacing was 3.0 m × 1.0 m, in the north–south direction, with a hedge frame and a ‘factory’ shape. Vines were managed with standard cultivation techniques, such as pruning, yield requirements, and harvest standards.</p>
<p>After the fruit was ripe (90 days after flowering), 10 clusters of each sample were randomly selected to measure the cluster weight and longitudinal and transverse diameters. Samples of similar size and lacking evidence of damage from pests or diseases were selected from the upper, middle, and lower parts of the cluster. In total, 6 to 8 berries were collected from each cluster, and 30 berries were randomly selected for the determination of single berry weight, as well as the longitudinal and transverse diameters. After measurement, fruit samples were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C for subsequent analysis.</p>
<p>Additional details about the materials used for bud mutation identification are listed in <a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Table S7</a>.</p></section><section id="sec4dot2-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.2. DNA Extraction and ISSR Amplification</h3>
<p>DNA extraction was performed using a Tiangen DNA secure plant genomic DNA extraction kit. The quality and quantity of DNA were detected by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and NanoDrop, respectively. The dilution of each DNA sample (until reaching a concentration of 20 ng/μL) was performed according to the concentration of each DNA sample. Extracted genomic DNA of genotypes was amplified by PCR (polymerase chain reaction) using nine ISSR primers, which are listed in <a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Table S8</a>. The parameters of the PCR reaction system are described in <a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Table S9</a>, and amplification in a thermal cycler was performed using the settings described in <a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Table S10</a>. The products obtained from the PCR were detected by 1.0% agarose gel electrophoresis. </p></section><section id="sec4dot3-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.3. Characteristic Evaluation </h3>
<p>The determination of single berry weight, longitudinal diameter, and transverse diameter was performed according to Wang [<a href="#B37-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B37-molecules-29-03671">37</a>]. The determination of total soluble solids (TSSs), titratable acid (TA), reducing sugar, total phenolic, and tannin was performed as previously described [<a href="#B38-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B38-molecules-29-03671">38</a>]. </p></section><section id="sec4dot4-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.4. Extraction of Volatile Metabolites</h3>
<p>The determination of volatile compounds was performed as described in [<a href="#B5-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B5-molecules-29-03671">5</a>], with slight modification. The grape was ground into 50 g homogenate in liquid nitrogen (during which 5 g PVPP was added). After soaking for 2.5 h, the supernatant was separated through centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 10 min. Next, 2 mL of grape juice was added to a headspace bottle containing 1.5 g of NaCl, and 40 μL (394 g/L) of 4-methyl-1-pentanol was added before the bottles were sealed. All samples were uniformly vibrated with a magnetically heated agitator at 40 °C set to 30 min. </p>
<p>HS-SPME was performed using automatic injection by AOC-6000 (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Kyoto). The sample was activated for 3 min before injection, and the activation temperature was 280 °C. After activation, the extraction head was automatically inserted into the sample headspace bottle and extracted at 40 °C for 20 min. Desorption for 3 min was used for GC-MS separation and identification.</p>
<p>GC–MS analysis was performed by separating the desorbed volatiles in an Agilent TQ8050 (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) gas chromatograph with the following conditions: chromatographic column, DB-WAX-U1 (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm) (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA); carrier gas, He; flow rate, 37 mL/min, split injection, with a split ratio chromatographic column flow rate of 1 mL/min; and a purge flow rate of 6 mL/min. The column oven temperature program was 68 min in length, with an initial temperature of 40 °C, for 1 min, followed by an increase to 130 °C at a rate of 3 °C/min, and then an increase to 250 °C at a rate of 4 °C/min for 8 min. The ion source was EI, and the ion source and interface temperature were 200 °C and 250 °C, respectively. The solvent delay time was 1.5 min, the start time was 2 min, and the end time was 67.5 min. The acquisition mode was Q3 full scan, with start at 35 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> and end at 500 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em>.</p>
<p>Qualitative and quantitative analyses of volatile metabolites were performed. VOCs were identified using the NIST 14 standard spectral library and further verified with retention indices (RIs) of Alkanes C8 to C40 (Sigma-Aldrich, Shanghai, China) on the DB-Wax column (30 m × 250 μm × 0.25 μm). The RIs were compared with those of the literature using the same column, and the same compound was identified as having an RI difference of five or less.</p>
<p>The peak area of each metabolite was normalized using the percentage of the identified component peak area in the area of the internal standard component multiplied by the inner standard mass.</p>
<p>The odor activity value (OAV) is widely used to evaluate the contribution of aroma components and is the ratio of the actual concentration of an aroma compound to the odor threshold [<a href="#B39-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B39-molecules-29-03671">39</a>]. In this study, OAVs were calculated for individual components by dividing the concentration by the previously reported odor threshold [<a href="#B11-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B11-molecules-29-03671">11</a>,<a href="#B27-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B27-molecules-29-03671">27</a>,<a href="#B40-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="B40-molecules-29-03671">40</a>].</p></section><section id="sec4dot5-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.5. RNA Extraction and Sequencing Analysis</h3>
<p>RNA extraction and the sequencing analysis of CH and CH09 were performed by Novogene Co., Ltd., Beijing, China (<a href="https://magic.novogene.com/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://magic.novogene.com/</a>, accessed on 1 September 2023). Sequencing libraries were generated using NEBNext<sup>®</sup> Ultra™ II RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina<sup>®</sup> (NEB #E7775L). PCR was performed with random oligonucleotides as primers and the polymerase provided in the NEB kit. HISAT2v2.0.5 was used to construct the index of the reference genome. FeatureCounts (1.5.0-p3) was used to calculate the FPKM of each gene by normalizing the read count based on gene length and the total number of mapped reads. The key candidate genes were selected for qRT-PCR verification. The gene-specific primers used in the analysis were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China), and the sequences are listed in <a href="#app1-molecules-29-03671" class="usa-link">Supplementary Table S11</a>. Actin was used as a reference gene to normalize all gene expression levels by the previous reports. The 2<sup>−ΔΔCt</sup> method was used to calculate the relative expression levels of genes.</p></section><section id="sec4dot6-molecules-29-03671"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">4.6. Data Statistics and Analysis</h3>
<p>All experimental data in this work are expressed as mean ±standard deviation (SD) for three biological replicates. The <em>t</em>-test was used to calculate the differences as <em>p</em>-values using SPSS26.0 (IBM Corporation, Endicott, NY, USA). Orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed using SIMCA 14.1 software, and predictions were calculated. SPSS 26.0 software was used for single-factor analysis, and <em>p</em> &lt; 0.05 and VIP ≥ 1 were used to screen the different aroma components.</p></section></section><section id="sec5-molecules-29-03671"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">5. Conclusions</h2>
<p>Compared with Chardonnay, the kinds and total content of volatile compounds were significantly lower in the bud mutant, but there were significantly higher numbers and concentrations of terpenes and aldehydes, especially linalool, geraniol, and phenylacetaldehyde. Transcription sequencing and correlation analysis revealed <em>HMGR</em>, <em>TPS1</em>, <em>TPS2</em>, <em>TPS5</em>, <em>novel.939</em>, and <em>CYP450</em> as the key genes involved in terpene synthesis. The downregulation of <em>MAO1</em> and <em>MAO2</em> reduced the conversion rate of phenylethylamine to phenylacetaldehyde but favored the main synthesis pathway of phenylacetaldehyde, that is, phenylalanine degradation directly to phenylacetaldehyde, thus explaining the high content of phenylacetaldehyde in the mutant. These findings provide experimental data and valuable insights into cultivating clonal varieties of Chardonnay to maintain the quality and quantity of aromatic compounds for wine production and can guide molecular marker development. The next step will be to functionally verify the screened key candidate genes and develop functional markers to determine the molecular mechanism of muscat aroma synthesis.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgments</h2>
