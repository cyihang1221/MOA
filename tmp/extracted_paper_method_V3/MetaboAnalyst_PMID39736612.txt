>Methods   Study design and subject management   Study setting and recruitment   This study adopted a comparative approach and was conducted in the neonatal intensive care units (NICU) of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital, a tertiary A-level specialist hospital focusing on maternal and child health care. The recruitment process took place from January 2023 to December 2023, targeting infants with very low birth weight. The study strictly adhered to ethical principles and was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital (Approval No.: [2022]YLSA No. (6)-07). Subject selection and consent   Eligible infants were identified based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) birth weight of less than 1500 g; (2) immediate transfer to the NICU after birth; and (3) signing of relevant informed consent forms by the families of the infants. Exclusion criteria included: (1) incomplete data collection; (2) receipt of parenteral nutrition for fewer than 14 days; and (3) the presence of congenital genetic metabolic diseases or severe congenital malformations. To ensure the protection of subject identity and maintain confidentiality, all personal identifiers were removed from the collected data, and a unique study identifier was assigned to each participant. Informed consent was obtained from the families of the infants prior to any study-related procedures, including the collection of samples and clinical information. Randomization and intervention   In this study, a simple random sampling method was employed to randomly assign eligible VLBW infants into two groups: the SMOF group and the soybean oil group. To ensure fairness in allocation, the randomization sequence was determined by computer-generated random numbers. During the study period, both groups of subjects adhered to the same parenteral and enteral nutrition protocols. For parenteral nutrition, the SMOF group was administered SMOF lipid emulsion (trade name: Hewen), while the soybean oil group received soybean oil-based lipid emulsion (trade name: Intralipid).   Regarding the nutritional regimen for the subjects, the specific details are as follows: on the first day of parenteral nutrition, the lipid emulsion dose was set at 1 g/kg/day; on the second day, the dose was increased to 2 g/kg/day; on the third day, the dose was further increased to 3 g/kg/day; and from the fourth day onwards, the dose was maintained at 3.5 g/kg/day or below. Both groups of subjects received a uniformly formulated glucose and amino acid solution. Enteral feeding was initiated within 24 h of birth, and if the mother’s milk supply was insufficient, donor human milk was provided as a supplement. As the volume of enteral feeding gradually increased, when the milk intake reached 60 ml/kg, the dose of lipid emulsion was gradually reduced based on its contribution to 35% of the calories provided by parenteral nutrition. Finally, when the enteral feeding volume reached 150 ml/kg, parenteral nutrition was completely discontinued. Sample collection and processing   Umbilical cord blood at birth and blood samples at 14 days of parenteral nutrition were collected from both groups for Ultra-high Performance Liquid Chromatography - Tandem Mass Spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). Specifically, the umbilical cord blood samples from the SMOF group were designated as the SMOF cord group, and the blood samples collected 14 days after birth were designated as the SMOF 14d group. Similarly, the umbilical cord blood samples from the soybean oil group were designated as the Intralipid cord group, and the blood samples obtained 14 days after birth were designated as the Intralipid 14d group. To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results, all samples were processed and analyzed according to standard protocols. Metabolomics detection   Blood sampling   Cord blood samples were collected following umbilical cord clamping in newborns. For the D14 samples, blood was obtained during routine monitoring of relevant indices in preterm infants receiving parenteral nutrition, utilizing blood draws conducted as part of standard care. These samples were drawn into Vacuum collection tubes (polyester gel/silica additives, Becton Dickinson, Shanghai, China) and allowed to rest at room temperature for 1 h. They were then centrifuged at ≤ 1300×g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant serum was subsequently transferred into 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes using a pipette, with each tube containing no less than 100 μL of serum. The tubes were then stored at -80 °C for future analysis. To ensure accurate identification and tracking of samples throughout the study, each collection tube was meticulously labeled. Reagents and instruments   Chromatographically pure methanol (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure acetonitrile (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure formic acid (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure water (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure isopropanol (Fisher Scientific). Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled with Fourier transform mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q Exactive), Thermo Fisher Scientific; HSS T3 chromatographic column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm), Waters Corporation, USA. JXDC-20 Nitrogen Evaporator, provided by Shanghai Jingxin Industrial Development Co., Ltd.; LNG-T88 Benchtop Rapid Centrifugal Concentration and Drying Apparatus, manufactured by Taicang Huamei Biochemical Instrument Factory; Wonbio-96c High-Throughput Tissue Homogenizer, supplied by Shanghai Wanbai Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; SBL-10TD Ultrasonic Cleaner with a capacity of 300 W-10 L, from Ningbo Xinzhi Biotech Co., Ltd.; Centrifuge 5430R High-Speed Refrigerated Centrifuge, produced by Eppendorf, Germany; and NewClassic MF MS105DU Electronic Balance, manufactured by Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland. Extraction of serum samples   After thawing the serum samples stored at -80 °C, 100 μL of the liquid sample was added to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube with 300 μL of a solution (acetonitrile: methanol = 1:1, v/v) to extract metabolites. The mixture was vortexed thoroughly for 30 s and then subjected to low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min(5 °C and 40 kHz ). Following centrifugation, the supernatant was collected, dried under nitrogen, and re-solubilized with 100 μL of a solution (acetonitrile: water = 1:1, v/v). A second round of ultrasonic extraction and centrifugation was performed. The supernatant was transferred to sample vials for UHPLC-MS/MS analysis. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal volumes of all samples, and a QC sample was inserted every 5–15 samples [ 20 – 23 ]. UHPLC-MS/MS analysis   The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water: acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile: isopropanol: water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v) (solvent B). The separation gradient in positive ion mode was as follows: 0–5 min, mobile phase B increased from 0 to 100%; 5–6.3 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%; 6.3–6.4 min, mobile phase B decreased from 100 to 0%; 6.4–8 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%. In negative ion mode: 0–5 min, mobile phase B increased from 0 to 100%; 5–6.3 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%; 6.3–6.4 min, mobile phase B decreased from 100 to 0%; 6.4–8 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was 40 °C.   MS conditions: The mass spectrometry signal acquisition used both positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scanning range of m/z: 70-1050. The ion spray voltage was set at 3400 V for positive ions and 2800 V for negative ions. The sheath gas was set at 40 psi, the auxiliary heating gas at 10 psi, and the ion source heating temperature was maintained at 400 °C. The collision energy cycled between 20-40-60 V. The MS1 resolution was set at 60,000, and the MS2 resolution was 7500. Data preprocessing and library searching   The UHPLC-MS/MS raw data were using Progenesis QI software (Waters, Milford, USA) through baseline filtering, peak identification, peak integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. The resulting data matrix, containing sample names, m/z, retention time, and peak intensities, was exported for further analysis. Metabolites were identified by searching the Human Metabolome Database(HMDB)( http://www.hmdb.ca/ ), Metlin Database ( https://metlin.scripps.edu/ ), and the self-compiled Majorbio Database (MJDB) of Majorbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).   The data matrix obtained from the database search was uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform ( https://cloud.majorbio.com ) for analysis. First, the data matrix was pre-processed as follows: At least 80% of the metabolic features detected in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, the minimum value in the data matrix was selected to fill in the missing values, and each metabolic signature was normalized to the sum. To reduce errors caused by sample preparation, the response intensities of the sample mass spectrometry peaks were normalized using the sum normalization method, resulting in a normalized data matrix [ 24 – 26 ]. Variables of QC samples with a relative standard deviation (RSD) > 30% were excluded and log10 transformed to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis. Differential metabolite analysis   The R package “ropls” (Version 1.6.2) was used to perform principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), with 7-cycle interactive validation to evaluate the stability of the model. Metabolites with variable importance in the projection (VIP) > 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05, adjusted using the Bonferroni correction method, were identified as significantly different metabolites, based on the VIP obtained from the OPLS-DA model and the FDR generated from the  p -values obtained using Student’s t-test [ 27 ].   The top 30 differential metabolites, ranked by VIP values, were mapped to their biochemical pathways through metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis using the KEGG PATHWAY database ( http://www.genome.jp/kegg/ ). These metabolites were classified according to the pathways they were involved in or the functions they performed. Enrichment analysis was used to determine whether a group of metabolites appeared in a functional node [ 28 ]. The principle was that the annotation analysis of a single metabolite developed into the annotation analysis of a group of metabolites. The Python package “scipy.stats” ( https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/ ) was used to perform enrichment analysis to identify the most relevant biological pathways and differential metabolites related to the experimental interventions [ 29 ]. Additionally, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of these metabolites and compute Area Under Curve (AUC) values [ 30 ].
