</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="Abs1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p id="Par1">Currently, with the bloom in artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms, various human-centered smart systems can be utilized, especially in cognitive computing, for the detection of various chronic brain diseases such as epileptic seizure. The primary goal of this research article is to propose a novel human-centered cognitive computing (HCCC) method for segment-wise seizure classification by employing multiresolution extracted data with directed transfer function (DTF) features, termed as the multiresolution directed transfer function (MDTF) approach. Initially, the multiresolution information of the epileptic seizure signal is extracted using a multiresolution adaptive filtering (MRAF) method. These seizure details are passed to the DTF where the information flow of high frequency bands is computed. Thereafter, different measures of complexity such as approximate entropy (AEN) and sample entropy (SAEN) are computed from the extracted high frequency bands. Lastly, a k-nearest neighbor (k-NN) and support vector machine (SVM) are used for classifying the EEG signal into non-seizure and seizure data depending on the multiresolution based information flow characteristics. The MDTF approach is tested on a standard dataset and validated using a dataset from a local hospital. The proposed technique has obtained an average sensitivity of 98.31%, specificity of 96.13% and accuracy of 98.89% using SVM classifier. The average detection rate of the MDTF approach is 97.72% which is greater than the existing approaches. The proposed MDTF method will help neuro-specialists to locate seizure information drift which occurs within the consecutive segments and between two channels. The main advantage of the MDTF approach is its capability to locate the seizure activity contained by the EEG signal with accuracy. This will assist the neurologists with the precise localization of the epileptic seizure automatically and hence will reduce the burden of time-consuming epileptic seizure analysis.</p>
<section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Cognitive computing, Directed transfer function, Epilepsy, Multiresolution, Segment</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p id="Par2">Recently, the rate of chronic diseases is constantly increasing and has become the greatest threat to human health. Currently, brain-inspired cognitive computing has received noticeable attention especially in the early detection of epileptic seizures (An et al. <a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">2020</a>). Epilepsy is a disorder of the brain characterized by recurrent seizures. Almost, fifty million people suffer from epilepsy globally (An et al. <a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">2020</a>). EEG is extensively utilized to capture brain activities and in the analysis of the behaviour of epileptic seizures. People with epilepsy, generally register significant changes in the EEG frequency and amplitude which are important features in the detection of seizures. Epileptic patients experience dangerous situations which can lead to permanent brain damage. Effective and timely seizure diagnosis will help neuro-experts in recording and analysing seizures accurately thereby enhancing the quality of life of epileptic patients.</p>
<p id="Par3">Several studies have shown usefulness in the epileptic seizure diagnosis. But, one of the crucial challenges lies in subject variability and the treatment outcomes. Hence, patient-specific methods are required to diagnose the seizure state accurately and help in choosing an optimal treatment method.</p>
<p id="Par4">Removing physiological artifacts from a given signal while analysing the EEG signal is a very crucial concern. The amplitude of artifacts is much higher in comparison to regular EEG data (Mannan et al. <a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">2016</a>). To accurately analyse an EEG signal, artifacts need to be removed from the signal as much as possible. In the past, numerous methods have been suggested for eliminating these artifacts (Mannan et al. <a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">2016</a>). Recently MRAF is one such method used to eliminate physiological artifacts effectively (Mannan et al. <a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">2016</a>).</p>
<p id="Par5">Several automated seizure detection approaches have been proposed based on epileptic EEG information to extract time-based, frequency-based, time–frequency based, and non-linear features (Sharma and Pachori <a href="#CR43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR43">2015</a>). These features are utilized in seizure classification methods to determine the highest accuracy. Statistical based time domain features include mean, variance, entropy, autocorrelation, and zero-crossing rate. Other time based methods include Welch spectral analysis (Polat and Güneş <a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">2007</a>), phase slope index (Rana et al. <a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">2012</a>), dynamic time warping (DTW) technique (Dalton et al. <a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">2012</a>), successive peaks and estimation of their minima (Runarsson and Sigurdsson <a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">2005</a>) which have also attained acceptable detection results.</p>
<p id="Par6">In order to investigate non-linear behaviour of the epileptic seizure signal entropy, correlation dimension, and Lyapunov exponent are incorporated as features for the EEG seizure diagnosis (Sharma and Pachori <a href="#CR43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR43">2015</a>). In addition, multiresolution analysis of seizure data is performed based on time–frequency based discrete wavelet transform (DWT) (Franaszczuk and Bergey <a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">1998</a>) and have obtained a true detection rate of 98.3%, sensitivity of 91.44%, and a specificity rate of 99.34%. The average epileptic detection rate for a seizure was computed at 95.39%.</p>
<p id="Par7">At present, epileptic detection approaches present in literature have less selectivity due to the presence of very large number of non-seizure segments and misidentification of non-seizure segments as seizure segments. Therefore, it is of great importance to further enhance the epileptic seizure detection performance thereby assisting neuro-experts in timely detection of epileptic EEG zones.</p>
<p id="Par8">In addition, segment-wise seizure classification plays a vital role in epilepsy detection (Franaszczuk and Bergey <a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">1998</a>). Segments are smaller sections of time within which considerable variations in the EEG signal can be seen when a seizure occurs and the variations last till the following few consecutive segments. Therefore, segment-wise seizure classification will be of great advantage in the identification of various stages of a seizure in the EEG signal.</p>
<p id="Par9">Moreover, HCCC can help in the development of various approaches for the early detection of an epileptic seizure effectively. Currently, much of the analysis focuses on exchange of information between the consecutive epileptic segments that an EEG signal includes (Franaszczuk and Bergey <a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">1998</a>). The DTF technique helps to extract details of the seizure data exchange between two consecutive seizure segments. Moreover, the multiresolution analysis of EEG frequency details present in each segment will provide more precise details related to the seizure information. The primary goal of this research paper is a proposal for a novel HCCC technique using the DTF method for analysis of seizure information flow, segment-wise. This research paper presents segment-wise epileptic seizure detection using a combined approach of multiresolution based adaptive filtering (MRAF) (Yedurkar and Metkar <a href="#CR53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR53">2020</a>) with the DTF method termed as multiresolution directed transfer function (MDTF). Multiresolution analysis along with DTF per segment is performed to properly distinguish seizure characteristics from the non-seizure EEG signal. The experimentation outcomes indicate that the MDTF approach successfully extracts information about the significant distinction between the various frequency bands of the scalp epileptic data. The proposed MDTF approach has high sensitivity, specificity, and average detection rate for epileptic seizure EEG diagnosis.</p>
<p id="Par10">This research work contributes in the following significant ways: (1) to provide an analysis of epileptic seizure zone using the MRAF method; (2) to compute the flow of seizure information from one segment to another segment particularly between seizure and non-seizure segments; (3) to apply the MDTF method for segment-wise classification of the epileptic EEG signal into seizure and non-seizure regions; and (4) computation of an average detection rate for the proposed MDTF method.</p>
<p id="Par11">The organization of the remainder of this research work ensues: section "<a href="#Sec2" class="usa-link">Proposed MDTF approach</a>" describes the proposed MDTF methodology, section "<a href="#Sec12" class="usa-link">Experimentation and discussions</a>" includes results of the experiments and discussions. Finally, section "<a href="#Sec24" class="usa-link">Conclusion</a> concludes the research paper highlighting the future scope.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Proposed MDTF approach</h2>
<p id="Par12">Figure <a href="#Fig1" class="usa-link">1</a> represents the workflow diagram of the MDTF proposed in this paper. The entire method is divided into three parts. The first part performs multiresolution EEG analysis using the MRAF technique (Yedurkar and Metkar <a href="#CR53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR53">2020</a>). This step localizes the epileptogenic zone present in the epileptic data accurately locating the start and end of a seizure. The second part is the computation of information flow between consecutive EEG segments using the DTF method (Dong et al. <a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">2018</a>), thereby calculating complexity measures like AEN and SAEN. Finally, the calculated seizure details formulated in the first two parts, referred to as the MDTF approach are classified in to seizure and non-seizure segments by making use of k-NN and SVM classifiers.</p>

<p id="Par14">In the MRAF technique, DWT is employed initially to decompose the seizure EEG information in to bands. In DWT, the volume of detailed information in the signal is modified by the filtering operations, and the scale is also altered by upsampling and downsampling operations. Upsampling a signal refers to raising the rate of sampling of a signal by appending new samples to the signal; but on the other hand, downsampling reduces the resolution in order to be able to visualize more details of the signal. In addition, an increase in the number of decomposition levels will result in narrowing down of every item of EEG band information which ends up with a better frequency resolution. The decision about the level of decomposition is on the basis of the frequency band features that are desired Yedurkar and Metkar (<a href="#CR53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR53">2020</a>). The decomposition of the data is followed by the application of the soft thresholding method to calculate the threshold values for the extracted wavelet coefficients. A soft thresholding technique is applied to the extracted wavelet coefficients. <p id="Par24">The DTF method is used to inspect the functional connectivity during various states of the brain (Franaszczuk et al. <a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">1994</a>). DTF is employed to localize the seizure zone in patients with partial epilepsy (Franaszczuk et al. <a href="#CR15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR15">1994</a>). The seizure regions computed by the DTF approach are highly correlated with the brain regions identified by clinicians as the epileptic onset zones (Ding et al. <a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">2007</a>). In this research work, the DTF method is used in order to extract seizure information flow between two consecutive EEG segments.</p>
<p id="Par25">The surrogate channel signal is segmented into M number of segments, <span xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="IEq15"><math id="M38"><mrow><msub><mi>Y</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="italic">surrogate</mi></mrow></msub><mrow></mrow><mfenced close=")" open="("><mi>m</mi></mfenced></mrow></math></span>, where m varies from 0 to M. In this research work, a segment size of 3 s without overlap is considered for analysis due to following reasons:</p>
<ul class="list" style="list-style-type:none">
<li>
<span class="label">(i)</span><p class="display-inline" id="Par26">There will be no significant effect among segments with and without overlaps in the detection of seizure regions in EEG (Franaszczuk and Bergey <a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">1998</a>).</p>
</li>
<li>
<span class="label">(ii)</span><p class="display-inline" id="Par27">Also, a longer segment size may lead to missing of seizure details in the signal. Hence, there is an insignificant effect for various non-overlapping segment sizes (Franaszczuk and Bergey <a href="#CR14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR14">1998</a>).</p>
</li>
</ul>

<p id="Par34">Lately, entropy is used for describing the complex nature of epileptic EEG information (Gupta and Pachori <a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">2019</a>; Aung and Wongsawat <a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">2020</a>). If the entropy value is lower, it means that the data includes more information and is more predictable than when the value of the entropy is higher. This research work makes use of AEN as well as SAEN to measure how complex the epileptic EEG signal is for analysing it so as to be able to describe the capability of MDTF to calculate the complexity in the detection of the epileptic seizures.</p>

<section id="Sec10"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">k-Nearest neighbour</h4>
<p id="Par37">k-NN is one of the simplest statistical discrimination approaches utilized in the classification of information. The functioning of this non-parametric technique depends on a set of objects that are labelled, a metric distance and lastly a number of nearest neighbours (k). k-NN is a distance-weighted technique that is utilized frequently (Schalk et al. <a href="#CR40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR40">2004</a>). It assigns weights to neighbours on the basis of a calculation of the distance. The weight coefficient is the reciprocal of the squared distance. This leads to the neighbours that are the closest being weighted more heavily. In this paper, a Euclidian distance is utilized to generate the distance metric, and the variation in ‘k’ is from 1 to 10. It is noted that the classifier performance improves for k = 10, hence this value is used in the proposed work.</p></section><section id="Sec11"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Support vector machine</h4>
<p id="Par38">The classification method helps to differentiate the unknown data into its appropriate classes depending on the training set containing known observations. SVM is a widely used classifier in the field of epileptic detection. SVM utilizes a non-linear transformation technique which classifies samples into linearly separable ones (Guler and Ubeyli <a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">2007</a>). The explanation of SVM working with different kernel functions is described in Cristianini and Shawe-Taylor (<a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">2000</a>). The radial basis function (RBF) -SVM is used for classifying the epileptic seizure EEG signals (Blondel et al. <a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">2008</a>; Ahmadlou et al. <a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">2010</a>). In this research paper, RBF-SVM is employed for classification of the seizure segments in to seizure and non-seizure segments, as RBF-SVM is able to handle high dimensional and non-linear data. <p id="Par41">The data bank used for this research is one set of gold standard datasets obtained from the Temple University Hospital Electroencephalography Corpus (TUH EEG) (Picone and Obeid <a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">2013</a>) and two sets of epileptic seizure EEG signals procured from a hospital that is local.</p>

<p id="Par51">Here, the MRAF extracted seizure signal is initially partitioned into segments of 3 s without any over-fitting of the segments. The choice of the segment size is very critical considering both situations such as segments with overlaps and with no overlaps. When accounting for DTF based information flow estimation, scientifically there is no major effect on epileptic detection outcomes when considering segment sizes between 1 to 5 s, with and without over-fitting of segments (Dong et al. <a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">2018</a>). The early diagnosis of a seizure will enable the clinicians to take preventive measures as soon as possible thereby decreasing the suffering of the epileptic patients. Therefore, smaller length segments are considered to facilitate the detection of the seizure arrival accurately.</p>
<p id="Par52">The DTF method is applied to the MRAF processed signal segment wise. The information flow is executed for various frequency bands both, for before and after the occurrence of the seizure signal. The resultant behaviour of the epileptic seizure details is shown in Fig. <a href="#Fig5" class="usa-link">5</a>. It is noted that the full band frequency data exhibits more similar behaviour to that of the frequency details contained in the gamma frequency band. This inference evidences the occurrence of epileptic seizure activity in the high frequency region which has proven to be important and useful in the early diagnosis of epileptic seizures (Sharma and Pachori <a href="#CR43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR43">2015</a>). This high frequency information can be marked visually, but such processing is subjective and time consuming. Hence, the proposed MDTF method helps in the extraction of these high frequency seizure details which increases the classification accuracy of epileptic diagnosis system.</p>

Another significant fact is that the p-values of all measures play a statistically crucial role in classifying epileptic seizures (Nkengfack et al. <a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">2021</a>).</p></section><section id="Sec20"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Classification</h3>

<p id="Par62">The proposed MDTF is executed in three steps: In the pre-processing step, an MRAF is adopted, the output of which is localised seizure information for the given input epileptic EEG signal. The input EEG signal is processed segment-wise. Each segment of the input signal is passed through a multiresolutional network of an adaptive filter which adapts a comparatively higher frequency range in each segment. The adaptive filter detects the high frequency variations in the EEG signal. Frequencies above 80 Hz are considered as a high frequency range for a seizure signal. The DTF extracts a flow of variations between consecutive segments. In the proposed work, the DTF stage is used to detect the flow of frequency variations between consecutive segments. This step helps in tracking abnormal seizure activities reflected as fluctuations in the EEG signal. Abnormal variations correspond to changes in frequencies of the EEG signal segments and hence reflect in the information flow of the DTF output. In the final step of the classification, an SVM classifier is adopted. The information flow detected by the DTF is used as a feature for the SVM. The information flow is calculated for all segments in the signal and represented in the feature matrix corresponding to each channel. This feature set is given as input to the SVM classifier. The experimentation is carried out for flow information using the SVM with a RBF with gamma values of 0.1, 0.5 and 1. The RBF kernel is used for the analysis to address the non-linear feature set characteristics to achieve non-linear classification. It is observed that the SVM classifier accuracy obtained is 69% for the gamma value of 0.1, 81% for 0.5, and 98.851% for the gamma value of 1. This clarifies the choice of the SVM.</p></section><section id="Sec23"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Comparison with previous research works</h3>

<div class="tw-foot p"><div class="fn" id="_fn_p77"><p><em>Sen</em> average sensitivity, <em>Spe</em> Average specificity, <em>Acc</em> Average accuracy, <em>AD</em> Average detection</p></div></div></section><p id="Par64">The authors of Nigam and Graupe (<a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">2004</a>) performed automatic seizure detection from an epileptic EEG signal by incorporating a multistage non-linear filter along with Artificial Neural Network. This approach was able to detect seizures with an overall accuracy of 97.2%. The authors also suggested the use of wavelets in order to find seizure patterns in various scales which will further improve the classification performance.</p>
<p id="Par65">Kiranyaz et al. (<a href="#CR25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR25">2014</a>) proposed a patient-specific approach using multi-dimensional particle swarm optimization. In this approach, seizure frames are computed using a collective network of a binary classifier ensemble from the classification data map. The presented method performs well independent of patient information. The average sensitivity and specificity of the proposed method is 89 and 93% respectively.</p>
<p id="Par66">Sunil Kumar et al. (<a href="#CR48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR48">2015</a>), features are used on the basis of a one-dimensional local binary pattern (1-D LBP) for classifying of an epileptic signal into seizure and non-seizure areas. Here, 1-D LBP is applied to segments of an EEG signal to compute the histograms of each segment. 1-D LBP histogram information is then given to the nearest neighbour classifier. This approach attained a classification accuracy of 98.33%. However, the authors suggest the testing of 1-D LBP approach on a larger database prior to the clinical trials.</p>
<p id="Par67">An ATFFWT based technique is used in Sharma et al. (<a href="#CR44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR44">2017</a>) for the extraction of information related to EEG sub-bands. FD is computed for all these sub-bands and given to the LS-SVM classifier. This system is able to attain a 100% sensitivity in seizure classification. But this technique requires validation on real time epileptic EEG signals prior to the use in real time seizure detection.</p>
<p id="Par68">Moura et al. (<a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">2015</a>) have presented feature extraction for an EEG event detection using the maximum likelihood on the Temple University database. The detection error rate obtained in this work is 33.2% whereas the proposed MDTF approach on the same database attained a detection error rate of 3.15%.</p>
<p id="Par69">Dong et al. (<a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">2018</a>) utilised a DTF based method which is based on wavelets. Hence the proposed technique is referred to as WDTF. This approach is prescribed for patient specific epilepsy detection. Here, initially, the signal is partitioned into five frequency bands by employing wavelets. Then the information flow is computed for all the five frequency bands including the full band. The information flow is computed by the DTF approach. Extracted feature information is then used with feature dimensionality reduction. The reduced features thus obtained are given to the SVM classifier to classify the EEG segments in to inter-ictal and ictal information. A fivefold cross-validation is utilised to assess the accuracy of the proposed approach. The average detection rate obtained is 95.8%.</p>
<p id="Par70">Gupta and Pachori (<a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">2019</a>) utilized WMRPE to extract EEG rhythms. This feature information is given to the RF and LS-SVM classifiers. The functioning of this method is tested by the addition of additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) for varying SNR levels. The proposed WMRPE based seizure detection approach performs well with a classification accuracy of above 97%. However, it requires validation in long term EEG recordings before application in clinical trials.</p>
<p id="Par71">Moreover, the functioning of the suggested MDTF method is compared with previous work related to major companies such as Persyst Development Corporation and the Kaggle competition. An epileptic spike detection method was proposed by Scheuer et al. (<a href="#CR41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR41">2017</a>). The authors have claimed a maximum sensitivity of 97% using a P13 algorithm. In comparison to this work we found that proposed MDTF approach obtained a sensitivity of 98.31%. Gupta and Pachori (<a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">2019</a>) used AUC to evaluate the performance of the classifier. The authors showed the AUC as 0.84. By carefully comparing this work with the proposed MDTF results, the AUC obtained for the classifier using MDTF was 0.88 which proved the effectiveness of the proposed approach.</p>
<p id="Par72">From Table <a href="#Tab6" class="usa-link">6</a> and the above discussion, it is observed that only a few research works have concentrated on segment-wise seizure detection in EEG data. In addition, some automatic seizure detection systems have obtained above 99% accuracy, but are unable to detect segment-wise accuracy, which carries more relevant epileptic information. Besides, as revealed by literature survey, the SVM classifier performs better than other classifiers such as artificial neural network (ANN), random forest, and decision tree in the classification of seizures (Dong et al. <a href="#CR11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR11">2018</a>; Subasi and Gürsoy <a href="#CR46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR46">2010</a>; Sharma et al. <a href="#CR44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR44">2017</a>; Gupta et al. <a href="#CR21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR21">2017</a>; Golmohammadi et al. <a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">2018</a>; Qaisar and Subasi <a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">2020</a>; Gupta and Pachori <a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">2019</a>). The novel significance of the approach in this paper is the fact that it has the capability of automatically classifying the epileptic seizure information occurring in the EEG signal far more effectively without having to rely on any complex approaches. Additionally, new MDTF technique suggested in this work has been applied by utilizing the multiresolution analysis for epileptic seizure detection for the first time. Its significance lies in the fact that it can be used for detecting epileptic seizures with precision facilitating the epileptic diagnosis task of physicians. Also, the proposed approach shows significant improvement in epileptic seizure detection accuracy and more important, in terms of average detection rate when compared to the other existing methods. This highlights the superiority of the MDTF approach over other methods. However, the proposed method can extract segment-wise epileptic seizure details existing in the surrogate EEG channel data. But it is not able to draw out the seizure information channel-wise. This channel-wise data will be helpful in the localization of the appropriate seizure origination, which may help clinicians in the evaluation of the pre-surgical conditions. This limitation will be addressed by using deep learning based methods such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) in large seizure datasets.</p></section></section><section id="Sec24"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h2>
<p id="Par73">In this research paper, a new HCCC method namely multiresolution based adaptive filtering is combined with the DTF method to report a novel MDTF approach to diagnose an epileptogenic zone in EEG readings. While comparing the presented method with existing epileptic detection approaches, the MDTF approach reports a significant increase in the average accuracy and the average detection rate, namely, 98.89% and 97.72% respectively. Moreover, the sensitivity improved in seizure diagnosis, which highlighted the ability of the MDTF technique to capture the actual ictal information occurring in the EEG signal. Also, the information flow showcased the movement of seizure data between each segment in accordance with various frequency bands existing in the epileptic signal. Based on the analysis of the frequency, it is was also evident that the seizure activity appeared in the high frequency area of the brain signal. Hence, the high frequency oscillations act as an important parameter in the examination of the epileptic seizure diagnosis. The significance of this approach is that by being able to isolate this parameter with the help of the proposed method, neurologists will be able to make instant diagnoses of epileptic zones accurately and thereby changing the lives of epileptic patients dramatically.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgements</h2>
