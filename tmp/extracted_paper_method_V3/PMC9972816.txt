<p>The traditional Mongolian medicine Hunqile-7 (HQL-7), which is mainly used to relieve pain in clinic, has certain toxicity. Therefore, toxicological investigation of HQL-7 is of great significance to its safety assessment. In this study, the toxic mechanism of HQL-7 was explored based on a combination of metabolomics and intestinal flora metabolism. UHPLC-MS was used to analyze the serum, liver and kidney samples of rats after intragastric administration of HQL-7. The decision tree and K Nearest Neighbor (KNN) model were established based on the bootstrap aggregation (bagging) algorithm to classify the omics data. After samples were extracted from rat feces, the high-throughput sequencing platform was used to analyze the 16s rRNA V3-V4 region of bacteria. The experimental results confirm that the bagging algorithm improved the classification accuracy. The toxic dose, toxic intensity, and toxic target organ of HQL-7 were determined in toxicity tests. Seventeen biomarkers were identified and the metabolism dysregulation of these biomarkers may be responsible for the toxicity of HQL-7 in vivo. Several kinds of bacteria was demonstrated to be closely related to the physiological indices of renal and liver function, indicating liver and kidney damage induced by HQL-7 may be related to the disturbance of these intestinal bacteria. Overall, the toxic mechanism of HQL-7 was revealed in vivo, which not only provides a scientific basis for the safe and rational clinical use of HQL-7, but also opens up a new field of research on big data for Mongolian medicine.</p>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="figure/ga1/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div></figure></section><section id="sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">1. Introduction</h2>
<p>Toxicology is a subject that involves the systematic evaluation of the safety of drugs according to their physicochemical and biological characteristics using modern toxicological theories and methods to clarify their toxic mechanisms. Toxicology is a branch of pharmacology, whose main purpose is to guide rational clinical drug use and reduce adverse reactions, the toxic side effects of drugs, and their potential harm to human health.<a href="#ref1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref1"><sup>1</sup></a> The traditional Mongolian medicine, “Hunqile-7” (HQL-7), also known as Xingqi Zhitong pill, is composed of <em>Strychnos nux-vomica L.</em>, <em>Terminalia chebula Retz.</em>, <em>Radix Aucklandiae</em>, <em>Resina Liquidambaris Formosanae</em>, <em>Concretio Silicea Bambusae</em>, <em>Carthamus tinctorius L.</em>, and <em>Aquilaria sinensis (Lour.) Gilg</em> in the ratio 15:9:6:6:6:6:2. HQL-7 promotes blood circulation, relaxes qi, and relieves pain. Accordingly, HQL-7 is mainly used to treat chest and flank punctures, shortness of breath, gangrene, and other diseases.<a href="#ref2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref2"><sup>2</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#ref3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref3"><sup>3</sup></a> The main component of HQL-7, <em>S. nux-vomica L.</em>, exerts good efficacy, but it is also toxic. When poisoned, it induces toxic reactions in multiple target organs of the central nervous, urinary, immune, and cardiovascular systems. Mild poisoning symptoms include dizziness, headache, nausea, vomiting, and increased heart rate. Severe poisoning may lead to systemic convulsions, dyspnea, and asphyxia due to persistent spasms of the respiratory muscles.<a href="#ref4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref4"><sup>4</sup></a> Therefore, the toxicity mechanism of HQL-7 must be elucidated while clarifying its efficacy.</p>
<p>Metabolomics is a research method that imitates the research ideas of genomics and proteomics. Metabolomics is employed for the quantitative analysis of all metabolites in organisms and identification of the relative relationship between metabolites and physiological, pathological, and toxicological changes.<a href="#ref5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref5"><sup>5</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#ref6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref6"><sup>6</sup></a> Bootstrap aggregation (bagging) is a group-learning algorithm in the field of machine learning. Bagging can be combined with other classification and regression algorithms to improve accuracy and stability and to prevent overfitting.<a href="#ref7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref7"><sup>7</sup></a> Bagging is suitable for the classification of data with small sample size, high dimension, and sparse characteristics; therefore, this algorithm has been widely used in the clinical diagnosis of diseases in recent years.<a href="#ref8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref8"><sup>8</sup></a> Novel coronavirus-19 (COVID-19) is a serious global epidemic. Zhang et al. applied the bagging algorithm to the dynamic deep learning network model of coronavirus disease and diagnosed COVID-19 by innovatively identifying the symptoms via chest X-ray images. Based on the calculation results, the prediction accuracy of the model was 98.8889%. Further, the model had the best diagnostic performance among the existing open image set diagnostic methods.<a href="#ref9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref9"><sup>9</sup></a> In recent years, the prediction of potential drug targets has become a hot research topic. Gong et al. developed a new predictor based on key features and a bagging algorithm to identify potential pharmaceutical proteins. According to their results, 80.0343% of potential medicinal proteins were identified. Further crossvalidation experiments revealed that 96.9944% of potential medicinal proteins could be accurately identified, and the accuracy of independent test sets reached 96.5665%.<a href="#ref10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref10"><sup>10</sup></a> At present, only few reports have been published on the integration of bagging algorithms into metabolomics. If the bagging algorithm is applied to the classification and calculation of omics data, the biological information contained in the data can be further mined and the accuracy of data classification can be significantly improved. Moreover, the expansion of metabonomics research methods and the deepening of big data research in Mongolian medicine could be of great significance.</p>
<p>There are 500–1,000 types of bacteria that reside in the human intestine, and the number of bacteria is up to 1 × 10,<a href="#ref11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref11"><sup>11</sup></a>, which is approximately10-fold higher than the number of human cells. The intestinal flora can participate in information transmission, nutrition absorption, mucosal protection, and intestinal immunity.<a href="#ref12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref12"><sup>12</sup></a> The intestinal flora has been found to be related to hepatopathy, nephropathy, obesity, and nervous system diseases.<a href="#ref11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref11"><sup>11</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#ref13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref13"><sup>13–15</sup></a> In addition, the intestinal flora can be regarded as the metabolic organ of the human body which participates in several metabolic processes, including drug metabolism.<a href="#ref16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref16"><sup>16</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#ref17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref17"><sup>17</sup></a> Compared with chemical drugs and biological agents, traditional Mongolian medicine has multiple targets, relatively low toxicity, and complex mechanisms of action, and its pharmacological and toxicological mechanisms may be related to the human intestinal flora. Therefore, studies on the relationship between drug toxicity mechanisms and the intestinal flora are of great significance for obtaining a comprehensive and objective understanding of Mongolian medicine.</p>
<p>In this study, Mongolian medicine HQL-7 was employed as the research object. First, the toxicity intensity, toxic dose, and toxic target organ of HQL-7 were determined through acute toxicity tests, cumulative toxicity tests, and subacute toxicity tests. Thereafter, the bagging algorithm was integrated into the metabonomics research to analyze the endogenous biological small molecules in the serum of rats before and after gavage to improve the accuracy of sample classification and to identify the endogenous markers related to toxicity. Finally, the relationship between flora and the hepatorenal toxicity of HQL-7 was established through the changes in the intestinal flora. Overall, the findings of this study will clarify the toxicity mechanism of the drug in general and will provide a scientific basis for the safe and rational clinical use of the Mongolian drug HQL-7.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. Materials and methods</h2>
<section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.1 Reagents and instruments</h3>
<p>HQL-7 was provided by the Preparation Room of the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Minzu University. Different concentrations of medicated aqueous solutions were prepared before the experiment. Rifaximin was purchased from Liqun Drugstore (China). Acetonitrile and formic acid were chromatographically pure (Thermo Fisher, United States), and deionized water was prepared using a Milli-Q instrument (Millipore, United States). Alanine aminotransferase test kit (ALT, batch No. 43546001), aspartate aminotransferase test kit (AST, batch No. 44713101), and creatinine test kit (CREP, batch No. 47756001) were purchased from Roche diagnostic products (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. Formaldehyde (batch No. 20180606) was purchased from Beijing Yili Jingxi Chemicals Co., Ltd. DNA extraction kit was purchased from TIANZHEN company (China). Agarose was purchased from Biowest (Spain). The library building kit was purchased from BiooScientific (United States) and the sequencing kit was purchased from Illumina (United States).</p>
<p>Ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography (Nexera UHPLC LC-30A, Shimadzu Corporation, Japan) with a mass spectrometer (Triple TOF 5600+, AB SCIEX, United States) was used for metabonomics analysis. The Waters BEH HILIC Column (1.7 μm, 100 mm × 2.1 mm, Waters, Milford, MA, United States) was used for separation. An M16710-33 Vortex oscillator was purchased from Thermo Fisher. A Microfuge22R high-speed refrigerated centrifuge was purchased from Beckman Coulter (United States). TheNanoDrop2000 ultramicro spectrophotometer was purchased from Thermo Fisher. An ELx800 microplate reader was purchased from BioTek (United States). The DYY-6C electrophoresis apparatus was purchased from Beijing 61 Biotechnology Co., Ltd (China). The GeneAmp PCR instrument was purchased from ABI Corporation (United States). The Miseq Sequencer was purchased from Illumina Company.</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2 Experimental process</h3>
<section id="sec5"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.1 Experimental animals</h4>
<p>This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Minzu University (NMMZDX2021[K]0156). Kunming breed rats (SPF grade, half male and half female, body weight 20 ± 2 g) were provided by Liaoning Changsheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd (certificate no. SCXK [Liao] 2015-0001). The animals were raised in the animal laboratory of Mongolian Medical College of Inner Mongolia Minzu University (indoor temperature 20–25 °C, humidity 40%–60%).</p></section><section id="sec6"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.2 Acute toxicity test</h4>
<section id="sec7"><h5 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.2.1 Pretest</h5>
<p>The 100% death dose (Dm) and 0% death dose (Dn) were determined in a preliminary experiment to provide the basic data for the formal experiment. Within the range of Dm and Dn, 5 doses were selected, and the common dose ratio <em>r</em> (<em>r</em> =<span xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><img class="inline" src="https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/6129/9972816/6c519ad99b3b/ineq01.gif" loading="lazy" id="ineq01" alt="Inline graphic"></span>) and group distance <em>k</em> (<em>k</em> = 1/<em>r</em>) of each group were calculated.</p></section><section id="sec8"><h5 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.2.2 Formal test</h5>
<p>Fifty Kunming rats (25 males and 25 females) were randomly divided into 5 groups according to body weight and sex, with 10 rats in each group (HQL-7 administration groups: A1-A5). Before the experiment, rats were fasted for 12 h, and the dosages calculated in the preexperiment were used for HQL-7gavage. Rats were continuously observed for 7–14 days after administration, and the poisoning performance, time of occurrence of poisoning, duration, recovery time, shortest time, longest time, average dead time, and number of dead animals were observed and recorded. The LD<sub>50</sub> and 95% confidence limit of the LD<sub>50</sub> for each group were calculated using the Bliss program.</p>
<p>At the same time, 50 Kunming rats were given HQL-7 and rifaximin (at a dose of 50 mg/Kg/day by gavage for 14 consecutive days),<a href="#ref18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref18"><sup>18</sup></a> and the rest of the steps were the same as above.</p></section></section><section id="sec9"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.3 Cumulative toxicity test</h4>
<p>Forty Kunming rats (20 males and 20 females) were randomly divided into normal and HQL-7 groups according to body weight and sex, with 20 rats in each group. Before the experiment, rats were fasted for 12 h. Four days were considered one stage. In the first stage, each animal was administered a daily dosage of 0.1 LD<sub>50</sub>, which was increased by a factor of 1.5 until half of the animals in the group died. The normal group was administered the equivalent dose of distilled water every day. The accumulation coefficient of HQL-7 (<em>K</em> = cumulative dose/LD<sub>50</sub>) was calculated on the last day of the experiment.</p></section><section id="sec10"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.4 Subacute toxicity test</h4>
<p>Thirty male rats were randomly divided into three groups (normal, high-dose, and low-dose HQL-7 groups, with 10 rats in each group). Oral gavage poisoning was performed. HQL-7 doses of 0.7888 g/kg (high dose) and 0.3940 g/kg (low dose) were administered. Rats were poisoned once per day for 4 weeks. Rats in the normal control group (CG) was administered with the equivalent dose of distilled water every day. Rats were fasted for 12 h before the last day of the experiment. Thirty minutes after poisoning, the rats were anesthetized. Blood was collected from the abdominal aorta and were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 min. Thereafter, serum was separated. The biochemical indices, including ALT, AST, and CREP, in serum were measured. The viscera of rats in each group were rinsed with normal saline, dried with filter paper, and weighed. The livers and kidneys were fixed, embedded in paraffin, and stained with H&amp;E for histopathological examination.</p></section><section id="sec11"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.5 Pretreatment of the metabolites</h4>
<p>The serum samples were thawed before analysis, and 100 μL was added to 300 μL of acetonitrile, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was subsequently filtered through a 0.22-μm filter membrane. Eight pooled quality control samples were used each day to test the stability of the instrument.</p>
<p>Ground glass beads and 100 μL of cold acetonitrile were added to 50 mg of kidney or liver samples, followed by crushing for 3 min. The sample was added to 900 μL of cold acetonitrile and was extracted by shaking for 30 min and were centrifuged for 10 min at 4 °C; 100 μL of sample was collected and concentrated to dry by vacuum centrifugation at 37 ° C, and the residue was dissolved in 100 μL of acetonitrile followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was to be measured.</p></section><section id="sec12"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.6 Chromatographic and mass spectrometric analysis conditions</h4>
<p>The chromatographic analysis conditions were as follows: column temperature, 35 °C; flow rate, 0.3 mL/min; and mobile phase A, 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution with 1 mM of ammonium acetate; mobile phase B, acetonitrile. The following gradient was employed: 0–16 min, 5%–50% B; 16–20 min, 50% B; 20–21 min, 50%–95% B; and 21–22.5 min, 95% B.</p>
<p>For mass spectrometry conditions, electrospray ionization positive ion and negative ion modes were used for detection. The following conditions were employed: ion source temperature, 500 °C (positive ion) and 450 °C (negative ion); capillary voltage, 5.5 kV (positive ion) and 4.4 kV (negative ion); scanning range m/z, 100–1,200 Da; product ion scanning range m/z, 50–1,000 Da; and product ion cumulative scanning time, 0.01 s. IDA and “high sensitivity” mode were selected for secondary mass spectrometry, with a declustering voltage of ±60 V and collision energy of 35 eV ± 15 eV.</p></section><section id="sec13"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.7 Screening and analysis of potential biomarkers</h4>
<p>MS-DIAL 4.10 software was used for the pretreatment, including peak extraction, noise removal, deconvolution, and peak alignment. The 3D data matrix (original data matrix) in CSV format was exported and imported into MATLAB 2012 software for data classification processing. The 3D matrix included the sample structural formula, retention time, mass charge ratio, and mass spectrum response intensity (peak area). The biomarkers were screened by comparing the extracted peak information with that in the database (MassBank, Respect, GNPS). The following screening conditions were employed for the biomarkers: (i) <em>P</em>-value≤ 0.05, and VIP ≥ 1; (ii) <em>P</em> ≤ .05, and FC ≥ 1.5 or ≤ .67. Thermographic analysis of the biomarkers was performed using HemI software,<a href="#ref19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref19"><sup>19</sup></a> and the network diagram between drugs and biomarkers was established using Cytoscape 3.2.1. The screened biomarkers were introduced into MBRole software (version 2.0) for pathway enrichment analysis.</p></section><section id="sec14"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.8 Extraction and PCR amplification of total DNA from rat fecal bacteria</h4>
<section id="sec15"><h5 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.8.1 Total DNA extraction and detection</h5>
<p>Under sterile conditions, the feces of rats in the control and HQL-7 groups were collected. DNA purity and concentration were detected using NanoDrop2000. DNA integrity was detected via 1% agarose gel electrophoresis.</p></section><section id="sec16"><h5 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.8.2 PCR amplification</h5>
<p>The amplified target region was the 16S V3-V4 region. The upstream and downstream primer sequences were 338F (ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG) and 806R (GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT), respectively.</p></section><section id="sec17"><h5 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.8.3 Identification, purification, and quantification of the PCR products</h5>
<p>Agarose gel electrophoresis was performed to detect the PCR products. Each sample had three PCR repeats, and these repeat products were mixed. The PCR products were purified using the AxyPrep DNA Gel Extraction Kit and were quantitatively analyzed using the Quantus Fluorometer. The Miseq library was constructed using NEXTFLEX Rapid DNA-Seq Kit, and the Miseq PE300 software platform was used for sequencing.</p></section></section><section id="sec18"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">2.2.9 Statistical processing of the intestinal flora metabolism data</h4>
<p>Qiime2 software was used to cluster/denoise all effective sequences to form feature sequences (OTUs). By comparing the OTUs to those in the Greengenes Database 13_8, Silva release 132, and UNITE databases, species annotation information was obtained. Based on the absolute abundance and annotation information of OTU, the community structure of different groups samples at the phylum, class, and order levels was statistically analyzed. At the “Genus” level, the relationship between intestinal flora and the biochemical indices of the liver and kidney was established to explain the toxic mechanism of the intestinal flora after the intragastric administration of HQL-7.</p></section></section></section><section id="sec19"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">3. Results and discussion</h2>
<section id="sec20"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.1 Acute toxicity test</h3>
<p>In the pretest, 30 s after administration, rats in each group successively display edpoisoning symptoms, including anxiety, shortness of breath, rapid heartbeat, shaking of the whole body, convulsions, straightening of the lower limbs, bending of the upper limbs, and finally death. The time of death was usually approximately 5–10 min after drug administration. If rats do not die within 30 min, their normal conditions would be restored. The severity of the toxic symptoms, speed of death, and level of mortality were directly related to the dosage. The Dm and Dn of HQL-7 were 1,716.19 mg/kg and 859.56 mg/kg, respectively (<em>r</em> = 1.14, <em>k</em> = 0.71).</p>
<p>In the formal experiment, the LD50 value of HQL-7 was 1,235.877 mg/kg, and the 95% confidence limit of LD50 was 1,127.273–1,357.183 mg/kg. <a href="#TB1" class="usa-link">Table 1</a> lists the acute toxicity test results.</p>

<p>According to cumulative toxicity, when drugs enter the body continuously and repeatedly within a fixed period, the digestion rate of the body exceeds the metabolism and excretion rate, resulting in a gradual increase in drug accumulation in the body and toxicity. The cumulative effect is the basis of chronic toxicity. Therefore, the cumulative toxicity test can further reveal the toxicity characteristics of HQL-7.</p>
<p>In the cumulative toxicity test, rats began to die in the third stage after administration, and the number of dead rats reached half that of the group in the fifth stage. At this time, the total amount of rats was 8,329.8 mg/kg, and the accumulation coefficient <em>K</em> was &gt;5, indicating a weak accumulation, as shown in <a href="#TB2" class="usa-link">Table 2</a>.</p>

<div class="p text-right font-secondary"><a href="table/TB2/" class="usa-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open in a new tab</a></div></section></section><section id="sec22"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.3 Subacute toxicity test</h3>
<p>The subacute poisoning test was performed to evaluate the toxic performance and characteristics of drugs through quantitative administration for 3–4 weeks. Of note, this test is indispensable in toxicological research. As Mongolian medicine has a long treatment course, the subacute poisoning test has an important role. In this experiment, the dose–effect and time–effect relationships of HQL-7 toxicity were assessed via the intragastric administration of HQL-7 to rats for 4 weeks.</p>
<p>In <a href="#TB3" class="usa-link">Table 3</a>, the biochemical index data of each group conform to the normal distribution. For example, through the test of Shapiro–Wilk, the ALT index data of the normal group conform to the normal distribution (<em>P</em> = 0.683&gt;0.05). After 4 weeks of continuous administration, the levels of AST, ALT, and CREP in the high-dose HQL-7 group increased significantly compared with those of the normal group, indicating abnormal liver and renal function indicators.</p>

<p>A) Positive ion mode; B) negative ion mode.</p></figcaption></figure><p>The aim of this study was to classify blood metabolomic data before and after the intragastric administration of HQL-7. The dataset consisted of 4 groups of samples (positive spectrum normal group, positive spectrum HQL-7 administration group, negative spectrum normal group, and negative spectrum HQL-7 administration group). Each sample was derived from eight rats. The dimension of the positive spectrum sample data was 2,213 and that of the negative spectrum sample data was 722. In general, these type of data are both high-dimensional and sparse, with the dimension of the variable being markedly larger than the number of samples. The amounts of data obtained are limited and are constrained by the cost of the medical experiments, which makes it difficult to establish a data classification model. By using bagging, the model can be trained with different datasets to reduce the variance of the classifier (i.e. bagging prevents overfitting). The effectiveness of bagging is derived from the differences in individual models on different training datasets, and the errors cancel each other during the voting process.</p>
<p>The bagging algorithm was used to complete the resampling of metabonomic data. Briefly, <em>k</em> new metabonomic datasets were selected from the original metabonomic data set through bootstrap sampling to train the classification model. The new metabonomic datasets were then repeated, and the trained multiple classifiers were used to classify the metabonomic samples. The classification results of all classifiers were counted by majority voting or the output averaging method. The category with the highest result was the final label. Overall, this method can reduce the overfitting problem associated with a single classification model, improve the learning effect, and generate accurate predictions.</p>
<p>When bagging trains the model for combination to improve the difference in the model, data are randomly extracted from the training set. For the bagging algorithm, samples of the same number as the training set were randomly collected. Thus, the number of samples in the sampling set was the same as that in the training set; however, the sample content was different. If random sampling is performed for <em>k</em> times for the training set containing <em>n</em> samples, the <em>k</em> sample sets differ owing to the randomness. This method usually considers homogeneous weak learners, learns from them independently in parallel, and combines them according to a certain deterministic average process. The bagging classification model is illustrated in <a href="#f3" class="usa-link">Fig. 3</a>.</p>

<p>Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that biomarkers related to the toxicity of HQL-7 mainly involved metabolic pathways, such as bile secretion, ABC protein transport, synaptic vesicle circulation, protein digestion and absorption, arginine and proline metabolism, vitamin digestion and absorption, and primary bile acid biosynthesis, as shown in <a href="#f6" class="usa-link">Fig. 6</a>.</p>

<p class="img-box line-height-none margin-x-neg-2 tablet:margin-x-0 text-center"><img class="graphic" src="https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/6129/9972816/4e1edf98330b/tfac081f6.jpg" loading="lazy" height="337" width="666" alt="Fig. 6"></p>

<figcaption><p>Bubble diagram of the metabolic pathway based on serum samples from rats.</p></figcaption></figure><p>Urea is the main end product of nitrogen metabolism in the human body and is mainly excreted by the kidneys through urine. Under pathophysiological conditions, urea concentration increases sharply, which can provide key information on renal function.<a href="#ref22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref22"><sup>22</sup></a>Urea is directly or indirectly toxic to the human body. The increase in urea concentration in uremic patients causes disintegration of the intestinal epithelial barrier, resulting in the transfer of bacterial toxins into the blood and eventually causing systemic inflammation. Urea induces the apoptosis of vascular smooth muscle cells and endothelial dysfunction, thereby promoting the development of cardiovascular disease. Urea stimulates oxidative stress and dysfunction of adipocytes, leading to insulin resistance. Due to the carbamylation reaction, the increase in urea concentration will have a wide range of indirect effects in which isocyanate (a product of urea catabolism) changes the structure and function of proteins in the body.<a href="#ref23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref23"><sup>23</sup></a></p>
<p>Proline is an amino acid that plays an important role in the primary metabolism and physiological functions. Proline metabolism can promote cell signal transduction and cell apoptosis or survival.<a href="#ref24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref24"><sup>24</sup></a> Proline is potentially toxic. As part of the pathogenic process, proline plays a role in the process of new protein synthesis, and proline metabolism can affect a wider metabolic network through its important role in redox homeostasis. Therefore, proline biosynthesis and catabolism are important processes that induce various diseases.<a href="#ref25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref25"><sup>25</sup></a> The disorder of proline catabolism causes its accumulation in tissues, which may lead to hyperprolinemia. Patients with hyperprolinemia may have neurological symptoms and brain abnormalities, indicating that high proline levels may be related to the neuropathology of some diseases. Further studies have confirmed that proline has a significant effect on brain injury and spatial memory impairment, which are closely related to energy metabolism deficiency, oxidative stress, and excitotoxicity.<a href="#ref26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref26"><sup>26</sup></a></p>
<p>Carnitine is an amino acid which has a variety of biological activities in the body, such as promoting the oxidation of fatty acids and utilization of carbohydrates and amino acids, improving body tolerance, preventing lactic acid accumulation, antiaging, and antioxidation, and reducing cholesterol and triglycerides in the blood.<a href="#ref27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref27"><sup>27</sup></a> The content of carnitine was found to decrease, while the content of its metabolites (acylcarnitine) increased. Carnitine deficiency is characterized by a decreased intracellular carnitine content, increased urinary carnitine loss, and low serum carnitine levels. Patients may develop hypoke to hypoglycemia and hepatic encephalopathy in the early stages or osteopathy and cardiomyopathy in the late stage.<a href="#ref28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref28"><sup>28</sup></a> Carnitine deficiency can damage important metabolic processes in the liver, such as gluconeogenesis, fatty acid metabolism, albumin biosynthesis, and ammonia detoxification in the urea cycle, leading to hypoproteinemia and hyperammonemia.<a href="#ref29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref29"><sup>29</sup></a> Carnitine deficiency is also closely related to the occurrence and development of autism, Alzheimer’s disease, uremia, and other diseases.<a href="#ref30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref30"><sup>30–32</sup></a></p>
<p>Indole sulfate is regarded as a uremic endothelial toxin that is closely related to the high prevalence of cardiovascular diseases. A high concentration of indole sulfate can lead to thrombosis and damage to neovascularization and induce the production of the transcription factors, NF-κB and AP-1, thereby causing inflammation. In addition, indole sulfate can not only promote oxidation and reduce the bioavailability of nitric oxide but can also change the migration and regeneration of endothelial cells and control the proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells.<a href="#ref33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref33"><sup>33</sup></a> Indole sulfate can further induce intestinal barrier damage through mitotic phagocytosis injury mediated by the IRF1-DRP1 axis. Dysfunction of the intestines and kidneys forms a vicious circle that eventually evolves into a catalyst for chronic kidney disease (CKD) and related complications.<a href="#ref34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref34"><sup>34</sup></a></p></section><section id="sec26"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">3.7 Toxic mechanism analysis of biomarkers of liver and kidney</h3>
<p>Liver and kidney biomarkers information are presented in <a href="#TB6" class="usa-link">Tables 6</a> and <a href="#TB7" class="usa-link">7</a>, respectively. As an unsupervised pattern recognition method, principal component analysis (PCA) can reflect the original state of data and can visually display the overall differences between different products. Among them, the content of D-alloisoleucine was significantly increased, and the content of palatinose was significantly decreased. In kidney tissue, there were 3 different metabolites between the normal group and the HQL-7 group. Among them, the content of L-glutamic acid increased significantly, and the content of L-asparagine and aspartate decreased significantly.</p>

The correlation index was expressed in <em>R</em>, and the results are shown in <a href="#f10" class="usa-link">Fig. 10</a>. The heat diagram analysis revealed that the relative abundance of some intestinal microflora was closely related to the indices of liver function (AST and ALT) and renal function (CREP).</p>

Detection of serum CREP is one of the main methods commonly used to understand renal function. When kidney function is incomplete, CREP accumulates and becomes toxic to the human body.<a href="#ref38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref38"><sup>38</sup></a> The metabolic disorders of the intestinal microbiota are closely related to chronic systemic inflammation, which is the main factor in the complication and progression of CKD. <p>AST and ALT are transaminases. Compared with other organs, the level of transaminase in liver is the highest. According to the mouse liver inflammation model induced by CCl<sub>4</sub>, as the abundance of pathogenic bacteria, such as <em>Aerococcus</em>, increased the expression levels of proinflammatory cytokines (NO, TNF- α, IL-1β), COX-2, and iNOS in liver also significantly increased.<a href="#ref41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref41"><sup>41</sup></a> Alcoholic liver injury is mainly caused by excessive long-term alcohol consumption. Total triglyceride, cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, AST, and ALT in the serum of mice with alcoholic liver increased abnormally, while the activities of catalase, superoxide dismutase, alcohol dehydrogenase, aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH), and glutathione decreased. The abundance of <em>Aerococcus</em> and <em>Jeotgaliccocus</em>also changed significantly, indicating the occurrence of intestinal microbial disorders.<a href="#ref42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref42"><sup>42</sup></a></p>
<p>AST content was positively correlated with the high abundance of <em>Aerococcus</em> and <em>Ruminococcus</em> (<em>r</em> = 0.4991, 0.5941) and negatively correlated with the low abundance of <em>Sutterella</em> (<em>r</em> = −0.5277). The severity of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is related to intestinal ecological disorders and the metabolic function of the intestinal microbiota. <em>Ruminococcus</em> was found to be closely related to NAFLD-induced liver fibrosis. Therefore, analysis of <em>Ruminococcus</em> provides valuable information for predicting the severity of NAFLD.<a href="#ref43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref43"><sup>43</sup></a> Intestinal microbiological disorders are associated with hepatocellular carcinoma. Increased abundance of bacteria, such as <em>Sutterella</em>, may also be considered a high-risk indicator of female HCC<a href="#ref44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref44"><sup>44</sup></a>.</p></section><section id="sec31"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">3.8.4 The correlation between the toxicity of HQL-7 and the intestinal flora</h4>
<p>Rifaximin is a broad-spectrum intestinal antibiotic. The experimental results showed that rifaximin had almost no side effects on rats after long-term intragastric administration.<a href="#ref45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref45"><sup>45</sup></a> Rifaximin has a very strong bactericidal effect on a variety of intestinal microflora, such as Bacteroidia, Streptococcus, Staphylococcus, and Anaeroplasma, which has changed significantly due to HQL-7 in this experiment.<a href="#ref46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="ref46"><sup>46</sup></a> Therefore, in order to verify the correlation between the toxicity of HQL-7 and intestinal flora, the rats interfered by rifaximin were taken as the research objects.</p>
<p>In the acute toxicity test, the LD50 value of HQL-7 (rifaximin interference) was 1,712.341 mg/kg, and the 95% confidence limit of LD50 was 1,602.462–1,833.280 mg/kg. Compared with the rats in the HQL-7 group, the LD50 value of the rats in the HQL-7 + rifaximin group increased significantly, indicating that some intestinal flora was indeed closely related to the toxicity of HQL-7. <a href="#TB8" class="usa-link">Table 8</a> lists the acute toxicity test results.</p>

<p>The toxic dose, toxic intensity, and toxic target organ of HQL-7 were determined in rats using acute, cumulative, and subacute toxicity tests. The bagging algorithm is integrated into metabonomics research to improve the accuracy of omics data classification. Twelve endogenous metabolites, including urea, proline, carnitine, and indole sulfate, were identified by metabonomics. The overexpression of urea, proline, and indole sulfate, and the decrease in carnitine content in rats may be the cause of the toxicity of HQL-7 in vivo. Based on the results of the intestinal flora metabolism, <em>Paraprevotella</em> was closely related to the physiological indices of renal function, while <em>Aerococcus, Jeotgalicoccus</em>, <em>Ruminococcus</em>, and <em>Sutterella</em> were closely related to the physiological indices of liver function. Therefore, the mechanism of the liver and kidney damage caused by HQL-7 may be related to the disturbance of these intestinal bacteria. The toxic mechanism of the Mongolian medicine HQL-7 in vivo was described in this study, which provides a scientific basis for the safe and rational clinical use of HQL-7.</p></section><section id="sec35"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Authors’ contributions</h2>
