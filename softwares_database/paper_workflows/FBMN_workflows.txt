sample_description: The study utilized dissolved organic matter (DOM) extracts prepared from 200 L of surface seawater collected at the Ellen Browning Scripps Memorial Pier, San Diego, California, USA, on February 26, 2021 (11:00–19:00 PDT), filtered through a 0.8/0.45 μm Supor membrane filter and acidified to pH 2 with 260 mL of 37% trace-metal-grade HCl. DOM was isolated via solid-phase extraction (SPE) using Agilent Bond Elut PPL cartridges (5 g bed mass, 60 mL column volume), activated with 30 mL methanol (MeOH), washed with 30 mL pH 2 water (LCMS grade), 30 mL MeOH (LCMS grade), and equilibrated with 30 mL pH 2 water; acidified seawater was loaded in parallel across 8 cartridges at ~20 mL/min/cartridge using an Agilent 20-port vacuum SPE station over 20 h. After desalting (60 mL pH 2 water) and N₂ drying, cartridges were eluted with 20 mL MeOH each (total 160 mL extract). An internal standard (IS) mix containing 5 μg each of domoic acid, kainic acid, isoxaben, irgarol, imazapyr, heroin, methamphetamine, and cocaine in MeOH was added. 150 mL of the pooled extract was aliquoted into 100 HPLC vials (1.5 mL each); blanks used 4 L acidified water processed identically and eluted with 10 mL MeOH. All vials were dried overnight in a vacuum centrifuge at room temperature; six dried aliquots weighed 1.8 mg dry mass per vial. Two representative aliquots were redissolved in 2 L pH 2 water for TOC analysis (30.0 μmol/L, 0.72 mg C/vial); overall extraction efficiency was 43.3%. Algae extract was prepared from lyophilized Synechococcus sp. cells (0.83 g in 50 mL LCMS-grade MeOH), sonicated (5 min), centrifuged (5000 RCF, 10 min), supernatant dried under N₂, redissolved in Milli-Q water, sonicated/centrifuged again, diluted to 150 mL, acidified with 150 μL HCl, and 8 mL extracted on a 1 g preconditioned PPL cartridge; eluate was dried under N₂, redissolved in 2 mL MeOH, and 0.1 mL used for DOC analysis (5.79 μg/mL after blank subtraction → 2.1 mg/mL C in stock). Three algae stock solutions (1800, 600, 200 μg/mL in MeOH) were prepared. For the ring study, 60 dried DOM extracts were each redissolved in 100 μL MeOH (Optima LCMS), and 50 μL transferred to new 2 mL glass vials (n = 120 total). Of these, 28 received 25 μL of 1800 μg/mL algae extract (A45M), 28 received 25 μL of 600 μg/mL (A15M), 28 received 25 μL of 200 μg/mL (A5M), and 28 new vials received 25 μL of 1800 μg/mL algae extract + 50 μL MeOH (A sample). Blank samples consisted of 4 blank aliquots redissolved in 100 μL MeOH, pooled, mixed with 150 μL standard mix, and 18 μL aliquoted into 28 vials. All vials were dried in a vacuum centrifuge at 35 °C, capped, and shipped.
complete_workflow_description: Data analysis was conducted using an open-source, standardized pipeline involving multiple software tools and platforms. Raw LC–MS/MS files were converted to .mzML format using msConvert (ProteoWizard). Files were uploaded to the MassIVE repository (https://massive.ucsd.edu/). Data analysis was performed both as individual datasets and as a unified global dataset via Classical Molecular Networking (CMN) and Feature-Based Molecular Networking (FBMN) on GNPS (https://gnps.ucsd.edu) and GNPS2 (https://gnps2.org), respectively. FBMN preprocessing was carried out with MZmine (v4.2.0) to generate feature tables based on MS1 XICs. CMN used raw data directly, relying on MS/MS spectral matching without MS1 chromatographic alignment. Both workflows included spectral library matching against public libraries (e.g., GNPS library) and molecular networking to cluster related MS/MS spectra. Downstream processing included: (1) blank subtraction using a 30% intensity threshold (features with >30% intensity in blanks vs. samples removed); (2) zero-intensity feature imputation; (3) TIC normalization; (4) PCoA and PERMANOVA using Bray–Curtis dissimilarity via the FBMN Stats App; and (5) UpSet plot generation using Intervene (https://asntech.shinyapps.io/intervene/). All GNPS2 molecular networking jobs (CMN and FBMN) used standardized parameters documented in Supporting Information, including minimum cosine score ≥0.7, minimum matched peaks ≥6, minimum matched fragment ions ≥6, and network edge filtering by spectral similarity and library match status. Chromatographic and MS acquisition settings were harmonized across 24 participating laboratories to ensure comparability, including C18 UHPLC columns (<2 μm particles, 2 mm × 10/15 cm), mobile phases A (water + 0.1% formic acid) and B (acetonitrile + 0.1% formic acid), flow rate 0.4 mL/min, and gradient: 5% B at 0 min → linear to 50% B at 7 min → linear to 99% B at 10 min.

related_tool: FBMN
sample_description: The study used ferrioxamine (Fe-1) as a model holo-hydroxamate siderophore to benchmark extraction performance of solid-phase extraction (SPE) resins (C-18, HLB, and Amberlite XAD series). For biological application, the fish pathogen Tenacibaculum maritimum LSP9.1 was cultured under iron-deficient conditions in minimal medium supplemented with the chelating agent EDDHA to induce siderophore production. Extracts were designated TMSX7M (supernatant-derived) and TMPX7M (pellet-derived). Additionally, Ga-1 (gallium-chelated ferrioxamine) and Fe-1 standards were included for calibration, validation, and network anchoring. All extractions and analyses avoided acidic conditions to prevent dechelation and degradation of metallophores.
complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow consisted of six integrated steps: (1) Untargeted HPLC-MS/MS analysis using reverse-phase C18 column (acidic mobile phase avoided; formic acid replaced with ammonium formate buffer, pH ~6.8) with gradient elution (water/acetonitrile + 0.1% formic acid omitted); (2) Feature detection and alignment in MZmine 3.4.0 using parameters: mass tolerance 0.01 Da, RT tolerance 0.2 min, minimum feature height 10000, minimum time span 0.05 min, and isotope grouping (m/z tolerance 0.01 Da, RT tolerance 0.1 min, charge state 1+); (3) FBMN on GNPS with cosine threshold ≥0.7, minimum matched peaks ≥6, and maximum shift 500 mDa; (4) Isotopologue screening using MZmine’s isotope peak scanner and CAMERA/R for ⁶⁹/⁷¹Ga (3:2 ratio) and ⁵⁴/⁵⁶Fe (6:92 ratio) adducts; (5) Molecular formula prediction using SIRIUS 5.8.3 (with CSI:FingerID and ZODIAC modules) on MS¹ and MS² data, followed by manual Ion Identity (MII) curation to group adducts ([M+H]⁺, [M+Na]⁺, [M+K]⁺, [M+Ga]³⁺, [M+Fe]³⁺) based on exact mass differences and isotopic pattern consistency; (6) Orthogonal validation via DI-IMS-MS using a Waters SELECT SERIES Cyclic IMS-MS system: direct infusion (5 μL/min flow rate), nitrogen drift gas, wave velocity 300 m/s, wave height 40 V, IMS wave velocity ramp 0–100 V, collision energy 10–30 eV, CCS calibration with tune mix (Agilent), and CCS database matching (CCS tolerance ±2%). All software tools used were open-source (MZmine, GNPS, SIRIUS, CAMERA, XCMS) or vendor-provided (Waters UNIFI for IMS-MS processing).

related_tool: FBMN
sample_description: The sample used in the research consisted of metabolite extracts from large-scale solid-state fermentation of the fungal strain Trichoderma harzianum NTU2180 cultivated on germinated brown rice (GBR): 600 g GBR mixed with 180 mL malt yeast (MY) broth and 180 mL H2O, sterilized and fermented in glass culture flasks for 40 days; the resulting biomass was extracted three times with methanol at room temperature to yield a methanolic syrup extract (62.0 g), which was then partitioned with ethyl acetate (EtOAc) and water (1:1) to obtain an EtOAc-soluble layer (15.2 g) — this EtOAc-soluble layer served as the primary analytical sample for untargeted metabolomics and molecular networking analysis. Additional samples included extracts from OSMAC (One-Strain-Many-Compounds) fermentations under three conditions: (1) liquid MY broth (100 mL in 250-mL flasks, 180 rpm, 10 days), (2) solid-state GBR (100 g GBR + 35 mL MY broth + 35 mL H2O, 20 days), and (3) grain mixture (GM) (100 g GM + 35 mL MY broth + 35 mL H2O, 20 days); all extracts were prepared by EtOAc (for MY) or methanol (for GBR and GM) extraction, followed by solvent partitioning where applicable. For UHPLC-MS/MS analysis, samples were dissolved in methanol at 5000 ppm and filtered through 0.2-µm PTFE syringe filters prior to injection.
complete_workflow_description: The computational process procedure involved four integrated software tools: (1) MSConvert (Chambers et al., 2012) converted raw Thermo .raw files to open mzML format; (2) MZmine3 (version 3.9.0; Schmid et al., 2023) performed data preprocessing including chromatogram building using the Automated Data Analysis Pipeline (ADAP) builder (Myers et al., 2017), peak detection, deconvolution, alignment, and gap filling; features were filtered by intensity (>5e4), minimum time span (0.05 min), and signal-to-noise ratio (>10); MS/MS spectra were matched to peaks using a 0.2 Da m/z tolerance and 10 s retention time window; (3) SIRIUS (versions incorporating Bocker et al., 2009/2016 and Duhrkop et al., 2015/2019) analyzed the MZmine3-exported feature list for molecular formula identification (SIRIUS), network-based formula ranking refinement (ZODIAC), structural fingerprint prediction and database searching (CSI:FingerID), and compound class prediction (CANOPUS, Djoumbou Feunang et al., 2016; Duhrkop et al., 2021); parameters included default settings for isotope pattern analysis, fragmentation tree computation, and CSI:FingerID’s deep neural network trained on >10 million spectra; (4) GNPS (Wang et al., 2016) executed FBMN with cosine score filtering (≥0.7 or ≥0.8), minimum matched peaks ≥6, and precursor m/z tolerance of 0.02 Da; spectral matching used normalized dot product (cosine) with dynamic background subtraction. All software versions, parameter values, and workflow dependencies (e.g., MZmine3 → SIRIUS → GNPS → Cytoscape) were explicitly reported.

related_tool: FBMN
sample_description: The study used a complex Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) formula composed of 10 medicinal herbs: Angelica sinensis, Chrysanthemum indicum, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Isatis tinctoria, Oldenlandia diffusa, Polygonum cuspidatum (synonym for Reynoutria japonica), Plantago vulgaris, Scutellaria baicalensis, Scutellaria glabra, and Sophora flavescens. Raw plant materials were sourced from Conba Pharmaceutical and verified against the Dictionary of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Chinese Pharmacopoeia, and www.theplantlist.org (April 27, 2019). Single-herb extracts were prepared by boiling 10 g of powdered herb in 250 mL water (twice, 1 h each), followed by filtration and freeze-drying. The full formula (5 g) was extracted similarly in 500 mL water. An enriched methanolic extract was prepared via maceration (200 g powder in 5 L methanol, eight times 24 h), evaporated, and yielded 18.0% (w/w). This enriched extract was further fractionated using Vacuum Liquid Chromatography (VLC) on RP Zeoprep 60 C18 (15–25 µm) with water and methanol elution, yielding aqueous (29.7% w/w) and methanolic (43.4% w/w) fractions. Additionally, C18 solid-phase extraction (SPE, 1000 mg/12 mL) was applied to 100 mg of formula dissolved in distilled water, eluting with 10 mL water (aqueous fraction, 65.9% ± 2.8% w/w) and 25 mL methanol (methanolic fraction, 17.5% ± 1.2% w/w); loss was 16.5% ± 3.1% w/w. All samples were solubilized in water:methanol mixtures (7:3 for formula, 2:8 for enriched extract) at concentrations of 5 mg/mL (UHPLC-UV-PDA-ELSD) or 1 mg/mL (UHPLC-HRMS-TOF), sonicated (15 min), and centrifuged (10 min, 6000 rpm). Blank analyses and single-herb extracts were included as controls throughout.
complete_workflow_description: Data processing and analysis employed multiple software tools and custom scripts. UHPLC-HRMS raw data (.RAW) were converted to .mzXML using ProteoWizzard (Kessner et al., 2008) and imported into MZmine 2.37 (Pluskal et al., 2010; Pluskal et al., 2012). Peak detection used the ADAP workflow, followed by deisotoping and alignment (parameters detailed in Supplementary Section S10, adapted from prior single-herb profiling protocols without modification for multi-herb complexity). Peak lists were filtered to retain only features with HRMS/MS scans. HRMS-level data (m/z, retention time, peak height) were exported as text files; HRMS/MS spectra were exported as .mgf for GNPS submission. For herb-specificity analysis, an in-house Jupyter Notebook script (hosted at https://gitlab.unige.ch/Arnaud.Gaudry/node_treater) processed the aligned quantification table (GNPS terminology) and cluster component indexes. It computed 'specificity percentage' per node (relative intensity across 10 herbs) and 'cluster specificity percentage' (average of node specificity percentages per cluster), applying a 90% threshold to define herb-specific features. The script also exported HRMS data (m/z, RT, peak height) per cluster for bar chromatogram generation in Prism 8. 2D feature maps were generated by exporting the aligned table to .csv, loading in Microsoft Excel 16.16.8, assigning formula feature intensities to herb-specific features, and plotting as scatter plots (black circles for formula features, colored dots for herb-specific features). ELSD peak areas were manually assigned to specific features in an additional column. Annotation integrated ELSD filtering (47 peaks ≥0.004 μV/s), QMS nominal m/z retrieval, HRMS formula calculation, DNP database searching, chemotaxonomic filtering (species/genus/family), and cross-validation with ISDB-DNP in silico MS/MS predictions (Supplementary Section S5). All metadata, including IUPAC names and InChIKeys, are archived in Cytoscape files at doi:10.25345/C5516P and raw data in MassIVE repository MSV000085648.

sample_description: Eleven biological replicates (n = 11) of each of three wild-harvested, naturally resinous wood morphotypes of Dracaena cochinchinensis were analyzed: hollow cork cambium (P), whole-body resin-containing wood (MZ), and resin-secreting aggregated wood (LZ). complete_workflow_description: Raw UPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS data (.wiff files) were first converted to .mzML format using MSConvert (ProteoWizard). The resulting feature table and corresponding MS/MS spectra (.mgf) were uploaded to GNPS for FBMN analysis with precursor and fragment mass tolerances of 0.02 Da, cosine score ≥ 0.7, and minimum 6 matched peaks per edge. Differential metabolites were selected using VIP > 1 and p < 0.05 (t-test). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was then applied to interpret biological context, culminating in the reconstruction of a metabolic regulatory network linking phenylpropanoid, flavonoid, steroid, fatty acid, and linoleic acid metabolism across the P→MZ→LZ continuum.
