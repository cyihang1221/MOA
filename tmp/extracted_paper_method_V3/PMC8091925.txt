</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="abs0010"><h2>Summary</h2>
<p>We describe a protocol for identifying bacteria-derived lipid metabolites produced in the guts using antibiotic-treated mice, liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry-based lipidomics, and feature-based molecular spectrum networking (FBMN). Untargeted lipidomics using the MS-DIAL 4 program provides information on known and unknown complex lipid molecules. The FBMN technique clusters similar MS2 spectra, facilitating the identification of bacterial lipids. Targeted analysis was used as a complementary method to cover oxylipins. Here, we provide details for targeted and untargeted analyses.</p>
<p>For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to <a href="#bib5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib5">Yasuda et al. (2020)</a>.</p>

<section id="sec1.1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Administration of antibiotics</h3>
<blockquote class="text-italic"><p><img class="inline" src="https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/3d8b/8091925/51ca064f7ff6/fx2.gif" loading="lazy" alt="Inline graphic"><strong>Timing: 6 weeks</strong></p></blockquote>
<p id="p0030">The fecal metabolome contains complex components of food, host, and bacterial origins. By comparing the metabolic profiles obtained from fecal samples of antibiotic-treated and control mice, bacteria-derived lipid candidates can be characterized, with marked decreases observed in the antibiotics group compared with the control group. Total bacteria were eliminated by the administeration of an antibiotic cocktail containing ampicillin, vancomycin, neomycin, and metronidazole.</p>
<ul id="olist0010" class="list" style="list-style-type:none">
<li id="o0010">
<span class="label">1.</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0035">Maintain wild type C57BL/6J mice for at least two weeks in a specific pathogen-free facility to equilibrate the gut microbiota to the breeding environment.</p>
</li>
<li id="o0015">
<span class="label">2.</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0040">Prepare the antibiotic cocktail as described in the “<a href="#sec2" class="usa-link">materials and equipment</a>” section.</p>
</li>
<li id="o0020">
<span class="label">3.</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0045">Provide antibiotic-treated drinking water to the treatment group of mice for four weeks. Replace with fresh antibiotic-treated drinking water every two weeks.</p>
</li>
<li id="o0025">
<span class="label">4.</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0050">After administration of antibiotics in the drinking water, collect feces into sterile microtubes.</p>
</li>
</ul>

Place the tubes with the collected feces on ice immediately to decrease unexpected enzymatic reactions and lipid oxidation.</p></blockquote>
<ul id="olist0015" class="list" style="list-style-type:none"><li id="o0030">

<p id="p0145">An overview of the procedures is summarized in <a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1</a>. The collected frozen fecal samples are homogenized by metal corn beating (steps 1–8). The total lipids are extracted by single phase extraction (steps 9–17) for untargeted lipidomics using LC-QTOF/MS (steps 18–23) (see the left track of <a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1</a>). The obtained data are analyzed using the MS-DIAL 4 software program (steps 24–25). The FBMN technique clustering similar MS2 spectra is employed to facilitate the identification of bacterial lipid metabolites (steps 26–27). The oxylipins contained in the fecal sample homogenate are enriched by solid phase extraction (steps 28–41) and analyzed by targeted lipidomics using LC-TripleQ/MS (steps 42–48) (see the right track of <a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1</a>).</p>

<figcaption><p>An overview of the procedures</p></figcaption></figure><section id="sec3.1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Homogenization of feces samples</h3>
<blockquote class="text-italic"><p><img class="inline" src="https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/3d8b/8091925/51ca064f7ff6/fx2.gif" loading="lazy" alt="Inline graphic"><strong>Timing: 1–2 h</strong></p></blockquote>
<p id="p0155">Feces contain various components, including bacteria, fiber, host cells, and mucus. To extract whole metabolites, it is important to homogenize feces into a complete powder form (<a href="#fig2" class="usa-link">Figure 2</a>). </p>
<ul id="olist0020" class="list" style="list-style-type:none">
<li id="o0035">
<span class="label">1.</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0160">Weigh the frozen fecal sample (10–100 mg) and place in a 3-mL reinforced homogenization tube.</p>
</li>
<li id="o0040">
<span class="label">2.</span><p class="display-inline" id="p0165">Place a metal corn in the tube.</p>
</li>

<p id="p0300">Untargeted lipidomic data are processed by MS-DIAL (<a href="#bib4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib4">Tsugawa et al., 2020</a>). <p id="p0645">Targeted analysis detected 139 oxylipins, 33 of which were reduced more than 10-fold in the antibiotic cocktail (Abx) group (<a href="#mmc1" class="usa-link">Figure S1</a>). These results demonstrated that a significant portion of oxylipins in the feces were dramatically affected by Abx treatment.</p>

<p id="p0655">The precise determination of acyl chain structures (straight, <em>iso</em>, or <em>anteiso</em>), unsaturation properties (unsaturated bond or cyclopropane), and sugar isomers (glucose or galactose) are not achieved in our untargeted analysis system. The complete chemical assignment of lipid structures should be confirmed using standards prepared from authentic reference materials.</p>
<p id="p0660">This method cannot rule out the possibility of including host-derived lipid metabolites that are induced in the presence of commensal bacteria. These should be referred to as bacteria-dependent lipid metabolites.</p></section><section id="sec6"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Troubleshooting</h2>
<section id="sec6.1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Problem 1</h3>
<p id="p0665">Excess pressure occurs in LC (steps 21 and 45).</p></section><section id="sec6.2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Potential solution</h3>
<p id="p0670">The LC lines may be clogged. After each batch analysis, the lines and column should be washed by flowing 2-propanol for 1–2 h.</p></section><section id="sec6.3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Problem 2</h3>
<p id="p0675">Peak shape worsens and/or retention time shifts (steps 23 and 46).</p></section><section id="sec6.4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Potential solution</h3>
<p id="p0680">The column is considered to have deteriorated. Analyze the system check standards before and after each analysis to check the condition of the column. We use deuterated mix of representative lipid classes.</p></section><section id="sec6.5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Problem 3</h3>
<p id="p0685">Sensitivity decreases and/or precision mass shifts (steps 23 and 46).</p></section><section id="sec6.6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Potential solution</h3>
<p id="p0690">The ion source may have been contaminated. The pores of the MS inlet should be wiped carefully using clean paper soaked in 50% (v/v) methanol and Milli-Q water solution and wrung out tightly. The electrode needs to be flushed with methanol solution. Soak the electrode in methanol solution in a glass beaker and place in an ultrasonic bath for 10 min. If the problem persists, stop the vacuum pump and clean Q0 according to the instruction manual of the instrument. After starting the vacuum pump and waiting until the MS reaches operational pressure, use the calibration solution to calibrate the instrument and to assess the mass error and sensitivity of specific calibrant ions across the mass range of the instrument.</p></section><section id="sec6.7"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Problem 4</h3>
<p id="p0695">A single cluster with too many nodes or many clusters with a few nodes appear by molecular spectrum networking (step 27-C).</p></section><section id="sec6.8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Potential solution</h3>
<p id="p0700">Optimize the similarity cut-off [%] in step 26 so that known lipid subclasses (e.g., phosphatidylethanolamine, and ceramide) are clustered in a single group.</p></section></section><section id="sec7"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Resource availability</h2>
