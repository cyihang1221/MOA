>Materials and methods   Materials   Download the original mRNA expression data of gastric cancer data based on TCGA database   A total of 393 samples associated with tumor lymph node metastasis were extracted for analysis. These samples were categorized into two groups: the lymph node non-metastasis group (n = 124) and the lymph node metastasis group (n = 269), which were utilized for subsequent analysis. The single-cell data file from  GSE163558  was retrieved from the GEO public database. This dataset included five samples with intact single-cell expression profiles, comprising three samples without lymph node metastasis and two samples with lymph node metastasis. Exposed data and outcome data   Extract the required eQTL data through eQTLGen consortium database. The participants in the outcome-related GWAS included predominantly individuals of European ancestry. The outcome summary data were obtained from the EBI database (ebi-a-GCST90018849), the GWAS Catalog encompasses a range of top-level associations, publications, and comprehensive summary statistics. The data from the GWAS Catalog data have been mapped to the Genome Assembly and dbSNP Build. Within this dataset, there were 1,029 cases and 475,087 controls pertaining to gastric cancer. Methods   Difference analysis   The limma package can analyze the differential genes between different groups. This study was categorized into two groups: one group consisted of patients with gastric cancer without lymph node metastasis, while the other group included patients with lymph node metastasis. Objective to analyze the molecular mechanism of lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. The molecular mechanisms underlying lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer were investigated. Gene expression differences were analyzed using the R package “Limma”, with the screening criterion of a p value less than 0.01. Differentially expressed genes between the two groups were identified, and a volcano plot illustrating these differentially expressed genes was generated. Metascape enrichment analysis   Metascape database ( www.metascape.org ) was utilized to annotate the functions of differential genes. GO pathway analysis was conducted on the selected genes, with a minimum overlap of ≥ 3 and a p-value of ≤ 0.01 being regarded as statistically notable. Mendelian randomization analysis   The outcome identifier retrieved from the EBI database was derived from the summary data of Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) ( https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/ ). The causal relationships associated with eQTL were extracted from this database. For each gene, the screened SNPs that met the criteria for potential IVs were selected, applying a genome-wide significance threshold of P < 1e-8. Calculate the LD among the SNPs, and among those with R2 < 0.001 (clumping window size = 10,000 kb), only SNPs with P2 < 5e-8 were retained. Four statistical methods—specifically, MR Egger, Weighted median, Inverse variance weighted, and Weighted mode—were employed sequentially. In instances where only one statistical approach was applicable to a SNP within the causal relationship, the Wald ratio was utilized exclusively. These methodologies were implemented to assess the reliability of causal relationships and to derive a thorough estimation of the influence of both cis and partially cross-regional gene expression in blood on gastric cancer. Sensitivity analysis   A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis utilizing MR was performed to evaluate the specific genetic variants on the risk of gastric cancer. This methodology employed a systematic approach to identify and exclude variants that have a disproportionate impact on the overall estimate. This was achieved by sequentially omitting each SNP and subsequently recalculating the pooled effect size of the remaining SNPs. The exclusion of each SNP resulted in the generation of a new point estimate accompanied by its respective 95% confidence interval. This process enabled the assessment of the unique contribution of each SNP and the overall robustness of the findings. A summary was provided that included the estimated value following the removal of each SNP, as well as the total estimated value when all SNPs were considered. By comparing the estimates of SNPs, it is possible to evaluate whether there is impact of excluding any individual SNP on the overall results, thus allowing for an assessment of the robustness of the analysis conducted. Go enrichment analysis of key genes   Following the identification of key genes, "ClusterProfiler" was employed to annotate the functions of these important genes, thereby facilitating a comprehensive exploration of their functional correlations. The enrichment analysis of GO pathways indicated that a q value and adjusted p value of less than 0.05 were established as the screening conditions for determining significant enrichment. GSEA analysis of key genes   Patients were classified into high and low expression groups based upon the expression levels of key genes. GSEA was utilized to investigate the differences in signaling pathways between these groups. The gene set was sourced from the Molecular Signatures Database (MsigDB), and subsequent analysis involved annotating the gene set to elucidate the differences in signaling pathways between the categorized groups. The screening conditions for determining significant enrichment were set at a p-value of less than 0.05. Subsequently, the correlations were ranked based on their consistency scores. GSVA analysis of key genes   Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) is a methodology employed for evaluating gene set enrichment in transcriptomes. GSVA enables the transformation of gene-level changes into modifications at the pathway level by systematically scoring relevant gene sets. This process allows for the identification of biological functions under investigation. In this study, gene sets were sourced from the MsigDB, also, the alterations in biological functions were assessed across various samples using GSVA comprehensive scoring. Transcription factor regulatory network of key genes   This research utilized the R package "RcisTarget" for the prediction of transcription factors. All analyses conducted by RcisTarget were grounded in motif analysis. The NES of a motif was contingent upon the total number of motifs present in the database. In addition to the motifs identified in the source data, supplementary annotation files were created based on the similarity between motifs and gene sequences. The initial step in evaluating the overexpression of specific motifs within a gene set involved calculating the Area Under the Curve (AUC) for each motif-motif pair. This was conducted based on the calculation of the recovery curve for gene sets associated with motif sequencing. Immune infiltration analysis of key genes   The CIBERSORT method was utilized to analyze the immune cells associated with key genes. Additionally, the relative proportions of 22 distinct immune cell types were evaluated, along with the expression levels of key genes within these immune cells. Drug sensitivity analysis of key genes   The R package “oncoPredict” was employed by utilizing the GDSC database to forecast the chemotherapy sensitivity of individual tumor samples. The IC50 estimates for each individual chemotherapeutic agent were derived through a regression analysis. The reliability of the regression and predictions was evaluated through tenfold cross-validation utilizing the GDSC database. Default values were applied for the parameters, including mitigating the impact of batch effect through "combat" and averaging the expression levels of identical genes. Nomogram model construction   Nomogram model was constructed utilizing regression analysis to visually depict the interactions among multiple variables within a predictive model. This representation involved plotting line segments with corresponding scales on a unified plane, in accordance with a predetermined ratio, thereby illustrating the relationship between gene expression and other variables. Through the development of a multifactor regression model, a score was allocated to each level of the influencing factors, reflecting their respective contributions to the outcome variable as demonstrated by the magnitude of the regression coefficients. The individual scores were then aggregated to yield a total score, which was employed to compute the predicted value. To validate the risk stratification capability of the nomogram, the linear predictor values for each sample were extracted from the established multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model using the predict() function, which served as risk scores. Subsequently, the samples were divided into low-risk and high-risk groups based on the median of the risk scores. Survival curves for both the low-risk and high-risk groups were plotted using the Kaplan–Meier method, and the survival differences between the two groups were compared using the log-rank test. Furthermore, separate Nomogram models were constructed using DSS and PFI as survival outcomes to evaluate patient prognosis from different perspectives. Single cell analysis   The expression profile was first examined utilizing the Seurat package, which involved filtering cells according to the total number of unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) per cell, the quantity of expressed genes, and the proportion of mitochondrial and ribosomal reads associated with each cell. The DoubletFinder tool (version 2.0.4) was employed to identify and filter double cells from each sample individually. Subsequently, the data underwent standardization and normalization processes, followed by PCA and sequential analysis. The optimal number of Pcs was identified utilizing an Elbow Plot, while the spatial relationships among the clusters were elucidated through UMAP analysis. The SingleR software was automated by querying the CellMarker and PanglaoDB databases, as well as relevant literature. An annotation-assisted methodology was employed to identify the cell types present in the respective tissue, along with their corresponding marker genes for cell annotation. In this study, the R language (version 4.3.0) was utilized for analysis. All statistical analyses were conducted as two-sided, a p-value of less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant. Results   Differential gene analysis and differential gene pathway analysis   There were two groups: the metastasis group, which consisted of patients with lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer, and the non-metastasis group, which included patients without lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer. The Limma package was employed to identify differential gene expression (p-value < 0.01) between these two groups. A total of 1,054 differentially expressed genes were identified, which included 317 related genes that were up-regulated and 737 related genes that were down-regulated (see Fig.  2 A-B). Go analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis were subsequently conducted on the screened differential genes. The Metascape analysis revealed that these genes were predominantly enriched in several biological processes, including acrosomal vesicle formation, regulation of neurotrophin TRK receptor signaling pathways, histone mRNA catabolic processes, protein catabolic processes, NAD biosynthetic processes, and monocarboxylic acid metabolic processes, among others (see Fig.  2 C). After excluding a broad array of pathways, the differentially expressed genes associated with lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer were primarily focused on the Wnt signaling pathway, pathways involved in cancer, the regulation of the neurotrophin TRK receptor signaling pathway, among other pathways. TRK signaling pathway regulates cell proliferation. The activation of the Wnt signaling pathway can influence the occurrence and progression of tumors. Serine/threonine protein kinase-related signaling pathways, including the MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways, are closely associated with the metastasis of gastric cancer. Consequently, these signaling pathways play a crucial role in the lymph node metastasis of gastric cancer.   Fig. 2. The exposure factors and outcome data were subsequently retrieved. Based on this data, a MR analysis was conducted, screening eight genes using the IVW method. These genes met the significance criterion of an IVW p-value of less than 0.05 in the causal inference process. Through MR analysis, a causal relationship was further identified among eight pairs of genes related to the positive outcome of eQTL, as illustrated in Fig.  3 A-H (IVW p-value < 0.05). The relevant genes encompass CDRT15P1, DENND3, F2R, FNDC3B, IRAK3, MS4A2, PDK4, and PKIA, totaling eight genes. The genes DENND3 (1.094; 1.021–1.173; p = 0.011), F2R (1.104; 1.031–1.182; p = 0.004), FNDC3B (1.308; 1.024–1.669; p = 0.031), and IRAK3 (1.087; 1.023–1.15 4; p = 0.007) may be linked to a heightened risk of gastric cancer. Conversely, CDRT15P1 (0.947; 0.900–0.997; p = 0.038), MS4A2 (0.784; 0.685–0.897; p = 0.000), PDK4 (0.807; 0.707–0.921; p = 0.001), and PKIA (0.922; 0.854–0.995; p = 0.037) may be linked to a decreased risk of gastric cancer. A sensitivity analysis was subsequently conducted to evaluate the causal relationships among the eight genes, utilizing the leave-one-out method to ascertain their reliability. The findings indicated that the exclusion of any individual SNP did not substantially affect the overall error margin, indicating that the identified pairs of causal relationships are stable and reliable (see Fig.  4 A-H).
