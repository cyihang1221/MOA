>Materials and methods   Study population   Patients with ESCC treated at Kaohsiung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital from September 2020 to December 2023 were included in this study. Clinical tumor staging was determined according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system (Ma et al.,  2017 ). The inclusion criteria for patients in this study were as follows: (1) a pathologically confirmed diagnosis of ESCC; (2) adults aged 20 years or older; (3) agreement to urine collection for OPFR testing; and (4) no treatment received prior to urine collection for OPFR testing. We excluded patients with adenocarcinoma, neuroendocrine tumors, small cell carcinoma, synchronous cancer such as head and neck cancer. Additionally, patients with a history of second primary cancers, either before or after ESCC diagnosis, and those with pregnancy or infectious diseases were excluded. Patients lacking complete data of regular laboratory blood/urine tests, imaging examinations, and survival follow-up were also excluded. Ultimately, 133 patients with ESCC who met the inclusion criteria were included. A control group consisting of 133 sex- and age-matched subjects (± 7 years) without any known history of malignancies was also included. For control group, the inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) adults aged 20 years or older, and (2) agreement to provide a urine sample for OPFR testing. The exclusion criteria were: (1) any known history of malignancies; (2) pregnancy or infectious diseases; and (3) hospitalization or surgery within three months prior to enrollment. All study participants were followed from the date of ESCC diagnosis until the date of death or the last known contact, with follow-up concluding on January 31, 2024. The dates of death or last contact were obtained from the patients; medical records. Measurement of urinary concentrations of OPFRs by ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS)   Urinary concentrations of 10 OPFR compounds were measured to evaluate exposure to OPFRs, including 5 triesters (TDCPP, TCEP, TBEP, TNBP, TPHP) and their corresponding diesters (BDCPP, BCEP, DBEP, DNBP, DPHP). First-void morning urine samples were collected within 1 week of inclusion in the study using polypropylene cups, and samples were stored in 1.5 mL amber microcentrifuge tubes at -80 °C.   OPFR measurements were conducted using a Waters Acquity UPLC system with a Waters Xevo TQ-XS mass spectrometer in negative or positive ESI mode (Tsai et al.,  2022 ). The separation used a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH Phenyl column with a Waters XBridge BEH C18 Direct Connect HP-isolated column. The mobile phases were 0.5% formic acid in water (A) and 0.5% formic acid in methanol (B). The gradient increased from 5 to 50% solvent B over 0.75 min, then to 100% over 3 min, was held for 4.5 min, and decreased back to 5% over 2 min.   Target analytes were identified by retention time and precursor-ion-product ion transitions, compared with standards. OPFR concentrations were quantified using 12-point calibration curves (0.02 ppb to 50 ppb). The limit of quantification (LOQ) was 0.02 ng/mL for all OPFRs except for BCEP, where the LOQ was 0.05 ng/mL BCEP. Recoveries were determined using individual isotope-labeled internal standards. Ethics statement   The current study was approved by the Chang Gung Medical Foundation Institutional Review Board (IRB number: 202001029B0). All procedures were performed in accordance with the ethical standards of the Institutional Research Committee and World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from each patient, and all methods were performed in accordance with approved protocol. Cell culture   KYSE-270 and TE11 cells were obtained from the European Collection of Cell Cultures (ECACC), and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, 100 μg/mL streptomycin, and 2 mmol/L L-glutamine (Shimada et al.,  1992 ; Takashima et al.,  2008 ). Het-1A, a human esophageal squamous epithelial cell line immortalized by SV40 transfection, was purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and cultured in BEBM medium (Lonza, Walkersville, MD, USA) (Tamagawa et al.,  2012 ). Cell viability assay and colony formation assay   The viability of sub-confluent cells was assessed using the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazole-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) reduction assay. Cells were seeded at a density of 5 × 10 3  cells per well in 96-well plates. The following day, cells were treated with TDCPP (0, 10, and 50 μM) for 72 h. It should be noted that samples with a TDCPP concentration of 0 were designated as the control group. Subsequently, MTT solution was added to each well, and the plates were incubated at 37 °C for 4 h. After incubation, 100 μL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added to each well and incubated in the dark for 10 min. Absorbance was measured at 540 nm using a microplate reader. The optical density (OD) values were normalized to the control group.   For the colony formation assay, cells were seeded in 60-mm dishes at a density of 5 × 10 3  cells per dish. The next day, cells were treated with or without TDCPP for 72 h. After 21 days, cell colonies were stained with 0.01% crystal violet and counted. Immunoblot analysis   For cell protein extraction, samples were homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer (50 mM Tris–HCl, pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, and 0.1% SDS). Protein concentrations were measured using the Bio-Rad Protein Assay (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Standard immunoblotting procedures were followed. Primary antibodies against FASN, EGFR, SREBP1, SCD1, COP1, ACC, STAT1, and β-actin (Sigma Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) were used due to their relevance to cancer development (M.-Y. Cui et al.,  2022 ; L.-Y. Li et al.,  2021 ; Z. Wang et al.,  2024 ). Detection was performed with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Bio/Can Scientific, Mississauga, ON, Canada) and Western Lighting Reagent, visualized on X-ray films. 4-Nitroquinoline 1-oxide (4-NQO)-induced ESCC murine model   4-NQO (Sigma Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) was dissolved in DMSO (50 mg/mL) and stored at − 20 °C. For administration, the stock solution was diluted with propylene glycol and added to autoclaved drinking water at 100 μg/mL. Six-week-old mice were randomized into 6 groups. One group received 4-NQO in drinking water for 8 weeks, followed by plain water for 20 weeks (n = 20). Another group received 4-NQO for 8 weeks and TDCPP (150 mg/kg) by oral gavage twice a week for 8 weeks, then TDCPP (350 μg/mL) in drinking water for 20 weeks (n = 20). Mice that died before the experiment;s end were excluded from the analysis.   At the end of the experiment, mice were autopsied, and the esophagus and stomach were opened and examined for macroscopic lesions. Tissues were fixed in 10% buffered formalin, paraffin-embedded, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE). Histological analysis, performed by a pathology expert, identified hyperplasia, dysplasia, papilloma, and invasive squamous cell carcinoma based on established criteria (Miyamoto et al.,  2008 ; Tang et al.,  2004 ). Analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) by RNA-sequencing in esophageal epithelium from mice treated with or without TDCPP   Total RNA from mouse esophagus was extracted using Trizol® Reagent (Invitrogen, USA), quantified with a ND-1000 spectrophotometer (Nanodrop Technology, USA), and assessed for quality using a Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technology, USA) with RNA 6000 LabChip kit (Agilent Technology, USA). Library preparation followed Illumina;s protocol using the SureSelect XT HS2 mRNA Library Preparation kit (Agilent, USA) with AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter, USA) for size selection. Sequencing was performed using Illumina;s SBS technology (150PE). Data were processed with Trimmomatic for quality trimming and HISAT2 for alignment. Expression levels were normalized to TPM, and differential expression was analyzed using StringTie and DESeq2. Hypergeometric p-values were used for statistical significance. Statistical analysis   In this study, an a priori power analysis was performed using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7) to estimate the required sample size for comparing BCEP detection rates between two groups using Fisher;s exact test. Based on preliminary data (Table  2 ) from a previous study (Cheng et al.,  2023 ), and assuming a two-tailed test with an alpha level of 0.05, a power (1–β) of 0.80, and a 1:1 allocation ratio, the estimated total sample size required was 260 participants (i.e., 130 per group). Multivariable analyses were adjusted for alcohol consumption, smoking, and betel nut chewing   To address the right-skewed distribution of OPFR data, urinary concentrations of OPFRs were reported using the geometric mean (GM) with its 95% confidence interval (CI), as well as the median and interquartile range (IQR). Detection rates of urinary OPFR concentrations exceeding the LOQ were compared between ESCC patients and control participants using the chi-square test. For undetectable values, a substitution of 0.5 times LOQ was employed in the calculation of GMs.   To assess distributional differences of OPFRs between the ESCC and control groups, the Mann–Whitney U test was used. GM differences of OPFRs between groups were evaluated using the independent-sample t-test. Given that control participants were matched to ESCC patients by age and sex, multivariable conditional logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess the association between OPFR concentrations and ESCC, adjusting for additional covariates including alcohol consumption, smoking, and betel nut chewing. Upon identifying OPFRs that significantly distinguished ESCC from controls, survival analysis was employed to explore the relations between specific OPFRs and mortality in ESCC patients. In the multivariable survival analyses, only those variables (e.g., tumor stage) that were significant in the univariate analyses were included as covariates.   For cell line experiments, the t-test was used for the statistical analysis. Each experiment was carried out independently at least twice, with 3 repeats each. For 4-NQO-induced ESCC mice experiments, statistical analyses of the incidence of esophageal tumors were performed using the Mann Whitney test.   The statistical analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS software, version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). All tests were 2-sided, and a  p -value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Results   Comparison of urinary OPFR concentrations between ESCC patients and controls   In this study, although we matched control patients to ESCC patients by sex and age, we also collected additional personal variables, including alcohol consumption, smoking status, betel nut chewing, and family history of cancer. The distribution of these variables between ESCC patients and controls is presented in Table  S1 .   A comparison of urinary OPFR concentrations between ESCC patients and control participants (Table  1 ) revealed that the detection rates of BDCPP (36.1% vs. 18.0%) and TDCPP (27.1% vs. 12.8%) were significantly higher in ESCC patients compared to control participants. Additionally, the GM concentration of BCEP was significantly higher in the ESCC group (GM = 0.21) than in the control group (GM = 0.12). In contrast, no significant differences were observed in the detection rates and concentrations of DBEP, TBEP, and TNBP between the 2 groups. Furthermore, DNBP, TCEP, and TPHP exhibited low detection rates in both groups, with no statistically significant differences. Detailed information on the distribution of urinary OPFR concentrations and creatinine-adjusted OPFR concentrations is presented in Table  S2 . The distributions of urinary OPFR concentrations (excluding missing values) and creatinine-adjusted OPFR concentrations (excluding missing values) in the ESCC and control groups are shown in Table  S3 . In conditional logistic regression models, higher urinary concentrations of BDCPP and TDCPP were associated with an increased risk of developing ESCC. Specifically, the odds ratio (OR) for TDCPP was 2.58 (95% CI: 1.33–5.03,  p  = 0.005) and for BDCPP was 2.50 (95% CI: 1.40–4.46,  p  = 0.002).   Survival analysis, including the log-rank test (Fig.  1 ) and Cox models (Table  3 ), revealed a significant association between TDCPP concentration and survival of ESCC patients after adjusting for other confounders significantly associated with ESCC survival in univariate Cox models (e.g., tumor stage). A TDCPP cutoff of 0.02 (i.e., LOQ) was associated with an increased hazard of mortality in ESCC patients (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] = 1.91, 95% CI: 1.10–3.30,  p  = 0.021). Detailed results of the log-rank tests and univariate Cox models are shown in Table  S4  and Table  S5 , respectively. Additionally, we also evaluated the dose–response relationship between urinary OPFR concentrations and survival of ESCC by converting the urinary OPFR concentration data into standardized continuous variables (i.e., Z-scores).
