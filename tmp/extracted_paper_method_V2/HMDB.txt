sample_description: The study population consisted of 572 middle-aged and older men (mean ± SD age: 59 ± 12 years) of White European (n=287), South Asian (n=143), and African Caribbean (n=142) ethnic origins, recruited from the Manchester area as part of the Human Serum Metabolome project (Dunn et al., 2015) and the European Male Ageing Study (EMAS). Exclusion criteria included missing metadata (age, BMI, comorbidity data), 'missing' or 'other' ethnicity classification, more than three reported comorbidities (to avoid multimorbidity confounding per Salisbury et al., 2011), and failure to match for age and BMI across groups. Samples were stored at −80 °C until analysis. For metabolomic profiling, two 50 µL aliquots per serum sample were prepared—one for LC–MS and one for GC–MS. Internal standards (lysine-d4, succinic-d4 acid, glycine-d5, benzoic acid-d5) were added to GC–MS aliquots prior to deproteinisation. Deproteinisation was performed using 200 µL ice-cold methanol. LC–MS samples underwent vortexing (10 s), centrifugation (13,500 g, 30 °C, 15 min), supernatant lyophilisation (overnight in vacuum centrifuge), and reconstitution in 100 µL methanol before transfer to glass vials. GC–MS samples underwent dual-step chemical derivatisation: first with 25 µL O-methoxyamine-HCl in anhydrous pyridine (65 °C, 40 min), then with 25 µL MSTFA (65 °C, 40 min); retention index (RI) standards (decane, dodecane, pentadecane, nonadecane, docosane, hexane) were used for chromatographic alignment.

sample_description: The sample used in the research consisted of 12 μm-thick cryosections from Wistar rat brain tissue, harvested following CO2 asphyxiation and rapid decapitation, then flash-frozen on powdered dry ice, wrapped in foil, and stored at −80°C until analysis. Adjacent tissue sections were thaw-mounted onto two distinct slide types: standard glass microscope slides for IR-MALDESI (infrared matrix-assisted laser desorption electrospray ionization) mass spectrometry imaging, and PEN-membrane slides for spatially resolved proteomic profiling using nanoPOTS (nanodroplet processing in one pot for trace samples). For IR-MALDESI, sections were analyzed immediately after mounting on a Peltier-cooled translational stage inside a nitrogen-purged enclosure with a controlled thin ice layer formed via humidity exposure. For nanoPOTS, sections were stained with 70% ethanol (pre-cooled to 4°C), Mayer’s hematoxylin (20 s), Scott’s Tap Water Substitute (10 s), eosin Y (5 s), and dehydrated through graded ethanol series (95% ×3, 100% ×2), dried in a fume hood for 10 min, and stored at −80°C. Five anatomically defined regions of interest (ROIs) — cerebral cortex, caudate putamen, corpus callosum, septal area, and an additional annotated region (ROI 1–5 per Figure 1) — were microdissected from the PEN-membrane slide using laser capture microdissection (LCM) on a PALM MicroBeam system with a 5× objective, laser energy level of 60, and 'CenterRoboLPC' catapulting function (delta energy 8, delta focus 15); four 100 × 100 μm tissue voxels per ROI were pooled into pre-filled 200 nL DMSO droplets in custom-fabricated nanowell chips and confirmed via 'CapCheck' function.
software_configuration_description: HMDB (Human Metabolome Database) was used for putative metabolite identification in the IR-MALDESI data analysis workflow. Ions were manually searched against HMDB versions 30–33 (cited as references 30–33) based on exact mass match and spectral accuracy; no specific version number or release date was stated, but the search was performed post-processing of imzML files generated from Thermo .RAW data. Identification criteria required ions to be either (i) at least 2-fold more abundant in one region of interest (ROI) relative to others, or (ii) present in ≥80% of pixels within one ROI and <20% in all other ROIs, as determined using the MSiPeakfinder tool in MSiReader. Additionally, HMDB was integrated into METASPACE — an online MSI annotation platform — where metabolite and lipid annotations were performed at a 20% false discovery rate (FDR), leveraging HMDB alongside LipidMaps and SwissLipids databases; parameters included spectral accuracy (mass tolerance unspecified but implied high-resolution, given Q Exactive Plus resolving power of 140,000 FWHM at 200 m/z), spatial informativeness (i.e., spatial distribution coherence), and detection of spatially correlated isotopic peaks.
