Overview
The overall architecture of TLCFormer is illustrated in Fig. 1. Given
an input video sequence of red-green-blue (RGB) frames Irgb ∈
R^(B×T×3×H×W) and thermal frames Ith ∈
R^(B×T×1×H×W), where B denotes batch size, T is the total
number of input frames, H and W are spatial resolution, we
uniformly sample S frames (S ≤ T) for spatio-temporal modeling. In
the present design, the internal sampled-frame number is fixed to a
short temporal context for efficient motion modeling, while the
effects of different temporal window lengths and sampling intervals
are further analyzed in “Temporal sensitivity analysis”. The
sampled sequence is then processed through the following stages:

sampled S frames into a 4D spatio-temporal cube
C∈R^(B×2×H×W×S).
2. MADA: Applies motion-aware difference attention to suppress
static background.
3. DLCM: Enhances local contrast to boost signal-to-clutter ratio.
4. Visual prior aggregation (VPA) Encoder: Extracts multi-scale
features using hybrid energy-preserving mixers.
5. Detection head: Produces classification, bounding box, and
centerness predictions.
Here, c1 and c2 denote the channel dimensions of different stages in
the hierarchical encoder. In our implementation, the backbone root
width is set as embed_dim = 96, and the subsequent stages follow a
standard stage-wise channel expansion strategy, resulting in stage
widths of 96/192/384/768. Therefore, the values c1 = 96 and c2 =
192 are determined by the backbone design rather than chosen
arbitrarily. As further validated by the width sensitivity analysis in
Sect.  4.8, this configuration provides a reasonable trade-off
between representation capacity and computational efficiency.
Motion-aware difference attention (MADA)
The key insight behind MADA is that small targets exhibit motion
continuity across frames, while background elements (clouds,
buildings, vegetation) remain relatively static. Unlike FFT-based
Doppler filtering that operates in the frequency domain and
struggles with high-frequency background noise, MADA directly
exploits temporal differences in the spatial domain. The detailed
architecture of MADA is illustrated in Fig. 2.
