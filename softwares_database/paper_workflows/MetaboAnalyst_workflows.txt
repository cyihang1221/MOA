sample_description: Sixteen male youth water polo athletes from the Chinese national team (mean age: 17.6 ± 0.5 years, mean height: 185.1 ± 3.9 cm, mean BMI: 23.9 ± 2.9) were recruited; all were field players (excluding goalkeepers), healthy, non-smoking, non-medicated, and free of metabolic disease or stimulant use; fasting venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected at 7:00 AM on two time points—E1 (pre-training, after a standardized one-week complete break/washout period to mitigate accumulated fatigue and establish a true resting metabolic baseline) and E2 (post-training, immediately after completion of one week of routine training); samples were drawn into 10 mL vacutainer tubes, centrifuged at 4 °C and 3000 rpm for 15 minutes, supernatant aliquoted (2 mL), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C; for analysis, 50 μL serum was mixed with 200 μL extraction reagent containing isotopic internal standards (¹³C- and ¹⁵N-labelled Alanine, Valine, Leucine, Phenylalanine, Glutamic acid, Tyrosine, Acetyl-L-carnitine, Propionyl-L-carnitine, Succinic acid, Fumaric acid, Glucose, Adenosine, Uridine), vortexed for 10 min at 1000 rpm, centrifuged at 15000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 min, filtered through a protein precipitation plate, freeze-dried, and reconstituted in 200 μL reconstitution solvent; 10 μL of reconstituted sample was injected for UPLC-Q-TOF/MS analysis; quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes of all sample homogenates, processed identically, and randomly interspersed throughout the analytical sequence to monitor system stability and performance.
complete_workflow_description: The metabolomic workflow involved: (1) Sample preparation as described above; (2) Chromatography–mass spectrometry analysis using HILIC separation on an Acquity™ BEH AMIDE column (1.7 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm) with mobile phase of water/acetonitrile (both containing 25 mM ammonium formate, pH 9.0), linear gradient from 2% to 98% aqueous over 10 min, flow rate 0.4 mL/min, column temperature 40 °C, and data acquisition in both positive and negative electrospray ionisation (ESI) modes; (3) Mass spectrometry performed on a Q Exactive HF-X hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with heated electrospray ionisation (HESI) source; full MS scans at resolution 70,000 (m/z 200), AGC target 1 × 10⁶, max injection time 100 ms, scan range m/z 70–1050; MS/MS spectra at resolution 17,500 (m/z 200), AGC target 1 × 10⁵, max injection time 50 ms, HCD fragmentation with NCE 40%, using ultrapure nitrogen; (4) Raw data processing using Compound Discoverer 3.0 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for peak picking, alignment, compound identification, and quantification; metabolite identification followed Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) levels: Level 1 (confirmed via RT and MS/MS match to in-house authentic standards: Glutamic acid, Succinic acid, Phenylalanine, Fumaric acid, Asparagine, Uric acid), Level 2 (putative annotation via MS/MS spectral matching to HMDB v5.0 and KEGG libraries with match score >0.7, validated additionally using mzVault and mzCloud); mass accuracy tolerances set at 20 ppm for MS¹ and 0.02 Da for MS², RT tolerance 0.2 min; adducts considered: [M+H]⁺, [M+Na]⁺, [M+NH₄]⁺ (positive mode); [M−H]⁻, [M+FA−H]⁻ (negative mode); (5) Statistical and pathway analysis conducted in MetaboAnalyst 6.0 as detailed; (6) Pathway analysis employed KEGG pathway mapping and MetPA (Metabolite Set Enrichment Analysis) for pathway enrichment and topology analysis, identifying lysine degradation (p < 0.01) and vitamin B6 metabolism (p < 0.05) as key altered pathways.

related_tool: MetaboAnalyst
sample_description: The study used liver tissue and serum samples from cats divided into two groups: feline hepatic lipidosis (FHL) cases (n = 7, referred to as FL group) and healthy controls (n = 7, referred to as CL group). FHL cases were newly diagnosed via histopathologic confirmation of liver biopsy specimens and abdominal ultrasonography, with no prior therapeutic interventions; all were first-opinion patients. Liver tissue samples (50 ± 1 mg each) were collected via percutaneous ultrasound-guided biopsy under general anesthesia using a 16G, 20-cm Pro MAG biopsy needle; samples were split for paraformaldehyde fixation (for immunohistochemistry and Oil Red O staining) and immediate storage at −80°C for metabolomics. Serum was obtained from fasting venous blood, centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C, and stored frozen. All samples underwent identical diagnostic and processing protocols. Quality control (QC) samples were generated by pooling 100 μL aliquots from individual extracts (n = 10), and 10% of total injections were QC samples to monitor system stability.
complete_workflow_description: The research employed a comprehensive metabolomics workflow involving GC–MS and LC–MS platforms, but did not use MetaboAnalyst software. Instead, data processing and statistical analysis were performed using ChromaTOF software (V4.3x, LECO) for peak extraction, baseline correction, deconvolution, integration, and alignment; qualitative identification used the LECO Fiehn Rtx5 database with mass spectral and retention time index matching. Peaks with detection rates <50% or relative standard deviation (RSD) >30% in QC samples were excluded. Data were centralized and analyzed in SIMCA (V16.0.2, Sartorius) for multivariate modeling: principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were applied, with rigorous validation including Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin testing (>0.6), 7-fold cross-validation, and 200 permutation tests to rule out overfitting. Differential metabolites were selected using VIP >1 and FDR-adjusted P < 0.05. Volcano plots were generated using R (v4.2.0) with the EnhancedVolcano package; ROC curves, AUC calculations (with 95% CIs from 2000 stratified bootstrap replicates), and Youden index–based cutoff determination were performed in GraphPad Prism v8.0. Internal validation included 1000 bootstrap iterations, 70/30 stratified train/validation splits, and synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) to address class imbalance; model performance was evaluated using the F₁-score. Analytical validation of serum 3-HB used enzymatic colorimetric assays on Hitachi 7180 and Beckman Coulter AU2700 analyzers. Statistical comparisons used IBM SPSS 18.0 with two-tailed Student’s t-tests and Holm–Bonferroni correction.

Metabolite annotation involved: (1) matching to an in-house physical standards library (RT, MS, MS/MS) via ADAP-KDB yielding 689 peaks mapping to 354 unique metabolites; (2) supplementary matching using public databases (NIST, HMDB) via MS and/or experimental MS/MS; (3) ontology-based evidence grading for match confidence; and (4) mummichog-driven functional annotation in MetaboAnalyst 5.0. GWAS was conducted in Plink 2.0 for race-stratified (Black: 8,534,915 variants; White: 5,886,255 variants) and joint meta-analyzed analyses (using METAL), adjusting for age, sex, metabolomic batch, and first 10 principal components; association residuals were inverse rank-normalized. Significance threshold was Bonferroni-corrected P = 6.6×10⁻¹³ (5×10⁻⁹ / 7,522 peaks); stepwise conditional analysis used GCTA-COJO to identify independent signals. Variant annotation used Ensembl VEP; literature contextualization leveraged GWAS Catalog, PhenoScanner v2.0, and manual curation. Validation used AMP-CMDKP (cmdkp.org) for PheWAS across 20 trait groups, querying regions defined as coding sequence ±50 kb.
