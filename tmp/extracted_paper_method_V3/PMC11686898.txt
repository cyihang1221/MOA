<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Background</h3>
<p id="Par1">To compare the impact of two different lipid emulsions, specifically a soybean oil-based emulsion and a multiple oil emulsion (soybean oil, medium-chain triglycerides, olive oil, and fish oil, SMOF), on serum metabolites of very low birth weight (VLBW) infants using untargeted metabolomics analysis.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<p id="Par2">A comparative study was conducted on 25 VLBW infants hospitalized in neonatal intensive care units (NICU) of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital in 2023. The infants were divided into the SMOF group (13 cases) and the soybean oil group (12 cases) based on the type of lipid emulsion used during parenteral nutrition. Serum samples were collected at birth and 14 days later from both groups. An untargeted metabolomics analysis was conducted on serum samples using Ultra-high Performance Liquid Chromatography - Tandem Mass Spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). Differential metabolites were identified using Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) modeling, with criteria of variable importance in the projection (VIP) &gt; 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) &lt; 0.05. Functional annotation of the top 30 differential metabolites, ranked by VIP value, was conducted using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database. Subsequently, enrichment and topological analyses were conducted with MetaboAnalyst software to identify key metabolic pathways. Finally, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed on metabolites associated with the identified key metabolic pathways.</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p id="Par3">The metabolomic analysis revealed that, in the OPLS-DA model, no significant metabolite differences were found in umbilical cord blood between the two groups. However, 84 differential metabolites were identified in the 14-day samples from the SMOF group compared to the soybean oil group. According to MetaboAnalyst analysis, glycerophospholipid metabolism emerged as the most significantly altered metabolic pathway following the administration of SMOF lipid emulsion. Additionally, ROC curve analysis confirmed a significant increase in LysoPC(20:5), PE(16:0/20:5), and PE-NMe(22:5/16:0) metabolites in the SMOF group.</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h3>
<p id="Par4">The administration of SMOF lipid emulsion in VLBW infants resulted in significant modifications of serum metabolites compared to traditional soybean oil-based lipid emulsions. Notably, glycerophospholipid metabolism was identified as the most prominently altered metabolic pathway. Metabolites, including LysoPC(20:5), PE(16:0/20:5), and PE-NMe(22:5/16:0), emerged as potential biomarkers.</p></section><section id="sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Clinical trial number</h3>
<p id="Par5">Not applicable.</p></section><section id="kwd-group1" lang="en" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Untargeted metabolomics, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, VLBW infants, SMOF lipid, Multiple oil lipid</p></section></section><section id="Sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Background</h2>
<p id="Par6">As the survival rate of very low birth weight (VLBW) infants gradually improves, reducing their complication rates and enhancing their quality of life have become increasingly important [<a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>, <a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>]. Common complications of VLBW infants include bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), neurological damage, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), infections, and parenteral nutrition-associated cholestasis (PNAC) [<a href="#CR1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR1">1</a>, <a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a>]. The pathogenesis of these complications is closely related to oxidative stress and inflammatory responses [<a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>, <a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a>]. Therefore, reducing inflammation and oxidative reactions in VLBW infants is crucial [<a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>].</p>
<p id="Par7">VLBW infants cannot meet their nutritional needs through enteral nutrition shortly after birth, highlighting the importance of parenteral nutrition [<a href="#CR5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR5">5</a>, <a href="#CR6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR6">6</a>]. In parenteral nutrition, lipid emulsions constitute the primary energy source. Clinically, soybean oil-based emulsions are the primary choice, composed primarily of linoleic acid (C18:2ω6), with lesser quantities of oleic acid (C18:1ω9) and α-linolenic acid (C18:3ω3). The ratio of ω-6 to ω-3 fatty acids commonly attains 6.5:1 or higher. Recent advancements have led to the use of multiple oil (soybean oil, medium-chain triglycerides, olive oil, and fish oil, SMOF) lipid emulsions in neonatal care [<a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>, <a href="#CR7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR7">7</a>]. SMOF lipid consists of 30% soybean oil, 30% medium-chain fatty acids, 25% olive oil, and 15% fish oil, providing comprehensive and balanced nutritional support [<a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a>]. It is enriched with ω-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids, specifically eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, C20:5ω3) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, C22:6ω3), ω-9 monounsaturated fatty acids, medium-chain fatty acids, abundant vitamin E, and has a low phytosterol content. The ratio of ω-6 to ω-3 fatty acids is approximately 2.5:1 [<a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a>, <a href="#CR9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR9">9</a>]. Its advantages include benefits in lipid metabolism, immune regulation, anti-inflammatory effects, nervous system development, and liver protection [<a href="#CR3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR3">3</a>, <a href="#CR8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR8">8</a>]. Current studies demonstrate the safety of SMOF for use in premature infants and its effectiveness in mitigating oxidative stress and inflammatory responses [<a href="#CR2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR2">2</a>, <a href="#CR4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR4">4</a>].</p>
<p id="Par8">Metabolomics technology has been widely used in researching various cancers and chronic diseases such as diabetes, coronary heart disease, and Alzheimer’s disease [<a href="#CR10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR10">10</a>–<a href="#CR12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR12">12</a>]. This technology has become a valuable tool for identifying biomarkers, investigating disease pathogenesis, and understanding the mechanisms of interventions and their outcomes [<a href="#CR13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR13">13</a>–<a href="#CR16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR16">16</a>]. Following the administration of SMOF lipid emulsion to VLBW infants, their serum metabolites are expected to undergo changes, which can be detected by metabolomics techniques. Researchers have used metabolomics to analyze the effects of feeding methods, such as breastfeeding or formula milk, on neonatal metabolic levels [<a href="#CR17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR17">17</a>–<a href="#CR19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR19">19</a>]. However, there is currently a paucity of metabolomics studies investigating the impact on serum metabolites following the administration of SMOF lipid emulsion in VLBW infants.</p>
<p id="Par9">This study aims to directly compare the changes in serum metabolites among VLBW infants following the administration of the soybean oil-based lipid emulsion and SMOF lipid emulsion using untargeted metabolomics techniques.</p></section><section id="Sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h2>
<section id="Sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Study design and subject management</h3>
<section id="Sec4"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Study setting and recruitment</h4>
<p id="Par10">This study adopted a comparative approach and was conducted in the neonatal intensive care units (NICU) of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital, a tertiary A-level specialist hospital focusing on maternal and child health care. The recruitment process took place from January 2023 to December 2023, targeting infants with very low birth weight. The study strictly adhered to ethical principles and was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Hangzhou Women’s Hospital (Approval No.: [2022]YLSA No. (6)-07).</p></section><section id="Sec5"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Subject selection and consent</h4>
<p id="Par11">Eligible infants were identified based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) birth weight of less than 1500 g; (2) immediate transfer to the NICU after birth; and (3) signing of relevant informed consent forms by the families of the infants. Exclusion criteria included: (1) incomplete data collection; (2) receipt of parenteral nutrition for fewer than 14 days; and (3) the presence of congenital genetic metabolic diseases or severe congenital malformations. To ensure the protection of subject identity and maintain confidentiality, all personal identifiers were removed from the collected data, and a unique study identifier was assigned to each participant. Informed consent was obtained from the families of the infants prior to any study-related procedures, including the collection of samples and clinical information.</p></section><section id="Sec6"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Randomization and intervention</h4>
<p id="Par12">In this study, a simple random sampling method was employed to randomly assign eligible VLBW infants into two groups: the SMOF group and the soybean oil group. To ensure fairness in allocation, the randomization sequence was determined by computer-generated random numbers. During the study period, both groups of subjects adhered to the same parenteral and enteral nutrition protocols. For parenteral nutrition, the SMOF group was administered SMOF lipid emulsion (trade name: Hewen), while the soybean oil group received soybean oil-based lipid emulsion (trade name: Intralipid).</p>
<p id="Par13">Regarding the nutritional regimen for the subjects, the specific details are as follows: on the first day of parenteral nutrition, the lipid emulsion dose was set at 1 g/kg/day; on the second day, the dose was increased to 2 g/kg/day; on the third day, the dose was further increased to 3 g/kg/day; and from the fourth day onwards, the dose was maintained at 3.5 g/kg/day or below. Both groups of subjects received a uniformly formulated glucose and amino acid solution. Enteral feeding was initiated within 24 h of birth, and if the mother’s milk supply was insufficient, donor human milk was provided as a supplement. As the volume of enteral feeding gradually increased, when the milk intake reached 60 ml/kg, the dose of lipid emulsion was gradually reduced based on its contribution to 35% of the calories provided by parenteral nutrition. Finally, when the enteral feeding volume reached 150 ml/kg, parenteral nutrition was completely discontinued.</p></section><section id="Sec7"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Sample collection and processing</h4>
<p id="Par14">Umbilical cord blood at birth and blood samples at 14 days of parenteral nutrition were collected from both groups for Ultra-high Performance Liquid Chromatography - Tandem Mass Spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). Specifically, the umbilical cord blood samples from the SMOF group were designated as the SMOF cord group, and the blood samples collected 14 days after birth were designated as the SMOF 14d group. Similarly, the umbilical cord blood samples from the soybean oil group were designated as the Intralipid cord group, and the blood samples obtained 14 days after birth were designated as the Intralipid 14d group. To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results, all samples were processed and analyzed according to standard protocols.</p></section></section><section id="Sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Metabolomics detection</h3>
<section id="Sec9"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Blood sampling</h4>
<p id="Par15">Cord blood samples were collected following umbilical cord clamping in newborns. For the D14 samples, blood was obtained during routine monitoring of relevant indices in preterm infants receiving parenteral nutrition, utilizing blood draws conducted as part of standard care. These samples were drawn into Vacuum collection tubes (polyester gel/silica additives, Becton Dickinson, Shanghai, China) and allowed to rest at room temperature for 1 h. They were then centrifuged at ≤ 1300×g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant serum was subsequently transferred into 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes using a pipette, with each tube containing no less than 100 μL of serum. The tubes were then stored at -80 °C for future analysis. To ensure accurate identification and tracking of samples throughout the study, each collection tube was meticulously labeled.</p></section><section id="Sec10"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Reagents and instruments</h4>
<p id="Par16">Chromatographically pure methanol (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure acetonitrile (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure formic acid (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure water (Fisher Scientific); chromatographically pure isopropanol (Fisher Scientific). Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled with Fourier transform mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q Exactive), Thermo Fisher Scientific; HSS T3 chromatographic column (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.8 μm), Waters Corporation, USA. JXDC-20 Nitrogen Evaporator, provided by Shanghai Jingxin Industrial Development Co., Ltd.; LNG-T88 Benchtop Rapid Centrifugal Concentration and Drying Apparatus, manufactured by Taicang Huamei Biochemical Instrument Factory; Wonbio-96c High-Throughput Tissue Homogenizer, supplied by Shanghai Wanbai Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; SBL-10TD Ultrasonic Cleaner with a capacity of 300 W-10 L, from Ningbo Xinzhi Biotech Co., Ltd.; Centrifuge 5430R High-Speed Refrigerated Centrifuge, produced by Eppendorf, Germany; and NewClassic MF MS105DU Electronic Balance, manufactured by Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland.</p></section><section id="Sec11"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">Extraction of serum samples</h4>
<p id="Par17">After thawing the serum samples stored at -80 °C, 100 μL of the liquid sample was added to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube with 300 μL of a solution (acetonitrile: methanol = 1:1, v/v) to extract metabolites. The mixture was vortexed thoroughly for 30 s and then subjected to low-temperature ultrasonic extraction for 30 min(5 °C and 40 kHz ). Following centrifugation, the supernatant was collected, dried under nitrogen, and re-solubilized with 100 μL of a solution (acetonitrile: water = 1:1, v/v). A second round of ultrasonic extraction and centrifugation was performed. The supernatant was transferred to sample vials for UHPLC-MS/MS analysis. A pooled quality control (QC) sample was prepared by mixing equal volumes of all samples, and a QC sample was inserted every 5–15 samples [<a href="#CR20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR20">20</a>–<a href="#CR23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR23">23</a>].</p></section><section id="Sec318"><h4 class="pmc_sec_title">UHPLC-MS/MS analysis</h4>
<p id="Par19">The mobile phases consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water: acetonitrile (95:5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile: isopropanol: water (47.5:47.5:5, v/v) (solvent B). The separation gradient in positive ion mode was as follows: 0–5 min, mobile phase B increased from 0 to 100%; 5–6.3 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%; 6.3–6.4 min, mobile phase B decreased from 100 to 0%; 6.4–8 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%. In negative ion mode: 0–5 min, mobile phase B increased from 0 to 100%; 5–6.3 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 100%; 6.3–6.4 min, mobile phase B decreased from 100 to 0%; 6.4–8 min, mobile phase B was maintained at 0%. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, and the column temperature was 40 °C.</p>
<p id="Par20">MS conditions: The mass spectrometry signal acquisition used both positive and negative ion scanning modes, with a mass scanning range of m/z: 70-1050. The ion spray voltage was set at 3400 V for positive ions and 2800 V for negative ions. The sheath gas was set at 40 psi, the auxiliary heating gas at 10 psi, and the ion source heating temperature was maintained at 400 °C. The collision energy cycled between 20-40-60 V. The MS1 resolution was set at 60,000, and the MS2 resolution was 7500.</p></section></section><section id="Sec12"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Data preprocessing and library searching</h3>
<p id="Par21">The UHPLC-MS/MS raw data were using Progenesis QI software (Waters, Milford, USA) through baseline filtering, peak identification, peak integration, retention time correction, and peak alignment. The resulting data matrix, containing sample names, m/z, retention time, and peak intensities, was exported for further analysis. Metabolites were identified by searching the Human Metabolome Database(HMDB)(<a href="http://www.hmdb.ca/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://www.hmdb.ca/</a>), Metlin Database (<a href="https://metlin.scripps.edu/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://metlin.scripps.edu/</a>), and the self-compiled Majorbio Database (MJDB) of Majorbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).</p>
<p id="Par22">The data matrix obtained from the database search was uploaded to the Majorbio cloud platform (<a href="https://cloud.majorbio.com" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://cloud.majorbio.com</a>) for analysis. First, the data matrix was pre-processed as follows: At least 80% of the metabolic features detected in any set of samples were retained. After filtering, the minimum value in the data matrix was selected to fill in the missing values, and each metabolic signature was normalized to the sum. To reduce errors caused by sample preparation, the response intensities of the sample mass spectrometry peaks were normalized using the sum normalization method, resulting in a normalized data matrix [<a href="#CR24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR24">24</a>–<a href="#CR26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR26">26</a>]. Variables of QC samples with a relative standard deviation (RSD) &gt; 30% were excluded and log10 transformed to obtain the final data matrix for subsequent analysis.</p></section><section id="Sec13"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Differential metabolite analysis</h3>
<p id="Par23">The R package “ropls” (Version 1.6.2) was used to perform principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), with 7-cycle interactive validation to evaluate the stability of the model. Metabolites with variable importance in the projection (VIP) &gt; 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) &lt; 0.05, adjusted using the Bonferroni correction method, were identified as significantly different metabolites, based on the VIP obtained from the OPLS-DA model and the FDR generated from the <em>p</em>-values obtained using Student’s t-test [<a href="#CR27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR27">27</a>].</p>
<p id="Par24">The top 30 differential metabolites, ranked by VIP values, were mapped to their biochemical pathways through metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis using the KEGG PATHWAY database (<a href="http://www.genome.jp/kegg/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://www.genome.jp/kegg/</a>). These metabolites were classified according to the pathways they were involved in or the functions they performed. Enrichment analysis was used to determine whether a group of metabolites appeared in a functional node [<a href="#CR28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR28">28</a>]. The principle was that the annotation analysis of a single metabolite developed into the annotation analysis of a group of metabolites. The Python package “scipy.stats” (<a href="https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/</a>) was used to perform enrichment analysis to identify the most relevant biological pathways and differential metabolites related to the experimental interventions [<a href="#CR29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR29">29</a>]. Additionally, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of these metabolites and compute Area Under Curve (AUC) values [<a href="#CR30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR30">30</a>].</p>

The permutation test of the OPLS-DA model yielded an R2Ycum of 0.957 and a Q2cum of 0.523, indicating good robustness of the model without overfitting, confirming its suitability for exploring metabolite differences between the two groups (Fig. <a href="#Fig3" class="usa-link">3</a>).</p>

<p id="Par35">No differential metabolites were found between the SMOF cord and Intralipid cord groups, indicating no differences in metabolites between the umbilical cord blood samples of SMOF and soybean oil group infants. However, 84 differential metabolites were identified between the SMOF 14d and Intralipid 14d groups (Table <a href="#Tab3" class="usa-link">3</a>), suggesting significant changes in metabolites between the SMOF group and the soybean oil group after 14 days of intervention. Among these, 47 metabolites were upregulated, and 37 were downregulated (Fig. <a href="#Fig4" class="usa-link">4</a>A). The supervised clustering analysis of the top 30 differentially expressed metabolites, ranked by VIP values, between the SMOF 14d and Intralipid 14d groups is shown in Fig. <a href="#Fig4" class="usa-link">4</a>B.</p>

<p id="Par38">The top 30 differential metabolites between the SMOF 14d and Intralipid 14d groups, ranked by VIP value, were analyzed using pathway topology analysis with the MetaboAnalyst 4.0 online platform. The analysis indicated that glycerophospholipid metabolism was a significant differential metabolic pathway between the SMOF 14d and Intralipid 14d groups (Fig. <a href="#Fig5" class="usa-link">5</a>). Within this pathway, the SMOF 14d group showed elevated levels of LysoPC(20:5), PE(16:0/20:5), and PE-NMe(22:5/16:0).</p>

<p id="Par40">ROC curves were generated using the three metabolites significantly elevated in the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway. The results indicated that, in differentiating between the SMOF 14d and Intralipid 14d groups, LysoPC(20:5) exhibited an AUC of 0.9861, with 100% specificity and 92% sensitivity. PE(16:0/20:5) displayed an AUC of 0.9514, with 75% specificity and 100% sensitivity. Similarly, PE-NMe(22:5/16:0) had an AUC of 0.9653, achieving 100% specificity and 92% sensitivity (Fig. <a href="#Fig6" class="usa-link">6</a>).</p>

<p id="Par42">Current clinical studies examining the administration of SMOF lipid emulsions, which contain fish oil components, to premature infants, have demonstrated an improvement in lipid tolerance among these infants [<a href="#CR31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR31">31</a>–<a href="#CR34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR34">34</a>]. This study utilized UHPLC-MS/MS untargeted metabolomics to compare serum metabolite profiles in VLBW infants receiving different lipid emulsions during parenteral nutrition. Our findings reveal that SMOF lipid emulsion administration significantly modifies the serum metabolite profile of these infants compared to soybean oil lipid emulsion.</p>
<p id="Par43">Our study found no substantial metabolite differences in the cord blood samples from both groups. However, after 14 days of parenteral nutrition, significant changes were observed in 84 metabolites within the SMOF group compared to the soybean oil group, with 56 (69%) of these metabolites belonging to the lipid category. This observation is in agreement with earlier research, which indicated that various lipid emulsions have differing impacts on the metabolism of premature infants [<a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>, <a href="#CR36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR36">36</a>].</p>
<p id="Par44">The results of metabolic pathway analysis indicated that the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway was the most significantly altered metabolic pathway in the SMOF group. Glycerophospholipids are components of biological membranes, bile, and membrane surfactants, participating in processes such as protein recognition and cell membrane signal transduction. They are also crucial components of neuronal membranes and brain phospholipids, playing a vital role in regulating synaptic function (20–22). Within this pathway, LysoPC(20:5), PE(16:0/20:5), and PE-NMe(22:5/16:0) were significantly upregulated. Notably, both LysoPC(20:5) and PE(16:0/20:5) contained an EPA chain. The elevation of these metabolites is likely associated with specific fatty acid constituents in the SMOF lipid emulsion, such as ω-3 fatty acids. This observation underscores that, following two weeks of infusion with fish-oil rich emulsions, specific fish-oil metabolites can be detected in the serum. Rita et al. [<a href="#CR35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR35">35</a>] conducted a study involving 80 very low birth weight infants, which revealed that infants in the SMOF group exhibited significantly higher plasma levels of phospholipid docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and EPA, as well as lower levels of arachidonic acid, compared to those receiving the soybean oil-based lipid emulsion.</p>
<p id="Par45">Numerous studies have demonstrated that omega-3 fatty acids exert extensive benefits on infants’ neurocognitive development, metabolism, and immune system, among other aspects [<a href="#CR37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR37">37</a>, <a href="#CR38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR38">38</a>]. Contemporary research indicates that LysoPC(20:5) elicits its biological functions by activating ion channels or binding to G protein-coupled receptors and Toll-like receptors (TLRs), playing a crucial role in various signaling pathways related to oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and apoptosis [<a href="#CR39" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR39">39</a>, <a href="#CR40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR40">40</a>]. PE (16:0/20:5) contains an EPA side chain. Studies have shown that dietary supplementation with PE rich in EPA (EPA-PE) can improve memory and cognitive functions, enhance stress resistance, and reduce cell apoptosis in SAMP8 mice (an animal model of Alzheimer’s disease) fed a high-fat diet [<a href="#CR41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR41">41</a>–<a href="#CR44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="CR44">44</a>]. These metabolites, serving as potential biomarkers, not only facilitate the monitoring of infants’ metabolic responses to lipid emulsions but also may enhance the assessment of their health status. However, additional research is necessary to elucidate the precise biological implications and long-term consequences of elevated metabolite levels after fat emulsion administration in VLBW infants.</p>
<p id="Par46">However, this study has several limitations. First, the limited sample size may compromise the generalizability and reliability of our findings. Second, the complexity of metabolite alterations hampers a comprehensive understanding of the specific roles and implications of certain differential metabolites. Therefore, we advocate for long-term follow-up studies in future research to assess the lasting health impacts of SMOF lipid emulsion on VLBW infants. Additionally, exploring other potential biomarkers can enhance our understanding of the mechanisms underlying the influence of SMOF lipid emulsion on infant metabolism. Furthermore, investigating the effects of different doses and ratios of lipid emulsion on infant metabolism and health status is a crucial step in refining clinical nutrition support protocols.</p>
<p id="Par47">In summary, this study utilized UHPLC-MS/MS for untargeted metabolomic analysis to elucidate the effects of SMOF lipid emulsion on serum metabolites in VLBW infants. Significant alterations observed in glycerophospholipid metabolic pathways, along with the identification of potential biomarkers, may provide additional guidance for the selection of suitable lipid emulsions for VLBW infants. However, to comprehensively understand the impact of SMOF lipid emulsion on the metabolism and overall health of VLBW infants, further validation and investigation are required through large-scale, longitudinal studies.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgements</h2>
