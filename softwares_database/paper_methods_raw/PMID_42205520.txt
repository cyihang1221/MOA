
Materials and methods
Study area
Camera trapping was conducted in Minami-Aizu, Fukushima
Prefecture, northern Honshu Island of Japan (Figure 1). The studied
area (ca. 30 km2) is an agroforest landscape interspersed with
settlements, rice-paddy fields, secondary deciduous forests (genera
Fagus and Quercus), and coniferous plantations (Chamaecyparis
obtusa, Cryptomeria japonica, and Pinus densiflora). The climate is
cool temperate, with a mean temperature ranging from ca. −9.0 °C
(in January) to 31.0 °C (in August) and annual precipitation of
1,000–1,250 mm for the studied period (observed at the Tajima
meteorological station of Japan Meteorological Agency: https://
www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/index.php). In general, the study
area has a heavy snow fall with a maximum snow depth of ca. 3.0
m. Both sika deer and Asiatic black bear range across the study area
with their stable populations (Enari et al. 2017, 2019; Fukushima
Prefecture 2021, 2022). Density of sika deer was estimated at 15–30
deer/km2, affecting significant degradations of understory
vegetations by the browsing pressures (Ministry of the Environment
2022; Yamashita et al. 2025). Although there were no scientific
estimations on bear abundances, the typical home range sizes of
bears are 20–100 km2 estimated by 100% minimum convex
polygon methods (e.g., Tamatani et al. 2001; Izumiyama and
Shiraishi 2004). They are typically less territorial, potentially
overlapping the home ranges among several bears in our studied
area.

Camera trapping
Ten camera traps were installed in forests (574–725 m above sea
level) for 3 studied years, 1.0–2.0 km apart between the nearest 2
camera stations (Fig. 1). We adopted deliberately biased placement
that cameras located on animal trails or footpaths to maximize
animals’ detections (Meek et al. 2014). The mean and standard
deviation of straight distances between 2 closest cameras each were
1.72 and 0.35 km, respectively (estimated from coordinate records
at each camera station in field device of eTrex 32×, Garmin Inc.).
Passive infrared cameras (TrophyCam 24 MP, Bushnell Inc.) with a
trigger speed of 0.3 s were used, which were programmed to take 1-
min video clips during detection, with a 5-min delay. The cameras
were mounted on trees 1.0–1.2 m above ground level, without baits
or lures, at an appropriate distance (5–15 m) from an animal trail/
footpath and angle to capture the whole body of large animals (if
needed, when a camera site was on a slope). The study period
covered summer and autumn seasons (i.e., nonhibernating seasons
of black bear) for 3 years, that is, from August 10 to November 23
in 2021, from June 20 to November 22 in 2022, and from October
6 to December 4 in 2023. Batteries were changed, and memory
cards were replaced every month for the studied durations.
Generally, antler casting in adult and subadult males of sika deer is
from March to May, antler regrowth starts within a week and, then,
completes for ca. a month after the casting (Miura 1984). Fawning
season of sika deer is typically from May to July and breeding (i.e.,
rutting) season is typically in autumn in Japan (Nagata 2015; Enari
et al. 2017, 2019). Therefore, our sampled periods covered the
breeding season of sika deer in each studied year.
After the field survey, the animal species in the video clips were
identified (if possible), and the date and time (24:00-h unit) of
capture were recorded. Videos that had captured black bear and
sika deer were compiled. Consequently, we found that Asiatic black
bears were more frequently detected across the whole studied area
in 2022 than in the remaining 2 years (i.e., 2021 and 2023; Fig. 2).
Therefore, we divided the datasets into 2 periods, that is, bear-
active (2022) and bear-inactive (2021 and 2023) years, to compare
the behavioral responses by sika deer. The abundant masting of
Fagaceae acorns in 2022 (Fukushima prefecture 2023) might be
attributed to the increase of bear detections caused by
concentrating their uses of Fagaceae forest patches surrounding our
camera stations (Appendix S1 in Supporting Information).
Furthermore, to test our hypothesis that the antipredator responses
by sika deer might vary by sex, we divided the video clips of sika
deer captured into females and males by identifying the presence of
antlers. Our sampled periods were out of the antler casting season,
enabling to identify sexual differences from videos. Videos in which
the sex was difficult to identify were removed from our datasets for
further analyses.

Spatial cooccurrence and temporal activity analyses
The relative abundance index (RAI) was estimated at each camera-
trapping station and the studied year as the total number of animal
detections per 100 camera-trapping days (O’Brien et al. 2003).
During estimation, 2 or more consecutive images of the same
species within 30 min at a station were treated as a single detection
event (Meek et al. 2014) and the latter detections removed from the
datasets for this analysis. In analyzing the patterns in spatial
cooccurrences between black bears and sika deer, the coefficients of
Spearman’s correlation (rs) between bear and deer RAIs (n = 30 for
each sex) were estimated using R ver. 4.3.1 (R Core Team 2023)
and the base package performed under R-Studio ver.2023.09.1.
In analyzing temporal activity overlaps between bear and deer,
Dhat as the coefficient of temporal overlap was estimated using
kernel density interference (Ridout and Linkie 2009), which ranges
from 0 (complete separation) to 1 (complete overlap). The data for
the time of day (0:00–23:59) that the animal images were captured
(i.e., the beginning times of video clips) were transformed to
circular data (i.e., 0–2π) before the analysis. The latter detections of
2 or more consecutive images of the same species within 30 min
were removed as same as the RAI estimations. Dhat was estimated
separately between the 2 periods defined above, that is, the bear-
active and -inactive years. In assessing the reliability of the
estimated Dhat, a smoothed bootstrap test was performed using
10,000 simulated samples to calculate the 95% confidence interval
(CI) (Meredith and Ridout 2021). By analyzing the procedures,
Dhat1 was adopted for datasets containing <50 samples of either
species, whereas Dhat4 was adopted for datasets containing >50
samples of both species, in accordance with the criteria given by
Meredith and Ridout (2021). Furthermore, Watson’s 2-sample test
was performed for circular data to analyze statistically significant
differences between bear and deer activity patterns. In this method,
the null hypothesis that the patterns in 2 activities homogenize was
assessed with P = 0.05 as a critical value. For these analyses,
“overlap” ver. 0.3.9 (Meredith and Ridout 2021) and “circular” ver.
0.5-0 packages (Agostinelli and Lund 2024) in R were used.
