</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="abstract1"><h2>Background:</h2>
<p>Nanofat is an injectable oily emulsion, rich in adipose-derived stem cells and growth factors. It is prepared from lipoaspirates through mechanical emulsification and filtration. Despite being successfully used in several procedures in regenerative medicine such as scar attenuation, skin rejuvenation, and treatment of chronic wounds, little is known about exactly how nanofat induces regeneration in treated skin at the molecular level.</p>
<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods:</h3>
<p>Microfat and nanofat samples were isolated from 18 healthy patients. Proteomic profiling was performed through untargeted mass spectrometry proteomics and multiplex antibody arrays. Pathway enrichment analysis of differentially expressed proteins between microfat and nanofat was performed using Gene Ontology, Reactome, and Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes as reference databases.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results:</h3>
<p>Untargeted proteomics showed that up-regulated genes in nanofat are involved in innate immunity responses, coagulation, and wound healing, whereas down-regulated genes were linked to cellular migration and extracellular matrix production. Secretome array screening of microfat and nanofat samples showed no significantly different expression, which strongly suggests that the mechanical emulsification step does not affect the concentration of tissue regeneration biomarkers. The identified proteins are involved in wound healing, cellular migration, extracellular matrix remodeling, angiogenesis, stress response, and immune response.</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusions:</h3>
<p>Mechanical processing of lipoaspirates into nanofat significantly influences the proteome profile by enhancing inflammation, antimicrobial, and wound healing pathways. Nanofat is extremely rich in tissue repair and tissue remodeling factors. This study shows that the effects of microfat and nanofat treatment are based on up-regulated inflammation, antimicrobial, and wound healing pathways. Mechanical emulsification does not alter the concentration of tissue regeneration biomarkers.</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Clinical Relevance Statement:</h3>
<p>In addition to adipose-derived stems cells, nanofat contains distinct tissue repair and remodelling factors, which explains its beneficial effects on tissue regeneration.</p></section></section><hr class="headless">
<p>Autologous fat grafting is a surgical technique that has been used for over a century in aesthetic procedures.<sup><a href="#R1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R1">1</a>,<a href="#R2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R2">2</a></sup> Through empirical observation of grafting outcomes in patients, it became evident that adipose tissue had a regenerative therapeutic effect with potential applications.<sup><a href="#R3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R3">3</a></sup> The view of adipose as an autologous cell therapy was reinforced by the discovery of adipose-derived stem cells, which are mesenchymal stem cells with multipotent differentiation capacity. Adipose-derived stem cells can differentiate into adipogenic, myogenic, neural, endothelial, osteogenic, and chondrogenic cells.<sup><a href="#R4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R4">4</a>,<a href="#R5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R5">5</a></sup> Besides adipose-derived stem cells and adipocytes, adipose also contains endothelial cells, fibroblasts, pericytes, hematopoietic cells, and immune cells.</p>
<p>Over time, lipoaspirate processing methods have developed to improve grafting success rates, adipose viability, and therapeutic outcome. Selection of the preferred processing method depends on the patient needs and clinical intention. Adipose mechanical emulsification and filtration is the main technique used to produce liquid grafts. Microfat is an injectable graft with intact adipocytes used to add volume.<sup><a href="#R1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R1">1</a>,<a href="#R6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R6">6</a></sup> Nanofat is obtained from microfat through further emulsification and filtration steps. Tonnard and colleagues demonstrated that, although the majority of adipocytes are destroyed in nanofat, adipose-derived stem cells are still present.<sup><a href="#R7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R7">7</a></sup> Nanofat also contains microvascular fragments that act as a niche for surviving pericytes and endothelial cells.<sup><a href="#R8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R8">8</a>,<a href="#R9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R9">9</a></sup> Because of its consistency, nanofat is therefore not used to add volume but rather to act as a regenerative booster.<sup><a href="#R10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R10">10</a></sup> Nanofat is injected intradermally and has proven to be clinically effective for skin rejuvenation,<sup><a href="#R10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R10">10</a>–<a href="#R13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R13">13</a></sup> skin discoloration attenuation,<sup><a href="#R14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R14">14</a></sup> hypertrophic and atrophic scar correction,<sup><a href="#R15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R15">15</a>–<a href="#R18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R18">18</a></sup> burn scars,<sup><a href="#R19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R19">19</a></sup> facial scleroderma,<sup><a href="#R20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R20">20</a></sup> and chronic wound healing.<sup><a href="#R21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R21">21</a>,<a href="#R22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R22">22</a></sup> Nanofat further improves tissue elasticity and quality of speech when used to treat vocal cord scars and velar scars in velopharyngeal incompetence.<sup><a href="#R23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R23">23</a>,<a href="#R24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R24">24</a></sup></p>
<p>Until now, the molecular components responsible for tissue regeneration have been investigated only superficially. The clinical success of nanofat has been speculated to be attributable to adipose-derived stem cells rather than adipocytes and connective tissue. In addition, there is a lack of identified biomarkers to predict the outcome of a procedure based on the patient’s health status. Therefore, it would be highly beneficial to correlate the profile and content of tissue regeneration biomarkers to the patient’s age, lifestyle, and medical history to determine whether nanofat would be a suitable strategy for treating hypertrophic scars, chronic ulcers, or especially unsuccessful standard-of-care therapy cases. It is also important to note that nanofat preparation is faster, more accessible, and more economical than adipose-derived stem cell isolation or stromal vascular fractions. In addition, nanofat production is associated with fewer regulatory barriers than adipose-derived stem cells or stromal vascular fractions in the operating room, as it does not require enzymatic digestion or to be prepared under good manufacturing practices.<sup><a href="#R25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R25">25</a>–<a href="#R28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R28">28</a></sup></p>
<p>Growth factor presence in nanofat has been previously investigated through immunoassays that determined that it is loaded with proangiogenic factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor-A, platelet-derived growth factor, and basic fibroblast growth factor, which can improve graft survival and neovascularization.<sup><a href="#R11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R11">11</a>,<a href="#R29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R29">29</a>–<a href="#R31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R31">31</a></sup> Nevertheless, despite these findings, a broad proteomic screening of tissue regeneration factors has never been performed. Interestingly, Banyard and colleagues found that mechanical processing of lipoaspirates into nanofat induces an up-regulation of pathways that promote the regenerative ability of mesenchymal stem cells.<sup><a href="#R32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R32">32</a></sup> Therefore, we investigated whether the processing of microfat into nanofat would induce changes in the protein profile. In this study, protein profiling of samples was performed through two approaches: untargeted proteomics (label-free mass spectrometry) and profiling through multiplex antibody arrays (Fig. <a href="#F1" class="usa-link">1</a>).</p>

<section id="sec6"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Patient Selection, Ethics, and Sample Collection</h3>
<p>Microfat and nanofat samples were harvested from subcutaneous abdominal adipose tissue during liposuction and fat grafting procedures at the Department of Plastic Surgery and Hand Surgery of the Zurich University Hospital. Eighteen male and female patients aged 16 to 45 years, without comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, obesity, and cancer), gave signed patient consent with respect to the ethical guidelines of the local ethics committee (Kantonale Ethikkommission Zürich, KEK-Nr. 2017-00261).</p>
<p>Samples were prepared according to Tonnard et al.<sup><a href="#R7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R7">7</a></sup> Microfat was rinsed with saline and connective tissue was removed. It was then mechanically shuffled 30 times between two 10-cc syringes connected by 2.4- and 1.2-mm Luer-lock connectors. Lastly, it was filtered through a Nano Transfer Cartridge (Tulip Medical Products, San Diego, Calif.).</p>
<p>The resulting nanofat is an injectable lipid emulsion (Fig. <a href="#F2" class="usa-link">2</a>).<sup><a href="#R8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R8">8</a></sup> For our study, microfat samples were used as nonmechanically processed controls. Samples were immediately mixed with a protease inhibitor cocktail (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany), snap-frozen, and stored at −80°C until delivery to the Functional Genomics Center Zurich for further processing.</p>

<p>Nanofat sample processing and liquid chromatography mass spectrometry were performed at the Functional Genomics Centre Zurich. Raw mass spectrometry data was processed with MaxQuant and with the SRM Service R Package.<sup><a href="#R33" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R33">33</a>,<a href="#R34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R34">34</a></sup> Protein fold changes were computed using the limma package.<sup><a href="#R35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R35">35</a></sup> Detailed protocols regarding sample processing and mass spectrometry are available on request.</p></section><section id="sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Antibody Arrays</h3>
<p>Human angiogenesis, apoptosis, protease/protease inhibitors, and XL cytokine protein array kits (R&amp;D Systems, Minneapolis, Minn.; and Bio-Techne, Zug, Switzerland) were used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Microfat and nanofat samples from six patients were homogenized (Minilys; Bertin, Frankfurt am Main, Germany) in 500 µl of lysis buffer (phosphate-buffered saline) (Gibco, Thermo Fisher, Waltham, Mass.), protease inhibitors (Merck), and 1% TritonX-100 (Merck) with Precellys lysing kits (Bertin), before storage at −80°C overnight. Samples were unthawed on ice and centrifuged for 5 minutes at 10,000 <em>g</em>. A Bradford assay (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, Calif.) was used to calculate 100 µg/ml protein per sample to be pooled (<em>n</em> = 3/array). Membranes were imaged on a chemiluminescence analyser (Fusion FX; Vilber, Eberhardzell, Germany) and the pixel intensity was measured by the microarray plugin (OptiNav, Bellevue, Wash.) for ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Md.). Average pixel density of the duplicate spots was normalized with negative control spots. Pixel density analysis was performed independently by two blinded researchers.</p>
<p>The expression of 195 proteins involved in angiogenesis, inflammation, proteolysis, and programmed cell death was assessed. We excluded 24 proteins from subsequent bioinformatics analyses because of low signals. Signal intensity values are considered as a relative protein expression profile.<sup><a href="#R36" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R36">36</a></sup></p></section><section id="sec9"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Bioinformatic and Statistical Analysis</h3>
<p>Overrepresentation enrichment analysis of differentially expressed proteins between nanofat and microfat was performed with the NetworkAnalyst (version 3.0) platform.<sup><a href="#R37" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R37">37</a></sup> The Reactome, Gene Ontology, and Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes databases were used for understanding the biological function of identified proteins and their involvement in biochemical pathways.<sup><a href="#R38" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R38">38</a>–<a href="#R40" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R40">40</a></sup> A value of <em>p</em> &lt; 0.05 was used as a cutoff for the significantly enriched pathways in NetworkAnalyst. Redundant pathways and subpathways (hierarchically belonging to previously selected pathways) were excluded.</p>
<p>Relative protein expression levels from the antibody arrays are presented as pixel intensity means. Protein expression values from nanofat versus microfat were compared. Statistical significance was assessed with GraphPad Prism Version 8 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, Calif.) with multiple <em>t</em> tests (two-stage linear step-up procedure of Benjamini, Krieger, and Yekutieli).</p></section></section><section id="sec10"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">RESULTS</h2>
<section id="sec11"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Untargeted Proteomics: Mechanical Processing of Microfat into Nanofat Up-Regulates Proinflammatory and Wound Healing Pathways</h3>
<p>Untargeted mass spectrometry showed significantly different proteomic profiles between microfat and nanofat that may be linked to mechanical processing. We detected 1702 proteins with mass spectrometry. Among them, 216 proteins were significantly up-regulated in nanofat, and 130 were down-regulated. <p>As the therapeutic applications of fat grafts are shifting from aesthetic procedures to treating issues such as hypertrophic scars and chronic wounds, characterization of tissue regeneration biomarkers at the molecular level is essential for the success of their future use. Fat grafting results are highly variable because of the patient’s background, the condition to be treated, and a variety of preparation protocols depending on the surgeon’s preferences. Detailed molecular characterization of fat grafts would contribute to understanding what it takes to ensure long-term therapeutic effects and prevent repeated interventions. Several attempts to improve the regenerative effect of fat grafts have been developed, such as supplementation with adipose-derived stem cells, growth factors, platelet-rich plasma, and stromal vascular fraction.<sup><a href="#R11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R11">11</a>,<a href="#R41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R41">41</a>–<a href="#R44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R44">44</a></sup> However, these procedures are expensive and time-consuming and face regulatory restrictions. This study confirms that the mechanical emulsification of microfat into nanofat induces changes in the proteome (Fig. <a href="#F6" class="usa-link">6</a>). Nanofat prepared from healthy patients contains tissue repair factors linked to wound healing and extracellular matrix remodeling, which could even make enrichment procedures with adipose-derived stem cells, stromal vascular fraction, or platelet-rich plasma unnecessary. Despite the conception that nanofat’s regenerative power was attributable to a concentration or purification of adipose-derived stem cells, the adipose-derived stem cell content has actually been reported to be lower in nanofat in comparison to unprocessed adipose.<sup><a href="#R7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R7">7</a>,<a href="#R22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R22">22</a></sup> However, interestingly, our multiplex antibody array results show no major differences in growth factor and cytokine concentration between nanofat and microfat. This makes nanofat a highly accessible source of tissue repair factors.</p>

<section id="sec14"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Antimicrobial Effect</h3>
<p>Our results reveal pathways that could explain nanofat’s antimicrobial effects (Figs. <a href="#F3" class="usa-link">3</a> and <a href="#F5" class="usa-link">5</a>).<sup><a href="#R21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R21">21</a>,<a href="#R22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R22">22</a></sup> We observed a clear enrichment of the complement and coagulation cascades, which are key innate immune responses responsible for fighting bacterial infection, promoting phagocytosis and clearance of apoptotic cells. Fc-gamma receptor–dependent phagocytosis of opsonized pathogens, another up-regulated pathway in nanofat (Fig. <a href="#F3" class="usa-link">3</a>), is crucial in fighting <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> infections, which frequently occur in chronic wounds. <em>S. aureus</em> has several strategies to escape phagocytosis, such as blocking of complement C3 protein,<sup><a href="#R45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R45">45</a></sup> blocking immunoglobulin Gs through staphylococcal protein A,<sup><a href="#R46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R46">46</a></sup> and even secreting toxins to evade neutrophils after internalization.<sup><a href="#R47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R47">47</a>,<a href="#R48" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R48">48</a></sup> Phagocytosis up-regulation in nanofat may significantly counteract <em>S. aureus</em> antiphagocytic strategies and may also explain the striking therapeutic effect of nanofat when used to treat infected ulcers.<sup><a href="#R22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R22">22</a></sup></p></section><section id="sec15"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Wound Healing</h3>
<p>We further observed an up-regulation of pathways in wound healing, such as fibrin clot formation (Fig. <a href="#F3" class="usa-link">3</a>) and acute inflammatory responses (Fig. <a href="#F3" class="usa-link">3</a>), which are essential in the early phases of wound healing. The interleukin-6 signaling pathway (Fig. <a href="#F3" class="usa-link">3</a>) plays a critical role in the early inflammatory response to injury, where leukocytes infiltrate to clear debris and start hemostasis.<sup><a href="#R49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R49">49</a></sup> Interleukin-6 signaling then promotes a transition into a proliferative phase, where angiogenesis, extracellular matrix synthesis, and reepithelialization occur. The relevance of interleukin-6 in nanofat was confirmed in our antibody arrays: interleukin-6 induced pathways such as MAPK, JAK-STAT, TNF, and interleukin-17 are enriched (Fig. <a href="#F5" class="usa-link">5</a>, <em>above</em>, <em>right</em>). Chronic activation of interleukin-6 signaling is present in chronic wounds, hypertrophic scars, and keloid pathogenesis.<sup><a href="#R50" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R50">50</a>–<a href="#R52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R52">52</a></sup> Therefore, it is unclear how an up-regulation of interleukin-6 signaling in nanofat can contribute to its therapeutic effect. Studies in neural injury and liver damage models have shown that interleukin-6 is an exceptionally versatile cytokine that could actually promote neuroprotection and liver regeneration.<sup><a href="#R53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R53">53</a>,<a href="#R54" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R54">54</a></sup> We hypothesize that nanofat could transiently induce acute inflammation and promote immune cell recruitment to trigger scar remodeling in mature hypertrophic scars (where interleukin-6 is back to basal levels).</p></section><section id="sec16"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Antifibrotic Effect</h3>
<p>AMPK tightly regulates lipolysis and fatty acid oxidation,<sup><a href="#R55" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R55">55</a></sup> which are both enriched pathways in up-regulated proteins in nanofat (Fig. <a href="#F5" class="usa-link">5</a>, <em>left</em>) (<strong>see Table, Supplemental Digital Content 2</strong>, <a href="http://links.lww.com/PRS/F212" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">http://links.lww.com/PRS/F212</a>). AMPK signaling is another pathway found to be significantly enhanced in nanofat compared to microfat (Fig. <a href="#F5" class="usa-link">5</a>, <em>left</em>). AMPK is a metabolic sensor that is activated when nutrients in the organism are low, which promotes subsequent adenosine triphosphate production and energy expenditure. Zhang et al. found that the expression of genes involved in fatty acid oxidation is altered in liver and kidney fibrosis models. Pharmacologic activation of AMPK signaling reduced fibrosis.<sup><a href="#R56" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R56">56</a></sup> The antifibrotic effect of AMPK signaling was also confirmed in skin fibrosis in vitro models.<sup><a href="#R57" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R57">57</a></sup></p>
<p>Dermal white adipose tissue is a recently identified subclass of white adipose tissue with unique functions in wound healing, hair cycling, and innate antimicrobial immunity.<sup><a href="#R58" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R58">58</a>,<a href="#R59" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R59">59</a></sup> Dermal white adipose tissue contributes to wound healing through fibroblast recruitment during the proliferative phase.<sup><a href="#R60" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R60">60</a></sup> Dermal adipose is reduced in fibrotic skin in systemic scleroderma patients.<sup><a href="#R61" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R61">61</a>,<a href="#R62" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R62">62</a></sup> Whether dermal white adipose tissue is significantly reduced in hypertrophic scars and keloids has not been yet verified because of a lack of human biomarkers. During wounding, adipocytes undergo lipolysis<sup><a href="#R63" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R63">63</a></sup> and differentiate into myofibroblasts, which can significantly contribute to fibrosis pathogenesis.<sup><a href="#R61" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R61">61</a>,<a href="#R64" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R64">64</a></sup> Lipid release into the wound promotes skin repair through inflammatory macrophage infiltration.</p>
<p>Hoerst and colleagues showed that hypertrophic scar–derived myofibroblasts cultured with adipose-conditioned media decreased expression of α-smooth muscle actin and other extracellular matrix genes. Furthermore, conditioned media harvested from mature adipocytes cultures had a stronger effect in decreasing expression of α-smooth muscle actin, collagen 1, and collagen 3 compared to adipose-derived stem cell–conditioned media.<sup><a href="#R64" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R64">64</a></sup> This correlates with significantly down-regulated proteins in nanofat being linked to collagen and elastin production, extracellular matrix degradation, and extracellular matrix organization (Fig. <a href="#F4" class="usa-link">4</a>). It is possible that nanofat, originating from lysed mature adipocytes, has a stronger antifibrotic effect and could promote myofibroblast reprograming into adipocytes because of a different growth factor and cytokine profile.</p></section><section id="sec17"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Programmed Cell Death</h3>
<p>Ferroptosis and necroptosis are two novel forms of programmed cell death that are induced by oxidative stress and may have a potential role in wound healing and scarring. Both ferroptosis and necroptosis were found to be enriched in nanofat (Fig. <a href="#F3" class="usa-link">3</a>). Necroptosis is a programmed form of necrosis that releases danger signals that induce inflammation. These danger signals, better known as death-associated molecular patterns and pathogen-associated molecular patterns, are highly proinflammatory and are released during tissue injury and pathogen infiltration.<sup><a href="#R65" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R65">65</a>,<a href="#R66" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R66">66</a></sup> Ferroptosis is caused by high iron levels and lipid reactive oxygen species accumulation.<sup><a href="#R67" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R67">67</a></sup> Oxidized lipids acting as death signals are strongly proinflammatory and are linked to neurodegenerative disease pathophysiology, ischemic injuries, and cancer.<sup><a href="#R68" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R68">68</a>,<a href="#R69" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R69">69</a></sup> Of note, polyunsaturated fatty acids are among the lipid types released during dermal adipocyte lipolysis in the inflammatory phase of wound healing,<sup><a href="#R63" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R63">63</a></sup> and their iron-dependent peroxidation induces ferroptosis.</p></section><section id="sec18"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Future Directions</h3>
<p>Because of the relevance of lipid signaling<sup><a href="#R70" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R70">70</a></sup> and its emerging role in wound healing,<sup><a href="#R71" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R71">71</a>–<a href="#R73" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="R73">73</a></sup> we are currently investigating the lipidome and metabolome of nanofat. Like the proteome, it is possible that mechanical processing of adipose tissue influences the lipidome and metabolome. The present work is one step further in understanding the mechanism of action of nanofat after engraftment in the skin. In addition, we proved that the clinical success of nanofat should be attributed not only to adipose-derived stem cells but also to nanofat being an emulsion of growth factors, cytokines, and lipids.</p></section></section><section id="sec19"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">CONCLUSIONS</h2>
<p>Nanofat is becoming a recognized scar and chronic wound therapy and is rich in tissue repair factors. Our study showed that mechanical processing of microfat into nanofat significantly changes the proteome profile and influences its regenerative potential. Differentially expressed proteins in nanofat are involved in innate immunity, coagulation, wound healing, and extracellular matrix remodelling. Autologous fat grafts have been widely applied in regenerative medicine in the past century, but it was always unclear how they promote tissue remodeling once engrafted. Rather than focusing on single pathways, our results provide the “big picture” of the mechanism of action.</p></section><section id="sec20"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">ACKNOWLEDGMENTS</h2>
