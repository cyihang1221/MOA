>Materials and methods   Chemical analysis of Jingui Shenqi pill   JGSQP is comprised of commercially available Jingui Shenqi Pill (Lot No. 180201, Henan, China), which is composed of eight botanical drugs, including  Rehmannia glutinosa  Libosch (Dihuang),  Dioscorea opposite  (Shanyao),  Cornus officinalis  (Shanzhuyu),  Alisma plantago-aquatica  (Zexie),  Wolfiporia cocos  (Schw.) Wolf (Fuling),  Paeonia delavayi  (Danpi),  Cinnamomum cassia  (Guizhi) and  Aconitum carmichaelii  Debx (Fuzi), were purchased from Zhongjing Wanxi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. According to the literature 19 , 20 , the main chemical components of JGSQP were analyzed by Ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-Q-TOF-MS) (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific, UK) in Shanghai Biotree Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd. Animal experiment   Eight-week-old FVB mice (weighing 23–25 g) were purchased from Beijing Spelford Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) (Certificate No. SCXK-Jing 2019-0010). After 1 week of adaptive feeding, FVB mice were used as a normal control group for further analysis. MKR mice were kindly provided by the laboratory of Dr. D. LeRoith at the Diabetes Research Center, National Institutes of Health (MD, USA). The MKR mice are generated by overexpressing the insulin-resistant diabetic muscle IGF-I receptor (IGF-IR)-lysine-arginine (MKR) in skeletal muscle, and a useful model for exploring the glucose-induced disease. In this study, MKR mice (8 weeks old) were bred by natural mating for experimental research and housed at the specific-pathogen-free Laboratory Animal Center of Hunan University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, License No. SYXK (Xiang) 2013-0005. All mice were maintained under standard laboratory conditions, with a temperature of 25 °C ± 2 °C, relative humidity of 50% ± 15%, and a 12-h dark–light cycle with free access to food and water. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the guidelines set by the same committee.   To investigate whether JGSQP rescues the progression of DOP in mice, fifty MKR mice were fed a special high-fat diet (HFD) for 8 weeks to induce diabetic osteoporosis 21 . HFD containing 10% lard, 20% sucrose, 5% cholesterol, and 65% standard feed was customized by XieTong biological company (Wuhan, Jiangsu Province, China). After 8 weeks of HFD feeding, DOP mice were randomly divided into five groups ( n  = 10 per group): the DOP model group (model); the high-dose JGSQP (2.6 g/kg)-treatment group (JGSQP-H); the middle-dose JGSQP (1.3 g/kg)-treatment group (JGSQP-M); the low-dose JGSQP (0.65 g/kg)-treatment group (JGSQP-L); and the alendronate (9.75 mg/kg)-treatment group (ALN). These doses were derived from the human equivalent clinical dose by conversion using body surface area normalization factors. All drug treatments were dissolved in normal saline and given orally for 4 weeks, and the dosages were derived from an equivalent conversion between animals and humans by body surface area based on the recommended daily human dosage. Additionally, mice in the normal and model groups were given an equal volume of distilled water daily. For the confirmation of diabetic osteoporosis, the bone trabecular damage was evaluated by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. The body weight of mice was recorded at regular time intervals, and blood glucose level was monitored weekly until the end of the animal experiment. The serum lipid profile, including total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL), was assessed across all study groups. After 4 weeks of treatment, the mice were euthanized using isoflurane anesthesia followed by cervical dislocation, in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines and the ARRIVE guidelines, to ensure humane treatment and confirm death. Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)   After 4 weeks treatment with JGSQP by gavage administration, the OGTT was performed using as previously described 22 . Briefly, after an overnight fast of 10 h, all mice were loaded with 2 g/kg body weight D-glucose (10% D glucose solution; Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) by oral administration. Blood was sampled from the tail vein at 0 (baseline), 30, 60, 90, and 120 min to detect glucose levels using a glucometer. The area under the curves (AUC; units: mmol/L·H) was used to calculate the glucose tolerance. Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining   Following harvesting of left femoral tissues, the samples were decalcified in 10% tetracyclin EDTA solution(0.5 M, pH = 8.0)(Servicebio༉ at room temperature for 2 weeks. Next, all samples were dehydrated through a gradient series of ethanol in a dehydrator (Leica, ASP300s) and then embedded in paraffin wax (Leica, 1150). After trimming the paraffin blocks, 4-µm-thick bone tissue slices were prepared using a paraffin tissue slicer (Leica, RM2245). The sections were stained with the H&E staining solution. The sections were imaged using an Olympus BX53 light microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). The histological analyses, including bone volume per tissue volume (BV/TV), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), and trabecular thickness (Tb.Th) were performed by Image Pro Plus 6.0 software. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)   On the last day of the animal experiment, the mice were anesthetized with isoflurane and blood samples were collected from the orbital veins plexus and placed in EDTA tubes. The serum was collected after centrifugation and stored in a refrigerator at −80℃ for subsequent assays. The serum concentrations of β-C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen (β-CTX), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and procollagen type 1-N-terminal propeptide (PINP) were measured using mouse ELISA kits (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The optical density (OD) values were determined using a microplate reader (EnVision, PerkinElmer, USA) at 450 nm. Microcomputed tomography (Micro-CT) imaging and analysis 23     After harvesting, for Micro-CT analysis, the right femurs of mice were quickly put into 4% paraformaldehyde solution and fixed for 24 h (4℃). Subsequently, the femurs were analyzed using a micro-CT imaging system (Quantum GX, PerkinEImer, Singapore) under the following settings: 80 kV voltage, current of 100 µA, rotation step of 0.4°, slice thickness of 5 μm, and a scanning duration of 14 min. Following the scan, the two-dimensional (2D) image sequence was automatically reconstructed into a three-dimensional (3D) volume with an isotropic voxel size of 15.9 μm, using Skyscan NRecon software and the Feldkamp-Davis-Kress algorithm. The trabecular microstructure of the femur was analyzed by selecting a 1.0 mm volume of interest, starting 0.45 mm below the proximal tibial growth plate and extending 1.0 mm distally. The structural parameters used to evaluate trabecular bone included trabecular number (Tb.N), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), bone volume (BV), percentage of bone volume per tissue volume (BV/TV), trabecular volume (TV), and bone mineral density (BMD). Additionally, the structure model index (SMI) was measured to assess cortical bone parameters. Preparation of JGSQP-medicated serum   The medicated serum approach was employed to simulate the in vivo metabolic processing of JGSQP. This method ensures that the cells are exposed to the bioavailable metabolites of the formula, thereby providing a more physiologically relevant model than the direct application of the herbal extract, which can be confounded by issues of solubility, osmolarity, and non-bioavailable components. Because cellular medications were needed for in vitro experiments, JGSQP - medicated serum was prepared as in previous studies 24 , 25 . After 7 days of adaptive feeding, ten Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (250 g) were randomly divided into the JGSQP and blank groups (5 rats per group). The rats in JGSQP group were administered at 6.5 g/kg/d (converted for the adult daily dose), and the blank group was given an equal volume of distilled water at the same time every day. Rats were gavaged twice a day for 5 days. The blood was obtained from the abdominal aortic 2 h after the final gavage of anesthesia, then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min at 4◦C. The supernatants were calefied in a water bath at 56℃ for 30 min and following of filtration using a 0.22 µM fine-pore filter, then finally packed and stored at −80 °C for future use. Cell culture and in vitro differentiation   The mouse pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 subclone 14 cell line was purchased from the Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). The MC3T3-E1 was cultured in α-minimum essential medium (α-MEM, Solarbio, Beijing, China) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Hycclone, Logan USA), and 1% (v/v) penicillin/streptomycin (P/S, Procell Life Science &Technology Co., China), All cells were maintained at 37 °C in 5% CO 2 . For the in vitro differentiation experiments, once the MC3T3-E1 cells reached confluence, they were seeded at a density of 1 × 10 4  cells/well in 6-well plates and cultured in complete cell culture medium supplemented with differentiation medium. The differentiation medium contained 10 mM β-Glycerol phosphate disodium salt pentahydrate, 5 µmol/L L-Ascorbic acid, and 1 mmol/L Dexamethasone (Sigma-Aldrich Trading Co.Ltd, Darmstadt, Germany), while the fresh differentiation medium was replaced every 2–3 days.   Subsequently, cells were cultured in various groups, including control medium (α-MEM), HG medium (25 mM glucose to mimic diabetic conditions), HG + serum medium (with the 25 mM glucose and blank medicated) and HG + drug serum (with the 25 mM glucose and JGSQP medicated) for 48 h to investigate the potential mechanism of JGSQP in osteoblasts. Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay   For cell viability assay, MC3T3- E1 cells were seeded into a 96-well plate with 3 × 10 3  well cell density, after culturing with complete α-MEM for 12 h, the cells were treated with increasing concentrations of JGSQP (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30%) for 48 h under HG (25 mM glucose)conditions and incubated with CCK8 solution (TransGen Bioech, Beijing, China)) at 37 °C for 1.5 h, at the end of the incubation period, the OD value was evaluated at 450 nm under a microplate reader (EnVision, PerkinElmer, USA), and the cell viability was calculated using the following formula: (OD  treated cells  – OD  blank )/(OD  control  – OD  blank ) × 100%. Western blotting   Protein samples from the cells were extracted using a lysis buffer (KeyGen Biotech. Co. Ltd, Nanjing, China) containing a cocktail of protease and phosphatase inhibitors for 30 min on ice. After determining protein concentration using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Keygen Biotech, Nanjing, China), equal amounts of protein samples (30 µg) were loaded onto sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were then blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature (RT). The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Next day, after three washes with Tris-buffered saline, 0.2% Tween 20 (TBST), the membranes were incubated with appropriate horseradish appropriate (HRP) conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at RT. Finally, following washing three times with TBST, the enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kits (Advansta, Menlo Park, CA, USA) were used to develop protein bands. The sensitive chemiluminescence images were quantified with the Bio-Rad gel imaging system and ImageJ software, and the relative expression of proteins were normalized to β-actin. ALP staining and activity assay 26     For the ALP activity assay in MC3T3-E1 cells, ALP staining was conducted using a BCIP/NBT alkaline phosphatase color development kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions. In brief, the medium was removed, and the cells were washed with PBS and fixed in 4% PFA for 15 min and then exposed to 1% ALP staining buffer in the dark for 20 min, and then observed under a light microscope. Alizarin red S staining 7     To measure the role of JGSQP on osteogenesis, after osteogenic differentiation for 21 days Alizarin Red S staining was performed to determine the matrix mineralization. The cells were gently washed twice with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 20 min, and rinsed with PBS. Then, cells were incubated with 1% Alizarin Red S staining (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) for 30 min at 37 °C. Cells were then washed with ddH2O to remove excess dye, and the stained matrix was captured under a light microscope (the calcified nodules are bright red in color). Network pharmacology analysis   First, the bioactive ingredients of JGSQP were identified using The Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology database (TCMSP,  http://tcmspw.com ), according to the filtering parameters of which was oral bioavailability (OB) >30%, and drug-likeness (DL) >0.18, as these are well-established benchmarks for identifying compounds with a high probability of adequate oral absorption. Then, we used ‘type 2 diabetes mellitus, osteoporosis’ as search terms in a cascade of databases including Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM,  https://omim.org/ ), GeneCards database ( https://www.genecards.org/ ), PharmGkb ( https://www.pharmgkb.org/ ), Therapeutic Target database (TTD,  http://db.idrblab.net/ttd/ ) and DrugBank database ( https://www.drugbank.com/ ), then we combined the results from databases to improve the accuracy of prediction, and screened potential disease targets related to DOP. Take the intersection of JGSQP targets and disease targets to ultimately obtain common targets, which are defined as the predicted targets of JGSQP for treating the DOP. Next, all intersecting targets were imported into universal Protein Resource database (UniProt,  https://www.uniprot.org/ ) to standardize the names into gene symbols.   To illustrate the roles of potential therapeutic targets, genes related to the targets of JGSQP in the treatment of DOP were obtained and uploaded into the STRING database ( http://www.string-db.org ) to identify human target proteins interaction. Additionally, herbs, bioactive ingredients and potential targets were imported into the Cytoscape software(National Institute of General Medical Sciences, USA) to construct the visual protein-protein interaction (PPI) network diagram. Afterwards, the Metascape database ( http://metascape.Org/ ) was utilized to conduct the ontology (GO) functional and KEGG enrichment analysis 27 – 29 . Metabolomics analysis and data acquisition   Mice were fasted for 8 h and blood samples were collected from the ocular vein in the morning. Blood samples were collected in a 2 mL vacuum blood collection tube containing the chelating agent ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA). The samples were centrifuged for 15 min (1500 rpm, 4 °C) to obtain plasma. Each equal sample (150 µL) of the plasma sample was quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at-80 °C for subsequent testing. Untargeted metabolomics analysis was performed using UHPLC (1290 Infinity LC, Agilent Technologies, CA, USA) coupled to an electrospray ionization source-linked quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (Q-TOF-MS, AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600, Framingham, MA, USA), provided by Shanghai Applied Protein Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Integration analysis of network pharmacology and metabolomics 30     By comparing the potential pathways enriched by the network pharmacological targets with the metabolic pathways enriched by differential metabolites, the common metabolic pathways which were regarded as the key pathways were screened out. The KEGG database was used to obtain the proteins contained in the key metabolic pathways to screen the key targets from the potential targets of network pharmacology. Immunofluorescence staining   For immunofluorescence staining, MC3T3-E1 (5 × 10 5 /mL) were seeded on coverslips and culture ed with differentiation medium for 7–14 days, treated with JGSQP or HG for 24 h. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, after washing with PBS for three times. Cells were permeabilized using 1% Triton X-100 in PBS for 20 min, and after blocking by 5% BSA at RT for 1 h, primary antibodies (anti-AGEs, anti-NFKB, Abcam) were incubated overnight at 4 °C.Subsequently, the fluorescent secondary antibodies (Invitrogen) were incubated for 1 h at RT. DAPI was used to counterstain the nucleus and cells images were observed under a fluorescent microscope (Olympus IX73). Statistical analysis   With the exception of the analysis software used for annotation, all data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Either t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare two groups or multiple groups. Analysis and plotting were performed by using GraphPad Prism version 8.0 software (San Diego, CA, USA). Significant differences were defined as  P  values < 0.05, < 0.01, < 0.001, and < 0.0001, and were denoted by * or #, ** or ##, *** or ###, and **** or ####, respectively.
