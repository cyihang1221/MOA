</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="ab005"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<section id="sec1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Objective</h3>
<p>This study is mainly to elucidate the characteristic components, hepatoprotective effect, and the underlying anti-hepatic fibrosis molecular mechanism of the total flavonoids of <em>Tetrastigma hemsleyanum</em> Diels &amp; Gilg (SYQTF).</p></section><section id="sec2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Methods</h3>
<p>The components of SYQTF were confirmed based on global natural products social molecular networking (GNPS) as well as the hepatoprotective effect on CCl<sub>4</sub>-induced acute liver injury. Cell toxicity and viability, cell cycle, and apoptosis analyses were then performed to determine the effects of SYQTF in transforming growth factor-<em>β</em>1 (TGF-<em>β</em>1) (10 ng/mL)-induced HSC-T6 hepatic fibrosis cell model. Finally, the lentiviral particles to package recombinant Ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate 1 (Rac1) over-expression and Rac1 knockdown plasmids were transfected into TGF-β1-induced HSC-T6 cells to elucidate the mechanism of anti-hepatic fibrosis of SYQTF targeting Rac1, respectively.</p></section><section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h3>
<p>Our findings demonstrated that 86 components in SYQTF were identified in MS<sup>E</sup> and FastDDA mode with the aid of GNPS. Moreover, S<sub>15</sub>-ZJ, the genuine functional food and medicinal materials of <em>T. hemsleyanum</em>, was significantly different from other regions by multivariate statistical methods based on progenesis QI. Remarkably, kaempferol-3-<em>O</em>-furananose7-<em>O</em>-rhamnosyl-glucoside, the highest response intensity in S<sub>15</sub>-ZJ, may act as the potential Q-marker of genuine functional food and medicinal materials of <em>T. hemsleyanum</em> in Zhejiang Province distinguished from other areas. In addition, the <em>in vivo</em> experiments revealed that SYQTF greatly alleviated CCl<sub>4</sub>-induced acute liver injury. Furthermore, SYQTF can reversible the activation and proliferation of HSC-T6 cells, block the cell cycle in the G<sub>0</sub>/G<sub>1</sub> phase, and promote cell apoptosis. Remarkably, the study is the first to reveal that SYQTF exhibited the anti-hepatic fibrosis effect through targeting Rac1 in the hedgehog signaling pathway.</p></section><section id="sec4"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Conclusion</h3>
<p>Rac1 is an important signaling molecule for the activation of the hedgehog pathway in HSC-T6. Importantly, SYQTF exerted its anti-hepatic fibrosis effect by inhibiting the Rac1-hedgehog signaling pathway. Furthermore, kaempferol-3-<em>O</em>-furananose7-<em>O</em>-rhamnosyl-glucoside could be a potential Q-marker of <em>T. hemsleyanum</em> in Zhejiang Province distinguished from other areas.</p></section><section id="kg005" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> anti-hepatic fibrosis, hedgehog signaling pathway, metabolomics, Rac1, <em>Tetrastigma hemsleyanum</em> Diels &amp; Gilg, total flavonoids</p></section></section><section id="s0005"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">1. Introduction</h2>
<p id="p0005"><em>Tetrastigma hemsleyanum</em> Diels &amp; Gilg (Sanyeqing in Chinese, SYQ) is consumed as both a unique and precious functional food and a Chinese herb medicine in China (<a href="#b0120" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0120">Sun et al., 2015</a>). The plant is mainly distributed in Zhejiang Province, China and is suited to grow in shady and moist hillsides and valleys. SYQ is pungent, bitter, and cool, with the effect of clearing away heat and detoxification, eliminating swelling and pain, promoting blood circulation and removing blood stasis, and dispelling wind and phlegm (<a href="#b0180" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0180">Zhu, Xu, Ying, Cao, &amp; Wu, 2020</a>). Moreover, both the roots and whole grass of SYQ can be used as medicine, with efficacies such as clearing heat, dissipating stasis, and relieving pain. A variety of complicated chemical constituents have been investigated in this plant, especially flavonoids and phenolic acids (<a href="#b0030" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0030">Hu et al., 2021</a>, <a href="#b0045" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0045">Li et al., 2021</a>, <a href="#b0080" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0080">Nebieridze et al., 2017</a>, <a href="#b0115" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0115">Sun et al., 2013</a>, <a href="#b0125" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0125">Sun et al., 2017</a>, <a href="#b0110" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0110">Sun et al., 2018</a>, <a href="#b0105" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0105">Sun et al., 2020</a>). Based on modern pharmacological studies, SYQ has the effects of hepatoprotective, anti-oxidation, anti-tumor, etc. (<a href="#b0020" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0020">Feng et al., 2014</a>, <a href="#b0050" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0050">Liu et al., 2020</a>, <a href="#b0040" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0040">Li et al., 2020</a>, <a href="#b0145" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0145">Wu et al., 2021</a>, <a href="#b0150" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0150">Xiao et al., 2023</a>, <a href="#b0155" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0155">Ye and Liu, 2015</a>, <a href="#b0175" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0175">Zhong et al., 2006</a>, <a href="#b0170" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0170">Zhang and Ni, 2008</a>). However, there were few studies on the anti-hepatic fibrosis effect of SYQTF.</p>
<p id="p0010">Hepatic fibrosis (HF) is a pathological state common to all types of liver disease, along with the duration of the injury. HF involves dynamic changes in multiple cytokines and the process of self-healing of liver injury, which is a necessary stage for cirrhosis and liver cancer (<a href="#b0090" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0090">Qu et al., 2019</a>, <a href="#b0100" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0100">Sommerhalder et al., 2021</a>). It was found that the development of HF is closely linked to the activation of HSCs (hepatic stellate cells) in liver tissue (<a href="#b0130" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0130">Tai et al., 2016</a>, <a href="#b0055" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0055">Ma et al., 2022</a>, <a href="#b0095" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0095">Shen et al., 2022</a>, <a href="#b0160" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0160">Ye et al., 2024</a>). Under normal conditions, HSCs are in a quiescent state, and when hepatocytes are injured, other cells in the vicinity secrete various cytokines by paracrine action, such as TGF-β1, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 (IL-1), which ultimately lead to the continued development of activation of HSCs, resulting in excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM). Therefore, activation of HSCs is considered to be a key link in the development of liver fibrosis and is important for the mechanism, effective prevention, and treatment of hepatic fibrosis. In addition, oxidative stress is a state of excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reduced antioxidant capacity in the body or cells, leading to inflammatory reactions and lipid peroxidation, resulting in tissue or cellular damage, and is one of the important factors of liver injury (<a href="#b0065" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0065">Malhi and Gores, 2008</a>, <a href="#b0135" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0135">Torok, 2016</a>, <a href="#b0140" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0140">Wang et al., 2016</a>). Moreover, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase (NADPH oxidase, NOX) is the main producer of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS). Meanwhile, NOX has multiple isoforms, which induce activation, proliferation, survival, and migration of HSCs and stimulate apoptosis and inflammatory responses in hepatocytes (<a href="#b0070" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0070">Mortezaee, 2018</a>). Most importantly, ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate 1 (Rac1), a subunit of NOX, regulates a variety of cellular functions (<a href="#b0035" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0035">Kabirifar et al., 2017</a>), and it is involved in more cell signaling pathways, which affect cell adhesion, proliferation, apoptosis, motility, and cell cycle (<a href="#b0075" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0075">Navarro-Lérida et al., 2015</a>). In the meantime, the hedgehog (Hh) signaling pathway is a highly conserved and complex cellular signaling pathway that plays a key role in embryonic development and in coordinating liver repair responses. It has been confirmed that the Hh signaling pathway can regulate a variety of cellular functions, namely cell activation, proliferation, and cell cycle (<a href="#b0005" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0005">Briscoe and Thérond, 2013</a>, <a href="#b0165" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0165">Zhang et al., 2019</a>). Literature has shown that the Hh signaling pathway plays an important role in the repair process of liver injury, and the level of the pathway is related to the degree of liver injury (<a href="#b0085" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0085">Omenetti et al., 2011</a>, <a href="#b0025" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0025">Gao et al., 2018</a>, <a href="#b0060" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0060">Machado and Diehl, 2018</a>). Furthermore, the Hh signaling pathway can facilitate the conversion of resting HSCs to an activated state or Muscle into fibrous hepatic stellate cells (MF-HSCs). In addition, studies have found that Rac1 is abnormally highly expressed in liver fibrosis models <em>in vitro</em>. Moreover, it has been shown that activation of Rac1 enhances Hh signaling and promotes the conversion of Quiescent hepatic stellate cells (Q-HSCs) into MF-HSCs (<a href="#b0015" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0015">Choi et al., 2010</a>). However, whether the anti-hepatic fibrosis effect of SYQTF is also regulated by Rac1-hedgehog has not been fully elucidated.</p>
<p id="p0015">Based on our group's previous study superfine particles of SYQ have significant hepatoprotective effects against CCl<sub>4</sub>-induced acute liver damage in rats (<a href="#b0010" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="b0010">Cao, Li, Chen, Cai, &amp; Tu, 2014</a>). In addition, we confirmed SYQTF components based on GNPS as well as the hepatoprotective effects on acute liver injury. Furthermore, we conducted relevant anti-hepatic fibrosis experiments of SYQTF targeting Rac1 with lentiviral particles to package recombinant Rac1 overexpression and Rac1 knockdown plasmids. Finally, the results suggested that SYQTF exerted its anti-hepatic fibrosis effect by inhibiting the Rac1-hedgehog signaling pathway.</p></section><section id="s0010"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">2. UPLC-XEVO G2-XS QTOF MS<sup>E</sup> and FastDDA analysis</h3>
<p id="p0030">The dried underground parts of SYQ were crushed and a 10.00 g sample was accurately weighed, added an ethanol/water (70:30, <em>v</em>/<em>v</em>) mixture with 30 mL, was reflux-extracted twice for 2 h. <p id="p0035">The sample detection was carried out on an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography coupled to an I-class Xevo G2-XS QTOF mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. A CORTECS®UPLC®C<sub>18</sub> (1.6 μm; 2.1 mm × 100 mm) with a column temperature of 35 °C was used, and the injection volume was 1 µL. Mobile phase A was H<sub>2</sub>O with 5 mmol/L Ammonium Formate, and mobile phase B was 100% acetonitrile. The samples were separated with the flow rate of 0.3 mL/min (FastDDA mode) or 0.4 mL/min (MS<sup>E</sup> mode) based on previous optimized conditions. Based on preliminary experiments, the final gradient programs were determined as follows: 0–1.6 min, 5% B; 1.6–2.1 min, 5 → 8% B; 2.1–3.3 min, 8 → 9% B; 3.3–3.8 min, 9% B; 3.8–4.8 min, 9 → 10% B; 4.8–5.2 min, 10% B; 5.2–6.0 min, 10 → 11% B; 6.0–6.2 min, 11% B; 6.2–7.2 min, 11 → 15% B; 7.2–9 min, 15 → 16% B; 9–11 min, 16% B; 11–12.5 min, 16 → 30% B; 12.5–14 min, 30 → 35% B; 14–14.5 min, 35% B; 14.5–15.5 min, 35 → 80% B; 15.5–16 min, 80% B; 16–18 min, 80 → 95% B; 18–19 min, 95 → 5% B; 19–20 min, 5% B. UV detection wavelengths were set at 320 nm and 254 nm.</p>
<p id="p0040">The mass spectrometer was operated both in positive and negative ion mode. The genuine medicinal material (S<sub>15-</sub>ZJ) was carried on collecting the mass data in multiple detection modes of MS<sup>E</sup> and FastDDA, comprehensively. Other samples were only identified under MS<sup>E</sup> mode. All samples were collected in five replications in positive and negative modes, respectively. The MS<sup>E</sup> conditions were as follows: mass scanning range was monitored from 50 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> to 1,200 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em>. The source temperature was 120 °C; the desolvation temperature of 500 °C; and the capillary voltage was set at 20–45 eV in positive ion mode and 30–60 eV in negative ion mode, respectively. In addition, FastDDA conditions were as follows: sample cone voltage, 40 V; source temperature, 100 °C; desolvation temperature, 500 °C; cone gas flow: 100 L/h and desolvation gas flow: 1 000 L/h. The mass scanning range was monitored from 50 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> to 1,200 <em>m</em>/<em>z</em>. The FastDDA was applied to trigger MS/MS acquisition of precursor ions with the intensity threshold of 5 000. Full scan spectra are acquired at a scan time of 0.25 s followed by MS/MS spectra acquisition at a scan time of 0.1 s. The top 5 most intensity ions were selected to perform MS/MS acquisition. Collision energy was set to a ramp of 60–100 V for MS/MS acquisition. Leucine-enkephalin was used as the lockmass (<em>m</em>/<em>z</em> 556.277 1 in positive ion mode and <em>m</em>/<em>z</em> 554.261 5 in negative ion mode). The data were acquired by Masslynx V 4.1 and processed by UNIFI and GNPS.</p></section><section id="s0025"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.3. Molecular networking analysis</h3>
<p id="p0045">Molecular networking of the components in SYQ extracts was created by the online workflow at GNPS (<a href="https://gnps.ucsd.edu/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://gnps.ucsd.edu/</a>) and MCnebula (<a href="https://mcnebula.org/" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://mcnebula.org/</a>). Further, consensus spectra that contained less than two spectra were discarded.</p></section><section id="s0030"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">2.4. <p id="p0155">CCl<sub>4</sub>-induced acute liver injury mouse model was generated to evaluate the hepatoprotective effect of SYQTF in S<sub>15</sub>-ZJ. However, Silymarin (150 mg/kg) decreased the serum ALT (*<em>P</em> &lt; 0.05), AST levels (<sup>**</sup><em>P</em> &lt; 0.01). The results of RT-qPCR (<a href="#f0040" class="usa-link">Fig. 8</a>) showed that compared with the control group, the mRNA expressions of Rac1, Sonic hedgehog (Shh), Ptch1, Smo, and Gli2 were significantly increased in the activated HSC-T6 cells, while the mRNA expression level of Hhip was significantly decreased. Moreover, SYQTF (8 μg/mL) can reverse the expression levels of Rac1, Shh, Hhip, Ptch1, Smo, and Gli2 proteins. In addition, in the HSC-T6 cells infected with Rac1 overexpression, the mRNA expression levels of Rac1, Shh, Ptch1, Smo, and Gli2 were significantly higher than those in the activated HSC-T6 cells, while the Hhip mRNA expression was significantly down-regulated, and there was no significant difference between the overexpression empty vector group and the activated HSC-T6 cells. On this basis, the intervention of SYQTF (8 μg/mL) can significantly down-regulate the mRNA expression levels of Rac1, Shh, Ptch1, Smo, and Gli2 in Rac1 overexpression HSC-T6 cells, and up-regulate Hhip mRNA expression level.</p>

Conclusion</h2>
<p id="p0210">86 components in SYQTF, which including four organic acids, two phenolic acids and their derivatives, 49 flavonoids, six fatty acids, 25 other compounds, were identified through a combination of methods including mass comparison, fragmentation analysis based on molecular networks, literature and reference components. 51 and 13 components were identified in SYQTF under the ESI<sup>-</sup> and ESI<sup>+</sup> in MS<sup>E</sup> mode, respectively. Moreover, 24 components in SYQTF were tentatively identified with the aid of spectral cosine similarity by GNPS in FastDDA mode. In addition, S<sub>15</sub>-ZJ from Zhejiang showed distinct differences in its chemical profile compared to other origins through PCA and OPLS-DA. A specific compound (Kaempferol-3-<em>O</em>-furananose-7-<em>O</em>-rhamnosyl-glucoside, <em>t</em><sub>min</sub> = 7.71) displayed high peak intensity and differential enrichment between S<sub>15</sub>-ZJ and other origins, which could potentially be used as a biomarker for quality control of <em>T. hemsleyanum</em> in future studies.</p>
<p id="p0215">Furthermore, SYQTF demonstrated dose-dependent reduction of serum ALT and AST levels as well as liver index, along with decreased TNF-α levels in liver tissue and LN concentrations in serum compared to CCl<sub>4</sub>-induced model group. Additionally, SYQTF alleviated various liver surface changes such as swelling, roughness, hardness, and miliary alterations while attenuating hepatic steatosis, inflammatory cell infiltration, and hepatocyte necrosis.</p>
<p id="p0220">Finally, SYQTF effectively inhibited activation and proliferation of TGF-β1 induced HSC-T6 cells by inducing cell cycle arrest at G<sub>0</sub>/G<sub>1</sub> phase and apoptosis. <em>In vitro</em> experiments also revealed that SYQTF (8 μg/mL) had an anti-hepatic fibrosis effect by intervening Rac1 to further inhibit hedgehog signaling pathway based on recombinant Rac1 over expression lentiviral vectors and Rac1 knockout lentiviral vectors. These findings reveal for the first time that SYQTF interferes with the hedgehog signaling pathway by regulating the expression level of Rac1, thus exerting an anti-hepatic fibrosis effect, which provides a new theoretical basis for Rac1 as a potential regulatory target of liver fibrosis in clinics.</p></section><section id="s0165"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Declaration of competing interest</h2>
