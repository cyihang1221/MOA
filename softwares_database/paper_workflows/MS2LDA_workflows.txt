related_tool: MS2LDA
sample_description: The sample used in the research was the marine sponge S. aurea, collected by hand at a depth of 4–7 m at the Wonderland Reef in the Florida Keys. Specimens were identified by visual inspection based on characteristic large oscula and a fleshy yellow interior, then frozen at −80 °C pending chemical extraction. Total DNA was isolated from RNAlater-preserved tissue for 18S rDNA amplification and Sanger sequencing to confirm taxonomic identity. Lyophilized sponge material was extracted with methanol (MeOH) at room temperature, yielding a bright green extract analyzed directly by LC-MS/MS without derivatization or prior fractionation. For microscopy and MALDI imaging, sponge tissue was embedded in 20% gelatin, sectioned at low temperature (−15 °C) into 8–10 μm thick cryosections using a cryomicrotome, and thaw-mounted onto prechilled conductive ITO slides. Sections were vacuum-desiccated for 60 min prior to fluorescence imaging or MALDI matrix application.
complete_workflow_description: The process procedure involved multiple integrated analytical workflows: (1) LC-MS/MS data acquisition using Agilent 1290 Infinity II UHPLC coupled to Bruker ImpactII Qq-TOF, with chromatographic gradient (5–95% MeCN/0.1% formic acid over 17 min), positive ion mode (m/z 100–2000), ESI source settings (capillary voltage 4500 V; nebulizer gas 2 bar; source temp 200 °C; dry gas 9 L/min), and MS² acquisition with dynamic exclusion (exclusion time 30 sec; exclusion count 2), stepped CID energies (50% and 125% of calculated values from Table S1), stepped collision RF (550/800 Vpp), and stepped transfer time (75/90 μs); (2) Molecular networking performed on GNPS (https://gnps.ucsd.edu) using cosine score threshold ≥0.7, minimum matched peaks ≥6, and minimum cosine score ≥0.7, with MS² spectra searched against MassBank of North America, European MassBank, ReSpect, CASMI, and other third-party libraries; (3) MS2LDA motif analysis conducted via the online MS2LDA platform to detect conserved fragmentation patterns across clusters; (4) MALDI-MSI data acquisition on Bruker rapifleX MALDI Tissuetyper™ in reflectron positive mode (m/z 200–2000), using DHB matrix (5 mg/mL in MeCN:H₂O 60:40 + 0.2% formic acid), 20 μm laser pitch, 200 shots/pixel, 10 kHz laser frequency, and smartbeam 3D single-scan mode; (5) Data processing and visualization using flexControl 4.0 and flexImaging 5.0 (Bruker) for instrument control and ion image generation, and SCiLS Lab Pro 3D software (Bruker) for discriminatory ion mapping (p < 0.05); (6) Fluorescence microscopy performed on Zeiss LSM 780 AxioObserver with Plan-Apochromat 20×/0.8 and alpha Plan-Apochromat 100×/1.46 Oil DIC objectives, using 488 nm excitation and spectral emission collection (493–556 nm for autofluorescence; 647–721 nm for chlorophyll), tile scanning with 10% frame overlap, and linear contrast enhancement in Zeiss Zen Black v14.0.12.201.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: MS2LDA
sample_description: The study used three types of biological samples: (i) 22 human urine samples, (ii) 19 beer samples, and (iii) fecal (stool) samples. For urine, 5 μL was extracted with 200 μL of chloroform/methanol/water (1:3:1 v/v/v) at 4 °C, vortexed for 5 min at 4 °C, centrifuged at 13,000 g for 3 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant stored at −80 °C. A pooled aliquot of the 22 urine samples was prepared prior to LC–MS analysis. For beer, the same metabolome extraction procedure was applied. For fecal samples, stool material was freeze-dried, and 5 mg of lyophilized material was extracted in 200 μL of chloroform/methanol/water (1:3:1) at 4 °C, homogenized using a FastPrep-24 homogenizer for 60 s at stroke setting 5, then vortexed, centrifuged, and processed identically to urine. All samples were analyzed via LC–MS/MS using HILIC chromatography and Orbitrap-based detection.
complete_workflow_description: Data preprocessing was performed using XCMS (version not specified) for peak detection, MzMatch (version not specified) for peak alignment and filtering, and RMassBank (version not specified) for detecting MS1–MS2 spectral pairs. Feature matrices were formed by aligning MS2 features across spectra and samples. MS2LDA+ was applied to decompose the entire corpus of MS2 spectra (from 41 samples: 22 urine + 19 beer) into 300 shared Mass2Motifs. For statistical analysis, PCA was performed in Python using scikit-learn’s PCA class on the 41 × 500 matrix of α values (normalized so each sample’s α vector sums to 1), after whitening (standardizing) the Mass2Motif variables. Differential prevalence of Mass2Motifs between groups was assessed using z-scores (difference of group means divided by sum of standard deviations) and t-tests. Mass2Motif similarity was computed via pairwise Pearson correlation of their α vectors across samples. Structural characterization involved manual inspection of Molecular Networking clusters, comparison to previously reported Mass2Motifs, matching against reference spectra in MzCloud (www.mzcloud.org), and expert knowledge. Retention time behavior of Mass2Motifs was visualized using m/z vs RT plots. All codes are publicly available on GitHub (https://github.com/sdrogers/lda), and raw/processed data are deposited in GNPS/MassIVE (accession IDs MSV000081118, MSV000081119, MSV000081120) and the University of Glasgow repository (http://researchdata.gla.ac.uk/402/).

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MS2LDA
sample_description: Dried blood spot (DBS) samples from newborns collected as part of the iPSYCH cohort; 37 case-control pairs (n = 74 total DBS samples), where cases were later diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and controls were matched on sex, birth year, and geographic region; samples were stored long-term and processed using methanol/acetonitrile/isopropanol/water/formic acid extraction prior to LC-MS/MS analysis.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow began with LC-MS/MS acquisition of extracted metabolites from newborn dried blood spots (DBS). Raw data (.raw files) were preprocessed using Compound Discoverer 2.1 SP1 for peak detection and alignment, and independently using MZmine 2.41 for feature detection and alignment. Features with MS2 spectra (n = 2217 out of 4360 total features) were uploaded to GNPS for feature-based molecular networking: MS2 spectra were filtered (removing fragments ±17 Da from precursor; retaining top 6 fragments in ±50 Da window), aligned with precursor tolerance 0.02 Da and fragment tolerance 0.02 Da, and networks built with cosine score >0.7 and ≥4 matched peaks; edge filtering required mutual top-10 similarity and molecular family size capped at 100. Spectral matching against GNPS public libraries and an in-house TraceFinder library yielded level 1–2 annotations for 150 features. The MolNetEnhancer workflow integrated MS2LDA-derived substructure annotations, Network Annotation Propagation, Dereplicator, and ClassyFire ontology mapping to assign level 3 (chemical class) annotations to an additional 859 features. For unannotated spectra, MASST was used for cross-dataset spectral matching and SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID for in silico molecular formula and structure prediction. Final visualization and interpretation were performed in Cytoscape v3.7.2, overlaying sample-group intensities and fold changes onto molecular network nodes.
source_paper: Julie et al. (2021), Journal of molecular neuroscience : MN, DOI: 10.1007/s12031-020-01787-2, PMID: 33515432

related_tool: MS2LDA
sample_description: The study analyzed metabolomic profiles from 12 bioactive fractions derived from a cyanobacterial natural products library (LEGE-NPL): six highly cytotoxic fractions (Group A) and six fractions selected for their effects on 3D HCT 116 spheroids (Group B). These fractions originated from 60 cyanobacterial strains and 4 environmental samples cultured at CIIMAR, extracted with methanol, and fractionated via semiautomatic reversed-phase HPLC (C8 column, H2O/MeCN gradient) into eight fractions per strain, yielding a total of 512 fractions. The 12 fractions subjected to in-depth metabolomic characterization were selected based on cytotoxicity screening against 2D and 3D HCT 116 colon carcinoma models and the non-cancerous hCMEC/D3 endothelial cell line.
complete_workflow_description: The global workflow began with cultivation of 60 cyanobacterial strains and 4 environmental samples, followed by methanol extraction of lyophilized biomass. Extracts were fractionated using semiautomatic HPLC on a C8 column with a water/acetonitrile gradient, collecting eight fractions (A–H) per strain into 96-well plates. Fractions were dried, resuspended in DMSO, and screened for cytotoxicity: 2D HCT 116 viability was assessed via MTT assay after 48 h; 3D spheroid viability was measured using acid phosphatase assay after 96 h. Based on bioactivity, 12 fractions were prioritized — six with high cytotoxicity in both 2D and 3D models (Group A) and six active only in 3D (Group B). Untargeted LC-MS metabolomics data from these 12 fractions were processed using the MolNetEnhancer workflow (which integrates molecular networking, spectral matching, and ontology-based annotation), followed by statistical analysis in MetaboAnalyst to identify mass features with highest fold change between groups. Bioactive candidates were prioritized by differential abundance and novelty (lack of database matches), supported by in silico annotation (e.g., GNPS, SIRIUS, CSI:FingerID) and manual dereplication against known cyanobacterial metabolites (e.g., pheophytins, pheophorbides). Seven novel bioactive molecule candidates were proposed from Group A; nine chlorophyll derivatives were tentatively identified in Group B.

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MS2LDA
sample_description: The paper describes four case studies involving plant and bacterial metabolomics datasets, but does not specify the exact number, species, tissue types, extraction methods, or instrument parameters for the samples used in the MS2LDA analysis. It states that MS2LDA was applied to MS2 fragmentation spectra derived from LC-MS/MS data processed either via MS-Cluster (directly on all MS2 spectra) or via MZmine2 (feature-based association of MS1 features with their corresponding MS2 spectra), as part of molecular networking workflows on the GNPS platform.
complete_workflow_description: The MolNetEnhancer global workflow begins with raw LC-MS/MS data, which is preprocessed either using MS-Cluster (to generate consensus MS2 spectra) or MZmine2 (for MS1 feature detection and MS2 spectrum association). These processed spectra are then submitted to GNPS for molecular networking. Concurrently, in silico structural annotation is performed using tools such as Network Annotation Propagation (NAP) or DEREPLICATOR (via GNPS) or alternatively SIRIUS+CSI:FingerID; and unsupervised substructure mining is carried out using MS2LDA. All outputs — molecular network (in graphml format), structural annotations, Mass2Motifs, and chemical classifications — are integrated by MolNetEnhancer: (1) Mass2Motifs are mapped onto network nodes as pie charts reflecting overlap scores and connected as multi-edges for shared motifs; (2) structural annotations from GNPS library matching and in silico tools are submitted to ClassyFire to obtain hierarchical chemical classifications (kingdom, superclass, class, subclass, direct parent); (3) per molecular family, chemical class consistency scores are computed as the proportion of nodes (weighted by number of matching structures per node) assigned to each class at each ontology level; (4) enhanced networks are exported in graphml format for visualization in Cytoscape. The workflow is implemented in Python and R, with interactive Jupyter notebooks and step-by-step tutorials provided.
source_paper: Madeleine et al. (2019), Metabolites, DOI: 10.3390/metabo9070144, PMID: 31315242

========== paper_workflow entry ==========
related_tool: MS2LDA
sample_description: Ten adult individuals of Stichopus cf. horrens (approximately 1 kg each) were obtained from aquaculture at the Bolinao Marine Laboratory, University of the Philippines. Body wall and viscera tissues were separately minced and extracted with absolute ethanol (1:20 g/mL) for 48 hours. Extracts were filtered, dried in vacuo, and resuspended in ethanol to 10 mg/mL. LC-MS/MS data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes using a Waters Acquity UPLC coupled to a Waters Xevo G2-XS QTOF mass spectrometer, with CID fragmentation across multiple collision energies (15–60 eV for compounds <1 kDa; 60–90 eV otherwise), covering m/z 50–2000.
complete_workflow_description: The end-to-end metabolomics workflow begins with tissue dissection (body wall and viscera) from ten cultured S. cf. horrens individuals, followed by ethanol extraction, filtration, and solvent removal. Extracts were reconstituted and analyzed by UPLC-QTOF-MS in both positive and negative ion modes using gradient elution on a Fluoro-Phenyl column and multi-energy CID fragmentation. Raw Waters.RAW files were converted to mzXML using ProteoWizard's MSConvert. Data were uploaded to the CCMS repository (UCSD) and processed on GNPS. Preprocessing included feature detection, deisotoping, and deconvolution using MZmine 2.52 (parameters in S9 Table), followed by gap-filling. Feature-based molecular networking (FBMN) was performed with precursor and product ion tolerances of m/z 0.02 and m/z 0.05, respectively; cosine similarity threshold ≥0.70; minimum matched peaks ≥8; ion correlation thresholds of 85% (shape) and 50% (height). Ion-identity networks collapsed adducts into single nodes. Merged polarity networks combined positive and negative mode results. Library search against GNPS public spectral libraries used the same mass tolerances and cosine threshold. MS2LDA motif analysis (overlap and probability >0.30) was integrated via MolNetEnhancer to annotate structural backbones. Networks were visualized in Cytoscape 3.8.2. Annotations leveraged diagnostic ions (e.g., m/z 168.044 for phosphocholine), neutral losses (e.g., 60 Da), and theoretical fragmentation mechanisms for lipids and saponins.
source_paper: M et al. (2023), PloS one, DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0294535, PMID: 38055702
