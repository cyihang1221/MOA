>Materials and methods   Data and study population   This retrospective study examined patients who were diagnosed with Bell's palsy at the Department of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation of our institution, and received medical rehabilitation from January 2020 to December 2022. Patients were included if they were diagnosed with idiopathic, acute facial palsy. Initially, 296 patients were assessed and 103 patients were excluded based on the presence of central nervous system disorders, bilateral facial palsy, history of previous facial palsy, pregnancy, absence of electrophysiological test results, previous receipt of facial botulinum toxin injections, or insufficient follow-up. A total of 193 patients were included in the final analysis. All patients'facial palsy severity was assessed using the House-Brackmann (H-B) grade at the initial visit and six months after symptom onset. The initial H-B grade distribution was as follows: grade II, 13 patients (6.7%); grade III, 113 patients (58.6%); grade IV, 42 patients (21.8%); grade V, 18 patients (9.3%); and grade VI, 7 patients (3.6%). All patients received oral corticosteroids (standard prednisone dosage: 80 mg/d for the first 4 days followed by tapering to 60 mg/d on days 5 and 6, 40 mg/d on days 7 and 8, and 20 mg/d on days 9 and 10) and physical rehabilitation therapy, including facial massage and expression exercises, with instructions for home practice. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Hospital (2024–10-007), with consent waived due to its retrospective nature. Clinical and electrophysiological assessment   Patient age, sex, and side of facial paralysis were recorded. The severity of facial paralysis was assessed using the H-B grade at the initial visit and at the 6-month follow-up. Outcomes were recorded at 6 months after symptom onset, and patients with complete recovery (H-B grade 1) and incomplete recovery (H-B grade 2–6) were compared according to sex, age at initial visit, and initial H-B grade. Electrophysiological tests, including a nerve conduction study (NCS), BR, NET, and nEMG, were conducted 14 days after the onset of symptoms. The NCS provides values for ENoG and CMAP latency. The nEMG provides values for abnormal spontaneous activity (ASA), motor unit action potential (MUAP) interference pattern grade and amplitude. All electrophysiological tests were conducted using the Nicolet EDX Machine (Natus, USA) in a controlled environment with specific settings and protocols to ensure consistency and accuracy. These tests were performed by two experienced operators to assure reliability and consistency. Electroneurography electroneurography degeneration index (ENoG DI)   NCS tests were conducted on the bilateral frontalis, orbicularis oculi, nasalis, and orbicularis oris muscles. Recording electrodes were placed on facial muscles, with reference electrodes on the contralateral side and a ground electrode on the shoulder. Preauricular stimulation used a bipolar stimulator (cathode near the anterior tragus, anode between the mandible and mastoid) with an initial current of 30 mA (pulse duration: 0.1 ms) increased to maximal response. Sensitivity was 1 mV/division, sweep speed 20 ms/division, and filter range 20 to 10 kHz [ 7 ,  9 ]. This procedure allows measurement of CMAP amplitude (peak to peak in mV) for ENoG and onset latency. The ENoG value indicates the proportion of intact nerve fibers. The ENoG degeneration index (DI), which represents the degree of facial nerve degeneration [ 8 ,  10 ], was calculated as:   E N o G D I = 100 % - ( a f f e c t e d E N o G a m p l i t u d e u n a f f e c t e d E N o G a m p l i t u d e ) * 100 % Blink reflex (BR)   BR measurements followed standardized 2-channel guidelines. Electrodes were placed on the orbicularis oculi muscles (active and opposite), side of the nose (reference), and chin (ground). Supraorbital nerve stimulation started at 0 mA and increased to supramaximal levels (pulse duration: 0.2 ms). Sensitivity was 100 µV/division, sweep speed 100 ms/division, and filter range 20 to 10 k Hz [ 11 ]. This test identified two components: the R1 response (evaluated for delays on the stimulated side) and the R2 response (latencies on both the stimulated and opposite sides) [ 11 ]. Nerve excitability test (NET)   NET recorded the minimal current intensity required to elicit visible facial muscle contractions using rectangular impulses (duration: 0.1 ms, frequency: 1 Hz) applied near the anterior tragus [ 7 ]. The minimal current intensities (mA) that caused visible contractions were recorded. The difference between the two sides is an indication of the degree of nerve damage and is related to prognosis. Needle electromyography (nEMG)   The nEMG examination used a bipolar needle electrode to assess electrical activity at rest and during voluntary contractions in the frontalis, orbicularis oculi, nasalis, and orbicularis oris muscles. Sensitivity ranged from 20 to 500 µV/division, with a sweep speed of 100 ms/division and a filter range of 100 to 1 k Hz. At rest, the nEMG was used to assess ASA, including fibrillation potentials and positive sharp waves (PSWs). Patients were asked to perform maximal voluntary contractions to evaluate the MUAP amplitude and interference pattern [ 5 ,  9 ,  12 ].   By convention, the interference pattern is categorized into five grades based on the density of electrical activity during muscle contraction: full interference pattern, indicating dense and complex electrical activity typical in healthy muscles (Grade 5); reduced interference pattern, indicating decreased activation of motor units (Grade 4); discrete activity, indicating that only a few motor units are active and their electrical potentials do not overlap (Grade 3); single voluntary MUAP, indicating that only one motor unit fires during contraction (Grade 2); and no voluntary MUAP, indicating no detection of motor unit activity (Grade 1) [ 9 ]. Statistical analysis   The relationship of complete and incomplete recovery (based on H-B grade at 6 months) with other variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test (categorical data) or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (continuous data). Multiple logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate relationships of different variables (age, sex, paralysis side) with initial H-B grade after adjustment for confounding. To determine the cut-off value for distinguishing complete and incomplete recovery of the follow-up H-B grade, receiver operating characteristics (ROC) analysis was used to calculate area under the curve (AUC). Decision tree analysis using a classification and regression tree (CART) was also conducted. To reduce bias caused by differences between complete and incomplete recovery, upsampling was applied during the CART analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc., Cary, NC) and R version 4.2.0. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered significant. Results   We analyzed the clinical and electrophysiological data of 193 patients with Bell's palsy to identify factors associated with outcome at 6 months. We first divided the patient cohort into a complete recovery group (H-B grade 1,  n  = 141) and incomplete recovery group (H-B grade 2–6,  n  = 52) based on measurements at 6 months after symptom onset (Table  2 ). These two groups had no significant differences in age, sex, and side of facial paralysis. However, patients whose initial H-B grade was 3 or less were more likely to achieve complete recovery. Thus, the ENoG DI values for the frontalis (odds ratio [OR], 1.05; 95% CI, 1.03–1.07), orbicularis oculi (OR, 1.06; 95% CI, 1.03–1.08), nasalis (OR, 1.04; 95% CI, 1.02–1.07), and orbicularis oris (OR, 1.04; 95% CI, 1.02–1.06) were greater for the incomplete recovery group. The CMAP latency values in the frontalis (OR, 3.18; 95% CI, 1.38–7.33), orbicularis oculi (OR, 4.37; 95% CI, 2.11–9.06), nasalis (OR, 2.33; 95% CI, 1.20–4.54), and orbicularis oris (OR, 2.90; 95% CI, 1.33–6.30) were greater for the incomplete latency group. The ipsilateral R1 of BR (OR, 1.44; 95% CI, 1.23–1.68) was greater for the incomplete recovery group. The NET values for the affected side (OR, 1.31; 95%CI, 1.17–1.47) and the side-to-side difference (OR, 1.34; 95% CI, 1.19–1.52) were greater for the incomplete recovery group.   These two groups also had several differences in the nEMG findings (Table  4 ). The univariate analysis showed that the prevalence of PSWs ( p  = 0.0213), and interference patterns of Grade 1 to 3 in the frontalis ( p  = 0.0001), orbicularis oculi ( p  < 0.0001), nasalis ( p  < 0.0001), and orbicularis oris ( p  = 0.0004) were more common in the incomplete recovery group. These results show that a diminished MUAP interference pattern was predictor of poor prognosis. The multivariable analysis showed that incomplete recovery was associated with PSWs (OR, 2.75; 95% CI, 1.09–6.93); and with Grade 1 to 3 interference patterns in the orbicularis oculi (OR, 3.84; 95% CI, 1.49–9.89), nasalis (OR, 5.61; 95% CI, 1.68–18.75), and orbicularis oris (OR, 2.93; 1.02–8.45).
