>Materials and methods   Sample collection, extraction, and fractionation   Three fresh sea cucumber species named  Holothuria atra  (533.48 g),  Holothuria impatiens  (495.48 g), and  Bohadschia vitiensis  (338.41 g) were collected by SCUBA diving off the coast of Hurghada in October 2023. The species were taxonomically identified by Prof. Sayed Abd-ElAziz at the National Institute of Oceanography and Fisheries. Samples were deposited at the Department of Pharmacognosy, Faculty of Pharmacy, Ain-Shams University with registration codes of PHG-M-HA-579, PHG-M-HI-580, and PHG-M-BV-581 for  H. atra ,  H. impatiens , and  B. vitiensis , respectively. Samples were transported in ice boxes to the lab of Faculty of Pharmacy, Ain Shams University, Cairo. The samples were washed with running water, the body walls were mechanically separated from the internal viscera, then cut into smaller pieces for easy handling. The viscera and walls of each species were individually homogenized and weighed to yield 415.8 g, 117.7 g, 291.8 g, 203.7 g, 302.7 g, and 35.7 g of  H. atra  wall,  H. atra  viscera,  H. impatiens  wall,  H. impatiens  viscera,  B. vitiensis  wall, and  B. vitiensis  viscera, respectively. The homogenized extracts were freeze-dried then extracted with 80% aqueous ethanol for 6 days at room temperature in the dark (solvent to sample ratio = 3:1). The extracts were filtered, concentrated under reduced pressure, and lyophilized to yield 14.5 g of  H. atra  wall extract (yield = 3.5%), 8.31 g of  H. atra  viscera extract (yield = 7%), 29.5 g of  H. impatiens  wall extract (yield = 10.1%), 15.9 g of  H. impatiens  viscera extract (yield = 7.8%), 2.5 g of  B. vitiensis  wall extract (yield = 0.8%), and 6.6 g of  B. vitiensis  viscera extract (yield = 18.5%). The extracts were stored at -20 ℃ for further analysis. Bioactive extracts of  H. atra  wall and  H. impatiens  viscera were redissolved in 80% ethanol and were separately partitioned against n-hexane, dichloromethane, ethyl acetate, and butanol. The fractions were concentrated under reduced pressure and stored at -20 ℃ for LC-qTOF-MS/MS analysis. The extraction was adopted in a way similar to that previously described by Wijesinghe et al. 38  with slight modifications. LC-qTOF-MS/MS analysis and data processing   Chromatographic analysis of the extracts and the bioactive fractions were performed on a SHIMADZU LCMS-9030 system (SHIMADZU Corporation, Kyoto, Japan). Chromatographic resolution was achieved on a C 18  column of SHIMADZU Shim-pack GIST (2 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) using water (solvent A, containing 0.1% formic acid) and MeOH (solvent B, containing 0.1% formic acid) as the mobile phase implementing a gradient elution protocol (0–7 min: 10–40% B, 7–15 min: 40–100% B, 15–20 min: 100% B). The column temperature was adjusted at 40 °C in an oven and the flow rate was fixed at 0.4 ml/min. The mass-to-charge ( m/z ) was set between 100 and 1800 to cover all possible masses of triterpene glycosides. In the MS 2  scans, the automatic data-dependent acquisition (DDA) method was employed, using a collision energy of 35 eV. Measurements were acquired in the positive and negative modes. In the positive mode scan, the interface voltage was set to 4 kV, the interface temperature was 300 ℃, the desolvation temperature was 526 ℃, the nebulizing gas flow rate was adjusted at 3 l/min, and the heating gas flow rate was at 10 l/min. In the negative mode scan, the interface voltage was adjusted at -3 kV with an interface temperature of 300 ℃ and desolvation temperature of 526 ℃. The nebulizing, heating, and drying gas flow rates were 3, 10, and 10 l/min, respectively. The mass spectra were recorded within  m/z  mass range between 100 and 1800 Da and the analyses were performed in the negative and positive modes. Peak areas were used to compare the relative percentage of saponins between the body walls and viscera in the three species. Raw mass files were converted to .mzML (using MSconvert software) which were processed using MZmine 2 for data centroiding, filtering, deconvoluting, deisotoping, and aligning. The obtained feature list was exported as feature quantification table (.csv) and MS/MS spectral summary file (.mgf) which were uploaded on GNPS ( https://gnps.ucsd.edu ) for FBMN (feature based molecular network) analysis. Identification of compounds was based on detailed MS/MS interpretation, review of literature, and online platforms such as massbank, MarinLit, metabolomics workbench, m/z cloud, GNPS libraries, and Sirius in silico tools. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay   Lovo (Cat# 60148) and DLD-1 (Cat# 60132) were obtained from the Bioresource Collection and Research Center (Hsinchu, Taiwan). The cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1 × antibiotic-antimycotic at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO 2 . The cells were treated with DMSO or the indicated extracts for 48 h then incubated with the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (Elabscience, Houston, TX, USA) at 37 °C for 2 h. The cell viability was determined by a spectrophotometer at 450 nm. HBSS (Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution) was used as the medium for the assay (pH 7.4). Each experiment was operated under 37 °C and performed in triplicate. The CCK-8 is a widely used kit for assessing cell viability and cytotoxicity based on the reduction principle of a water-soluble tetrazolium salt (WST-8). Cell culture   Human prostate cancer cell line (PC3), fibroblast cell line (HFB-4), and human lung cancer cell line (H-460) (provided by the tissue culture unit, center for drug discovery research and development, Faculty of Pharmacy, Ain-Shams University) were maintained in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 100 µg/mL streptomycin, 100 units/mL penicillin and 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum in a humidified, 5% (v/v) CO 2  atmosphere at 37 °C. Sulforhodamine B (SRB) assay   Cell seeding was done at a density of 2000 cells/ well in 96-well plates. Cells were treated for 72 h with five different concentrations (0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1000 µg/ml) of tested samples. Cytotoxicity was assessed at the end of drug exposure using SRB assay as previously described 39 . Absorbance was measured at 545 nm using microplate reader (BioTek instruments, Vermont, USA). Results were expressed as the relative percentage of absorbance compared to the control. Experiments were done in triplicates. Half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC 50 ), the drug concentration at which 50% growth inhibition is achieved, was calculated using GraphPad Prism software version 5.00 (GraphPad Software, Inc. La Jolla, CA, USA). Bioactivity-based molecular network   The MS/MS data were processed on MZmine 2.53 (an open-source software for mass spectrometry data processing allowing the downstream analysis of the processed data on different platforms through multiple export modules). The masses were first centroided and the noise levels were set to 4000 for MS and 100 for MS 2 . The chromatograms were built using the ADAP algorithm (the minimum number of scans was set to 3, the group intensity threshold and the minimum highest intensity were set to 10,000 and 150,000, respectively, and the mass tolerance was set to 0.005 or 15 ppm). The resulting feature lists were deconvoluted, deisotoped, and aligned. The aligned feature list was gap filled (intensity tolerance = 10%,  m/z  difference = 0.005, rt difference = 0.25) and exported as CSV file. In the resulting CSV file, blank features were removed using a threshold of 0.5. The raw data of the fractions of  H. impatiens  visceral extract were uploaded to the GNPS platform to construct classical networks with precursor and fragment ion mass tolerance of 0.02 Da. Edges were formed when the cosine score exceeded 0.7 and with six minimum matched fragment ions. Network for  H. impatiens  visceral fractions could be accessed at ( https://gnps.ucsd.edu/ProteoSAFe/status.jsp?task=708f21e9317143b0abab954aeb50e032 ), and was visualized on Cytoscape. The CSV file was exported from MZmine, showing the relative quantities of mass ions, was modified to incorporate the cell viability results of the bioactive extract and its fractions. The file was then uploaded to the “bioactive molecular network” workflow ( https://github.com/DorresteinLaboratory/Bioactive_Molecular_Networks ) where a CSV file containing the Pearson correlation scores ( r  values) (between the bioactivities of the mass ions and their relative quantities) and their significance ( p  values) is downloaded and imported to Cytoscape for network visualization. Large nodes with high bioactive scores ( r  > 0.7 and  p  < 0.05) are suggested to be high cytotoxic candidates. Network pharmacology studies   Targets of the main bioactive components identified in  H. impatiens  bioactive fractions were collected from Swiss prediction ( http://swisstargetprediction.ch/ ) and binding databases ( https://bindingdb.org/ ). Genes related to cancer were gathered from the GeneCards platform ( https://www.genecards.org/ ). The shared genes between the components and the disease were obtained from biotools ( https://www.biotools.fr/misc/venny ). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed on the String database ( https://string-db.org/ ). GO terms (including BP, MF, and CC) and pathway enrichment analysis were performed using Enrichr ( https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/ ) and gprofiler ( https://biit.cs.ut.ee/gprofiler/gost ) databases. Cytoscape ( https://cytoscape.org/ ) was used to visualize and analyze the networks. Molecular docking investigations   Molecular docking was conducted on bivittosides C and D which are the abundant triterpenes detected in the bioactive dichloromethane, ethyl acetate, and aqueous ethanolic fractions of the visceral extract of  H. impatiens .Docking was performed on AKT1 (PDB ID: 3MVH; 2.01 Å), STAT3 (PDB ID: 6NJS; 2.7 Å), SCR (PDB ID: 3F3V; 2.6 Å), and EGFR (PDB ID: 1M17; 2.6 Å). The co-crystallized inhibitors were used as a control WFE (for AKT1), KQV (for STAT3), 1BU (for SCR), and AQ4 (for EGFR). The X-ray crystal structures of the targets were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) and were processed by removing water molecules on USF Chimera X 1.7.1. The compounds were drawn on Chemdraw and their energies were minimized using Avogadro v.1.2.0. The least energy conformer was converted to (.pdb) files separately. Docking was performed on the DINC 2.0 webserver ( https://dinc.kavrakilab.org/ ) which is a parallelized meta-docking method for large molecular sized ligands or those with high number of torsions using Autodock vina. Compounds were submitted to DINC as “.pdb” file. The DINC web server uses Autodock vina scoring function to calculate binding affinities. The binding site of each protein was identified from the co-crystallized inhibitors to set the binding box parameters. The complexes were visualized on the Discovery studio visualizer v.21.1.0.20298 and the interactions of the highest binding energies were studied. Results and discussion   LC-qTOF-MS/MS-based analysis of triterpene saponins in the Red Sea Holothurians   As shown in Table  1 , a total of 35 saponins were identified in the wall and visceral extracts of the three holothurian species, of which 15 were sulfated. They belong to the holostane-series where the aglycone consists of a lanostane skeleton with 18(20)-lactone ring. Identification of compounds was based on their calculated elemental composition, their MS/MS fragmentation spectra, comparison with previously published data and with references to dictionary of natural products as well as databases such as massbank, metabolomics workbench, lipidmaps, MarinLit, GNPS library, and comprehensive marine natural products database along with software like SIRIUS.
