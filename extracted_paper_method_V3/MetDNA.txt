sample_description: The study enrolled 15 patients with pathologically confirmed prostate adenocarcinoma (PCa) from July 2020 to December 2020. Urine samples were collected in two time points: (1) within 4 hours before receiving the first carbon ion radiotherapy (CIRT) fraction, and (2) within 4 hours after completing the last CIRT fraction. Each urine sample (200 μL) was processed by filtration through a 0.22-μm membrane to remove bacteria, followed by addition of 800 μL chilled methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v), centrifugation, supernatant extraction, evaporation to dryness, and reconstitution in 200 μL chilled acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v). Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal volumes (50 μL each) from all individual samples. Three blanks and six QC replicates were injected at the start of the analytical batch for column conditioning, and QC samples were analyzed every ten injections; auto-calibrations were performed every five analyses.

sample_description: The sample used in the research consists of metabolomic data acquired via liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS), including MS1 peak tables (.csv format) containing annotated m/z and retention times (RTs), and MS2 spectral data files in .mgf or .msp format. The MS1 peak table is generated from raw MS files using peak-picking software such as XCMS (version not specified, cited as ref 27) or MS-DIAL (cited as ref 36). MS2 data are converted from raw files using ProteoWizard (version 3.0.6150); support includes data from data-dependent acquisition (DDA), data-independent acquisition (DIA), and targeted MS2 acquisition. For DIA data processed with MS-DIAL, .msp-formatted MS2 files are directly supported. Theoretical RT prediction relies on experimental RTs from annotated seed metabolites matched to an in-house standard spectral library, and the chemical space is restricted to 6439 organic metabolites with strict chemical structures (excluding 1200 non-organic or structurally ambiguous formulas from the original KEGG-derived metabolic reaction network of 7639 nodes).
software_configuration_description: MetDNA is configured with multiple user-adjustable and default parameters across its annotation modules: (1) MS1–MS2 matching tolerances: ±25 ppm for m/z and ±10 s for RT; top-abundant MS2 spectrum selected if multiple matches exist; intensities of top 10 fragment ions summed to represent MS2 abundance. (2) Spectral matching: modified dot-product (DP) score (Eq. 1) with n=1, m=0; intensities rescaled so highest fragment = 1; forward and reverse DP scores computed; threshold ≥0.8 for retention; RT filtering applied at ±30% of theoretical RT. (3) Isotope peak annotation: calculates [M] to [M+4] isotopes using R package Rdisop (binomial/McLaurin expansion); tolerances: ±25 ppm (m/z), ±3 s (RT), ±500% (relative intensity); annotation score (Score_iso, Eq. 2) uses weights W_m/z = 0.45, W_RT = 0.45, W_int = 0.1. (4) Adduct peak annotation: uses adduct tables for HILIC/RP and positive/negative polarity (Supplementary Table 5); tolerances: ±25 ppm (m/z), ±3 s (RT); Score_adduct (Eq. 6) uses weights W_m/z = 0.8, W_RT = 0.2. (5) Reaction-paired neighbor annotation: maximum reaction step = 3 (default); surrogate MS2 spectra transferred from seed to neighbor metabolites with m/z-filtering of fragment ions (removing fragments outside precursor m/z range of target); Score_iden (Eq. 7) uses weights W_m/z = 0.25, W_RT = 0.25, W_spec = 0.5; RT tolerance = ±30%; spectral match tolerance = 0.5 (dot-product threshold implied by scoring context). (6) Confidence grading: Grade 1 (standard-library match), Grade 2 (isotope peaks present), Grade 3 (reliable adducts: e.g., [M+H]+, [M+Na]+, [M+NH4]+ in positive mode; [M−H]−, [M+Cl]−, [M+CH3COO]− in negative mode), Grade 4 (all others). (7) Redundancy removal: recursive process prioritizing higher-grade annotations; peak redundancy = metabolite annotations / annotated peaks; metabolite redundancy = annotated peak groups / metabolite annotations; mean redundancy = (peak redundancy + metabolite redundancy)/2. (8) Pathway analysis: dysregulated peaks selected via t-test or Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon test (FDR correction optional); hypergeometric test (p < 0.05) for KEGG pathway enrichment across 16 species; quantitative pathway expression computed as average Pareto-scaled peak intensities (most abundant peak per peak group; highest Score_iden peak per metabolite). (9) RT prediction: QSRR model built using random forest (R package randomForest); 346 molecular descriptors (MDs) calculated via rcdk from SMILES (obtained from PubChem); MD filtering: remove MDs with >50% missing values, impute remaining missing values via KNN (R package impute), remove invariant MDs; optimized MD subsets selected based on RF importance (>50 appearances in top-5 across 100 models) separately for HILIC and RP separations.
