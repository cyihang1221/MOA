complete_workflow_description: The paper describes an end-to-end computational workflow for detecting diagnostic spectral features of post-translational modifications (PTMs) from MS/MS data. The workflow begins with open (mass-offset) searches to identify peptides bearing PTMs, followed by grouping of peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) into 'mass shift bins' using PTM-Shepherd’s peakpicking algorithm. Within each bin, redundant PSMs are filtered to retain only the highest-quality representative PSM per peptide ion (defined by sequence, modification state, and precursor charge). For each representative PSM, three types of spectral features are calculated: (1) diagnostic ions (e.g., immonium/oxonium ions), after stripping matched a-, b-, and y-ions (both unshifted and delta-mass-shifted); (2) peptide remainder masses, computed as the pairwise mass difference between remaining spectrum ions and the theoretical unmodified peptide mass; and (3) fragment remainder masses for b- and y-ion series, computed as pairwise differences between remaining ions and theoretical fragment masses. These features are binned into histograms (0.2 mDa bin width), smoothed using a mass-dependent rolling mean, and peaks are identified based on prominence and signal-to-noise criteria. Recurring features across PSMs in a bin are then statistically tested for enrichment against a background of unmodified peptides (zero-mass-shift bin) using abundance and significance thresholds. Features passing both filters are reported as diagnostic for the PTM associated with that mass shift bin. The method is integrated into PTM-Shepherd and FragPipe, and was applied to diverse PTM classes including cysteine-reactive chemoprobe adducts, RNA-crosslinked peptides, sialylated glycopeptides, and ADP-ribosylated peptides.

related_tool: KPIC
sample_description: The study used benchmark samples prepared by spiking eight commercially available pesticide mixes (Agilent LC/MS Pesticide Comprehensive Mix) into human plasma metabolite extracts. Human plasma was obtained as lyophilized material from Sigma-Aldrich and reconstituted per supplier instructions. Plasma metabolite extracts were prepared by mixing one part plasma with three parts precooled acetonitrile (4 °C), followed by centrifugation at 15,871 × g at 4 °C for 15 min; the supernatant was collected, solvent evaporated, and residue redissolved in 20% acetonitrile at the original plasma volume. Pesticide mixes were diluted in this plasma matrix across a 5-order-of-magnitude concentration gradient (as detailed in Supplementary Table 2 and Supplementary Fig. 5). For DDA library generation, each pesticide mix was diluted to 1 ng/µL in either solvent or plasma matrix; for DIA analysis, a pooled stock of all eight mixes in plasma matrix at 1 ng/µL was used.
complete_workflow_description: The paper describes a fully automated, open-source end-to-end workflow named DIAMetAlyzer for data-independent acquisition (DIA) metabolomics, integrating data-dependent acquisition (DDA) for assay library generation and rigorous false-discovery rate (FDR) control. The workflow consists of four sequential stages: (1) Candidate identification — DDA data (.mzML) is centroided using ProteoWizard qTofPeakPicker and converted via msconvert; features are detected, adducts grouped, and accurate mass search performed against a compound database. (2) Library construction — AssayGeneratorMetabo (a C++ tool built on OpenMS) processes DDA-derived .mzML and .featureXML files to perform precursor correction, isotope trace filtering, feature mapping, and fragment annotation using SIRIUS4; n highest-intensity fragments per precursor are selected to build a targeted assay library (exportable as tsv, traML, or pqp); decoy spectra are generated via Passatutto using SIRIUS4 fragmentation trees to enable FDR estimation. (3) Targeted extraction — The target-decoy assay library is applied to DIA/SWATH data (acquired on TripleTOF 6600 with variable SWATH windows optimized for plasma matrix) using a metabolomics-extended version of OpenSWATH for chromatogram extraction and peak group scoring. (4) Statistical validation — PyProphet (extended for metabolomics) merges OpenSWATH results, scores peaks at both MS1 and MS2 levels using a metabolomics-specific score filter, and estimates FDR via target-decoy analysis, exporting compound-level results via export-compound. The entire pipeline is implemented as a KNIME workflow leveraging OpenMS, pyOpenMS, SIRIUS4, and Passatutto, and is publicly available in the OpenMS Tutorials repository and on the OpenMS website.
source_paper: O et al. (2022), Nature communications, DOI: 10.1038/s41467-022-29006-z, PMID: 35292629
