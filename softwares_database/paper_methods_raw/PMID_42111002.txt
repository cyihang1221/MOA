
Methods reconstruct the full field Jones matrix from two holograms
acquired with orthogonal incident linear polarizations (0°/90° or
45°/135°). A motorized half wave plate sets the input states, and a
polarization-sensitive CMOS camera with a 0°/45°/90°/135° micro
polarizer mosaic records off-axis holograms containing four
analyzer channels per exposure. This configuration removes
analyzer rotation and avoids specialized multiplexing hardware,
while retaining a simple, replicable setup.
Despite the progress in single shot and few shot designs, a practical
gap remains between single shot & multiplexed architectures that
provide temporal simultaneity but often require specialized optical
components, additional multiplexing channels, or tighter calibration
and alignment, and simpler few shot approaches that are easier to
replicate but remain sensitive to inter frame phase drift. The dual
shot PDHM presented here targets this gap by retaining the
simplicity and alignment tolerance of a standard off-axis DHM
layout while reducing the acquisition burden to two exposures.
Because the two frames are acquired sequentially, the measurement
remains vulnerable to a time varying inter shot phase offset. We
therefore introduce a phase bias equalization that leverages non
birefringent (isotropic) regions to estimate and remove this offset,
restoring phase consistency and amplitude accuracy across Jones
matrix elements. This dual shot strategy explicitly trades temporal
simultaneity for optical simplicity and is therefore best suited to
static or quasi static specimens in its current implementation. We
discuss the method’s scope and limitations (e.g., fully anisotropic
fields lacking isotropic patches) and show that equalization
improves reconstruction robustness under realistic drift. We
validate the system on uniform birefringent samples, biaxially
oriented polypropylene (BOPP) films [27], and then apply it to
biological samples (mouse brain sections and human cornea),
demonstrating utility for quantitative polarization microscopy in
biomedical contexts.
2. Methods
2.1. Optical setup
A modified off-axis Mach - Zehnder interferometer (Fig. 1(a)) was
built around a 632.8 nm He-Ne laser. A fixed wire grid polarizer
(Thorlabs WP25M-VIS) followed by a half wave plate (Thorlabs
AHWP10M-600) in a motorized rotation mount (Thorlabs K10CR2/
M) sets the incident linear polarization to 0°, 45°, 90°, or 135°. A
non-polarizing beam splitter (Thorlabs CCM1-BS013/M) divides the
polarized beam into object and reference arms with 50:50 splitting
ratio. The object arm uses a Nikon 4×/0.13 NA objective and a
500 mm focal length tube lens (Thorlabs AC254-500-A-ML),
yielding an effective magnification of 10×. The reference arm
includes a Keplerian telescope (f1 = 50 mm, Thorlabs LB1471-A; f2 
= 500 mm, Thorlabs LA1908-A) for beam expansion and wavefront
curvature matching. A second non-polarizing 50:50 beam splitter
(Thorlabs BS016) recombines the object and reference beams at a
small angle. This beam splitter was mounted on a kinematic cage
cube platform (Thorlabs B4C/M and Thorlabs C6WR) allowing the
fine adjustment of the angle between the two beams. Besides, the
beam folding mirrors (Thorlabs BB1-E02) on both arms of the
interferometer were mounted on kinematic mirror mounts
(Thorlabs KCB1C/M and Thorlabs KM100) for further alignment of
the angle and the overlap of the reference and the object beams on
camera plane. This configuration allows flexible adjustments of the
off-axis carrier spatial frequency to avoid aliasing of the DC and
twin-image terms.

Holograms are recorded with a polarization sensitive CMOS camera
(The Imaging Source DZK-33UX250, Sony IMX250MZR, 3.45 µm
pixels) featuring a 2 × 2 micro polarizer mosaic (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°).
This provides four analyzer resolved channels per exposure. The
incident polarization state angles are calibrated with respect to the
camera’s analyzer axes by angle of linear polarization (AoLP)
analysis from Stokes measurements without a sample, and the half
wave plate was indexed to these axes (down to motor accuracy ±
0.14°).
