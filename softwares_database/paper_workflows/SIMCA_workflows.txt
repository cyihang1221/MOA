========== paper_workflow entry 1 ==========
related_tool: SIMCA
sample_description: The study used serum samples from 5 male patients aged 2 to 12 years (mean age 4.4 ± 4.3 years) diagnosed with acid sphingomyelinase deficiency (ASMD) types A and B. Pre-treatment baseline serum samples were collected prior to intervention, and post-treatment serum samples were collected after 12 weeks of weekly intravenous trehalose infusions (15 g per dose, administered over 90 min). Serum was separated by centrifugation (unspecified speed and time but implied standard protocol), aliquoted into 1.5 mL Eppendorf microtubes, and stored at −80 °C until analysis. For metabolomic analysis, 50 µL of thawed serum (thawed at 4 °C for 30–60 min) was deproteinized using 150 µL of cold solvent (water/methanol, 1:9 v/v) containing 25 ppm 4-nitrobenzoic acid as a preservative/analyte stabilizer; samples were vortexed for 15 s, settled for 8 min, then centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was dried under nitrogen prior to derivatization. Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots (50 µL each) of all individual serum samples; process blanks used water instead of serum, and equipment blanks used GC-MS-grade methanol.
complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow involved GC-MS-based untargeted metabolomics. Serum metabolite extraction and derivatization were performed as follows: dried extracts were first treated with 50 µL of methoxyamine HCl (20 mg/mL in pyridine) and incubated for 1 h at 70 °C for oximation; then 50 µL of MSTFA (N-methyl-N-trimethylsilyl-trifluoroacetamide) with 1% TMCS (trimethylchlorosilane) was added, vortexed for 30 s, and re-incubated for 1 h at 70 °C for silylation. Samples were centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 8 min, and supernatants were transferred to 450 µL internal microinsert tubes placed in GC-MS vials. Prior to injection, 100 µL of standard solution (10 ppm methyl stearate in heptane) was spiked into each sample. GC-MS analysis was conducted on an Agilent 5975 system equipped with a DB-5MS column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 µm), PAL RTC 120 autosampler, and helium carrier gas (1.0 mL/min, 99.999% purity). Injection volume was 1 µL in splitless mode; injector temperature was 250 °C; oven program started at 80 °C, ramped at 10 °C/min to 340 °C; electron ionization (EI) source energy was 70 eV, source temperature 200 °C; solvent delay was 5 min; mass scan range was m/z 35–550. Data preprocessing was performed using MS-DIAL software (version 4.90): raw chromatograms were exported to 'abf' format and imported; preprocessing included peak picking, baseline correction, noise reduction, smoothing, deconvolution, retention time alignment, peak integration, internal standard-based normalization (using methyl stearate), and final output generation. Metabolite identification relied on NIST14 spectral library matching (>80% similarity threshold), Kovats retention index (RI) calculation using C8–C20 alkane standards analyzed under identical GC-MS conditions, and cross-validation with HMDB. Statistical analysis included univariate paired t-tests (performed in GraphPad Prism 9) and FDR correction via Benjamini–Hochberg method; multivariate analysis used OPLS-DA in SIMCA-P; pathway enrichment and topology analysis were conducted using MetaboAnalyst 5.0 with hypergeometric test and Relative-betweenness Centrality.

========== paper_workflow entry 2 ==========
related_tool: SIMCA
sample_description: The study used plasma samples collected from 52 human participants divided into four groups: 14 patients with papillary thyroid cancer (PTC; 8 females, 6 males), 8 with follicular thyroid cancer (FTC; 5 females, 3 males), 15 with medullary thyroid cancer (MTC; 10 females, 5 males), and 15 healthy controls (10 females, 5 males). Blood was drawn pre-surgery in EDTA-containing tubes, centrifuged immediately to separate plasma, and stored at −80°C until analysis. Exclusion criteria included use of thyroid-affecting medications, other cancers, or metabolic diseases (e.g., diabetes, metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance). A quality control (QC) sample was generated by pooling 15 µL aliquots from each resuspended plasma sample and inserted every ten study samples during GC/MS analysis to monitor instrumental stability and calculate relative standard deviation (RSD%). Sample extraction and analysis order was randomized to minimize batch effects, instrumental drift, and biological variation bias.
complete_workflow_description: The analytical workflow involved multiple software tools and specific processing steps: (1) Raw GC/MS data were converted from instrument output to CDF (NetCDF) format using ChemStation Data Analysis software (Agilent Technologies, v.B.04.03), then transformed to 'abf' format using the Reifycs Abf converter; (2) Automatic deconvolution, peak identification, gap filling, and peak alignment were performed using MS-DIAL software (v4.60) with integrated MS/MS reference libraries; metabolite identification was validated against NIST Mass Spectral Search Program (v2.0) using replib, mainlib, and Fiehn libraries with a similarity index cutoff ≥70%; (3) Data normalization (probabilistic quotient normalization, PQN), log transformation, and mean centring were applied using MetaboAnalyst (v6.0); (4) Multivariate modeling (PCA, PLS-DA, OPLS-DA) and statistical validation (CV-ANOVA, permutation testing) were carried out in SIMCA-P 14.0; (5) Univariate statistical analysis (one-way ANOVA, Tukey’s HSD) and false discovery rate (FDR) correction were implemented in MetaboAnalyst; (6) Visualization (boxplots, heatmaps, ROC curves, confusion matrices) was performed using R packages: 'ggplot2', 'ggpubr', 'tidyverse', 'corrplot', and 'pROC'; (7) Machine learning classification employed Random Forest (RF) with nested cross-validation (nested CV) in R, optimizing the mtry parameter in inner loops and aggregating out-of-fold (OOF) predictions; feature importance was assessed via Mean Decrease Accuracy (MDA) and Mean Decrease Gini (MDG); model validity was confirmed via 100-iteration permutation testing with label shuffling; (8) Metabolic pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using MetaboAnalyst (v6.0).

related_tool: SIMCA
sample_description: Adult sea lampreys were collected from the Ocqueoc River (Presque Isle, Michigan, USA) during the summer of 2020 migratory season by USGS Hammond Bay Biological Station staff. Animals were grouped by sex and sexual maturity status into four categories: preovulatory females (POF), ovulatory females (OF), prespermiating males (PSM), and spermiating males (SM). Sexual maturation was assessed daily via visual inspection of secondary sex characteristics and gentle gamete expression. Upon arrival at Michigan State University, animals were immediately processed: anesthetized with 0.2 g/L tricaine methanesulfonate (MS222), euthanized with >0.2 g/L MS222 plus decapitation, and liver tissues dissected. Liver samples (99.13 ± 15.67 mg per animal; n = 10 per group) were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C. Additional liver subsamples (246 ± 39 mg for glycogen assay; 265 ± 42 mg for triglyceride assay) were separately collected from the same individuals for biochemical assays. All animal procedures complied with Michigan State University’s Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (AUF # Li-02-17-030-99) and NIH Guide standards.
complete_workflow_description: Untargeted metabolomics raw data files (.raw) from UPLC-Q-Exactive Orbitrap analysis were converted to .mzML format using ProteoWizard (MSConvert; Chambers et al., 2012). Peak picking, retention time alignment, and deconvolution were performed using Progenesis QI (Waters Corp.) and Compound Discoverer 3.1 (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.), with parameters including abundance filter >10,000, retention time tolerance of 0.2 min, and mass error tolerance of 5 ppm. Discriminating features were selected using ANOVA p-value < 0.01 and fold change > 1.2. Metabolite annotation required MS2 spectra and applied dual confidence criteria: (1) ≥3 matches among mzCloud, Metabolika, ChemSpider, and predicted composition in Compound Discoverer 3.1; and (2) Progenesis QI annotation score > 45/60 and fragmentation score > 50/100. Additional annotation cross-referenced HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/) and KEGG (https://www.genome.jp/kegg) databases. Pathway analysis was conducted using MetaboAnalyst 4.0 (http://www.metaboanalyst.ca/), HMDB, and iPath v3 (https://pathways.embl.de/). Statistical analyses included ANOVA with Bonferroni/Dunn post hoc tests for glycogen and triglyceride concentrations, and multivariate modeling (PCA/PLS-DA) in SIMCA 17. Data visualization and simplified metabolic mapping were performed manually by the first author based on KEGG pathway maps.

related_tool: SIMCA
sample_description: The study used nine tobacco extract samples (labeled 1-9) prepared from American tobacco leaves (upper tobacco B2F, middle tobacco C2F, and lower tobacco C3F) sourced from Henan Zhongyan Industrial Co., Ltd. Samples 1–5 were prepared by extracting five 20 g portions of crushed tobacco in 200 g of 60% ethanol via heated reflux for durations ranging from 4 to 12 h (in 2 h increments), followed by filtration and vacuum concentration. Samples 6–8 were obtained from three sequential 12 h heated reflux extractions of 50 kg crushed tobacco in 500 kg of 60% ethanol/water solution. Sample 9 was derived from sample 6: 50 g of sample 6 was mixed with 500 g of 95% ethanol, heated at 80 °C for 1 h, frozen at −18 °C for 24 h, then filtered and vacuum-concentrated to isolate pure tobacco oil. Prior to analysis, each of samples 1–9 was prepared by accurately weighing 10 mL of extract or oil into a 500 mL volumetric flask and allowing gaseous components to fully evaporate at room temperature for 4 h; this preparation was repeated nine times per sample to confirm stability. Additionally, leaf position samples (B2F, C2F, C3F) were roasted and air-dried, with 10 g of each placed in 500 mL glass injection vials and allowed to settle overnight for complete evaporation; this was repeated 40 times per leaf position. All samples were analyzed using a homemade E-nose system equipped with 40+ sensor types (e.g., TGS813, TGS2610D, MS1100, WSP2110, PID-AH, MEMS, electrochemical, and metal oxide sensors) and validated against GC–MS (Shimadzu Corporation). Sensor signals were acquired at 1 Hz sampling frequency, resulting in 300 × 40 matrices (300 s measurement time × 40 sensors), and subjected to feature extraction using five defined features: (1) baseline value in air, (2) median sensor data during sampling stage, (3) median of top 5 maximum values during sampling minus baseline, (4) second-to-last slope from sorted slope vector (K = (y₁−y₂)/(x₁−x₂), x₁−x₂ = 5), and (5) sum of differences between each sampling point and baseline.
complete_workflow_description: The data processing procedure involved multiple software-based analytical steps: (1) Feature extraction from raw 300 × 40 sensor signal matrices using five manually defined features (Table 2); (2) Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction and redundancy removal — PCA was applied to extract dominant variance-capturing components, reducing high-dimensional sensor spectra into two dimensions for visualization and preliminary discrimination (e.g., Fig. 2a); (3) Statistical Quality Control (SQC) and Soft Independent Modeling of Class Analogy (SIMCA) algorithms were sequentially applied to the PCA-transformed feature data to evaluate stability against reference confidence intervals of standard stable tobacco extracts; (4) For tobacco leaf position identification, seven machine learning algorithms were implemented — Adaboost (Nlearn = 121, Learners = Tree), Random Forest (nTree = 59), K-Nearest Neighbors (K = 37), Linear SVM (SVM type s = 0, kernel t = 0, penalty C = 0.1), Radial-basis SVM (s = 0, t = 1, C = 0.01), Extreme Learning Machine (nHidden = 45), and Ensemble of ELMs (number of ELMs = 7); (5) All models underwent hyperparameter optimization via grid search and performance evaluation using five-fold cross-validation repeated 20 times to compute average accuracy; (6) The homemade E-nose system’s embedded software supported real-time acquisition at 1 Hz, with configurable parameters including flush time (20 s), zero point trim time (20 s), pre-sampling time (5 s), measurement time (30 s), sensor flow (400 mL/min), and initial injection flow (100 or 200 mL/min); (7) GC–MS analysis used Shimadzu instrumentation with nitrogen blower concentration (60 °C water bath) to reduce sample volume to 3–5 mL, followed by vortex mixing and top-layer injection.

sample_description: The study used 64 male C57BL/6 mice (5 weeks old, 16–17 g) divided into normal control (NC, n=24) and high-fat diet (HFD, n=40) groups for 8 weeks to establish an obesity model. After successful modeling (HFD group weight >10% higher than NC), 20 obese and 20 normal-weight mice were further assigned to four experimental groups (n=10 per group): high-altitude obese (HOB), high-altitude normal (HN), low-altitude obese (LOB), and low-altitude normal (LN). Fresh fecal samples (~5 g per mouse) were collected using sterile plastic cassettes, immediately placed on ice, transported to the lab within 2 h, and stored at −80°C prior to analysis. Fecal microbiota and metabolomics analyses were performed after 30 days of hypoxic/ambient exposure.
complete_workflow_description: The untargeted metabolomics workflow involved: (1) stool sample homogenization and centrifugation to obtain supernatant; (2) preparation of pooled quality control (QC) samples by equal-volume mixing of all supernatants; (3) UHPLC-MS/MS analysis using a Vanquish UHPLC system coupled to an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer with a Hypesil Gold column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm); (4) gradient elution over 17 min at 0.2 mL/min with mobile phases optimized for positive mode (0.1% formic acid in water / methanol) and negative mode (5 mM ammonium acetate pH 9.0 / methanol); (5) mass spectrometry operated in dual polarity mode with spray voltage 3.5 kV, capillary temperature 320°C, sheath gas 35 psi, aux gas 10 L/min, S-lens RF level 60, and aux gas heater temperature 350°C; (6) raw data processing using Compound Discoverer 3.1 (Thermo Fisher) for peak alignment, peak picking, quantitation, and metabolite annotation against KEGG, HMDB, and LIPIDMaps databases; (7) integration of positive/negative datasets into a single matrix; (8) multivariate statistical analysis in SIMCA-P+14.0 (PCA and OPLS-DA); (9) univariate analysis including fold change (FC > 1.2 or FC < 0.833) and t-tests with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction (P < 0.05); (10) pathway enrichment and mapping using MetaboAnalyst 5.0; and (11) final differential metabolite identification based on VIP > 1, |FC| > 1.2 (or < 0.833), and FDR-adjusted P < 0.05.
